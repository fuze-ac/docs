# FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix

## Executive Summary

This matrix controls how FUZE describes public status across products, shared platform rails, token and wallet mechanisms, market access, evidence, and documentation.

Its purpose is to prevent a concept from being presented as a design, a design from being treated as an implementation, a prototype from being treated as a release, or technical deployment from being read as operational activation.

The current public paper system establishes FUZE's public position, product definitions, intended workflows, policy models, and documentation structure. Public documentation does not by itself prove implementation, testing, release, live operation, adoption, revenue, contract activation, token circulation, liquidity, or market access.

Readers should use this matrix together with the most specific product, evidence, token, governance, or market-access record available for the claim being reviewed.

## Purpose and Authority

This paper provides:

- controlled public status vocabulary;
- claim-to-evidence requirements;
- the current public status of FUZE platform and product areas;
- distinctions among roadmap, implementation, release, and activation;
- rules for status changes and corrections; and
- routing to deeper specialist evidence and policy papers.

This matrix controls public status language. It does not replace the specialist paper that controls a product definition, token allocation, legal rule, contract, wallet mechanism, market venue, or risk category.

## Status Vocabulary

| Status | Public meaning | Minimum evidence direction |
|---|---|---|
| Public position | An approved principle, explanation, or ecosystem direction used in public communication | Current public paper and terminology review |
| Concept | A user problem, intended audience, and proposed capability are identified | Concept brief, research summary, or product paper |
| Research | User, market, technical, legal, security, or operating questions are being investigated | Research notes, interviews, comparisons, or review records |
| Design | Scope, workflow, controls, data relationships, or architecture are defined | Specifications, flows, interfaces, data models, or policy design |
| Prototype | A partial implementation demonstrates selected behavior in a bounded environment | Reviewable build, sandbox, demonstration, or sample output |
| Internal testing | Authorized internal users are evaluating defined behavior | Test scope, participants, results, defects, and remediation records |
| Limited release | A controlled user, customer, or partner group can access the product or feature | Release record, onboarding scope, support process, and monitoring |
| Public beta | Public access exists with known limitations and an active feedback process | Public route, current terms, version notes, issue process, and monitoring |
| Live | The intended service is available under current published operating terms | Production access, support, monitoring, current terms, and operational evidence |
| Paused | Development, access, or operation is temporarily stopped | Pause record, affected scope, reason, owner, and review criteria |
| Under review | Scope, controls, status, or continued operation is being reassessed | Review record, responsible owner, and next-decision criteria |
| Activation-gated | Preparation may continue, but operation requires specified approvals and readiness conditions | Gate checklist, technical evidence, policy evidence, authorization, and public status |
| Retired or replaced | A product, feature, policy, or mechanism is no longer the current path | Status notice, effective date, and replacement or archive reference |

A status applies only to the scope named in its supporting record. One module may be live while another remains in design. A product may be available while its token-connected feature remains activation-gated.

## Status Progression Rules

Status progression is evidence-based rather than automatic.

| Earlier state | Stronger state | What must change before the stronger state can be used |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Design | Scope, workflows, controls, and boundaries are defined |
| Design | Prototype | A reviewable implementation demonstrates the stated behavior |
| Prototype | Internal testing | A defined test process is active and results are recorded |
| Internal testing | Limited release | Authorized external access, support, and operating scope exist |
| Limited release | Public beta | Public access and known-limitations communication exist |
| Public beta | Live | Production terms, support, monitoring, and operating evidence are established |
| Release | Adoption | Measured use by relevant users is evidenced |
| Adoption | Paid delivery | A customer or user has paid for the delivered product or service |
| Paid delivery | Revenue | Payment and accounting records are confirmed and reconciled |
| Contract preparation | Deployment | The contract is verified on the correct network with deployment evidence |
| Deployment | Activation | Governance, security, legal, operational, reporting, and other required gates are completed |
| Market planning | Live market access | The venue, route, contract, pool, network, and public availability are verified |

Roadmap language does not advance status. A stronger status should be recorded only after the evidence supporting that status exists.

## Claim-to-Evidence Rules

FUZE should match evidence to the exact statement being made.

| Claim | Evidence that supports the claim |
|---|---|
| A product is defined | Product purpose, primary users, workflow, boundaries, and current status |
| A prototype exists | Reviewable demonstration of the specified behavior |
| Testing is underway | Test scope, authorized participants, results, defects, and unresolved issues |
| A limited release exists | Access records, operating scope, onboarding, support, and monitoring |
| A public beta exists | Public route, current limitations, terms, feedback path, and operational support |
| A product is live | Production access, current terms, monitoring, support, and operating evidence |
| Usage exists | Product logs or public-safe aggregate activity records |
| Adoption exists | Sustained or relevant use by the intended audience under a defined measurement method |
| Paid delivery exists | Payment matched to the product or service that was delivered |
| Revenue exists | Confirmed and reconciled commercial or accounting records |
| A policy is approved | Versioned policy and authorized approval record |
| A contract is deployed | Verified network, address, code or audit reference, and deployment record |
| A gated mechanism is active | Completed readiness gates, governance authorization, public status, and operating process |
| Market access is live | Verified public availability through the named venue and route |

A screenshot may support interface evidence. A demonstration may support bounded implementation. Neither independently proves production availability, adoption, revenue, or operational reliability.

## Product Commercial Status

Product definition, product availability, paid delivery, and revenue are separate statuses.

| Commercial claim | Current public treatment | Evidence required before the claim is used |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing is defined | Product-specific pricing design or published terms | Current approved pricing and applicable conditions |
| A product is available for purchase | Requires a current release and purchase route | Public or controlled access, checkout or contract process, terms, and support |
| Paid delivery exists | Not inferred from pricing or interest | Payment matched to the delivered product or service |
| Revenue exists | Not inferred from fundraising, deposits, or token activity | Confirmed and reconciled commercial records |
| Recurring revenue exists | Not inferred from one payment | Reconciled period records and a defined recurring measurement method |
| Product-market adoption exists | Not inferred from followers, interest, or demonstrations | Relevant usage, retention, customer, or operator evidence |

Fundraising proceeds, investor commitments, token purchases, treasury transfers, or internal test payments should not be classified as product revenue.

## Roadmap Interpretation

A roadmap expresses intended sequence and priorities. It does not establish delivery or a guaranteed date.

Roadmap items should identify, where useful:

1. the product, rail, policy, or mechanism in scope;
2. the current status;
3. the next evidence milestone;
4. material dependencies;
5. whether the milestone is product, technical, commercial, governance, legal, security, or market related; and
6. the status record that will confirm completion.

A roadmap milestone should be marked complete only when the evidence required for that milestone exists.

## Status Changes and Corrections

A material status update should identify:

1. the product, module, policy, contract, venue, or mechanism affected;
2. the previous status and the new status;
3. the scope and effective date or time of the change;
4. the evidence supporting the update;
5. remaining limitations or dependencies;
6. the approving owner or governance reference where applicable; and
7. any correction, rollback, pause, support, or replacement path.

Public records should be versioned when a change affects user access, eligibility, custody, token movement, product availability, payment, market access, or material risk.

A correction should explain the changed statement without exposing confidential evidence, protected identity, credentials, signer information, or security-sensitive procedures.

## Public Boundary

Status reporting improves clarity but cannot replace product testing, professional review, security work, audits, customer evidence, accounting records, legal analysis, governance authorization, or market reality.

Roadmap direction and design status do not establish delivery dates, adoption, revenue, AI accuracy, wallet eligibility, approved value, token demand, liquidity, listing, price behavior, distribution, or investment outcomes.

Readers should rely on the most specific current status record and the evidence appropriate to the exact claim being evaluated.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE status language separates public position, concept, research, design, implementation, testing, release, live operation, adoption, commercial proof, and activation.
- A stronger status requires stronger evidence; it does not arise from roadmap language or time passing.
- Product papers define intended workflows but do not independently prove implementation or availability.
- Allocation is separate from release and circulation.
- Contract deployment is separate from mechanism activation.
- Product payment, paid delivery, and reconciled revenue are separate commercial states.
- DEX-first direction is not a live-liquidity claim, and CEX stages must be described precisely.
- Status applies to the specific scope named in the supporting record.
- Public corrections should preserve clarity without exposing protected or security-sensitive evidence.
