# FUZE Public Documentation Map

## Executive Summary

The FUZE Public Documentation Map explains how the full FUZE paper system connects across public index papers, core platform papers, AI SaaS product papers, tokenomics and governance papers, investor and partner papers, website summaries, private wallet-participation structure papers, and whitepaper materials.

This paper is different from the Public Paper Index. The index lists the papers. This documentation map explains how the papers work together, which papers support each other, and how different readers can move from high-level understanding to deeper review.

FUZE is a transparency-first AI SaaS platform building practical products on shared infrastructure for identity, credits, payments, AI orchestration, wallet-based records, reporting, and ecosystem participation.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token. FUZE token supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability. Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and remain separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. FUZE uses wallet-level transparency and does not publish personal identity publicly.

This documentation map helps readers understand where each concept lives and prevents confusion between product usage, token utility, credits, wallet-based participation, investor materials, and private review materials.

## 1. Purpose of This Documentation Map

The FUZE paper system covers many connected topics. A reader may enter the documentation from different angles:

- A community member may want to understand FUZE token and ZAGA.
- A product user may want to understand HerHelp, ShopOS AI, or Platform Credits.
- An investor may want to understand revenue direction, token design, risks, and use of funds.
- A partner may want to understand collaboration surfaces.
- A reviewer may want to understand risk boundaries, legal messaging, data privacy, and treasury controls.
- A technical reader may want to understand smart-contract readiness, wallet records, vaults, and reporting rails.

This paper maps those paths.

The purpose is to make the documentation system easier to navigate without repeating every paper’s content.

## 12. Documentation Boundaries

The documentation map follows these boundaries:

### Public papers

Public papers explain FUZE clearly without exposing private agreements, private identity, sensitive treasury details, restricted legal analysis, or investor-specific private terms.

### Investor papers

Investor papers explain FUZE from a business and review perspective, but they do not replace legal documents, private agreements, investment documents, tax advice, or financial advice.

### Private wallet participation papers

Private wallet participation papers prepare deeper legal, accounting, treasury, eligibility, smart-contract, and investor review. They are not public marketing documents.

### Website summaries

Website summaries are shorter public explanations. They must not remove important boundaries.

### Whitepaper materials

Whitepaper materials synthesize the system and must preserve product, token, credit, wallet, risk, and reporting boundaries.

## 13. How Updates Should Flow Through the System

When FUZE changes a major decision, updates should flow in this order:

1. Update the relevant private or source-of-truth paper if the topic is sensitive.
2. Update the affected tokenomics, product, platform, or investor paper.
3. Update the public FAQ if the change affects common questions.
4. Update the status and roadmap matrix if readiness or status changes.
5. Update the public language and boundary index if the wording changes.
6. Update website summaries only after the deeper source papers are stable.
7. Update whitepaper materials after the paper system is aligned.

This update flow prevents website or community materials from moving ahead of the source papers.

## 14. Reader Guidance

Readers should not treat one paper as the whole FUZE system.

A product paper explains a product.

A tokenomics paper explains a token, credit, wallet, treasury, or governance topic.

An investor paper explains an investor review topic.

A private wallet participation paper explains internal or private review material.

A website summary gives a simplified public version.

A whitepaper gives the master narrative.

The strongest understanding comes from reading the paper that matches the reader’s question.
