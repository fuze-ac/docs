
# FUZE Public Language and Boundary Index

## Executive Summary

This index controls how FUZE public papers choose, structure, and review language across products, platform rails, tokenomics, wallets, market access, investor communication, and risk.

FUZE public communication should begin with the reader's practical question. Product papers lead with users and workflows. Platform papers explain shared capabilities. Investor and partner papers lead with evidence, value, responsibilities, and review needs. Token, wallet, market-access, and risk papers explain their own specialist mechanisms without taking over unrelated product narratives.

This index also protects essential distinctions among product usage, Platform Credits, FUZE token, stablecoin operations, wallet-based records, public-safe transparency, and protected identity. When a statement depends on implementation, testing, release, activation, approval, or external acceptance, the wording must match the current verified status.

## Purpose and Authority

Use this paper when creating, shortening, reviewing, or correcting public FUZE material.

It helps writers and reviewers answer five questions:

1. What should this paper explain first?
2. Which concepts belong in this paper?
3. Which topics should be summarized and linked to a specialist source?
4. Does each statement match current evidence and status?
5. Does the draft reveal, promise, or imply more than FUZE can safely publish?

This paper controls editorial routing and public wording boundaries. It does not independently establish product availability, token rights, wallet eligibility, revenue, legal status, market access, or implementation.

For the approved paper inventory, use the [FUZE Public Paper Index](./00-FUZE_PUBLIC_PAPER_INDEX.md). For current status language, use the [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](./02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md).

## Public Writing Principles

FUZE public writing should be:

- product-first;
- audience-specific;
- evidence-aware;
- status-accurate;
- public-safe;
- practical and human-readable;
- precise about credits, token, stablecoins, wallets, and market access;
- concise where a specialist paper already owns the detail; and
- clear about what is defined, designed, implemented, tested, released, live, or activation-gated.

Consistency means stable facts, definitions, and boundaries. It does not mean repeating the same introduction, token paragraph, risk list, or closing section in every paper.

## Communication Priority by Paper Type

| Paper type | Lead with | Add when material | Route to specialist papers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public index or FAQ | Reader navigation, common questions, status interpretation | Short references to controlling papers | Deep mechanism and risk detail |
| Core platform | Platform purpose, shared capabilities, and system relationships | Credits, payments, AI, permissions, wallets, and reporting | Individual product detail and complete risk treatment |
| Individual product | Primary user, problem, workflow, capabilities, controls, status, and examples | Product-specific credits, rails, data, permissions, reporting, or token relationship | General tokenomics, participation, market access, and unrelated platform detail |
| Product policy or bridge | The named product-system relationship or policy | Evidence gates, launch sequence, credit or token connection | Repeated product introductions |
| Investor | Product thesis, current evidence, commercial model, execution priorities, and evaluation questions | Funding, governance, treasury, token context, and risk where relevant | Private terms and protected diligence evidence |
| Partner | Mutual value, qualification, integration, responsibilities, data boundaries, and lifecycle | Commercial rails or ecosystem utility required by the model | Unrelated token, wallet, or market narratives |
| Tokenomics or governance | The named token, wallet, vault, circulation, utility, or governance mechanism | Credits, stablecoins, eligibility, market, or legal context needed by that mechanism | Repeated product catalogues |
| Website summary | One audience, one main message, current status, and a route to deeper material | Only the distinctions required for first contact | Long definitions, full inventories, and disclaimer lists |
| Whitepaper or architecture | Connected ecosystem model, dependencies, architecture, and evidence direction | Product, rail, credit, token, wallet, governance, roadmap, and risk synthesis | Silent changes to specialist facts |
| Risk and disclosure | The applicable uncertainty, dependency, limitation, and reader boundary | Cross-references to the affected product or mechanism | Promotional claims |

Within a paper, explain practical purpose before secondary infrastructure. Platform Credits should follow the product action they support. FUZE token should appear only where ecosystem utility or the paper's mechanism makes it relevant. Detailed risk treatment should not displace the paper's primary reader objective.

## Controlled Core Terms

### FUZE

Use **FUZE** for the ecosystem, platform direction, or organization context described by the paper.

A suitable high-level description is:

> FUZE is a product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem building practical products on shared platform rails for identity, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, wallet-based records, reporting, and ecosystem participation.

Do not use this full description mechanically in every paper. Adapt it to the reader and topic while preserving the product-first position.

### Platform Credits

Use **Platform Credits** for product usage credits.

Appropriate language includes:

- Platform Credits support product consumption;
- a product action may consume Platform Credits;
- credit balances and usage records may support billing and limits; and
- product-specific terms control pricing, expiry, refunds, and consumption.

Platform Credits must remain separate from FUZE token. Do not imply that buying or holding Platform Credits automatically creates token ownership, investment exposure, wallet participation, or payment rights.

### FUZE Token

Use **FUZE token** for the single ecosystem token.

Appropriate contextual roles may include:

- product-connected utility;
- ecosystem participation;
- community or partner programs;
- wallet-based records;
- governance direction where applicable; and
- participation frameworks where defined, approved, and activated.

Do not present FUZE token as an automatic payout instrument, guaranteed income instrument, guaranteed yield, guaranteed profit share, guaranteed listing asset, or guaranteed market-access instrument.

### Stablecoins

Use **stablecoins** for approved operational functions such as:

- product payment;
- settlement;
- treasury movement; or
- compensation.

Stablecoin use does not by itself establish revenue, token rewards, holder distributions, investment returns, or public payout promises.

### Wallet-Based Records

Use **wallet-based records** for relevant public-safe technical records such as:

- token-holding records;
- snapshot references;
- vault balances or transactions;
- eligibility status;
- claim status;
- governance references;
- report hashes; or
- product-connected access records.

A wallet address is not a public identity directory. Personal identity, account evidence, tax records, customer information, and wallet-to-person mappings remain protected unless specifically approved for disclosure.

### Wallet-Based Participation

Use **wallet-based participation** only for the activation-gated framework connected to eligible FUZE-holding wallets where supported and approved.

Token holding may be relevant, but it does not by itself establish eligibility, approved value, a claim, or payment. Applicable rules may also depend on custody, snapshots, verification, jurisdiction, governance, reconciliation, reporting, and completed activation gates.

### Approved Distributable Value

Use **approved distributable value** only when the paper specifically requires the term.

It refers to value that has passed the applicable reconciliation, deductions, reserves, accounting, treasury, legal, audit, governance, and reporting process for an activated framework.

Do not rewrite it as automatic revenue share, guaranteed holder payment, guaranteed distribution, or entitlement based only on token holding.

## Status-Sensitive Language

Statements about readiness, availability, activation, adoption, revenue, approval, or performance must match current evidence.

| Evidence state | Suitable public language |
|---|---|
| A product problem and direction are identified | Concept, direction, intended use, or proposed capability |
| Workflows, controls, or architecture are defined | Designed to, design-stage, defined model, or planned workflow |
| A bounded implementation can be reviewed | Prototype demonstrates, prototype-stage, or sandbox implementation |
| Authorized internal evaluation is occurring | Under internal testing, with the tested scope identified |
| A controlled external group has access | Limited release, controlled pilot, or scoped access |
| Public access exists with known limitations | Public beta, with the current route and limitations identified |
| Production service is available under current terms | Live, with production access, support, monitoring, and terms |
| Operation awaits defined approvals or controls | Activation-gated, pending the named readiness conditions |
| Scope or operation is being reassessed | Under review |
| Operation or development is temporarily stopped | Paused |
| A capability is no longer the current path | Retired or replaced |

Use **is intended to** for intended behavior.

Use **is designed to** for design-stage behavior.

Use **a prototype demonstrates** only when prototype evidence exists.

Use **under internal testing** only when current test evidence exists.

Use **limited release**, **public beta**, or **live** only when the evidence required for that status exists.

Do not silently upgrade:

- concept into design;
- design into prototype;
- prototype into testing;
- testing into release;
- release into adoption;
- adoption into paid delivery;
- paid delivery into reconciled revenue;
- allocation into release or circulation;
- contract preparation into deployment;
- deployment into activation;
- DEX planning into live liquidity; or
- CEX discussion into application, approval, or live listing.

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](./02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) controls these distinctions.

## Product and AI Claims

Product writing should describe:

- the intended user;
- the problem being addressed;
- the workflow;
- inputs and outputs;
- permissions and controls;
- current status;
- reporting or review; and
- product-specific limitations.

Practical examples should be framed as examples rather than proof that every user will receive the same outcome.

AI features may assist with generation, analysis, interpretation, moderation, reporting, education, or decisions. Public language should identify human review, permissions, source limits, confidence limits, or escalation paths where they materially affect safe use.

Avoid language that presents AI output as:

- infallible;
- guaranteed accurate;
- professionally conclusive;
- autonomous beyond its approved authority;
- a substitute for required human judgment; or
- certain to create a business, market, community, or financial outcome.

Apply the following specialist boundaries:

- QTB should not be framed as autonomous trading execution or assured trading performance.
- AIMM should not be framed as protecting price, guaranteeing liquidity, guaranteeing depth, or controlling venue decisions.
- ToolGrid AI sponsored visibility must remain distinguishable from neutral assessment or editorial recommendation.
- Botmad must remain permission-controlled and subject to human authority.
- ZAGA gameplay language must remain separate from real-token, stablecoin, withdrawal, earning, or reward claims unless those features are specifically evidenced and activated.

## Token, Market, and Financial Claims

Token, market, and financial wording requires the narrowest accurate statement.

### Supply and Allocation

Use the current controlling allocation paper for supply, categories, percentages, and amounts.

Allocation does not prove:

- release;
- circulation;
- utility activation;
- market access;
- contract deployment; or
- demand.

### Revenue

Use **revenue** only when confirmed and reconciled commercial or accounting records support the claim.

Do not classify the following as product revenue without the required evidence:

- investor funds;
- token-sale proceeds;
- deposits;
- treasury transfers;
- internal test payments;
- unpaid orders;
- expressions of interest; or
- partnership discussions.

### Market Access

FUZE's public direction is DEX-first. This is an intended sequence, not a live-liquidity claim.

Use precise stages:

- planning;
- preparation;
- application;
- review;
- approval; and
- live access.

Do not treat these stages as interchangeable.

Avoid claims of guaranteed liquidity, resale, market depth, listing, application success, approval, market support, or price behavior.

The [FUZE Liquidity and Listing Policy](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/21-FUZE_LIQUIDITY_AND_LISTING_POLICY_PUBLIC.md) and [FUZE Exchange and Market Access Boundary](../INVESTOR-PARTNER-PAPERS/19-FUZE_EXCHANGE_AND_MARKET_ACCESS_BOUNDARY_PUBLIC.md) control detailed market language.

## Investor, Partner, and Community Wording

### Investor Communication

Investor-facing material should support evaluation through:

- product purpose;
- current status;
- implementation and evidence;
- commercial model;
- execution priorities;
- use of funds;
- governance;
- token context where relevant;
- material risks; and
- public versus permissioned diligence boundaries.

Public investor material should not be written as an offer document unless a separately authorized process establishes that role.

Do not invent or overstate:

- valuation;
- completed financing;
- investor commitments;
- revenue;
- adoption;
- return;
- exit;
- acquisition;
- token price; or
- listing outcomes.

### Partner Communication

Partner material should identify:

- mutual value;
- qualification criteria;
- scope;
- responsibilities;
- integration dependencies;
- permissions and data handling;
- evidence and reporting;
- commercial boundaries; and
- review, pause, renewal, or exit conditions.

Do not publish an organization's identity, negotiated terms, customer information, credentials, integration secrets, or confidential records without approval.

### Community Communication

Community communication may be concise and energetic, but brevity does not remove the evidence requirement.

Announcements should identify:

- what changed;
- the affected scope;
- the effective date where relevant;
- the current status;
- any material limitation; and
- where the official record can be reviewed.

Questions about financial, legal, eligibility, claim, exchange, custody, or privacy consequences should be routed to the responsible specialist paper or reviewer.

## Public and Private Information Boundaries

Public papers may include approved public information and approved public-safe summaries.

Do not publish, infer, or expose:

- investor identities or contact details;
- customer personal information;
- wallet-to-person mappings;
- private round terms;
- negotiated investor rights;
- signed agreements;
- private legal opinions;
- accounting workpapers;
- bank or tax records;
- identity documents;
- private contributor compensation;
- private exchange, custody, or market-maker discussions;
- multisig signer identities;
- credentials, API keys, private keys, or seed phrases;
- sensitive treasury procedures;
- exploitable vulnerabilities; or
- restricted infrastructure details.

Public wallet addresses, contract addresses, transaction references, vault records, and report hashes may appear only when verified, approved for public disclosure, linked to the correct network and status, and relevant to the claim.

When a private fact is relevant but not publishable, use public-safe language such as:

- detailed terms are available through qualified private review;
- protected records remain permissioned;
- public reporting uses aggregate or wallet-level records where appropriate; or
- sensitive operational details are excluded from public documentation.

## Claim Escalation

Escalate a statement before publication when it:

- assigns a date, quantity, allocation, price, supply figure, revenue amount, or performance result;
- says a product, contract, token mechanism, claim process, venue, partnership, or funding round is active;
- describes legal status, regulatory treatment, exchange action, or external approval;
- links token holding to value, eligibility, payment, income, yield, distribution, or investment outcome;
- introduces a wallet, contract, pool, custody instruction, payment route, or treasury process;
- publishes data that could identify a customer, investor, contributor, signer, or wallet owner;
- characterizes AI, market, game, business, or community outcomes as certain;
- claims adoption, paid delivery, recurring revenue, or commercial proof; or
- changes a controlling term, status, number, or public boundary.

The reviewer should:

1. locate the controlling public source;
2. confirm that it is current;
3. identify the exact evidence category;
4. reduce the wording to the supported scope;
5. confirm public disclosure approval; and
6. obtain the appropriate product, finance, legal, privacy, security, governance, or leadership review.

When approval or evidence cannot be verified, use the narrower documented position or omit the nonessential claim.

## Editorial Review Workflow

1. Write a one-sentence objective naming the primary audience and the question the paper answers.
2. Confirm the exact requested path and the paper's controlling topic.
3. Read the current file and the smallest complete set of controlling sources.
4. Confirm product names, numbers, status, evidence, and public/private classification.
5. Order the draft around reader value before secondary infrastructure, token, market, or risk detail.
6. Remove generic paragraphs that do not advance the file's objective.
7. Replace repeated specialist explanations with contextual wording and a correct internal link.
8. Check that Platform Credits, FUZE token, stablecoins, wallet records, and private identity remain separate.
9. Test every material claim against the status and evidence rules.
10. Escalate sensitive statements and narrow or omit unsupported details.
11. Validate internal links, exact filenames, title case, headings, tables, lists, and Markdown syntax.
12. Read the paper once as the intended audience and remove material better handled elsewhere.

A successful review leaves the reader with a clear understanding of the requested paper rather than a compressed duplicate of the entire FUZE corpus.

## Public Boundary

This index governs public language, topic routing, and editorial consistency. It does not establish product availability, token ownership rights, wallet eligibility, approved value, commercial terms, market access, legal conclusions, or professional advice.

FUZE public material should reveal enough approved evidence to support the stated purpose while protecting personal identity, confidential operations, security-sensitive information, and private partner, customer, contributor, or investor records.

Persuasive wording cannot replace implementation, testing, release evidence, reconciled accounts, governance approval, legal review, exchange confirmation, or market reality.

## Key Takeaways

- Each FUZE paper should perform one clear job for one primary audience.
- Practical user or reader value should come before secondary infrastructure, token, market, or risk detail.
- Platform Credits, FUZE token, stablecoins, wallet records, and private identity have separate roles.
- Product descriptions must not silently upgrade design into implementation or live operation.
- Allocation is not circulation, deployment is not activation, and market planning is not live access.
- AI and specialist-product language must preserve human authority and product-specific boundaries.
- Public-safe transparency should expose only the evidence required for the claim.
- Sensitive, numerical, financial, legal, token, wallet, and market statements require source and approval review.
- Specialist papers control their own facts; summary papers should link rather than rewrite them.
