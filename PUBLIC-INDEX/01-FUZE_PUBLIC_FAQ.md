# FUZE Public FAQ

## Executive Summary

This FAQ answers common public questions about FUZE in plain language.

FUZE is a product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem building practical products on shared platform rails for identity, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, permissions, wallet-based records, and reporting. Readers can begin with the product or question relevant to them rather than learning the entire ecosystem first.

The answers below explain what FUZE is building, how its products relate to the platform, why Platform Credits and FUZE token have separate roles, how stablecoins and wallets may support approved workflows, and how readers can distinguish public design from implementation, release, live operation, adoption, or revenue.

## FUZE Basics

### What is FUZE?

FUZE brings together practical AI SaaS products, game products, specialist intelligence tools, and Web3-ready ecosystem infrastructure.

Its products are designed for focused workflows in areas such as shops, spreadsheets, training, communities, events, games, market research, utility discovery, and permission-controlled digital work. Where useful, those products may connect to shared platform capabilities rather than rebuilding the same account, credit, payment, AI, permission, wallet, and reporting functions separately.

The [FUZE One Page Overview](../WEBSITE-SUMMARY-PAPERS/01-FUZE_ONE_PAGE_OVERVIEW_PUBLIC.md) provides the shortest public introduction.

### Why is FUZE described as product-first?

FUZE begins with useful product workflows because products give people a practical reason to use the ecosystem.

A shop operator may first care about orders, queues, customers, stock, or reporting. A trainer may care about lessons and quizzes. A community operator may care about support, moderation, summaries, and escalation. Shared rails, Platform Credits, wallets, and token utility become relevant only where they improve or connect those experiences.

The [FUZE Product-First Execution Model](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/03-FUZE_PRODUCT_FIRST_EXECUTION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) explains this sequence in more detail.

### Is FUZE one application?

No. FUZE is an ecosystem of distinct products connected by a common platform direction.

Each product has its own primary users, workflows, permissions, data requirements, commercial model, and readiness status. Shared platform rails can reduce repeated infrastructure work without forcing every product to look or behave the same way.

### Who are FUZE products intended for?

Intended audiences include small businesses, shops, spreadsheet-based teams, trainers, educators, creators, community operators, Web3 communities, event teams, gamers, market researchers, strategic partners, and authorized internal operators.

Availability, supported features, pricing, regions, integrations, and operating terms depend on the status and rules of each product.

### Is every FUZE product live?

No. The current public papers define product roles, workflows, controls, and roadmap direction, but publication does not prove that every product is implemented, released, publicly available, adopted, or generating revenue.

The current public status matrix records the product portfolio primarily at design and public-documentation stages. Readers should use the [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](./02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) and product-specific evidence records before treating a capability as operational.

## Products

### What is HerHelp?

HerHelp is FUZE's practical AI SaaS product family. It is designed to turn recurring business and community tasks into structured, reviewable workflows rather than offering only a general chat interface.

The family includes SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, and CommunityLayer AI. See [HerHelp AI SaaS](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/02-HERHELP_AI_SAAS_PUBLIC.md).

### What does SheetLayer AI do?

SheetLayer AI supports spreadsheet and business-data workflows. Its design direction includes interpreting structured data, mapping fields, creating useful views, assisting with repeated processes, and helping teams work with business information more consistently.

A product description establishes the intended workflow. Stronger claims require prototype, testing, release, integration, and usage evidence appropriate to the specific capability.

### What does ShopOS AI do?

ShopOS AI is designed as an AI-supported shop operating system for practical shop, order, customer, queue, staff, and reporting workflows.

Its purpose is to help small operators coordinate daily work through focused tools rather than require a large enterprise system. The current public status should be checked before assuming that any particular device integration, payment path, customer feature, or automation is available.

### What do SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, and CommunityLayer AI do?

SpeakShop AI supports promotional scripts, sound packs, voice content, and speaker-announcement workflows.

TrainLayer AI supports learning materials, guides, quizzes, onboarding, and staff or customer education.

CommunityLayer AI supports moderation, safety, verification, support, reporting, summaries, and community operations with permission and escalation controls.

### What is ZAGA?

ZAGA is FUZE's game and product-connected token-utility family.

ZAGA Arena is designed as a fast battle-arena experience where players survive, fight bosses, collect in-game value representations, build Net Worth, and compete in Telegram-ready multiplayer arenas.

ZAGA Districts is a separate Telegram-native cyberpunk MMORPG where communities are designed to build cities, manage economies, hire NPCs, and compete for power.

ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts are separate products under the ZAGA brand. ZAGA Districts is not a mode inside ZAGA Arena.

### Does ZAGA currently provide real-token or stablecoin earnings?

The public game descriptions do not by themselves establish active real-token rewards, stablecoin rewards, withdrawals, earnings, or live token utility.

Any real-value or token-connected feature requires specific implementation evidence, applicable operating rules, technical and security readiness, governance approval, legal and jurisdiction review, reporting, and completed activation gates.

### What are QTB and AIMM?

QTB supports AI-assisted market research, market interpretation, structured intelligence, and decision-support workflows. It should not be read as autonomous trading execution or a substitute for independent judgment.

AIMM supports authorized liquidity-operations analysis, monitoring, decision support, controls, and reporting. It should not be described as protecting price, guaranteeing liquidity, autonomously trading without authority, or assuring market outcomes.

### What do AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad do?

AIE supports event intelligence and event-related workflows.

ToolGrid AI is designed as an AI utility discovery and sponsored-visibility network. Sponsored visibility should remain distinguishable from neutral assessment or editorial recommendation.

Botmad is a permission-controlled AI desktop work assistant. It can be described with the marketing label “AI Desktop Employee” only when human authority, permissions, approvals, and task boundaries remain clear.

## Platform Rails and Product Access

### What are FUZE's shared platform rails?

Shared platform rails are reusable capabilities that may support multiple products where genuinely required. The planned rail set includes identity, permissions, Platform Credits, payments and settlement, AI orchestration, wallet-aware records, evidence, and reporting.

The rails are a platform design direction. Their implementation and integration status must be supported by technical, test, release, and operating evidence.

### Can someone use a FUZE product without managing a wallet?

The platform direction supports familiar SaaS access for ordinary product workflows. A wallet should appear only where a specific Web3 feature, eligibility rule, public-safe record, token utility, or participation mechanism requires it.

The exact access model belongs to the product and its current operating rules.

### How can users pay for FUZE products?

Payment options can differ by product, market, jurisdiction, and release stage. Approved paths may include conventional payment methods or stablecoin settlement where the relevant operating process supports them.

A payment method is an operational rail. It does not change the underlying nature of the product service or prove revenue, token utility, rewards, or investment returns.

## Platform Credits

### What are Platform Credits?

Platform Credits are product usage credits. They may support access to or consumption of implemented FUZE product services such as AI generation, reports, workflow steps, training content, community operations, sponsored services, or work-assistance sessions.

The exact price, consumption rule, refund treatment, expiry treatment, and usage record belong to the product providing the service.

### Are Platform Credits the same as FUZE token?

No. Platform Credits and FUZE token have separate roles.

Platform Credits support product consumption, billing, limits, and usage records. FUZE token supports ecosystem participation and product-connected utility where defined, implemented, approved, and activated.

Buying, receiving, holding, or spending Platform Credits does not automatically create FUZE token rights, wallet participation rights, or investment exposure. The [FUZE Platform Credits Relationship](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/10-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_RELATIONSHIP_PUBLIC.md) controls the detailed distinction.

### Are Platform Credits freely tradable?

The public model treats Platform Credits as product usage credits rather than freely tradable investment assets.

Any transfer, refund, account, expiry, or promotional treatment must come from the applicable product terms rather than assumptions based on token markets.

## FUZE Token and Tokenomics

### Why does FUZE use one ecosystem token?

A single FUZE token identity reduces fragmentation across products and communities. Different products may define different utility surfaces without requiring a new public token for every product or feature.

The one-token model still keeps Platform Credits, stablecoin operations, product revenue, wallet records, and participation mechanisms functionally separate. The [FUZE One-Token Model One-Page](../WEBSITE-SUMMARY-PAPERS/06-FUZE_ONE_TOKEN_MODEL_ONE_PAGE_PUBLIC.md) provides the concise public explanation.

### What is the approved FUZE token supply?

The approved fixed supply is 500,000,000 FUZE across ten purpose-specific allocation categories.

The [FUZE Token Allocation Table](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/02-FUZE_TOKEN_ALLOCATION_TABLE_PUBLIC.md) is the controlling source for supply and allocation. Allocation policy does not establish contract deployment, token release, circulating supply, market access, or utility activation.

### Does allocation mean all FUZE tokens are circulating?

No. Allocation identifies the approved purpose assigned to a quantity of tokens.

Release and circulation depend on the applicable vault, vesting, eligibility, governance, readiness, technical, and reporting controls. An allocated balance, reserved balance, released balance, transferred balance, and circulating balance describe different states.

### What can FUZE token be used for?

Defined utility direction may include product-connected features, ZAGA experiences, community programs, ecosystem participation, partner activity, wallet-based records, and governance direction where applicable.

Each utility requires a clear product purpose, technical implementation, operating rules, permissions, status, and evidence. A general utility roadmap does not mean that every utility is active.

### Does holding FUZE guarantee payment, income, yield, or profit?

No. FUZE token should not be presented as an automatic payout instrument or as a guarantee of income, yield, profit, liquidity, listing, price behavior, or market access.

Any wallet-based participation framework is conditional, activation-gated, and governed by its applicable eligibility, reconciliation, approval, custody, reporting, legal, accounting, security, and jurisdiction requirements.

## Stablecoins

### What role do stablecoins play in FUZE?

Stablecoins may serve as payment, settlement, treasury, or compensation rails where approved and operationally supported.

Stablecoin use does not by itself establish product revenue, token rewards, holder distributions, investment returns, or public payout promises.

## Wallets, Participation, and Privacy

### What are wallet-based records?

Wallets may support public-safe records such as token-holding records, snapshot references, eligibility status, claim status, vault references, transaction references, product-connected access, or governance activity where the relevant framework is implemented and approved.

A wallet address is a technical identifier. FUZE does not need to publish the personal identity behind every address to provide useful wallet-level transparency.

### What is wallet-based participation?

Wallet-based participation is an activation-gated framework connected to eligible FUZE-holding wallets where supported and approved.

Holding FUZE may be one relevant condition, but it does not by itself establish eligibility, approved value, a claim, or payment. Applicable rules may also depend on custody, snapshots, verification, jurisdiction, governance approval, reporting, and completed readiness gates.

### Is wallet-based participation currently automatic or universally active?

No. Token holding, contract preparation, or deployment alone does not activate participation.

The current status matrix describes wallet-based participation as activation-gated. Operation requires completed legal, accounting, treasury, audit, security, custody, eligibility, privacy, reporting, governance, and jurisdiction gates, together with a clear public status and operating process.

The [FUZE Wallet-Based Participation One-Page](../WEBSITE-SUMMARY-PAPERS/07-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PARTICIPATION_ONE_PAGE_PUBLIC.md) provides a concise introduction.

### What is approved distributable value?

Approved distributable value is a controlled term used for value from defined product revenue pools that has passed the applicable reconciliation, deductions, reserves, accounting, treasury, legal, audit, governance, and reporting process for an activated framework.

Product revenue is an input to that process. It is not automatically approved distributable value, and token holding alone does not create an unconditional entitlement.

### How are self-custody and exchange custody different?

A self-custody wallet can generally provide direct address and signature evidence.

An exchange balance may sit behind an omnibus wallet controlled by the exchange. Eligibility or claim support may therefore require exchange cooperation, account evidence, an approved custody process, or different rules.

Custody treatment must be defined in the active framework rather than assumed from a displayed exchange balance.

### Does wallet transparency reveal a person's identity?

Public reporting may show a wallet address, transaction, snapshot reference, eligibility status, claim status, report hash, vault record, or correction reference where relevant.

Names, contact details, identity documents, account evidence, tax records, wallet-to-person mappings, and private agreements remain protected unless specifically approved for disclosure. Public reporting should expose only what is needed to understand or verify the relevant mechanism.

## Status, Evidence, and Reporting

### How can readers tell whether a feature is live?

Readers should look for an explicit current status supported by evidence. FUZE uses statuses such as public position, concept, research, design, prototype, internal testing, limited release, public beta, live, paused, under review, activation-gated, and retired or replaced.

A product paper can establish an intended workflow or design. It does not prove implementation, testing, release, adoption, revenue, contract activation, or market access.

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](./02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) defines the public status vocabulary.

### What evidence supports different claims?

Different claims require different evidence:

- a product definition requires a clear purpose, audience, workflow, and boundary;
- a prototype requires a reviewable demonstration;
- testing requires a defined scope, results, and open issues;
- a release requires access, operating scope, support, and monitoring records;
- live status requires production access, current terms, support, and operational evidence;
- usage requires product logs or public-safe aggregate activity records;
- revenue requires confirmed and reconciled payment or accounting records;
- contract deployment requires verified network, address, code or audit references, and deployment records; and
- activation requires completed gates, authorization, public status, and an operating process.

### What evidence can FUZE publish publicly?

Public-safe evidence may include product demonstrations, status records, release notes, aggregate usage categories, wallet or vault activity, transaction references, report hashes, governance actions, dashboards, and correction records.

Sensitive customer information, personal identity, security details, confidential commercial records, private investor terms, credentials, and protected operational evidence should remain permissioned.

### Why are there 99 public papers?

FUZE covers products, platform rails, Platform Credits, stablecoins, wallets, tokenomics, governance, investor review, architecture, market access, and risk.

Separate papers allow each topic to answer a distinct primary question while giving readers a clear path to deeper specialist sources. The [FUZE Public Paper Index](./00-FUZE_PUBLIC_PAPER_INDEX.md) lists every approved paper.

- Products remain distinct while sharing platform rails where useful.
- Public product definitions do not automatically establish implementation, release, adoption, or revenue.
- Platform Credits support product usage and remain separate from FUZE token.
- Stablecoins serve approved operational roles rather than automatic public payout promises.
- FUZE token utility requires specific product purpose, implementation, rules, approval, status, and evidence.
