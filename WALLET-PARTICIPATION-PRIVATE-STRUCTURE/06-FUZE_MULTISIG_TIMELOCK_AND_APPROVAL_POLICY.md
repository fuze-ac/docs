# FUZE Multisig Timelock and Approval Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE Multisig Timelock and Approval Policy defines the private control structure for approvals, signer responsibilities, timelock requirements, emergency pause authority, vault movements, contract actions, report publication, approved distributable value review, claim activation, distribution activation, correction records, and public language controls under the FUZE wallet-based participation framework.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE.

FUZE token supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token.

Wallet-based participation ability is not automatic for every wallet.

Wallet-based participation ability is not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Multisig controls and timelock controls exist to reduce operational, treasury, smart-contract, reporting, and governance risk.

Multisig approval does not create approved distributable value by itself.

Timelock activation does not create approved distributable value by itself.

Emergency pause authority does not create treasury control rights for the public.

Approval records do not guarantee payout, token price, liquidity, listing, resale ability, exit, or investment return.

Eligible FUZE-holding wallets may participate in approved distributable value from defined FUZE product revenue pools only if the wallet-based participation framework becomes active and all required gates are complete.

Required gates may include legal, accounting, treasury, audit, reporting, smart-contract, privacy, eligibility, operator, jurisdiction, product revenue pool, and approved distributable value gates.

Approved distributable value is separate from product revenue, gross revenue, net revenue, stablecoin balances, Platform Credit purchases, treasury balances, vault balances, token allocation categories, seed-round funds, investor funds, token sale proceeds, reserves, partner pass-through funds, and non-revenue transfers.

FUZE uses wallet-level transparency by default, but FUZE does not publish personal identity publicly.

Public records may show wallet addresses, vault activity, report hashes, audit records, snapshot records, eligibility status, claim status, transaction hashes, approval status, and timelock status where applicable.

Private signer details, identity verification records, legal records, tax records, accounting workpapers, treasury procedures, security controls, private agreements, and sensitive operational records stay permissioned where required.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

FUZE’s public market access direction is DEX first. CEX expansion may come later when product evidence, legal review, exchange review, liquidity readiness, custody treatment, operational readiness, market conditions, and strategic timing support that path. CEX expansion is not guaranteed.

FUZE does not guarantee approved distributable value, product revenue, payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, exit, game earnings, business revenue, AI accuracy, user acquisition, community growth, or investment return.

## 3. Master Model

The master control statement is:

FUZE multisig, timelock, and approval controls govern sensitive actions under the wallet-based participation framework so that token movement, vault control, approved distributable value, eligibility, claim activation, distribution activation, public reporting, and emergency actions occur only after the required review gates and approval workflows are complete.

The control model has ten main parts.

| Control Part | Private Meaning |
|---|---|
| Multisig Control | Sensitive actions require approval from multiple authorized signers. |
| Timelock Control | Sensitive actions may require a delay before execution. |
| Role Separation | Engineering, treasury, legal, accounting, reporting, privacy, and operator roles should not collapse into one uncontrolled actor. |
| Approval Workflow | Each action type has a defined review and approval process. |
| Emergency Pause | Selected emergency conditions may allow temporary pause under documented authority. |
| Correction Process | Errors, restatements, and classification changes require controlled review. |
| Public Reporting Approval | Public-safe reports require review before release. |
| Private Review Control | Sensitive identity, legal, tax, accounting, and treasury details remain permissioned. |
| Audit and Evidence Record | Important approvals should have evidence records and report references where applicable. |
| Activation Boundary | Approval controls do not create payout, claim, liquidity, listing, or investment-return guarantees. |

The model does not mean:

- every signer can act alone
- every approval activates wallet-based participation
- every timelock action is safe by default
- every vault movement is public claim value
- every approved report is a full audit
- every contract action is legally approved
- every claim module action creates payout availability
- every distribution module action guarantees payout
- emergency pause creates public treasury control
- approval status guarantees token price, liquidity, listing, or exit

The model is security-aware, review-based, phased, privacy-aware, and activation-gated.
