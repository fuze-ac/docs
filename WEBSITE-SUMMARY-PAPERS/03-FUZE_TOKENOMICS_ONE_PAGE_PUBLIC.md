# FUZE Tokenomics One-Page

## Executive Summary

FUZE tokenomics organizes a fixed supply of 500,000,000 FUZE around product execution, community participation, long-term stewardship, ecosystem growth, and controlled market operations.

The model uses ten purpose-specific allocations. Allocation establishes why tokens are reserved; separate release, vesting, eligibility, vault, governance, and reporting rules determine how each category can move into use or circulation.

FUZE token connects ecosystem utility through one token framework. Product customers can still use Platform Credits for eligible services, while stablecoins can support selected operational payments and settlement. Keeping these roles distinct makes supply, usage, and financial records easier to understand.

This page gives community members, product users, partners, and investors a concise view of the supply structure and its operating principles. The dedicated tokenomics papers contain the detailed rules for each mechanism.

## What the Allocations Support

The allocation design balances several ecosystem needs.

**Community and continuity:** The Community Participation Allocation supports eligible community access, product-user participation, contributor recognition, and future community programs. BOARD / Surfboard Migration is a separate allocation for eligible legacy-holder continuity.

**Builders and contributors:** Team and advisor allocations support long-term alignment under their applicable vesting, contribution, and release controls.

**Stewardship and resilience:** Foundation, Treasury, and Transparency / Stability reserves support platform continuity, strategic flexibility, governance, infrastructure, and exceptional trust-related needs.

**Growth and activity:** Holder Incentives and Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships can support product-linked programs, integrations, distribution, grants, campaigns, and partnership milestones under approved rules.

**Market structure:** Liquidity & Market Operations reserves tokens for controlled market-structure needs. The category provides capacity for those operations; it does not establish a listing schedule, market depth, or price outcome.

## Allocation Is Not Circulation

A token category can exist in the allocation table while remaining locked, vested, reserved, gated, or otherwise outside active circulation.

Movement can depend on:

- the category's approved purpose;
- vault or custody controls;
- release schedules or access windows;
- eligibility and vesting conditions;
- legal, treasury, technical, or operational review;
- multisig, timelock, or other governance approval; and
- public-safe reporting when disclosure is appropriate.

This distinction matters most for large reserves and specialized categories. A published balance shows where tokens are assigned, while release records explain what has actually moved and why.

The policy framework is covered in [FUZE Controlled Circulation Policy](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/12-FUZE_CONTROLLED_CIRCULATION_POLICY_PUBLIC.md).

## Product Utility and System Separation

FUZE tokenomics supports a product-first ecosystem rather than replacing product economics.

| Component | Primary purpose |
|---|---|
| FUZE token | Ecosystem utility, alignment, and token-specific participation under defined policies |
| Platform Credits | Metering and access for supported product actions, AI work, modules, and reports |
| Stablecoins | Selected payment, settlement, treasury, vendor, and contributor workflows |
| Product revenue | Commercial records governed by product and accounting treatment |
| Wallet records | Public-safe evidence for token activity or controlled participation where relevant |

A user can purchase or consume a product service without receiving FUZE token. A stablecoin payment can settle an operational obligation without becoming a token allocation. Product revenue remains subject to its own accounting and treasury treatment.

This separation lets FUZE serve familiar SaaS workflows while developing Web3 utility around products that have a clear user purpose.

## Utility, Participation, and Governance

FUZE token can connect to product features, ZAGA experiences, community programs, partner activity, platform alignment, reporting, and governance direction where those functions are defined and ready.

Some mechanisms require deeper controls. Community access follows the rules for its allocation and round. Wallet-based participation, if activated for a defined scope, requires eligible-wallet treatment, approved value, custody rules, reporting, and completed activation gates. Governance mechanisms require documented authority and technical readiness.

Using one token framework keeps these mechanisms connected without treating every utility surface as identical. The shorter public explanation is available in the [FUZE One-Token Model One-Page](./06-FUZE_ONE_TOKEN_MODEL_ONE_PAGE_PUBLIC.md).

## Transparency Direction

FUZE can use public allocation tables, labeled vaults, supply summaries, release records, governance references, and report hashes to help readers follow token movement.

Wallet-level evidence can support this transparency without exposing personal identity. Names, contact details, identification records, private agreements, account evidence, and sensitive treasury procedures remain permissioned.

Reporting should distinguish allocated, locked, vested, released, circulating, reserved, and used amounts where the applicable system is ready to publish them. That vocabulary helps readers evaluate the token structure without treating every recorded balance as immediately available.

## Public Boundary

Token allocation and utility describe system design. They do not assure product adoption, community access, wallet eligibility, distributions, token demand, price, liquidity, listing, resale, or investment performance.

Market access follows separate readiness and review. FUZE's public direction can begin with decentralized access, while any later centralized-exchange path remains conditional on legal, operational, custody, liquidity, exchange, and market considerations.

The consolidated limitations are maintained in the [FUZE Risk and Disclosure Appendix](../WHITEPAPER-PAPERS/05-FUZE_RISK_AND_DISCLOSURE_APPENDIX_PUBLIC.md).

## The Takeaway

FUZE tokenomics combines a fixed 500,000,000-token supply with ten explicit purposes and controlled paths from allocation to use. Products create the practical foundation, shared rails support operation, and token utility develops through rules that keep credits, payments, reserves, circulation, and participation understandable.
