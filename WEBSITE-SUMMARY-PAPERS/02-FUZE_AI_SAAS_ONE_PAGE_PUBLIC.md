# FUZE AI SaaS One-Page

## Executive Summary

FUZE AI SaaS turns everyday work into focused product workflows for shops, spreadsheet-based teams, trainers, communities, event operators, creators, and growing businesses.

Instead of asking every user to adapt their work to a general chatbot, FUZE organizes AI around the job being done. A shop owner needs operational summaries, stock questions, promotions, and staff follow-up. A trainer needs reusable guides and knowledge checks. A community operator needs support routing, moderation assistance, and reporting. Each workflow has different sources, permissions, outputs, review steps, and consequences.

The HerHelp family provides the main practical AI SaaS experience. AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad extend that foundation into event intelligence, utility discovery, identified sponsored visibility, and permission-controlled work assistance.

Products can reuse FUZE services for accounts, workspaces, roles, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, data controls, reporting, and evidence. This allows each product to remain specific to its audience while benefiting from a shared operating foundation.

This page explains the intended product model and public boundaries. It does not establish that every product, workflow, integration, control, or shared service is implemented, released, or operating.

## Built Around Real Work

Many people understand that AI may save time, but they still face practical barriers:

- information is spread across messages, documents, spreadsheets, business tools, and team knowledge;
- generic outputs rarely fit the exact workflow, format, audience, or responsibility involved;
- source quality and current context can be unclear;
- teams need permissions, review, correction, and usable records before AI can support daily operations;
- sensitive or high-consequence actions require human authority.

FUZE AI SaaS addresses these barriers through products that begin with a defined user need and end with a reviewable output or next action.

Examples include:

- turning shop activity into an owner summary and staff checklist;
- mapping spreadsheet fields into a clearer dashboard or recurring workflow;
- converting approved source material into a training guide and quiz;
- preparing a community operations report from repeated support themes;
- creating an event brief, sponsor recap, or follow-up plan;
- discovering relevant tools while clearly separating sponsored visibility;
- transforming rough notes into a structured document under user approval.

## How a Workflow Can Operate

A product workflow can follow seven practical stages.

1. **Define the need.** The user selects the product, workspace, audience, source, and intended result.
2. **Check access.** Roles and permissions determine which data, files, tools, or actions are available.
3. **Prepare context.** The workflow identifies approved sources, sensitive fields, assumptions, and missing information.
4. **Run the AI task.** The product generates, classifies, summarizes, maps, retrieves, or prepares the requested output.
5. **Review the result.** The user checks accuracy, source use, tone, completeness, and operational suitability.
6. **Approve or correct.** High-consequence actions remain under human confirmation, revision, escalation, or rejection.
7. **Record or continue.** The approved output can become a report, checklist, message, lesson, dashboard, task, or next workflow step.

This pattern keeps AI attached to a real outcome and creates explicit places for permissions, source review, human authority, correction, and evidence.

## Practical User Journeys

**Shop owner:** A small food shop uses ShopOS AI to prepare a daily sales summary, identify stock questions, draft a loyalty message, and create staff follow-up tasks. SpeakShop AI can turn an approved promotion into a short in-store announcement.

**Spreadsheet-based team:** A manager uses SheetLayer AI to map columns, explain formulas, identify sensitive fields, and prepare a dashboard view. The team reviews the mapping before enabling recurring actions or wider access.

**Trainer:** A team provides approved source material to TrainLayer AI, creates a role-based guide, generates a knowledge check, and revises unclear or unsupported questions before publishing.

**Community operator:** CommunityLayer AI groups recurring support topics, drafts an FAQ update, prepares an admin summary, and flags conversations that require human moderation or escalation.

**Event team:** AIE helps organize opportunities, agendas, sponsor information, audience feedback, and post-event reporting. Botmad can prepare follow-up documents and task lists within the team's approved permissions.

These journeys are representative workflow examples. They are not evidence that the named products are released, adopted, or commercially operating at a particular scale.

## Platform Credits and Product Access

Platform Credits can support eligible AI tasks, reports, modules, and workflow actions across FUZE products.

A product can define its own usage logic. A simple transformation may consume fewer resources than a larger report, multi-step workflow, or workspace operation. Product rules can also support subscription, add-on, granted, promotional, or partner credits.

Credit records should identify:

- product and action;
- quoted or reserved amount where applicable;
- completed consumption;
- reversal, refund, expiry, bonus, or adjustment;
- remaining balance;
- relevant time and workspace.

Platform Credits are product-consumption units. They are separate from FUZE token, investment rights, revenue sharing, and promises of future value.

The detailed model appears in [FUZE Platform Credits Usage Examples](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/06-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_USAGE_EXAMPLES_PUBLIC.md).

## Payments and Commercial Evidence

FUZE products may support subscriptions, usage packages, services, reports, partner deployments, sponsored placements, or other approved commercial formats.

Commercial stages should remain distinct:

```text
offer -> order -> payment -> fulfillment -> adjustment
-> completed paid delivery -> repeat use -> period reconciliation
```

A payment receipt, Platform Credit purchase, stablecoin transfer, invoice, or partner settlement does not automatically establish completed delivery or revenue.

Stablecoins, where supported, remain operational payment or settlement rails. Their use does not change the underlying business classification.

## Data, Permissions, and Human Review

AI usefulness depends on responsible handling of context.

FUZE product controls can include:

- workspace ownership and roles;
- least-privilege access;
- approved source selection;
- sensitive-field treatment;
- provider and tool boundaries;
- retention, deletion, export, and logging rules;
- action confirmation;
- output review and correction;
- escalation to a person;
- incident reporting and recovery.

Public product reporting can summarize activity without exposing private customer, employee, business, community, partner, or workspace content.

Users remain responsible for checking outputs before relying on them for customer communication, business decisions, financial interpretation, regulated work, safety-sensitive activity, or other high-consequence actions.

AI assistance should make judgment and accountability clearer—not invisible.

The wider approach appears in [FUZE Data Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

## Why the Shared Platform Matters

Reusable services can give product teams common capabilities for identity, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI routing, data controls, reporting, and evidence.

This can reduce duplicated platform work and allow improvements in one area to inform the wider product system.

Users should still encounter a focused product rather than a generic infrastructure layer. The shop owner sees shop operations. The trainer sees learning workflows. The community operator sees community tools. Shared infrastructure should remain behind the experience until it adds practical value.

Shared-platform leverage should be treated as demonstrated only when:

- the service is implemented;
- more than one operating product uses it;
- ownership and support are clear;
- reliability and incidents are monitored;
- reuse creates an observable cost, speed, quality, or control benefit.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE AI SaaS organizes AI around real product workflows rather than one general chatbot experience.
- Each product should define its user, sources, permissions, outputs, review steps, and boundaries.
- Platform Credits meter eligible product usage and remain separate from FUZE token and investment rights.
- Payment, fulfillment, completed paid delivery, repeat use, and reconciled revenue are different evidence stages.
- Permissions, source awareness, human authority, correction, and incident handling are part of the product—not optional additions.
- Shared-platform value requires implemented reuse and measurable benefit across operating products.
- Public documentation establishes direction and boundaries, not release, adoption, revenue, or commercial success.
