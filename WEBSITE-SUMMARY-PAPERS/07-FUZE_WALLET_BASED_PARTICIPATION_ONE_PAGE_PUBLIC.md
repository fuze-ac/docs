# FUZE Wallet-Based Participation One-Page

## Executive Summary

FUZE wallet-based participation is a conditional framework that could connect eligible FUZE-holding wallets with approved value from defined product revenue pools.

It belongs within the FUZE one-token model and would operate only after the required product, accounting, treasury, legal, tax, custody, privacy, security, technical, reporting, governance, and jurisdiction controls are ready.

Holding FUZE token can be one condition, but token ownership alone does not create eligibility, a claim, a distribution, or a right to product revenue.

An active framework would also depend on:

- the current approved scope;
- the applicable snapshot;
- supported custody method;
- wallet category;
- jurisdiction and verification requirements;
- source and reconciliation of approved value;
- claim availability;
- current operating status.

Wallet records can make the process reviewable without exposing personal identity. Public reporting can show address-based status and evidence references, while identity, account, tax, legal, custody, and support records remain permissioned.

This page explains the public model and boundaries. It does not establish that the framework is active, that any wallet is eligible, or that approved distributable value exists.

## 1. The Core Idea

FUZE wallet-based participation is a specialized ability within the FUZE token framework, not a separate token or automatic feature of ownership.

If activated for a defined scope, the operating sequence would be:

1. define the active framework, period, and participant scope;
2. identify the product revenue pools included in the period;
3. classify completed paid delivery and reconcile source records;
4. apply refunds, fees, costs, partner shares, taxes, reserves, exclusions, and other approved adjustments;
5. determine whether any value is available for further consideration;
6. obtain the required accounting, treasury, legal, tax, governance, and other approvals;
7. define and publish the snapshot and custody rules;
8. determine which wallets satisfy the active requirements;
9. open the approved participation or claim process;
10. operate reporting, support, dispute, correction, pause, and closure processes.

Each stage depends on the previous stages.

Product activity, payment receipts, treasury balances, token ownership, wallet visibility, contract deployment, snapshot preparation, or dashboard availability do not by themselves complete this sequence.

## 2. Activation Comes Before Operation

FUZE can prepare contracts, reports, snapshot tools, vault records, dashboards, policies, and review procedures before participation becomes active.

Preparation and operation are different states.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Documented | The public or internal framework is described |
| Designed | Rules, roles, data, and control requirements are defined |
| Configured | Systems or processes are prepared for a limited scope |
| Tested | The process has been exercised with test or pilot evidence |
| Approved | Required decision-makers have authorized a defined scope |
| Active | Eligible wallets can participate under current published rules |
| Paused | New activity is temporarily stopped while records or risks are reviewed |
| Restricted | Only a limited scope, wallet class, custody route, or jurisdiction is supported |
| Closed | The active period or mechanism has ended |

One state does not imply the next.

A blocked requirement can keep the framework inactive, restrict the supported scope, delay claims, or require closure.

The detailed readiness framework appears in [FUZE Participation Activation Gates](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/08-FUZE_PARTICIPATION_ACTIVATION_GATES_PUBLIC.md).

## 3. What Makes a Wallet Eligible?

An eligible FUZE-holding wallet is a wallet that satisfies every requirement for the exact active period or process.

The active rules can consider:

- FUZE balance or holding conditions;
- inclusion in the applicable snapshot;
- snapshot time, block, network, and token contract;
- proof of wallet control or beneficial ownership;
- self-custody, exchange custody, institutional custody, multisig, or contract-wallet treatment;
- controlled, related-party, restricted, sanctions, or excluded wallet categories;
- jurisdiction and required private verification;
- duplicate or overlapping claims;
- current claim, dispute, correction, pause, or reporting status.

Eligibility can change by period because balances, custody, rules, locations, evidence, and framework status can change.

Eligibility for one period, product, program, or custody route does not create eligibility for another.

The complete operating model appears in [FUZE Wallet-Based Participation Model](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/07-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PARTICIPATION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

## 4. Product Revenue and Approved Distributable Value

FUZE products create the business activity behind the wider ecosystem.

Commercial stages should remain distinct:

```text
offer -> order -> payment -> fulfillment -> adjustment
-> completed paid delivery -> repeat use -> period reconciliation
```

Product revenue first remains subject to normal business responsibilities, which can include:

- refunds and disputes;
- payment and platform fees;
- direct delivery costs;
- partner or contributor shares;
- taxes and professional obligations;
- reserves and working capital;
- support and infrastructure;
- operating continuity;
- accounting corrections and exclusions.

Approved distributable value is the reviewed amount, if any, that could enter an activated participation framework after those stages and approvals.

It may be zero or unavailable for a period.

The following do not by themselves establish approved distributable value:

- gross sales;
- payment receipts;
- invoices;
- Platform Credit purchases;
- stablecoin transfers;
- treasury balances;
- token allocations;
- wallet balances;
- expected future revenue.

A wallet holder has no standing claim on gross revenue, product revenue, stablecoin balances, treasury assets, reserves, or future distributions merely by holding FUZE token.

## 5. Custody Changes the Evidence

Different custody methods create different evidence, dependencies, and risks.

### Self-Custody

A self-custody wallet can provide direct on-chain balance and signature evidence.

The user remains responsible for private keys, recovery phrases, approvals, addresses, networks, and transaction security.

FUZE should never ask a user to disclose a private key or recovery phrase.

### Exchange or Custodian Accounts

A centralized exchange or custodian can hold many users behind an omnibus address.

The visible blockchain address may belong to the venue rather than the individual account holder.

Individual eligibility may require:

- venue cooperation;
- supported account-level evidence;
- internal balance records;
- snapshot compatibility;
- claims processing;
- jurisdiction and verification treatment.

FUZE may be unable to confirm or support individual participation where the venue does not provide an approved process.

### Institutional, Contract, and Multisig Wallets

Institutional custody, smart-contract wallets, multisignature wallets, delegated wallets, and account-abstraction systems can require additional compatibility, authority, and beneficial-ownership review.

The active rules must state which custody types are supported and what evidence is required.

A wallet can remain under review, restricted, unsupported, disputed, or excluded when evidence or technical support is insufficient.

## 6. Snapshots, Claims, and Corrections

A snapshot is a defined record of wallet or custody status at a stated time, block, or period.

An active snapshot method should identify:

- network and token contract;
- block, time, or period;
- included and excluded wallet categories;
- balance or holding treatment;
- custody treatment;
- duplicate or related-wallet controls;
- methodology and responsible owner;
- correction and dispute window;
- report version and evidence references.

A snapshot does not by itself create eligibility or a claim.

Where claims are activated, the process should define:

- claim opening and closing dates;
- supported wallet or account routes;
- proof and verification requirements;
- approved amount calculation;
- transaction or settlement method;
- fees or deductions;
- failed or incomplete claim treatment;
- dispute and correction process;
- unclaimed-value treatment;
- pause, cancellation, and closure authority.

Incorrect, duplicated, disputed, unsupported, sanctioned, compromised, or suspicious records may require restriction, correction, rejection, recovery, or escalation.

## 7. Public Records and Private Identity

The public layer can include:

- wallet addresses;
- snapshot references;
- current framework status;
- eligibility or restriction status where appropriate;
- claim status where activated;
- transaction references;
- report hashes or signatures;
- pause, correction, and supersession records;
- aggregate period totals.

Permissioned records can include:

- personal identity and contact details;
- exchange-account evidence;
- beneficial-ownership records;
- tax and jurisdiction information;
- wallet-control evidence;
- legal and compliance records;
- support tickets and dispute materials;
- private agreements and custody records.

These records should be available only to authorized reviewers for an approved purpose.

Public blockchain visibility does not justify exposing the person or organization behind an address.

A report hash can support file integrity, but it does not prove that the underlying data is complete, accurate, current, or correctly interpreted.

More detail appears in [FUZE Wallet-Based Privacy and Eligibility](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/26-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PRIVACY_AND_ELIGIBILITY_PUBLIC.md).

## 8. Separation From Other FUZE Systems

Wallet-based participation remains separate from adjacent FUZE systems.

| System or record | What it does not automatically establish |
|---|---|
| FUZE token holding | Eligibility, claim, distribution, or approved value |
| Platform Credits | Token ownership or wallet eligibility |
| Product usage | Completed paid delivery or participation rights |
| Customer payment | Reconciled revenue or approved distributable value |
| Stablecoin transfer | Revenue, holder entitlement, or participation status |
| Treasury balance | Availability for distribution |
| Community Participation Round | Eligibility under this wallet framework |
| Token allocation | Circulation or recipient ownership |
| DEX or CEX access | Participation eligibility or practical exit |
| Wallet snapshot | Approved claim or final eligibility |

The systems can interact, but their records, approvals, and public language must remain distinct.

## 9. Governance, Security, and Operating Controls

An active framework requires accountable operating roles.

These can include:

- framework owner;
- product revenue and finance owner;
- accounting and reconciliation reviewer;
- treasury or custody owner;
- technical operator;
- privacy and security reviewer;
- legal, tax, or compliance reviewer;
- governance approver;
- reporting owner;
- support and dispute owner;
- independent reviewer or auditor where appropriate.

Controls can include:

- multisignature or timelock approval;
- least-privilege access;
- separate preparation and execution roles;
- transaction simulation and review;
- address and network verification;
- rate, amount, and period limits;
- monitoring and reconciliation;
- incident response and emergency pause;
- correction, recovery, and dispute procedures;
- versioned reports and audit trails.

Designed controls do not establish operating effectiveness. Operating controls do not establish independent assurance unless they have been reviewed within a defined scope.

## 10. Current Public Position

The public corpus establishes the intended participation model, activation gates, privacy boundaries, custody cases, source-value separation, reporting direction, and risk controls.

It does not by itself establish:

- an active framework;
- an approved participation period;
- eligible wallets;
- a completed snapshot;
- supported exchange custody;
- approved distributable value;
- open claims;
- completed distributions;
- regulatory or tax approval;
- recurring future participation.

Current conclusions should rely on dated, scoped evidence for the exact period, product revenue pool, wallet, custody route, snapshot, claim process, and governance decision being discussed.

## 11. Public Boundary

Wallet-based participation is conditional and activation-gated.

It does not guarantee:

- eligibility;
- approved distributable value;
- a claim or distribution;
- recurring participation;
- product revenue or profitability;
- tax or regulatory treatment;
- custody support;
- token demand;
- liquidity, listing, price, resale, or exit;
- investment return.

FUZE can delay, narrow, pause, restrict, correct, cancel, or discontinue the framework or a participation period where evidence, law, security, custody, accounting, treasury, governance, or operational conditions require it.

## Key Takeaways

- Wallet-based participation is a conditional ability within the FUZE token framework, not a separate asset or automatic token-holder right.
- Holding FUZE can be relevant, but eligibility requires the complete active rule set for the exact period and custody route.
- Product revenue, payment receipts, stablecoins, treasury balances, token allocations, and approved distributable value remain separate classifications.
- Approved distributable value, if any, follows completed paid delivery, reconciliation, exclusions, reserves, and formal approval.
- Preparation, testing, approval, activation, claims, and distributions are different states.
- Custody determines which evidence and claim process may be available.
- Public wallet reporting can support review while personal identity and account evidence remain protected.
- Platform Credits, product usage, community-round access, token ownership, snapshots, and market access do not automatically establish wallet eligibility.
- The framework can be paused, restricted, corrected, or closed and provides no financial or market assurance.
