# Development Governance / CMMI / Compliance Standards

## Executive Summary

FUZE uses **Development Governance / CMMI / Compliance Standards** to explain how FUZE builds faster with AI while keeping execution controlled, reviewable, documented, and compliance-aware.

FUZE’s development governance sentence is:

**FUZE uses AI to move faster, but uses structured standards to stay controlled.**

This means FUZE can use AI-assisted work, code generation, documentation support, research preparation, product planning, testing assistance, artifact generation, and delivery acceleration. FUZE still keeps human review, source-of-truth documentation, file mapping, acceptance criteria, security boundaries, version control, issue tracking, quality gates, evidence records, and public-safe reporting.

FUZE uses standards-inspired and standards-aligned processes. FUZE does not claim formal CMMI, ISO, SOC 2, audit, security, privacy, legal, or compliance certification unless certification has been completed.

Development governance supports FUZE’s product-first execution model:

**Product usage first. Platform rails second. Broader ecosystem participation after that.**

The first launch focus is **HerHelp.com** and **ZAGA.io**, supported by FUZE Core Platform rails and accelerated internally by **Botmad**.

This paper defines FUZE’s public development governance model, standards-aligned execution approach, AI-assisted work controls, source-of-truth system, evidence model, product delivery controls, security/privacy practices, compliance messaging, reporting direction, and risk safeguards.

## 1. FUZE Position

FUZE presents development governance as a structured execution system.

FUZE position:

This model makes AI-assisted execution safer and more understandable.

## 6. Risk Boundaries and Safeguards

FUZE applies risk boundaries around development governance.

### 6.1 Development Standards Boundary

FUZE uses standards-inspired or standards-aligned processes. FUZE does not claim formal certification unless certification has been completed.

### 6.2 AI-Assisted Work Boundary

AI-assisted work supports speed, document preparation, review, research, code support, documentation, and artifact generation. It does not remove human review, guarantee perfect accuracy, or replace legal, security, privacy, financial, tax, medical, or other consequential professional review.

### 6.3 Security Boundary

Security controls reduce risk but do not eliminate all vulnerabilities, operational risks, human errors, third-party risks, platform risks, infrastructure risks, smart contract risks, or incident risks.

### 6.4 Privacy Boundary

Privacy controls support responsible data handling. Product use can still require user consent, role permissions, retention rules, data-processing terms, platform rules, and jurisdiction-specific review.

### 6.5 Product Delivery Boundary

Product priority and development governance do not guarantee launch timing, feature availability, product adoption, revenue, customer growth, enterprise adoption, partner adoption, or product-market fit.

### 6.6 Platform Credits Boundary

Platform Credits are for product usage and are not investment assets, payout assets, or FUZE tokens.

### 6.7 General Token Boundary

FUZE does not guarantee token price, liquidity, listing, profit, return, payout, or market performance.

### 6.8 Product-Specific Boundary

QTB, AIMM, ZAGA Arena, ZAGA Districts, ToolGrid, TrainLayer AI, CommunityLayer AI, SpeakShop AI, and Botmad each retain their product-specific public boundaries across development, launch, reporting, and public communication.

### 6.9 Public Reporting Boundary

FUZE can report development status, public artifacts, release notes, and policy updates while protecting private source code, confidential contracts, customer data, security-sensitive details, legal materials, credentials, keys, and private operational records.
