
# FUZE Product Launch Sequence

## Executive Summary

This paper defines how FUZE products move from documented concepts to controlled release without assigning unsupported launch dates or treating roadmap priority as evidence of availability.

The sequence is dependency-led and evidence-led.

A product or module advances when the named release scope has sufficient:

- user value;
- working product behavior;
- source and data controls;
- permissions and authority;
- reliability;
- security and privacy;
- support and incident handling;
- commercial or Platform Credit treatment where applicable;
- reporting and correction capability;
- product-specific review; and
- evidence for the next release level.

The common lifecycle is:

```text
candidate -> defined -> demonstrable -> internally testable
-> limited pilot -> public beta -> live for stated scope
-> measured -> expanded, corrected, paused, narrowed, or retired
```

FUZE may develop several products in parallel.

Parallel development does not mean simultaneous launch.

A public paper, roadmap entry, mockup, code repository, prototype, internal test, partner discussion, wallet screen, token reference, campaign concept, or target date does not independently establish:

- implementation;
- public availability;
- active users;
- paid delivery;
- revenue;
- token utility;
- market access;
- liquidity;
- adoption; or
- financial outcome.

The sequence gives priority to complete user journeys rather than broad but incomplete feature sets.

The initial practical path centers on narrow HerHelp workflows and the shared platform capabilities those workflows require.

ZAGA follows separate game and community readiness tracks.

Botmad advances through bounded, permission-controlled work rather than unrestricted autonomy.

QTB, AIMM, AIE, and ToolGrid AI progress through domain-specific pilots and controls.

Token-connected features follow their own utility qualification and activation process and do not replace normal product launch gates.

Current public status belongs in the [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md).

## Sequence Principles

### User Journey Before Feature Count

A narrow journey that users can complete, understand, and receive support for is more launch-ready than a broad feature list with incomplete controls.

A launch scope should identify:

- intended user;
- user problem;
- entry point;
- source data;
- main action;
- output or result;
- reviewer or authority;
- error states;
- support route;
- current limitations; and
- completion evidence.

### Dependency Before Date

A release should be tied to measurable dependencies rather than a date alone.

Dates may support planning, but a target date does not override:

- product defects;
- missing permissions;
- privacy risk;
- security risk;
- provider failure;
- unsupported claims;
- incomplete support;
- unclear payment or Platform Credit treatment;
- unresolved legal or market review; or
- absent operating evidence.

### Scope Before Product-Wide Status

A product may contain modules at different levels.

Public communication should identify the exact:

- module;
- audience;
- geography;
- device;
- plan;
- workspace;
- event;
- campaign;
- network;
- provider;
- integration;
- market;
- or other release scope.

One successful module test should not upgrade the entire product to “live.”

### Evidence Before Expansion

Expansion should follow evidence from the current release scope.

Evidence may include:

- completion;
- failure;
- support load;
- reliability;
- privacy or security findings;
- correction volume;
- repeat use;
- user understanding;
- operating cost;
- commercial fulfillment;
- retention;
- provider stability; and
- product-specific outcome measures.

### Reversibility Before Irreversible Scale

Early releases should prefer:

- small audiences;
- bounded data;
- limited permissions;
- feature flags;
- reversible changes;
- pause controls;
- rollback paths;
- low-risk destinations;
- controlled providers; and
- clear correction procedures.

### Product Status Before Marketing Strength

Public language should match the exact evidence-backed release state.

Marketing emphasis must not upgrade:

- candidate to planned;
- planned to implemented;
- prototype to beta;
- test to live;
- live to adopted;
- payment to revenue;
- user count to retained users;
- product activity to token demand; or
- partner discussion to partnership.

## Release Levels

| Release level | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | A possible product, module, integration, or feature has been identified. | Approval, implementation, or delivery |
| Defined | User, problem, workflow, scope, owner, controls, and public boundary are documented. | Working software |
| Demonstrable | A prototype or demonstration shows the main journey and known limitations. | Production readiness or public access |
| Internally testable | Authorized testers can use the stated workflow and record defects and evidence. | External validation or general availability |
| Limited pilot | A controlled external audience uses the stated scope with onboarding, support, monitoring, and terms. | Public availability or broad adoption |
| Public beta | Public access exists under beta scope, limitations, support, feedback, and release notes. | Final quality, permanence, or scale |
| Live for stated scope | Production access and operating support exist for the named scope. | Universal availability, adoption, paid delivery, or revenue |
| Actively used | Current use is evidenced under a stated method and period. | Retention, customer status, paid use, or profitability |
| Paid delivery | A paid service has been delivered under current terms and fulfillment evidence. | Revenue recognition, retention, or profitability |
| Revenue confirmed | Reconciled payment, completed service, period, and accounting treatment are evidenced. | Profitability or future revenue |
| Expanded | Scope has widened after review of current evidence and dependencies. | Permanent success |
| Paused | Access or work is temporarily stopped. | Retirement |
| Narrowed | Scope has been reduced to control risk, cost, quality, or support. | Failure of the whole product |
| Retired | The product or capability is no longer active. | Continued support or availability |
| Archived | Historical records are retained but not current. | Current status |

These levels are not interchangeable.

## Launch Decision Record

Each movement to a stronger release level should use a reviewable launch-decision record.

The record should identify:

- product and module;
- current level;
- proposed level;
- exact audience and access scope;
- geography or jurisdiction where relevant;
- device, platform, network, venue, or provider scope;
- product owner;
- release owner;
- support owner;
- security and privacy reviewers;
- legal or specialist reviewers where required;
- user journey;
- dependencies;
- completed gates;
- open limitations;
- monitoring;
- rollback or pause plan;
- public language;
- support and correction route;
- release time;
- review date;
- evidence package; and
- final decision.

Possible decisions include:

- approve;
- approve with limits;
- approve for internal test;
- approve for limited pilot;
- approve for public beta;
- approve for stated production scope;
- delay;
- revise;
- narrow;
- pause;
- reject;
- replace; or
- retire.

A launch decision applies only to its stated scope and version.

## Common Launch Gates

### 1. Product Definition Gate

The release scope has a clear:

- user;
- problem;
- product action;
- source;
- output;
- owner;
- status;
- limitation;
- public description; and
- success or completion method.

### 2. Core Journey Gate

The intended user can:

- enter the product;
- understand the task;
- provide or select authorized inputs;
- perform the core action;
- review the result;
- correct or retry where supported;
- find help; and
- leave or close the workflow safely.

Onboarding, empty states, permission failures, provider failures, and error states are part of the journey.

### 3. Product Quality Gate

The team can distinguish:

- expected behavior;
- known limitation;
- defect;
- provider limitation;
- user error;
- unsupported use;
- partial completion;
- and failure.

The main workflow has defined acceptance criteria and current test evidence.

### 4. Data and Permission Gate

The release defines and tests:

- source authority;
- data classification;
- minimum required data;
- workspace separation;
- roles;
- permissions;
- approval steps;
- public and private boundaries;
- retention;
- deletion;
- export;
- provider routing;
- correction; and
- incident handling.

### 5. Security Gate

The team has reviewed the risks appropriate to the release, including where relevant:

- authentication;
- authorization;
- session handling;
- secrets;
- connectors;
- prompt injection;
- external content;
- file access;
- payments;
- wallets;
- signatures;
- smart contracts;
- market data;
- destructive actions;
- abuse;
- moderation;
- logging;
- incident response; and
- recovery.

### 6. Reliability and Provider Gate

The release defines:

- provider dependencies;
- availability assumptions;
- timeouts;
- retries;
- duplicate prevention;
- fallback behavior;
- degraded mode;
- pause behavior;
- monitoring;
- alerting;
- status communication;
- support escalation; and
- correction.

A fallback provider should not silently weaken product, privacy, security, metering, or reporting controls.

### 7. Operations and Support Gate

The release has:

- product owner;
- operational owner;
- support route;
- incident contact;
- user communication;
- monitoring;
- correction tools;
- pause control;
- rollback or withdrawal process;
- known-issue handling;
- provider escalation;
- record retention; and
- after-hours or availability expectations where applicable.

### 8. Commercial and Platform Credit Gate

Where a paid service or Platform Credit action applies, the release defines:

- service or usage unit;
- price, quote, allowance, or commercial basis;
- authorization;
- reservation where needed;
- completion event;
- partial-completion treatment;
- cancellation;
- failure;
- retry;
- refund, release, adjustment, or reversal;
- payment separation;
- reconciliation;
- support;
- current terms; and
- public status.

The [FUZE Product to Platform Credits](18-FUZE_PRODUCT_TO_PLATFORM_CREDITS_PUBLIC.md) defines the cross-product consumption lifecycle.

### 9. Reporting and Evidence Gate

The team can measure and distinguish:

- eligible audience;
- access;
- attempted use;
- completion;
- failure;
- partial completion;
- support demand;
- correction;
- repeat use;
- retention where applicable;
- paid delivery;
- revenue where applicable;
- incidents;
- downtime;
- provider failure;
- current limitations; and
- public-safe evidence.

### 10. Product-Specific Gate

Additional review applies to:

- games and simulated values;
- community moderation;
- market research;
- liquidity operations;
- events and attendance;
- sponsored placement;
- wallet functions;
- token utility;
- payments;
- autonomous-looking desktop actions;
- child or vulnerable-user contexts;
- regulated professional workflows; and
- other high-impact domains.

Passing a gate is evidence for the stated scope at a point in time.

It is not permanent certification.

## Sequence at a Glance

FUZE organizes launch work through six connected waves.

| Wave | Focus | Intended result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Public and operating foundation | Product definitions, status language, permissions, data, Platform Credits, reporting, support, and evidence rules | Teams and readers understand what is being built and how stronger status is earned |
| 2. Practical AI SaaS | Narrow HerHelp workflows and the shared services they require | Users complete useful, bounded AI-assisted business tasks |
| 3. Game and community | ZAGA Arena, ZAGA Districts, community support, and event operations | Testable game experiences with clear game-state, moderation, and community records |
| 4. Cross-product assistance | Botmad with bounded sources, actions, approvals, and artifact delivery | Approved product information becomes reviewable practical work |
| 5. Specialized intelligence and distribution | QTB, AIMM, AIE, and ToolGrid AI through focused pilots | Domain-specific products advance only after their controls and evidence are operating |
| 6. Product-connected utility and ecosystem expansion | Qualified token utility, partner programs, wider integrations, and cross-product journeys | Ecosystem features expand after product, technical, security, legal, operational, and public-readiness review |

The waves are not rigid calendar phases.

They may overlap where dependencies and owners allow.

## Foundation Wave

The foundation wave establishes the reusable conditions for product work to progress consistently.

Key outputs include:

- approved product names and objectives;
- current status vocabulary;
- evidence and public-claim rules;
- account, workspace, role, and permission patterns;
- data classification and AI handling;
- source and destination authority;
- Platform Credit quote, authorization, consumption, and correction patterns;
- payment separation;
- support and incident ownership;
- audit and correction records;
- product and public reporting definitions;
- privacy and security controls;
- provider and connector review; and
- pause, rollback, withdrawal, and retirement patterns.

The foundation should develop alongside real product needs.

Examples:

- a HerHelp team workspace can define role and permission requirements;
- a SheetLayer workflow can define source-scope and data-quality controls;
- a SpeakShop workflow can define content approval and playback boundaries;
- a CommunityLayer workflow can define moderation and appeals;
- a ToolGrid campaign can define sponsorship disclosure and attribution;
- a Botmad task can define destination-action approval and rollback;
- a later token-connected feature can define wallet, eligibility, and activation controls.

The foundation is not a single infrastructure launch that must be completed in full before any product test.

It is a governed set of reusable capabilities developed through bounded product journeys.

## Practical AI SaaS Wave

HerHelp AI SaaS is the primary practical business-facing release path.

Its products should begin with narrow workflows that are easy to explain, test, support, and correct.

| Product | Suitable early scope | Evidence for wider release |
|---|---|---|
| [SheetLayer AI](03-HERHELP_SHEETLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) | Bounded sheet inspection, field mapping, selected-range processing, formula assistance, or report workflow | Data-quality tests, permission behavior, source traceability, useful artifacts, error handling, and reviewer feedback |
| [ShopOS AI](04-HERHELP_SHOPOS_AI_PUBLIC.md) | Selected operator workflow, menu or promotion task, queue support, or AI-assisted shop report | Operator trial, device behavior, payment and customer-data boundaries, correction, support, and daily-use evidence |
| [SpeakShop AI](05-HERHELP_SPEAKSHOP_AI_PUBLIC.md) | Script or announcement preparation for a defined language, format, venue, or campaign | Reviewed outputs, pronunciation and language handling, approval, playback boundary, accessibility, and user feedback |
| [TrainLayer AI](06-HERHELP_TRAINLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) | Source-to-guide, quiz, onboarding, or selected training module | Source traceability, reviewer evaluation, learner activity, correction, accessibility, and outcome-language controls |
| [CommunityLayer AI](07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) | Summary, support draft, onboarding, recurring report, or moderation-assistance workflow | Admin trial, role controls, escalation, appeals, privacy, abuse handling, and review evidence |

The products do not need to launch simultaneously.

Priority should consider:

- clearest user problem;
- shortest useful journey;
- lowest unresolved data and permission risk;
- manageable provider dependency;
- available product owner;
- support capacity;
- measurement readiness;
- reusability of shared rails;
- commercial learning value; and
- potential to inform later products.

The module has:

## Game and Community Wave

ZAGA follows a distinct release path because gameplay, persistence, anti-abuse, community authority, and simulated economic language create different dependencies from business SaaS.

### ZAGA Arena

Arena should first establish the playable run:

```text
enter -> understand objective -> move or fight
-> interact with boss or collection mechanics
-> complete, fail, or leave run -> calculate result
-> review score and state -> report or correct
```

Early scope should prioritize:

- entry and onboarding;
- movement or combat;
- boss and collection mechanics;
- run completion;
- failure and reconnect behavior;
- scoring;
- result records;
- latency;
- device behavior;
- anti-cheat signals;
- moderation; and
- support.

Later expansion may include:

- multiplayer;
- Telegram integration;
- events;
- leaderboards;
- roles;
- team or faction mechanics;
- community tools;
- digital-asset functions;
- and separately qualified token utility.

Useful evidence includes:

- session starts;
- completed runs;
- failed runs;
- reconnect success;
- defects;
- latency;
- balance observations;
- abuse reports;
- anti-cheat findings;
- support demand;
- repeated participation; and
- player feedback under a stated method.

### ZAGA Districts

Districts requires a persistent community path:

```text
create or join district -> receive role and permissions
-> interact with people, NPCs, buildings, resources, and quests
-> make or review shared decisions
-> preserve state -> moderate conflict and abuse
-> report, correct, or continue
```

Early scope should prioritize:

- district creation or membership;
- roles;
- permissions;
- city or state records;
- NPC or building systems;
- resource and economy testing;
- shared decisions;
- moderation;
- correction;
- persistence;
- recovery; and
- support.

Useful evidence includes:

- onboarding completion;
- role and permission behavior;
- state consistency;
- economy review;
- moderation load;
- abuse patterns;
- correction burden;
- community understanding;
- repeat participation; and
- operating cost.

### Supporting Products

CommunityLayer AI and AIE may support game communities, events, moderation, and reporting when a defined integration is implemented and approved.

Ecosystem proximity does not establish integration.

Game values, simulated USDT, Token Value, Net Worth, salary, tax, treasury, resources, drops, property, score, and rewards must remain clearly framed under the applicable game rules.

Any FUZE-token-connected game feature follows [FUZE Product to Token Utility Bridge](19-FUZE_PRODUCT_TO_TOKEN_UTILITY_BRIDGE_PUBLIC.md).

## Cross-Product Assistance Wave

Botmad may connect approved FUZE outputs to practical desktop work.

Its release sequence depends on permission, destination, approval, verification, rollback, and artifact-ownership maturity.

An early Botmad release should prefer bounded tasks such as:

- preparing a document from an approved workspace;
- turning a reviewed report into a checklist;
- comparing approved document versions;
- organizing a defined set of permitted files;
- drafting a support response for human approval;
- compiling follow-up actions from an AIE event summary;
- preparing a ToolGrid correction package;
- preparing a QTB research handoff;
- preparing an AIMM operational report package; or
- generating a recurring internal brief without external sending.

### Entry Gate

The task has:

- permitted sources;
- permitted destination;
- action class;
- maximum scope;
- product owner;
- responsible reviewer;
- artifact owner;
- data classification;
- rollback or correction route;
- usage ceiling where applicable; and
- current limitations.

### Limited Pilot Gate

The pilot demonstrates:

- exact source access;
- exact destination access;
- readable plan;
- approval gates;
- reviewed output;
- version or diff evidence;
- safe failure;
- correction;
- stop or revoke control;
- prompt-injection resistance appropriate to scope;
- and support.

### Stronger Controls Required

The following require stronger review than draft preparation:

- credentials;
- external sending;
- publishing;
- form submission;
- calendar changes;
- record mutation;
- deletion;
- file moves across authority boundaries;
- purchases;
- refunds;
- payments;
- permission changes;
- code merge;
- deployment;
- production changes;
- wallet signing;
- financial operations; and
- recurring actions with external effect.

Botmad should not advance through “autonomy” claims.

It should advance through bounded, observable, reversible, and supportable work.

## Cross-Product Integration Sequence

A product should become useful on its own before a broad cross-product journey is treated as a release dependency.

An integration should progress through:

```text
use case -> source authority -> destination authority
-> data contract -> permissions -> handoff design
-> error and correction model -> test -> limited release
-> monitoring -> wider activation
```

An integration record should identify:

- source product;
- destination product;
- purpose;
- exact records;
- authority;
- sensitivity;
- user consent where required;
- timing;
- retention;
- correction;
- failure handling;
- destination owner;
- public status;
- and current version.

A shared login, link, export, or product mention does not independently establish an active integration.

## Platform Credit and Commercial Timing

A product may test a workflow before final commercial packaging.

Broader paid use should not begin until the service has:

1. a recognizable product action;
2. a defined usage or commercial unit;
3. a visible amount, estimate, range, allowance, package, or quote basis;
4. authorization;
5. payment or grant confirmation where applicable;
6. completion criteria;
7. partial-completion, cancellation, failure, and retry rules;
8. readable usage and balance records;
9. refund, release, adjustment, reversal, and support handling;
10. reconciliation;
11. current terms;
12. privacy and permission controls;
13. evidence of fulfillment; and
14. clear separation between payment, Platform Credit use, delivery, revenue, and profitability.

Platform Credits should attach to services users recognize.

Ordinary navigation, unreadable internal events, or ambiguous AI processing should not become hidden metering units.

Product revenue readiness has its own gate in [FUZE Product Revenue Readiness](21-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_READINESS_PUBLIC.md).

## Token-Utility Timing

Token-connected product features should not be used to make an incomplete product appear more mature.

A token-connected candidate should advance only after:

- product value is established;
- the user action is specific;
- a simpler non-token alternative has been evaluated;
- the token role adds practical ecosystem value;
- the wallet experience is bounded;
- the token condition is exact;
- network and contract scope are defined;
- eligibility and transfer effects are defined;
- technical, security, privacy, legal, operational, and communication review is complete for the stated stage;
- support and correction exist;
- pause and withdrawal exist;
- and an activation decision is recorded.

Candidate, qualified, specified, implemented, tested, pilot, beta, active, paused, withdrawn, and retired utility states are different.

Product launch does not imply token utility activation.

Token utility activation does not imply:

- token demand;
- exchange listing;
- market access;
- liquidity;
- price effect;
- claim eligibility;
- payout;
- revenue;
- or financial return.

## Launch Communication

A public launch update should state:

- product and module;
- release level;
- previous status;
- current status;
- audience;
- access scope;
- geography, platform, network, venue, or provider scope where relevant;
- core action;
- evidence completed;
- known limitations;
- support route;
- correction route;
- next dependency;
- current-as-of date; and
- whether the update concerns product, integration, commercial service, Platform Credit use, or token utility.

Example structure:

> `<Product>` is now `<release level>` for `<named audience and scope>`. Users can `<core product action>`. The release currently supports `<scope>` and remains limited by `<material limitations>`. The next dependency is `<dependency>`. Support and correction are available through `<route>`.

A launch update should not claim:

- adoption from availability;
- retention from account creation;
- revenue from payment intent;
- profitability from revenue;
- partnership from discussion;
- integration from a logo;
- token utility from a wallet screen;
- listing from outreach or application;
- liquidity from deployment;
- or guaranteed outcomes from product access.

- security incidents;
- abuse or moderation load;
- user understanding;
- accessibility findings;
- product satisfaction under a stated method;
- Platform Credit usage;
- paid delivery;
- commercial fulfillment;

- timezone;
- audience;

## Expansion Decision

A release may expand when:

- users complete the intended journey;
- failures are understood and controlled;
- support is manageable;
- privacy and security findings are acceptable for the next scope;
- providers are sufficiently reliable;
- operators can monitor and correct the service;
- public communication is accurate;
- commercial treatment is supportable where applicable;
- product-specific risks are controlled;
- and the expansion produces more value than complexity.

Expansion may change:

- audience;
- geography;
- plan;
- platform;
- device;
- provider;
- data scope;
- integration;
- campaign inventory;
- event scope;
- market scope;
- wallet scope;
- or token-utility scope.

Each material expansion should receive its own decision record.

## Reprioritization

The sequence may change when:

- user evidence reveals a stronger or weaker problem;
- a core journey is not useful enough;
- a shared dependency blocks several products;
- data, security, privacy, or permission risk increases;
- operating cost or support demand changes materially;
- a provider or partner dependency changes;
- another product produces stronger readiness evidence;
- commercial assumptions change;
- a product needs redesign;
- an integration becomes unnecessary;
- a simpler non-token or non-AI design is preferable;
- legal or market requirements change;
- or a product should pause, narrow, replace, or retire.

A priority-change record should identify:

- affected product;
- previous priority;
- new priority;
- evidence;
- reason;
- dependencies;
- owner;
- effect on users;
- effect on public status;
- next review point;
- and communication requirement.

- game-integrity issue;
- event-safety issue;
- market-conduct issue;
- destination compromise;

The record should identify:

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE sequences products through dependencies and evidence rather than unsupported dates.
- A complete narrow user journey is more launch-ready than a broad incomplete feature set.
- Candidate, defined, demonstrable, internally testable, limited pilot, public beta, live, actively used, paid delivery, and revenue-confirmed are different states.
- HerHelp leads the practical AI SaaS path through narrow, supportable workflows.
- ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts follow separate game and community readiness tracks.
- Botmad advances through bounded permissions, approvals, reversible actions, and artifact delivery rather than unrestricted autonomy.
- QTB, AIMM, AIE, and ToolGrid AI should launch through focused domain-specific pilots.
- Platform Credit, payment, commercial, token-utility, wallet, market-access, and product-launch gates remain separate.
- Cross-product integrations require source authority, destination authority, permissions, data contracts, error handling, testing, and monitoring.
- Expansion, pause, narrowing, rollback, and retirement should all use reviewable decision records.
- Roadmap priority, launch, adoption, paid delivery, revenue, token demand, liquidity, price, and financial outcome must not be treated as interchangeable.
