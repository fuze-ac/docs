# ZAGA Districts

## Executive Summary

ZAGA Districts is the persistent, Telegram-native cyberpunk MMORPG and community city-builder in the ZAGA family. A participating group can form a district, assign game roles, recruit members, hire NPCs, construct facilities, manage simulated resources, respond to world events, defend against rivals, and build reputation over time.

The community is the central game identity. Member decisions shape the district's development, game economy, alliances, conflicts, and quest progress. Telegram-ready surfaces can bring updates and coordination into the environment where the group already communicates.

Treasuries, taxes, salaries, resources, markets, and city power are gameplay systems. Their names do not establish real financial accounts, employment, ownership, income, or investment value. ZAGA Arena remains a separate short-session battle game.

---

## 1. Product Purpose

Many online groups organize around discussion or temporary campaigns but have limited shared activity between major events. ZAGA Districts gives a community an ongoing world to build and manage together.

The product is intended to support:

- a persistent district or city identity;
- member roles and permissions;
- group objectives and long-term progression;
- NPC hiring and assignment;
- structures and upgrades;
- simulated treasury and resource decisions;
- alliances, rivalries, defense, and conflict;
- quests and world events;
- Telegram-ready coordination; and
- public-safe city records and reports.

The game should give members meaningful choices at both individual and group levels while keeping the operating rules understandable.

## 3. District Lifecycle

### Step 1: Establish the district

An authorized organizer selects a district name, visual identity, starting community, and initial settings. The game creates the district record and applicable starting resources.

### Step 2: Assign roles

Members can receive supported roles such as leader, planner, builder, defender, scout, trader, or another game function. Roles determine game permissions rather than real-world employment or legal authority.

### Step 3: Choose development priorities

The community decides whether to invest simulated resources in construction, NPCs, production, defense, exploration, diplomacy, or an event objective.

### Step 4: Complete activity

Members perform quests, contribute to projects, respond to events, and take part in conflict or cooperation according to the game rules.

### Step 5: Resolve outcomes

The game applies relevant resource, time, building, NPC, role, and event conditions. Outcomes update the district's status, progress, reputation, and available choices.

### Step 6: Review and coordinate

Telegram-ready notices, dashboards, and reports can help members understand what changed and what decisions are pending.

### Step 7: Continue the world

New quests, upgrades, alliances, threats, or seasonal events create the next set of group decisions.

## 4. Community Identity and Membership

A district can represent a city, gang, faction, alliance, or another cyberpunk group identity. Its profile can include:

- name and emblem;
- description and public status;
- level or reputation;
- current leaders and game roles;
- active structures and NPCs;
- alliances or rivalries;
- quest and event progress;
- leaderboard position; and
- approved public achievements.

Recruitment can occur through invitations, applications, event entry, or another supported route. Membership controls should address duplicate accounts, inactive roles, abusive behavior, automated farming, and changes in community administration.

Contribution records can recognize game activity, but the product should explain which actions count and how disputes or corrections are handled.

## 5. NPCs and Structures

### NPCs

Districts can hire and assign non-player characters for game functions such as:

- building;
- guarding;
- scouting;
- trading;
- engineering;
- medical support;
- technical or hacking activity;
- resource production;
- quest operations; and
- district administration.

An NPC can have a cost, salary, skill, assignment, cooldown, productivity level, loyalty state, or event outcome as defined by the game.

### Structures

Possible facilities include:

- headquarters;
- defense tower;
- marketplace;
- factory;
- training center;
- energy station;
- treasury vault;
- communication hub;
- clinic;
- workshop;
- warehouse;
- transport hub; and
- faction hall.

Construction and upgrades can require simulated resources, time, district level, member activity, assigned NPCs, or a quest result. The interface should show the requirements and expected game effect before the community confirms the action.

## 6. Simulated Economy

The district economy creates shared strategy through game resources and constraints.

### Treasury and resources

A simulated treasury can record resources available for buildings, NPCs, defense, events, maintenance, or other game uses. Production and spending rules determine how the balance changes.

### Taxes

A district can select a game tax or contribution setting that affects resource collection, member experience, production, market activity, or another defined system.

### Salaries and maintenance

NPC salaries and structure maintenance can create recurring game costs. Communities may choose between stronger staffing, faster growth, greater reserves, or lower ongoing expense.

### Decision records

Important allocations can identify the initiating role, applicable rule, amount of game resources, outcome, and correction status.

All values in this section are simulated game mechanics. A district treasury is not a bank or investment account, an NPC salary is not compensation to a real worker, and a game tax is not a governmental levy.

## 7. Defense, Conflict, and Diplomacy

Districts can face rivals, raids, territory contests, resource pressure, hacking events, or other cyberpunk conflict.

Relevant choices can include:

- assigning NPCs to defense;
- upgrading defensive structures;
- protecting resources;
- forming an alliance;
- scouting a rival;
- preparing an attack;
- responding to city damage;
- negotiating a truce; or
- prioritizing repair over expansion.

Conflict rules should identify timing, eligibility, targeting, resource effects, cooldowns, victory conditions, and dispute processes. Community competition should also include moderation and anti-abuse controls appropriate to the connected platforms.

## 8. Quests and World Events

Quests can provide individual and district objectives, including:

- recruitment and onboarding;
- construction;
- production;
- exploration;
- NPC missions;
- alliance work;
- defense;
- rival challenges;
- community contribution; and
- seasonal campaigns.

World events can alter the shared environment through a blackout, resource shortage, faction conflict, cyber attack, NPC dispute, district election, festival, market-themed shock, or another scripted or AI-assisted event.

AI may help generate event text or variations where supported, but approved rules determine the actual state change. Operators should review public event wording and game effects before release.

## 9. Telegram-Native Operation

A supported Telegram integration can provide:

- district entry or linking;
- bot commands;
- Mini App access;
- role and quest notices;
- construction and NPC updates;
- defense alerts;
- event reminders;
- voting or decision prompts where implemented;
- status summaries; and
- links back to the authoritative game surface.

Group messages can help coordination, but chat should not become the sole authoritative record for significant game state. Official commands and updates should be distinguishable from ordinary member content or impersonation attempts.

[CommunityLayer AI](./07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) may assist with approved support, moderation, and community summaries. Game-state changes continue to follow Districts rules and authorized systems.

## 10. Platform Credits and Wallet-Aware Utility

Platform Credits may apply to defined product services such as:

- creating or customizing a district;
- using selected city-management tools;
- preparing an event or quest;
- generating an approved city report;
- using an AI-assisted world-event function;
- accessing a premium interface or utility; or
- processing another stated Districts action.

The product should show the chargeable operation before confirmation. Credits are product-consumption units, not district resources or a tradable game currency.

Some activated features may use wallet status for access, recognition, role assignment, or game-related utility. The applicable asset, network, snapshot, and eligibility rule must be stated for that feature. Wallet holding alone does not create an automatic right to every district, role, resource, event, or outcome.

The [ZAGA family paper](./08-ZAGA_PUBLIC.md) provides the wider utility context.

## 11. Data and Permission Controls

Districts may process:

| Record | Examples |
|---|---|
| Community | Telegram group reference, district, faction, alliance, and public profile |
| Member | Game account, display identity, role, contribution, and status |
| World | Structures, NPCs, resources, events, quests, and conflicts |
| Operational | Commands, errors, anti-abuse findings, moderation, and corrections |
| Wallet | Address and eligibility status only where an activated feature requires it |
| Usage | Platform Credit action and related product record |

Leaders should see information needed for district management without receiving unrelated private member data. Public city profiles and leaderboards can use chosen game identities and aggregated records.

Wallet-level reporting must not publish or infer personal identity. Security, moderation, and conflict evidence should remain restricted to authorized reviewers.

For wider principles, see [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

## 12. Reporting

District reports may cover:

- level and reputation;
- structures and upgrade status;
- NPC assignments;
- simulated resource and treasury movement;
- quests and world events;
- alliances, attacks, defense, and repair;
- member contribution under stated rules;
- campaign and leaderboard results;
- invalid actions or corrections;
- Platform Credit usage; and
- product or integration status.

A report should identify its period, source, and whether values are current, delayed, simulated, or provisional. Public views should protect private group discussion, member identity, wallet linkage, security records, and operator notes.

---

## 13. Product Status and Boundary

ZAGA Districts is presented as a developing product. Telegram integration, persistent world systems, NPCs, buildings, simulated economy, conflict, AI-assisted events, wallet utility, and reporting may reach availability in stages.

Game roles do not create employment. District resources and treasuries do not represent deposits or investments. Taxes, salaries, production, markets, and city power remain gameplay concepts unless a separate approved feature explicitly defines an external utility.

[ZAGA Arena](./09-ZAGA_ARENA_PUBLIC.md) is a separate battle game. Arena scores and runs do not automatically become Districts resources, buildings, roles, or community progress.

---

## 14. Conclusion

ZAGA Districts turns a community into the long-term player. Members coordinate roles, NPCs, structures, resources, quests, and conflict to shape a persistent cyberpunk district.

Its success depends on clear game rules, meaningful group choices, reliable state records, controlled Telegram connections, and firm separation between simulated economy language and real-world financial claims.
