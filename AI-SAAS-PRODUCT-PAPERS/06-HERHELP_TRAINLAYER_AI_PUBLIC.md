# HerHelp TrainLayer AI

## Executive Summary

TrainLayer AI helps organizations turn approved source knowledge into structured, reviewable, and maintainable learning material.

A shop, small business, product team, support team, community, trainer, event operator, or other authorized workspace may use it to prepare:

- quick-start guides;
- onboarding modules;
- standard operating procedures;
- role-based learning paths;
- checklists;
- scenario exercises;
- quizzes;
- refresher material;
- customer tutorials;
- trainer notes;
- revision notices; and
- learning reports.

TrainLayer AI connects:

- source ownership;
- learning objectives;
- audience and role design;
- AI-assisted drafting;
- subject review;
- publication approval;
- assignment;
- learner access;
- progress records;
- source-change review;
- version control; and
- retirement of outdated material.

The product helps prepare and operate learning workflows. It does not independently certify source accuracy, learner competence, professional qualification, employment readiness, legal compliance, safety compliance, or regulatory approval.

A completed module or passing quiz is evidence of interaction with a defined learning activity. It is not automatically evidence of real-world mastery, safe independent performance, or professional certification.

## Product Purpose

Operational knowledge is often scattered across:

- chat messages;
- documents;
- spreadsheets;
- screenshots;
- videos;
- support articles;
- policy files;
- product notes;
- staff memory;
- repeated verbal explanations;
- old training decks; and
- untracked local copies.

This creates common problems:

- new staff depend on whoever is available;
- different trainers explain the same task differently;
- outdated instructions remain in use;
- important exceptions are omitted;
- public and internal instructions are mixed;
- private cases are reused inappropriately;
- quiz answers are generated without an approved source;
- content owners are unclear;
- source changes do not reach affected modules; and
- completion records are mistaken for competence.

TrainLayer AI provides a controlled route from approved source knowledge to a defined learning experience.

It may help users:

- organize a process into teachable steps;
- identify missing or conflicting source information;
- adapt approved material for different roles;
- prepare examples and practice activities;
- generate knowledge-check drafts;
- create revision and refresher material;
- connect training to operational workflows;
- maintain reviewed versions;
- identify modules affected by a source change; and
- report on content and learner activity where supported.

The product can support one short guide or a collection of related modules.

## Source Authority and Content Ownership

TrainLayer AI should identify the role and authority of each source.

| Source type | Typical role |
|---|---|
| Approved policy | Governing rule for the defined scope and effective period |
| Operating procedure | Current process instruction owned by the responsible team |
| Product documentation | Release-specific usage or support source |
| Spreadsheet or data export | Structured source requiring field, version, and access review |
| Support article | Reviewed answer or troubleshooting source |
| Approved script | Communication or service source approved for reuse |
| Diagram or screenshot | Supporting illustration, not necessarily complete authority |
| Video or recording | Demonstration source requiring ownership, date, and version review |
| Expert note | Specialist input requiring confirmation before publication |
| Previous training module | Derived content that may be outdated or superseded |
| Learner submission | Assessment or practice evidence, not a governing source |

A source record should identify, where possible:

- owner;
- title;
- purpose;
- audience;
- version;
- effective date;
- review date;
- approval state;
- confidentiality level;
- permitted use;
- retirement state; and
- correction contact.

TrainLayer AI should distinguish:

- directly supplied source content;
- AI interpretation;
- author-added content;
- reviewer-approved content;
- unresolved conflict;
- unsupported addition;
- retired source; and
- current approved source.

A polished module does not become authoritative merely because it is easy to read.

The appropriate source owner remains responsible for governing content.

## Learning Objective Design

A learning objective should identify what the learner is expected to:

- know;
- recognize;
- explain;
- prepare;
- demonstrate;
- complete;
- review;
- escalate; or
- avoid

after the learning activity.

A useful objective should specify:

- audience;
- context;
- expected behavior;
- required accuracy;
- supervision level;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- completion condition; and
- any boundary on independent action.

Examples include:

- identify the correct opening checklist for the assigned shop role;
- explain which order states require manager review;
- prepare a public-safe community update without exposing member identity;
- demonstrate the approved workbook update sequence using sample data;
- recognize when a customer issue must be escalated;
- complete a supervised setup task using the current product release; or
- distinguish payment confirmation from reconciliation and revenue treatment.

Objectives such as “understand everything” or “be fully qualified” are too broad to support reliable content or assessment.

## Source-to-Learning Workflow

### 1. Define the Learning Need

The author identifies:

- audience;
- role;
- purpose;
- expected behavior;
- context of use;
- required depth;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- risk level;
- supervision requirement;
- completion condition; and
- review owner.

### 2. Add Authorized Sources

Sources may include:

- process notes;
- policies;
- product instructions;
- approved scripts;
- spreadsheet explanations;
- diagrams;
- support articles;
- approved examples;
- screenshots;
- videos;
- prior learning material; or
- other content the author is permitted to use.

TrainLayer AI may organize and compare these sources.

Conflicts, missing steps, unsupported claims, unclear ownership, or outdated material should return to the source owner for resolution.

### 3. Classify the Material

The author or reviewer classifies source material as:

- public;
- internal;
- role-restricted;
- customer-specific;
- employee-related;
- safety-sensitive;
- finance-sensitive;
- legally sensitive;
- partner-confidential; or
- otherwise restricted.

This classification should control:

- author access;
- learner access;
- AI processing;
- examples;
- exports;
- publication; and
- reporting.

### 4. Design the Learning Structure

The author selects a format such as:

- quick-start guide;
- onboarding module;
- step-by-step procedure;
- role-based learning path;
- customer tutorial;
- scenario practice;
- reference checklist;
- refresher lesson;
- trainer-led session;
- supervised practical task; or
- assessment pack.

The structure may include:

- title;
- purpose;
- objectives;
- audience;
- prerequisites;
- source references;
- sections;
- examples;
- common errors;
- exceptions;
- escalation paths;
- practice;
- knowledge checks;
- completion rules;
- review date; and
- current version.

### 5. Generate a Draft

TrainLayer AI prepares a draft from the selected sources and instructions.

Generated content should retain enough source context for reviewers to verify important statements.

Drafts should show, where relevant:

- source references;
- assumptions;
- unresolved questions;
- AI-added transitions;
- proposed examples;
- proposed assessment items;
- missing evidence; and
- content requiring specialist review.

### 6. Add Practice and Assessment

The author may add:

- multiple-choice questions;
- true-or-false questions;
- short-answer questions;
- sequence questions;
- matching tasks;
- scenario decisions;
- error-identification tasks;
- escalation-choice tasks;
- observed demonstrations; and
- supervised practical tasks.

Answer guidance should come from approved source material or an explicitly approved specialist standard.

The product should not invent a rule merely to complete a quiz.

### 7. Review the Draft

Review may involve:

- source owner;
- subject-matter reviewer;
- training owner;
- operational reviewer;
- safety reviewer;
- legal or compliance reviewer;
- language reviewer;
- accessibility reviewer;
- publisher; and
- data or privacy reviewer.

Not every module requires every reviewer.

Review depth should match the subject and risk.

### 8. Approve and Publish

An approved module should identify:

- owner;
- audience;
- role scope;
- current version;
- effective date;
- source set;
- reviewer;
- approval state;
- review date;
- expiry or retirement rule; and
- correction contact.

Only approved material should be assigned or published as current training.

### 9. Assign and Deliver

Where supported, approved material may be:

- assigned to individuals;
- assigned by role;
- assigned by team;
- shared by link;
- exported;
- presented by a trainer;
- used in a supervised session;
- connected to another supported learning surface; or
- embedded into an operational workflow.

Learners should see only material appropriate to their role and purpose.

### 10. Record Participation

Where supported, the product may record:

- assignment;
- start;
- progress;
- completion;
- quiz response;
- score;
- attempt;
- trainer review;
- practical observation;
- acknowledgement;
- overdue state;
- correction; and
- reassignment.

Participation records should not be interpreted beyond what the metric actually demonstrates.

### 11. Revise When Sources Change

A change to:

- price;
- policy;
- product behavior;
- interface;
- operating process;
- safety instruction;
- legal requirement;
- role;
- escalation path;
- payment process;
- privacy rule;
- event plan; or
- customer communication

may trigger a learning review.

Affected modules should be identified, reviewed, updated, republished, reassigned, or retired as appropriate.

### 12. Retire or Archive

Outdated material should move to a visible non-current state.

Possible states include:

- draft;
- under review;
- approved;
- published;
- assigned;
- active;
- revision required;
- expired;
- superseded;
- corrected;
- withdrawn;
- retired; and
- archived.

Learners should be able to identify the current approved version.

## Learning Components

### Guides and Lessons

A guide may combine concise explanation with a sequence of actions.

Longer subjects may be divided into modules so learners can complete and revisit them in manageable sections.

A guide should identify:

- audience;
- source;
- version;
- objective;
- prerequisites;
- current scope;
- exceptions;
- escalation path; and
- review state.

### Standard Operating Procedures

TrainLayer AI may convert an approved process into a procedure containing:

- purpose;
- roles;
- prerequisites;
- required tools;
- ordered steps;
- decision points;
- exceptions;
- evidence;
- completion checks;
- escalation path;
- safety notes; and
- correction route.

The generated procedure remains subject to operational and specialist review.

A learning procedure should not silently become the governing operational policy if another approved source holds that role.

### Checklists

Checklists may support:

- opening;
- closing;
- setup;
- cleaning;
- handover;
- stock count;
- event preparation;
- moderation;
- device checks;
- customer service;
- quality review;
- safety review; or
- another repeatable task.

A checklist should distinguish:

- learning practice;
- live operational use;
- supervised completion;
- required evidence;
- reviewer approval; and
- exception handling.

### Examples and Scenarios

Examples show how a rule or process applies in context.

Scenario material may help learners practice:

- judgment;
- escalation;
- customer communication;
- moderation;
- safety response;
- service recovery;
- exception handling; and
- operational prioritization.

Real cases should be anonymized, redacted, synthesized, or otherwise controlled where identity or confidential information is unnecessary.

A scenario should not misrepresent a fictional or simplified example as a complete professional standard.

### Quizzes and Knowledge Checks

Assessment formats may include:

- multiple choice;
- true or false;
- short answer;
- ordering steps;
- identifying an error;
- selecting an escalation path;
- interpreting a scenario;
- explaining a rule; and
- completing a supervised practical task.

Assessment design should address:

- source alignment;
- difficulty;
- ambiguity;
- answer validity;
- distractor quality;
- retake policy;
- pass rule;
- feedback;
- attempt limit;
- accommodations where appropriate;
- practical observation; and
- review of disputed questions.

A quiz score may show how a learner responded to a defined set of questions.

It does not independently prove:

- practical competence;
- safe independent work;
- professional qualification;
- lawful authorization;
- employment suitability;
- performance quality; or
- regulatory certification.

An organization may assign different modules to:

- owners;
- managers;
- counter staff;
- preparation staff;
- finance reviewers;
- moderators;
- volunteers;
- support agents;
- trainers;
- publishers;

- required modules;
- optional modules;
- prerequisites;
- order;
- due dates;
- supervised tasks;
- assessment rules;
- renewal or refresher requirements;
- manager approval; and
- completion state.

Shared content may be reused while restricted procedures remain visible only to the relevant role.

### Refresher and Change Training

A change-focused module may explain:

- what changed;
- why it changed;
- effective date;
- affected roles;
- previous process;
- new process;
- transition steps;
- exceptions;
- acknowledgement requirement;
- practical review; and

A change notice alone may not be sufficient for high-impact operational, safety, legal, financial, or regulated changes.

## Practical Workflows

### Shop Onboarding

A shop owner supplies approved opening, order, queue, stock, service, payment-review, handover, and closing procedures from [ShopOS AI](04-HERHELP_SHOPOS_AI_PUBLIC.md).

TrainLayer AI prepares:

- role-specific modules;
- short checks;
- shift checklists;
- example scenarios;
- manager review points; and
- refresher material.

The owner or process owner reviews each procedure before assignment.

A completed module does not replace supervised operational practice.

### Spreadsheet Process Training

[SheetLayer AI](03-HERHELP_SHEETLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) prepares an approved explanation of a workbook's tabs, fields, formulas, update rules, permissions, and correction process.

TrainLayer AI turns that explanation into:

- maintenance guide;
- sample-data practice;
- formula-review exercise;
- update checklist;
- error-identification scenario; and
- escalation guidance.

Live sensitive data should not be used where sample or redacted data is sufficient.

### Customer Product Education

A product team provides current setup steps, interface notes, feature status, supported workflows, limitations, and support answers.

TrainLayer AI prepares:

- beginner path;
- setup guide;
- troubleshooting guide;
- common-error lesson;
- feature-specific module; and
- customer knowledge check where appropriate.

The product owner confirms that screenshots, feature status, and instructions match the relevant release.

A customer tutorial should not imply that an unreleased or unavailable function is live.

### Moderator Preparation

A community administrator supplies:

- rules;
- role boundaries;
- escalation paths;
- appeals process;
- safety guidance;
- anonymized examples; and
- current moderation procedures.

TrainLayer AI creates scenario exercises for moderators.

[CommunityLayer AI](07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) remains the operating workspace for live community cases.

A training scenario should not expose real member identity or become the final decision in a live case.

### Event Briefing

An event team creates separate modules for:

- registration;
- booth support;
- stage work;
- attendee guidance;
- sponsor handling;
- accessibility;
- safety escalation;
- equipment;
- closing; and
- incident reporting.

Each volunteer or staff member receives only the modules relevant to the assigned role.

### Communication Practice

Approved greeting, announcement, pronunciation, or issue-response material from [SpeakShop AI](05-HERHELP_SPEAKSHOP_AI_PUBLIC.md) may become staff practice content.

TrainLayer AI may add:

- speaking guidance;
- role-play scenarios;
- customer-response practice;
- pronunciation review; and
- supervisor observation.

Unrelated customer, campaign, payment, or private source records should not be imported.

### Policy Update

A policy owner replaces an approved source.

TrainLayer AI identifies modules linked to the previous version and marks them for review.

The content owner decides whether to:

- revise selected sections;
- create a change module;
- reassign affected learners;
- require acknowledgement;
- require new assessment;
- retire the old module; or
- preserve the old version for historical reference.

## Competency, Certification, and Employment Boundaries

TrainLayer AI may support learning, practice, observation, and reporting.

It does not independently:

- accredit a course;
- certify a profession;
- license a worker;
- authorize regulated activity;
- prove legal competency;
- prove safe independent performance;
- make an employment decision;
- replace supervised practice;
- replace a qualified assessor;
- establish regulatory compliance; or
- create a legally recognized qualification.

A stronger competency process may require:

- approved curriculum;
- qualified trainer;
- practical demonstration;
- supervised observation;
- identity verification;
- validated assessment;
- repeat assessment;
- evidence review;
- manager or assessor approval;
- refresher requirement; and
- external certification where applicable.

TrainLayer AI may record elements of such a process where supported, but it should not overstate what those records prove.

## Key Takeaways

- TrainLayer AI turns approved source knowledge into structured, reviewable, and maintainable learning material.
- Source ownership, objective design, audience, review, publication, assignment, progress, revision, and retirement are all part of the product workflow.
- AI-generated explanations, scenarios, quizzes, and answer keys remain drafts until reviewed.
- Role-based learning paths should expose only the material appropriate to the learner's role.
- Completion, quiz results, practical observation, internal competency, and external certification are different states.
- Learner and assessment data require purpose-based access and should not become public or be reused automatically for unrelated decisions.
- Platform Credits meter defined authoring and processing tasks and remain separate from scores, certificates, payments, wallets, and FUZE token.
- Learning activity does not independently prove mastery, compliance, adoption, or revenue.
- This paper does not prove implementation, live release, learner tracking, certification capability, paid delivery, or revenue.
- TrainLayer AI succeeds only when learning remains current, source-grounded, appropriately reviewed, proportionately assigned, and honestly interpreted.
