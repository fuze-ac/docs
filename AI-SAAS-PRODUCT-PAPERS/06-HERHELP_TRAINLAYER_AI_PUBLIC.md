# HerHelp TrainLayer AI

## Executive Summary

TrainLayer AI helps organizations turn approved source knowledge into structured learning material. A shop, small business, product team, community, trainer, or event operator can use it to prepare guides, lessons, onboarding modules, checklists, practice scenarios, quizzes, and revision material for a defined audience.

The product keeps source material, learning design, reviewer decisions, and published versions connected. This makes it easier to update training when a process changes and to distinguish reviewed content from an AI-generated draft.

TrainLayer AI supports content preparation and learning operations. It does not certify subject accuracy, learner competence, regulatory compliance, or professional qualification. Source owners and designated reviewers remain responsible for the material and its use.

---

## 1. Product Purpose

Operational knowledge is often scattered across messages, documents, spreadsheets, videos, screenshots, staff memory, and repeated verbal explanations. New staff or community members then depend on whoever is available to explain the process.

TrainLayer AI provides a reusable route from that source material to a learning experience. It may help users:

- organize a process into teachable steps;
- adapt one source for different roles;
- create examples and practice activities;
- prepare knowledge checks;
- identify missing or conflicting source information;
- maintain a reviewed version of a guide;
- update affected modules after a source change; and
- report on content and learning activity where supported.

The product is intended for practical instruction and onboarding. It can support one short guide or a collection of related modules.

| User | Typical training objective |
|---|---|
| Shop owner | Teach opening, service, stock, queue, and closing routines |
| Small-business manager | Standardize internal processes and role onboarding |
| Product team | Explain product setup, use, support, and common tasks |
| Customer-support lead | Convert recurring questions into reviewed learning content |
| Community administrator | Prepare member orientation and moderator scenarios |

Different audiences require different detail. A customer guide, staff procedure, moderator scenario, and manager checklist should not be produced as interchangeable versions of the same document.

## 3. Source-to-Learning Workflow

### Step 1: Define the learning need

The author identifies the audience, purpose, expected behavior, and appropriate depth. A useful objective describes what the learner should understand or be able to do after completing the material.

### Step 2: Add authorized sources

Sources may include process notes, policies, product instructions, approved scripts, spreadsheet explanations, diagrams, support articles, or other material the author is permitted to use.

TrainLayer AI can organize and compare these inputs. Conflicts, missing steps, unsupported claims, or unclear ownership should return to the source owner for resolution.

### Step 3: Design the structure

The author chooses a format such as:

- quick-start guide;
- onboarding module;
- step-by-step procedure;
- role-based learning path;
- scenario practice;
- reference checklist;
- customer tutorial; or
- refresher lesson.

The structure can include objectives, prerequisites, sections, examples, common errors, practice, and review points.

### Step 4: Generate a draft

TrainLayer AI prepares content from the selected sources and instructions. Generated material should retain enough source context for a reviewer to check important statements.

### Step 5: Add checks and activities

The author may request multiple-choice, short-answer, sequence, scenario, or practical-task questions. Answer guidance should come from the approved material rather than an invented standard.

### Step 6: Review and approve

A subject owner checks factual accuracy, a training owner checks clarity and audience fit, and an authorized publisher approves release. One person may hold several roles in a small organization, but the responsibilities should remain clear.

### Step 7: Deliver and monitor

Approved material can be assigned, shared, exported, or connected to a supported learning surface. Learner access and progress records follow the workspace's permission and retention settings.

### Step 8: Revise when the source changes

A change to price, policy, product behavior, safety instruction, or operating process can trigger a content review. Older versions should be archived or retired so learners can identify the current approved material.

## 4. Learning Components

### Guides and lessons

A guide can combine concise explanation with a sequence of actions. Longer subjects can be divided into modules so learners can complete and revisit them in manageable sections.

### Standard operating procedures

TrainLayer AI can convert an approved process into a procedure with roles, prerequisites, ordered steps, exceptions, and completion checks. The generated procedure remains subject to operational review.

### Checklists

Checklists help learners apply a process during work. They can support opening, closing, setup, handover, event preparation, moderation, or another repeatable task.

### Examples and scenarios

Examples show how a rule or process applies in context. Scenario material can help learners practice judgment, escalation, or customer communication without exposing real private cases.

### Quizzes and knowledge checks

Assessment formats may include:

- multiple choice;
- true or false;
- short answer;
- ordering steps;
- identifying an error;
- choosing an escalation path; and
- completing a supervised practical task.

Quiz scores provide limited evidence about a learner's interaction with the material. They should not be treated as proof of real-world competence without an appropriate assessment process.

### Role-based learning paths

An organization can assign different modules to owners, managers, counter staff, moderators, volunteers, support agents, or customers. Shared material can be reused while restricted procedures remain visible only to the relevant role.

## 5. Practical Workflows

### Shop onboarding

A shop owner supplies approved opening, order, queue, stock, service, and closing processes from [ShopOS AI](./04-HERHELP_SHOPOS_AI_PUBLIC.md). TrainLayer AI creates role-specific modules, short checks, and shift checklists. The owner reviews each procedure before assigning it to staff.

### Spreadsheet process training

[SheetLayer AI](./03-HERHELP_SHEETLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) prepares an approved explanation of a workbook's tabs, fields, formulas, and update rules. TrainLayer AI turns that explanation into a guide for staff who maintain the records, including a practice task that uses sample rather than live sensitive data.

### Customer product education

A product team provides current setup steps, interface notes, and support answers. TrainLayer AI prepares a beginner path and troubleshooting guide. The product owner confirms that screenshots, feature status, and instructions match the relevant release.

### Moderator preparation

A community administrator supplies rules, escalation paths, and anonymized examples. TrainLayer AI creates scenario exercises for moderators. [CommunityLayer AI](./07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) remains the operating workspace for live community cases.

### Event briefing

An event team creates separate guides for registration, booth support, stage work, safety escalation, and closing. Each volunteer receives only the modules relevant to the assigned role.

### Communication practice

Approved customer greeting or announcement text from [SpeakShop AI](./05-HERHELP_SPEAKSHOP_AI_PUBLIC.md) can become staff practice material. Delivery guidance is taught without importing unrelated customer or campaign records.

## 6. Authoring and Review Controls

TrainLayer AI can support distinct roles for:

- source owner;
- content author;
- subject reviewer;
- publisher;
- trainer or manager;
- learner; and
- reporting viewer.

Useful controls may include:

- source and module permissions;
- draft, review, approved, and retired statuses;
- version history;
- reviewer comments;
- required approval before publication;
- assignment by role or group;
- expiry or review dates;
- restricted exports; and
- change notices for affected material.

AI-generated additions should be visible as draft content until a reviewer confirms them. A published module should identify its owner and current version.

## 7. Platform Credit Use

TrainLayer AI may use Platform Credits for metered authoring and processing actions, including:

- converting source material into a module draft;
- creating a role-based variant;
- generating practice scenarios or quiz items;
- restructuring a long procedure;
- preparing a translation draft;
- comparing a revised source with current training;
- updating selected modules; or
- producing a training summary.

Learner access to finished material may follow a different product model from authoring activity. A workspace administrator should be able to distinguish content-creation usage from learner participation.

Platform Credits measure supported product processing. They remain separate from the FUZE token.

---

## 8. Data and Permission Controls

Training workspaces can contain internal processes, employee information, learner records, customer-support patterns, moderation cases, or confidential product details.

Controls should address:

- authority to use each source;
- separation of public and internal material;
- role-based module access;
- removal or anonymization of real case identities;
- limited use of learner data;
- assessment visibility;
- retention of progress and completion records;
- export permissions;
- deletion and correction requests; and
- revocation of obsolete connections.

Learner reporting should not expose personal results beyond the people who need them for the defined training purpose. Public examples should use aggregated or non-identifying information.

The wider handling model is described in [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

## 9. Reporting

TrainLayer reporting may cover two separate areas.

### Content reporting

This can show:

- modules by status and owner;
- source and revision dates;
- review or approval progress;
- assigned audiences;
- expired or outdated material;
- changes awaiting publication; and
- Platform Credit usage for authoring.

### Learning reporting

Where supported and appropriate, this can show:

- assignments issued;
- modules started or completed;
- knowledge-check results;
- repeated problem areas;
- overdue learning; and
- learner or group progress.

Reports should identify what the metric actually demonstrates. Completion shows that a learner reached a defined stage; it does not by itself establish mastery or workplace performance.

---

## 10. Product Status and Boundary

TrainLayer AI is presented as a developing product. Source processing, module design, quizzes, assignments, learner tracking, translations, integrations, and reporting may have different release status.

Subject owners must validate material that affects safety, employment, financial activity, regulated work, professional practice, or legal rights. The product is an authoring and learning-support system rather than an accredited institution or certification authority.

Its training purpose does not require FUZE token or wallet participation. Any separate ecosystem activity is governed through dedicated documents.

---

## 11. Conclusion

TrainLayer AI gives organizations a controlled method for converting existing knowledge into reusable learning. It connects source ownership, audience design, AI-assisted drafting, human review, assignment, and revision in one workflow.

The result is most useful when the material stays current, reviewers remain accountable, learner access is proportionate, and reports are interpreted according to what they actually measure.
