# FUZE Product Risk Boundaries

## Executive Summary

This paper defines how FUZE product capabilities, limitations, and outcomes should be described publicly. It covers AI-assisted work, business operations, games, market interpretation, liquidity operations, events, sponsored discovery, and desktop execution.

The central rule is to describe the supported workflow and the evidence available for it. Product access, an AI output, a game result, a report, a sponsored placement, or an operational tool does not by itself establish a commercial, financial, safety, learning, market, or adoption outcome.

Detailed legal, token, investment, privacy, and market disclosures remain in their dedicated papers. This document provides the product-level review framework.

---

## 1. Purpose

FUZE products address different users and operating environments. Their public claims therefore require different evidence and boundaries.

This guide helps authors and reviewers:

- identify the actual product function;
- separate current capability from roadmap direction;
- state the user or operator responsibility;
- distinguish AI assistance from an approved decision;
- keep game values separate from financial value;
- describe market tools without advice or outcome promises;
- label sponsored placement;
- protect private identity and records; and
- route detailed disclosure to the correct paper.

It is a public-language and product-risk framework rather than a legal opinion or product certification.

---

## 2. Claim Review Method

Every material product claim should answer six questions.

| Question | Review point |
|---|---|
| What is the function? | Name the task, workflow, output, or supported action |
| What is the status? | Distinguish design, testing, limited release, and active availability |
| What is the source? | Identify the data, user input, game rule, or operating record |
| Who decides? | State the user, reviewer, moderator, operator, or other authority |
| What can vary? | Describe relevant data, market, provider, device, user, or implementation conditions |
| What evidence exists? | Use a report, test, record, reference, or clearly labeled example |

A statement that cannot answer these questions should be narrowed, supported, or removed.

## 3. Cross-Product Boundaries

### AI output

FUZE products can draft, summarize, classify, compare, explain, route, and prepare actions. Important outputs require review appropriate to their use. Source quality, context, model behavior, and permissions affect the result.

### Product outcomes

Software can support a business, team, community, learner, event, campaign, or operator. Revenue, customer growth, learning, safety, attendance, conversion, and operational success also depend on real-world factors outside the product.

### Reporting

Reports show what their source and method support. They should distinguish observed activity from estimates, examples, targets, interpretation, and future plans.

### Status and roadmap

Planned integrations, devices, wallet functions, campaigns, listings, and other roadmap items should not be presented as active. Status language must match the current public record.

### Privacy

Public transparency uses aggregated, non-identifying, or otherwise suitable records. Wallet visibility does not authorize publication of personal identity, private files, customer data, staff records, or restricted operational information.

## 4. Product Risk Classes

### Business and AI SaaS

HerHelp, SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, and CommunityLayer AI support defined business, data, communication, learning, and community workflows.

Review should focus on:

- source accuracy;
- permissions;
- human approval;
- customer and staff data;
- device or integration availability;
- professional and regulated decisions; and
- evidence for any claimed result.

These products should be described through the task completed, not through promised sales, staff performance, community growth, or perfect accuracy.

### Games

ZAGA, ZAGA Arena, and ZAGA Districts use scores, roles, drops, USDT, Token Value, Net Worth, treasuries, taxes, salaries, resources, and other game terminology.

Game rules determine those values. Public materials should state when a term is simulated, event-specific, account-bound, or connected to a separately activated utility. Ordinary gameplay does not create an unstated payout, investment, ownership, or resale right.

### Market interpretation

QTB organizes market research, watchlists, journal material, chart context, and public information. Its outputs are research support rather than individualized financial advice, execution instructions, or predictions of profit.

Source time, venue, uncertainty, conflicting signals, and missing data should remain visible.

### Liquidity operations

AIMM supports supervised venue, depth, spread, pool, provider, treasury, and incident workflows. Authorized people and connected systems control custody, orders, liquidity changes, provider selection, and public statements.

DEX-first direction and possible later CEX evaluation must remain conditional. Neither language establishes a listing, liquidity level, price outcome, or exit capacity.

### Events

AIE supports discovery, prioritization, planning, operations, evidence, and reporting. Event recommendations and participation do not establish attendance, sponsorship, partnership, revenue, adoption, or investment demand.

Attendee, sponsor, partner, staff, and investor-event information requires purpose-based access.

### Tool discovery and sponsored visibility

ToolGrid AI supports listings, comparisons, collections, destination review, sponsored inventory, and campaign reports.

Paid placement must be labeled and separated from neutral discovery. Impressions, clicks, and referrals are activity measures; they do not prove customers, sales, profitability, ranking, or tool quality.

### Desktop work execution

Botmad prepares artifacts and performs supported actions within defined permissions. Draft approval is separate from authority to send, publish, delete, submit, purchase, or change access.

Consequential actions require clear scope, approved destinations, appropriate review, and a record of what occurred.

## 6. Credits, Token, Stablecoins, and Wallets

These components have different product meanings.

| Component | Product-risk treatment |
|---|---|
| Platform Credits | Meter or fund defined product consumption; no ownership or investment rights |
| FUZE token | Used only where an approved ecosystem utility or participation mechanism defines it |
| Stablecoins | May support payment, settlement, treasury, or compensation functions under the applicable process |
| Wallet records | May support access, eligibility, claims, or public-safe references where activated |

Product papers should mention a component only when it affects the described workflow. Wallet-level reporting must preserve the boundary between a public address and private personal identity.

## 7. High-Risk Claims

The following claims require removal unless a precise, approved, and supportable context clearly changes their meaning:

- guaranteed revenue, customers, adoption, or community growth;
- guaranteed learning, safety, accuracy, or operational success;
- guaranteed game earnings, payout, income, or resale value;
- guaranteed token price, appreciation, yield, or return;
- guaranteed liquidity, exit, exchange listing, or price support;
- risk-free participation or investment;
- autonomous financial execution without defined control;
- guaranteed event attendance, sponsorship, or opportunity;
- guaranteed campaign conversion, ranking, sales, or profitability; and
- unrestricted autonomous access to files, systems, funds, or users.

Authors should replace the outcome claim with the actual supported function and its evidence.

---

## 8. Controls and Escalation

Product controls may include:

- identity and workspace access;
- source and destination restrictions;
- human approval;
- role separation;
- spending or action limits;
- retention and deletion rules;
- correction and appeal;
- model and tool monitoring;
- sponsored-content disclosure;
- incident response;
- audit or evidence records; and
- public-safe reporting.

Legal, financial, market, security, privacy, employment, safety, and regulated professional questions should move to the appropriate qualified reviewer.

---

## 9. Public Language

Useful verbs describe the product action:

- supports;
- prepares;
- summarizes;
- organizes;
- compares;
- routes;
- assists;
- records;
- reports; and
- enables where supported.

Qualifiers should be specific. “Where supported” is useful for an integration or staged feature, but it should not substitute for an actual status statement.

Examples and scenarios must be labeled so readers do not confuse them with live customers, completed partnerships, operating results, or financial projections.

## 11. Conclusion

FUZE product communication should explain the real workflow, current status, evidence, responsible decision-maker, and material conditions. That approach gives readers useful information without turning product support into an outcome promise.

Dedicated product papers explain mechanics; this paper supplies the common review standard.
