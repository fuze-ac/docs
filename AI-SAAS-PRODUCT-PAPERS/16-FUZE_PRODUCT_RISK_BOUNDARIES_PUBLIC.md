# FUZE Product Risk Boundaries

## Executive Summary

This paper defines the common public-risk and claim-review standard for FUZE products.

It applies across:

- AI-assisted business software;
- spreadsheet and shop operations;
- spoken communication;
- training and learning workflows;
- community operations;
- games and simulated economies;
- market research;
- liquidity-operations intelligence;
- event intelligence;
- tool discovery and sponsored visibility;
- desktop work execution; and
- connected product workflows.

The central rule is:

```text
describe the supported function
-> state the current status
-> identify the source and authority
-> explain material conditions and limitations
-> show the evidence that supports the claim
-> avoid converting capability into an outcome promise
```

A product screen, AI output, game record, alert, recommendation, event report, listing, sponsored placement, workflow approval, connected action, token reference, wallet reference, or public paper does not by itself establish:

- accuracy;
- legal sufficiency;
- safety;
- business success;
- learning success;
- attendance;
- customer conversion;
- adoption;
- partnership;
- investment;
- token demand;
- exchange access;
- liquidity;
- price support;
- revenue;
- profitability;
- payout; or
- financial return.

Every material product claim should preserve the difference between:

- designed;
- prototyped;
- implemented;
- internally tested;
- limited release;
- public beta;
- live;
- actively used;
- paid;
- revenue-confirmed; and
- publicly reported.

These states are not interchangeable.

Detailed legal, token, investment, market, privacy, security, product, and operational risks remain in the relevant dedicated papers.

This paper supplies the common product-level review framework.

## Core Product-Risk Principle

FUZE product communication should explain:

1. what the product does;
2. who uses it;
3. what source or input it relies on;
4. what authority controls the result;
5. what status the capability has;
6. what may vary;
7. what evidence supports the statement;
8. what the product does not establish; and
9. where detailed risks are explained.

A claim should be narrowed, qualified, supported, or removed when it cannot answer these questions.

## Claim Review Method

Every material product claim should be tested through the following review.

| Review question | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| What is the function? | Name the task, workflow, output, record, decision support, or supported action |
| Who is the user? | Identify the intended user, operator, reviewer, customer, community, player, or organization |
| What is the current status? | Distinguish design, prototype, implementation, testing, release, live use, paid use, and revenue evidence |
| What is the source? | Identify the data, user input, file, game rule, public record, provider record, operational system, or authoritative source |
| Who decides or acts? | Identify the user, moderator, owner, reviewer, operator, destination system, signer, or other authority |
| What can vary? | Describe data, venue, provider, device, jurisdiction, model, implementation, market, user, or operating conditions |
| What evidence exists? | Cite or describe tests, records, reports, references, operating evidence, contracts, or clearly labeled examples |
| What does the claim not prove? | State the nearby outcome that must not be inferred |
| Which paper controls the detail? | Route the reader to the relevant product, privacy, token, market, or disclosure paper |

A useful internal test is:

```text
Would a reasonable reader infer more than the evidence proves?
```

When the answer may be yes, the claim should be rewritten.

## Claim Components

A strong product claim usually contains:

- product or feature name;
- user or audience;
- supported function;
- source or input;
- current status;
- operating condition;
- responsible authority;
- evidence basis;
- limitation; and
- date or as-of context where time-sensitive.

For example:

> QTB can organize selected market sources into a reviewable research brief with venue, timeframe, source, uncertainty, and follow-up context. The output remains research support and does not predict profit or authorize execution.

This describes the workflow without converting it into a financial outcome claim.

## Status Discipline

FUZE papers should distinguish the following states.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concept | A product or capability idea has been defined |
| Designed | Purpose, users, workflow, controls, and boundaries have been documented |
| Prototype | A reviewable demonstration or limited workflow exists |
| Implemented | The cited capability works in a stated environment |
| Internally tested | Defined internal tests have been completed with recorded results |
| Limited test | Controlled external users or environments are involved |
| Public beta | Public access exists under beta scope, terms, support, and known limitations |
| Live | Production access and current operating support exist |
| Actively used | Current user or operational activity is evidenced under a stated method |
| Paid delivery | A paid service was provided under current terms and fulfillment evidence |
| Revenue confirmed | Reconciled payment, completed service, period, and accounting treatment are evidenced |
| Suspended | The capability or service is temporarily unavailable or restricted |
| Retired | The capability or service is no longer active |

A roadmap, mockup, paper, code repository, local build, screenshot, video, demonstration, test, contract discussion, provider proposal, token reference, wallet reference, or announcement does not independently establish a stronger status.

## Evidence Discipline

Different claims require different evidence.

| Claim type | Example evidence direction |
|---|---|
| Product designed | Current specification, workflow, role, control, and boundary documentation |
| Feature implemented | Working feature in the cited environment with version and test evidence |
| Integration active | Current connection, authorization, supported scope, error handling, and operating evidence |
| User activity | Defined counting method, period, timezone, unique-user treatment, and exclusions |
| Paid delivery | Pricing, authorization, payment, fulfillment, support, and customer record |
| Revenue | Reconciled payment, completed service, accounting treatment, period, and review |
| Partner relationship | Current agreement or approved public confirmation with stated scope |
| Sponsor relationship | Current approved or contracted sponsorship evidence with stated deliverables |
| Event attendance | Registration, check-in, observation, concurrency, or verified attendance under a stated method |
| Campaign performance | Defined impressions, clicks, referrals, invalid-activity treatment, attribution, and corrections |
| Token utility active | Exact function, activation, network, contract, eligibility, controls, and current evidence |
| DEX liquidity active | Current network, contracts, pool state, transactions, monitoring, and public evidence |
| CEX trading live | Current exchange market, pair, trading state, deposit and withdrawal state, and exchange evidence |

Evidence should preserve:

- source;
- owner;
- date and time;
- timezone where relevant;
- version;
- method;
- scope;
- exclusions;
- limitations;
- correction state; and
- current validity.

## Cross-Product Boundaries

### AI Assistance

- draft;
- summarize;
- classify;
- compare;
- explain;
- route;
- translate;
- organize;
- calculate;
- prepare options;
- identify conflicts;
- generate checklists;
- propose actions;
- monitor configured conditions; and
- prepare reports.

AI output does not automatically establish:

- truth;
- completeness;
- causation;
- professional suitability;
- legal compliance;
- financial suitability;
- safety;
- authorization;
- execution;
- settlement;
- publication approval; or
- outcome success.

Important outputs require review appropriate to their impact.

### Human Authority

The responsible user, organization, moderator, operator, owner, reviewer, signer, or destination system remains authoritative for consequential decisions.

The following should remain separate:

1. source observation;
2. AI interpretation;
3. draft;
4. recommendation-like decision support;
5. human review;
6. approval;
7. destination action;
8. external confirmation;
9. settlement or completion;
10. reconciliation;
11. publication; and
12. correction.

A draft is not approval.

Approval is not execution.

Submission is not completion.

Completion is not reconciliation.

A report is not necessarily independent verification.

### Product Outcomes

Software can support:

- business operations;
- customer service;
- staff work;
- learning;
- community activity;
- gameplay;
- event delivery;
- market research;
- liquidity operations;
- tool discovery;
- desktop execution; and
- reporting.

Real-world outcomes also depend on:

- users;
- staff;
- customers;
- communities;
- counterparties;
- providers;
- venues;
- networks;
- devices;
- jurisdictions;
- markets;
- timing;
- budgets;
- external services;
- operating quality;
- legal requirements;
- safety conditions; and
- other factors outside the product.

A supported workflow should not be presented as a guaranteed outcome.

### Reporting

Reports should distinguish:

- source observation;
- calculated value;
- target;
- threshold;
- estimate;
- example;
- scenario;
- self-reported value;
- verified value;
- interpretation;
- completed action;
- corrected value; and
- public claim.

Reports should identify their method and limitations.

### Roadmap and Future Direction

Planned:

- integrations;
- devices;
- connectors;
- wallet functions;
- token utilities;
- campaigns;
- sponsored inventory;
- venue access;
- listings;
- liquidity programs;
- payment methods;
- partner programs; and
- product releases

should not be presented as active before evidence supports that status.

Roadmap language should remain conditional and should not promise:

- delivery dates;
- adoption;
- listing;
- liquidity;
- revenue;
- demand;
- price;
- partner completion; or
- investment results.

### Privacy

Public transparency should use:

- aggregated;
- de-identified;
- role-appropriate;
- purpose-limited;
- public-authorized; or
- otherwise suitable records.

Public visibility does not authorize publication of:

- personal identity;
- private files;
- customer data;
- staff records;
- investor records;
- private wallet-person mappings;
- support cases;
- medical or safety information;
- private contracts;
- private commercial terms;
- credentials;
- security evidence;
- private operational strategy; or
- restricted incident information.

- purpose limitation;
- data minimization;
- workspace separation;
- role-based access;
- source authorization;
- destination authorization;
- retention;
- deletion;
- correction;
- public and private separation;
- consent and notice where required;
- de-identification;
- security controls;

A public identifier, username, device, account, cookie, wallet, or IP address does not automatically prove:

- legal identity;

- identity and workspace access;

- role separation;
- permission limits;
- human approval;

- sponsored-content disclosure;
- correction and appeal;
- incident response;
- audit or evidence records;
- public-safe reporting;
- rollback or reversal; and

The following questions should move to an appropriate qualified reviewer:

- legal;
- financial;

- child safety;

## Error, Correction, and Withdrawal

FUZE public materials should support correction when:

- product status changes;
- a feature is delayed;
- a provider changes;
- a source is corrected;
- an integration is disabled;
- a destination changes;
- a claim is overstated;
- a metric is wrong;
- a partner or sponsor relationship changes;
- a market or listing status changes;
- a privacy issue occurs;
- an incident changes the public position;
- a report uses the wrong method;
- a product is suspended or retired; or
- another material fact changes.

A correction record should identify:

- original claim;
- affected product or feature;
- source;
- correction reason;
- corrected language;
- reviewer;
- publication date;
- withdrawal or replacement requirement;
- downstream paper or report effect; and
- archive state.

Corrected, suspended, withdrawn, or superseded information should not remain represented as current without an explicit historical label.

- data exposure;

- destructive action;

- market-data error;
- moderation error;
- event-safety issue;
- game-integrity issue;

Public incident communication should balance:

- accuracy;
- timeliness;
- user protection;
- legal obligations;
- privacy;
- security;
- evidence quality;

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE product claims should describe the supported function, current status, source, authority, conditions, evidence, and limitations.
- Product capability must not be converted into a promise of business, learning, safety, market, adoption, revenue, or financial outcome.
- Design, prototype, implementation, testing, release, live use, paid delivery, and revenue are different states.
- AI observation, draft, review, approval, execution, confirmation, reconciliation, publication, and correction are different states.
- Product-specific game, market, event, sponsored-discovery, and desktop-action terminology requires explicit context and boundaries.
- Platform Credits, payments, stablecoins, wallets, FUZE token utility, claims, payouts, market access, liquidity, and price remain separate.
- Public reporting must identify method, source, period, exclusions, limitations, and correction state.
- Sponsored visibility, editorial selection, neutral relevance, personalization, partnership, certification, and endorsement must remain distinguishable.
- Privacy, security, legal, financial, market, safety, and professional questions require the applicable qualified controls and reviewers.
- Dedicated product papers explain the mechanics; this paper supplies the common public-risk and claim-review standard.
