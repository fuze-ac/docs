# ZAGA

## Executive Summary

ZAGA is the FUZE game family and the public entry point for two separate game products:

- **ZAGA Arena** — short, replayable battle-arena sessions focused on survival, bosses, scores, strategy, and competition; and
- **ZAGA Districts** — a persistent cyberpunk MMORPG and community city-building experience focused on roles, coordination, districts, resources, and long-term progression.

ZAGA provides the family-level layer for:

- product discovery;
- current-status communication;
- player and community entry;
- campaign and event coordination;
- public-safe identity;
- support and moderation routing;
- approved recognition;
- Platform Credit usage where applicable;
- wallet-aware utility where separately activated; and
- family-level reporting.

Arena and Districts remain distinct products.

They have different:

- gameplay loops;
- session structures;
- progression systems;
- records;
- communities;
- economies;
- roles;
- operating rules;
- product statuses; and
- evidence requirements.

A score, role, badge, simulated balance, treasury, salary, tax, resource, token value, market role, or city-economy term inside a ZAGA product should be understood according to that product's rules.

It does not automatically represent:

- money;
- a redeemable asset;
- legal ownership;
- a financial claim;
- a payout;
- an investment;
- token utility;
- market access; or
- future financial value.

ZAGA is product-led.

Gameplay and community experience should stand on their own before wallet-aware access, FUZE token utility, token-linked participation, or broader ecosystem mechanisms become relevant.

## Product Purpose

ZAGA gives FUZE one clear public home for its game products without merging them into one game.

A user should be able to:

- understand the difference between Arena and Districts;
- choose the experience that matches their interest;
- review current product status;
- understand supported access routes;
- review rules before participation;
- join an approved campaign or event;
- understand which profile or community record is required;
- see which product values are simulated or contextual;
- understand when Platform Credits may apply;
- understand when a wallet is or is not relevant;
- distinguish game recognition from financial rights;
- find product-specific support; and
- review suitable public activity information.

The family model also helps product teams, community operators, campaign organizers, and partners coordinate game-related activity without copying or changing gameplay rules inside every campaign document.

## ZAGA Family Structure

| Product | Core experience | Typical activity | Primary operating identity | Persistent scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ZAGA Arena](09-ZAGA_ARENA_PUBLIC.md) | Fast battle-arena play with survival, bosses, scores, strategy, and competition | Short, replayable run, match, or event | Player, run, match, and event | Limited to product-supported history, progression, recognition, and campaign records |
| [ZAGA Districts](10-ZAGA_DISTRICTS_PUBLIC.md) | Persistent cyberpunk MMORPG and community city-builder | Ongoing group progression, quests, roles, and district coordination | Community, district, city, faction, role, and member | Persistent product-supported district, resource, role, progression, and governance records |

Arena is action-first.

Districts is coordination-first.

A ZAGA campaign may reference one or both products, but each product retains its own:

- mechanics;
- entry rules;
- progression;
- records;
- leaderboards;
- recognition;
- economy;
- moderation;
- status; and
- correction process.

Cross-product recognition, progress, access, or utility must be expressly defined, technically supported, authorized, activated, and reported.

The existence of both products under ZAGA does not create automatic interoperability.

## Player, Community, and Wallet Identity Boundaries

ZAGA may use several identifiers.

| Identity or reference | Purpose | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Player profile | Gameplay and player-facing history | Not automatically legal identity |
| Display name | Public or community presentation | May be pseudonymous |
| Community profile | Group, role, and community activity | Not universal identity authority |
| District or faction role | Districts participation | Product-specific role only |
| Event reference | Campaign or event participation | Limited to the defined activity |
| Wallet address | Onchain condition where activated | Public address does not prove private identity |
| Exchange account evidence | Custodial participation where supported | Exchange address may not be user-controlled |
| Private account identity | Support, security, or compliance where required | Restricted and not public by default |

Wallet, player, community, and account records should not be merged merely because they appear in the same workspace.

A wallet address may be visible onchain, but ZAGA public reporting should not connect it to a person's private identity.

Self-custody and exchange custody require different treatment.

An exchange account may show a balance without giving the user direct control of the visible onchain holding address.

## Key Takeaways

- ZAGA is the FUZE game family, not one combined game.
- ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts have separate mechanics, identities, economies, records, rules, status, and evidence.
- Arena is action-first; Districts is coordination-first.
- Gameplay values and market-inspired terms do not automatically represent real-world assets, payments, token rights, or financial value.
- Game identity, community identity, wallet identity, and private identity remain separate.
- Wallet-aware and FUZE token utility are activation-gated and must be defined for each specific surface.
- Platform Credits meter defined product services and remain separate from scores, simulated balances, wallets, and FUZE token.
- Campaigns, leaderboards, recognition, support, moderation, appeals, and anti-abuse controls require explicit rules and correction paths.
- This paper does not prove that Arena, Districts, wallet utility, token utility, events, adoption, paid delivery, or revenue are live.
- ZAGA succeeds only when each game remains enjoyable and understandable on its own while ecosystem utility remains controlled, optional, specific, and honestly reported.
