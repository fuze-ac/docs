# ZAGA Arena

## Executive Summary

ZAGA Arena is the short-session battle game in the ZAGA family. Players enter an arena, move and dodge, fight enemy waves and bosses, collect game drops, make strategy choices, build a run score called Net Worth, and compare results through supported leaderboards, badges, and share cards.

Its visual and scoring language draws from markets. Terms such as USDT, Token Value, Liquidity Nodes, Market Roles, and Net Worth identify game mechanics unless a separately approved utility feature expressly states otherwise. They are not, by default, cash balances, financial products, investment results, or redeemable rewards.

The product is designed for browser and Telegram-ready access, replayable competition, and community events. Detailed persistent city-building belongs to ZAGA Districts, which remains a separate game.

---

## 1. Player Experience

ZAGA Arena is intended to deliver a run that a new player can enter quickly and an experienced player can replay for a better result.

A typical journey is:

1. open the supported browser or Telegram-ready surface;
2. select a profile, event, or available arena;
3. review controls and the current scoring rules;
4. enter the match;
5. move, aim, attack, dodge, and manage position;
6. survive enemy waves and boss encounters;
7. collect game values or activate strategy choices;
8. complete the run through defeat, time, objective, or another defined end condition;
9. review the result and any earned game recognition; and
10. replay, join another event, or share an approved result card.

The interface should explain why the score changed and which rules applied during the run.

## 3. Combat Loop

### Movement and positioning

Players navigate the arena while avoiding enemies, hazards, and telegraphed attacks. Position affects access to drops, safe space, targets, and objectives.

### Attacks and feedback

Supported combat may include aiming, shooting, projectiles, power-ups, damage effects, and other attack systems. Visual and audio feedback should help the player understand successful hits, incoming danger, and character status.

### Enemy waves

Waves can increase pressure by changing enemy type, number, speed, route, or behavior. The game may use ordinary mobs, stronger variants, event enemies, and market-themed opponents.

### Boss encounters

Bosses create distinct high-pressure moments. A boss can use readable attack patterns, phases, arena effects, special objectives, and game drops. Event bosses may have their own score or completion rules.

### Run ending

A run can conclude when the player is defeated, reaches a time limit, completes an objective, defeats a final boss, or meets another stated condition. The result screen should identify the end condition.

## 4. Market-Inspired Game Mechanics

ZAGA Arena uses market terminology to create a recognizable game theme.

| Term | Game meaning |
|---|---|
| USDT | A named in-game value or drop when used inside ordinary Arena scoring |
| Token Value | A game variable that can affect a run, score, or strategy |
| Net Worth | The principal game score or run-value summary |
| Liquidity Node | A contestable or drainable game objective where implemented |
| Market Role | A selectable play style or game class |
| Strategy Option | A run-specific choice affecting risk, movement, collection, combat, or scoring |

The detailed calculation should be available in the relevant game or event rules. If any value gains a separately activated external utility, that utility requires a distinct description, eligibility basis, and control process.

Market-inspired language does not provide trading guidance or a prediction about any real asset.

## 5. Strategy and Progression

Arena replayability can come from changing the player's choices rather than only increasing enemy health.

Possible strategy dimensions include:

- aggressive or defensive movement;
- boss-focused or survival-focused play;
- collecting values versus maintaining position;
- contesting a Liquidity Node;
- selecting a Market Role;
- choosing a run modifier;
- using a temporary power-up; and
- adapting to an event rule.

Progression may be expressed through game achievements, unlocked options, cosmetics, profile history, improved scores, or access to supported challenge types. Each progression item should state whether it is temporary, account-bound, event-specific, or persistent.

## 6. Results, Leaderboards, and Badges

### Run summary

A run summary may contain:

- arena and event identifier;
- survival time;
- enemies or bosses defeated;
- Net Worth score;
- game values collected;
- Market Role or strategy selected;
- completion or defeat condition;
- badges earned;
- rank at the time recorded; and
- a suitable share-card version.

### Leaderboards

Leaderboards can be daily, weekly, seasonal, event-based, boss-based, community-based, or tied to another defined metric. Every board should identify:

- scoring basis;
- eligible runs;
- start and end time;
- tie treatment;
- update timing;
- correction process; and
- anti-abuse review.

A rank can change as new eligible results arrive or invalid results are removed.

### Badges

Badges can recognize survival, boss defeat, event participation, streaks, strategy, milestones, or community activity. They represent the achievement described by their rule and do not create an unstated financial or governance right.

## 7. Community Events

An organizer can use ZAGA Arena for a time-bounded challenge.

### Event setup

The event definition can include:

- arena or boss;
- participation period;
- eligible players or communities;
- scoring rule;
- allowed devices or versions;
- leaderboard type;
- badge or other recognition;
- dispute and correction route; and
- public reporting plan.

### Telegram-ready participation

A supported Telegram surface may provide entry links, reminders, score sharing, event updates, or community leaderboards. Players should be able to distinguish official product messages from user-posted claims.

### Result publication

Organizers review anti-abuse and correction status before presenting a final result. A public recap can describe runs, scores, bosses, participation, and recognized players through chosen game identities.

[CommunityLayer AI](./07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) may assist with approved event summaries and support queues, while final rulings remain with the event operator.

---

## 8. Fair Play and Operational Review

Arena competition depends on consistent rules and reviewable telemetry. Relevant controls may include:

- supported-client and version checks;
- duplicate or automated run detection;
- impossible score or timing review;
- connection and error handling;
- event eligibility checks;
- moderation of display names and share content;
- correction and appeal records;
- separation of test data from live results; and
- operator access controls.

Automated detection can identify anomalies, but an anomaly is not automatically proof of misconduct. Material leaderboard removal or account action should follow the applicable review process.

---

## 9. Platform Credits and Utility

Platform Credits may be used for defined Arena product services, such as:

- creating an enhanced run recap;
- generating a share card;
- administering a community event;
- preparing a leaderboard or boss report;
- using an AI-assisted support or recap function;
- accessing a premium product feature; or
- processing another stated game service.

The interface should identify the chargeable action and applicable basis before confirmation. Platform Credits remain product-consumption units rather than a game score or tradable asset.

Any FUZE-token or wallet-aware feature is governed at the [ZAGA family](./08-ZAGA_PUBLIC.md) or dedicated token-document level. Ordinary game mechanics should not imply that a player holds, earns, or can redeem the FUZE token.

## 10. Data and Privacy

ZAGA Arena may process:

- profile and chosen display name;
- device and session information;
- control and gameplay events;
- run scores and results;
- achievement and leaderboard records;
- event and community association;
- support, moderation, and correction history;
- Platform Credit usage; and
- wallet status only where a separately activated feature requires it.

Public leaderboards can use a player-selected game identity. Wallet addresses, if used for eligibility or utility, should remain separated from private identity and should not be exposed beyond the purpose of the feature.

Players should have appropriate information about public display, result sharing, retention, and correction. Security and anti-abuse records may require restricted access and different retention from ordinary run history.

The broader treatment of game and account data follows [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

## 11. Reporting

Arena reports may cover:

- supported release and arena status;
- runs started and completed;
- survival and completion distributions;
- boss attempts and defeats;
- leaderboard methodology and updates;
- event participation;
- badges issued under defined rules;
- errors, invalid runs, and corrections;
- Platform Credit activity; and
- public community recaps.

Metrics should distinguish unique players, profiles, sessions, and runs. Test activity, bots, retries, or incomplete telemetry can materially affect a headline number and should be handled according to the report method.

---

## 12. Product Status and Boundary

ZAGA Arena is presented as a developing game. Browser support, Telegram access, multiplayer, bosses, market-inspired mechanics, events, leaderboards, badges, wallet utility, and reporting may reach availability at different stages.

USDT, Token Value, Net Worth, Liquidity Nodes, Market Roles, and Strategy Options are game terms in the ordinary Arena context. Their presence does not promise payout, income, liquidity, token appreciation, or exchange access.

[ZAGA Districts](./10-ZAGA_DISTRICTS_PUBLIC.md) is a separate persistent community MMORPG and city-builder. Arena runs do not automatically create Districts resources, roles, or city progress.

---

## 13. Conclusion

ZAGA Arena is built around a direct loop: enter, fight, make choices, build a score, review the result, and play again. Competition and sharing add community value when rules, telemetry, and corrections remain clear.

The market-inspired theme gives the game its identity, while explicit product boundaries keep game values distinct from financial claims.
