# AIE

## Executive Summary

AIE, or Event Intelligence, helps people discover relevant events and manage the information lifecycle around an event they choose to attend, sponsor, host, or review. It can filter event signals, explain relevance, organize planning, coordinate approved records, prepare public communication, and turn post-event evidence into reports and reusable lessons.

The product can support conferences, online sessions, community meetups, product launches, game tournaments, shop campaigns, sponsor activations, and internal briefings. Its role is broader than a calendar but narrower than an autonomous event manager.

AIE assists with awareness and operations. Organizers remain responsible for contracts, venues, budgets, attendee safety, permissions, sponsor commitments, public claims, and final event decisions.

---

## 1. Product Purpose

Event information is often scattered across social feeds, group chats, newsletters, websites, calendars, and private planning notes. Even after an event is found, teams must decide whether it matters, assign work, manage guests and partners, communicate changes, collect evidence, and complete follow-up.

AIE provides a structured path for:

- event discovery and watchlists;
- relevance and priority review;
- event briefs and objectives;
- agendas, timelines, and task ownership;
- speaker, sponsor, guest, and staff coordination;
- announcements and attendee information;
- operating notes and issue tracking;
- evidence and feedback capture;
- recaps and partner reports; and
- lessons for the next event.

The product can be used for one event or a workspace portfolio of opportunities.

## 3. Event Lifecycle

### Stage 1: Discover

AIE can collect or accept supported event signals and organize them by category, date, location, format, source, and audience.

### Stage 2: Qualify

The user defines relevance criteria such as product fit, community value, geography, schedule, sponsor interest, cost, speaker relevance, or follow-up potential. AIE can rank candidates and explain the factors used.

### Stage 3: Decide

The team records whether to watch, attend, sponsor, host, partner, decline, or request more information. The decision remains visible with its assumptions and owner.

### Stage 4: Plan

For an active event, AIE can prepare an objective, audience, agenda, work plan, responsibilities, communication schedule, evidence plan, and follow-up structure.

### Stage 5: Operate

During the event, authorized users can update status, capture notes, record issues, track commitments, and preserve evidence needed for later reporting.

### Stage 6: Report

AIE organizes approved attendance, agenda, sponsor, activity, feedback, content, and issue records into an internal or public-safe recap.

### Stage 7: Follow up and learn

Owners complete partner, attendee, customer, content, sales, or community follow-up. Lessons can update a reusable checklist or playbook.

## 4. Discovery and Relevance

### Event records

An event record may contain:

- title and organizer;
- source link;
- date, time, and time zone;
- physical, online, or hybrid format;
- location or access channel;
- category and intended audience;
- application, registration, or ticket deadline;
- speakers or partners from public sources;
- current status; and
- source freshness.

### Explainable relevance

AIE can rank an event against user or workspace criteria. A recommendation should show why the event was surfaced, such as a matching product category, target geography, partner, community, or deadline.

The ranking is an aid to review. It does not establish that attendance will produce revenue, investment, adoption, partnership, or another business outcome.

### Watchlists and alerts

Users can save events, categories, locations, organizers, or themes. Alerts may cover a new matching event, a deadline, a schedule change, a saved-event update, or a requested weekly digest.

The product should identify source and observation time because event details can change quickly.

## 5. Planning Workspace

### Brief and objectives

The event brief can state the purpose, target audience, success measures, format, constraints, and decision owners. Measures should be proportionate to the event and based on available evidence.

### Agenda and timeline

AIE can help structure:

- setup and opening;
- sessions, panels, workshops, or matches;
- breaks and transitions;
- sponsor or partner activity;
- booth or customer-service periods;
- content capture;
- closing; and
- teardown and follow-up deadlines.

### Roles and tasks

Tasks can identify an owner, due time, dependencies, status, and escalation route. Roles may cover organizer, speaker, moderator, sponsor lead, registration, booth operator, technical support, safety contact, content owner, and report reviewer.

### Operating checklists

Checklists may address registration, venue setup, equipment, speaker materials, sponsor deliverables, signage, accessibility, communications, data permissions, emergency contacts, and post-event evidence.

---

## 6. People, Partners, and Sponsors

AIE can organize approved information about:

- speakers and session requirements;
- attendees and registration status;
- guests and access needs;
- staff and volunteers;
- partners and introductions;
- sponsors and deliverables;
- media or content contacts; and
- follow-up ownership.

Sponsor records can show agreed placement, speaking activity, booth requirements, content, evidence, reporting, and outstanding actions. The workspace should distinguish a proposed benefit from a contracted deliverable and a completed result.

Commercial terms, private contact details, VIP notes, investor questions, and sensitive partner discussions require restricted access.

## 7. Communication and Content

AIE can prepare drafts for:

- event announcement;
- registration reminder;
- agenda update;
- speaker introduction;
- sponsor acknowledgment;
- venue or access instruction;
- live status update;
- thank-you message;
- recap; and
- follow-up email.

[CommunityLayer AI](./07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) can support event-community questions and moderation. [SpeakShop AI](./05-HERHELP_SPEAKSHOP_AI_PUBLIC.md) can prepare approved booth or public-address scripts.

The event owner checks dates, links, names, commitments, prices, access, sponsor wording, and public claims before publication.

---

## 8. Event Operations and Evidence

During an event, the workspace can capture:

- attendance or check-in status where appropriate;
- agenda changes;
- session and speaker notes;
- booth or activation activity;
- questions and feedback;
- technical or venue issues;
- sponsor evidence;
- content references;
- decisions and commitments; and
- follow-up items.

Evidence quality matters. A photo, scan, attendee count, survey, sales record, community message, and staff estimate each support different conclusions.

AIE can organize records and flag missing items, but the event team determines which evidence is authoritative.

## 9. Practical Workflows

### Ecosystem opportunity briefing

A workspace defines categories, regions, and deadlines. AIE prepares a weekly source-linked list with relevance explanations. Team members save, reject, or assign deeper review.

### Web3 community meetup

The team creates a brief, agenda, speaker plan, sponsor checklist, community communication, and recap template. During the meetup, staff record questions and issues. AIE then prepares a reviewed public summary and private follow-up list.

### ZAGA tournament

The organizer defines schedule, rules, player communication, evidence, and result reporting. [ZAGA Arena](./09-ZAGA_ARENA_PUBLIC.md) remains the source for game runs and leaderboard rules, while AIE manages the event-level plan and recap.

### District campaign

A ZAGA Districts community plans a time-bounded quest or faction event. AIE coordinates announcements, roles, timetable, and post-event evidence; Districts remains authoritative for city and game state.

### Shop promotion

ShopOS AI supplies approved offer and operating details. AIE organizes the campaign period, staff work, content schedule, and evidence plan. SheetLayer AI may help review approved sales or stock records after the event.

### Sponsor activation

The sponsor lead records deliverables, due dates, approval, placement, activity evidence, and follow-up. AIE prepares a report that distinguishes completed deliverables from estimated exposure or future opportunity.

## 10. Platform Credit Use

AIE may use Platform Credits for metered actions such as:

- generating an event discovery digest;
- ranking a selected event list;
- preparing an event brief or plan;
- creating an agenda or checklist;
- organizing speaker or sponsor notes;
- drafting event communications;
- summarizing selected feedback;
- producing a public-safe recap;
- preparing a sponsor report; or
- converting lessons into a reusable playbook.

Third-party tickets, venues, travel, advertising, feeds, and event services fall outside Platform Credits unless a specific product integration states otherwise. Credits remain separate from the FUZE token.

## 11. Data and Permission Controls

Event work can combine public information with personal, commercial, and operational data. Relevant controls include:

- authorization for imported contacts and attendee records;
- role-based access to guest, sponsor, staff, and investor information;
- consent and notices for photos, recordings, and surveys;
- limited collection at registration or check-in;
- separate public and internal reports;
- retention and deletion rules;
- restricted exports;
- partner and sponsor confidentiality;
- workspace ownership of shared event records; and
- correction of inaccurate attendee or event information.

Wallet records are generally unnecessary for event planning. If an activated event uses wallet-based eligibility, the check should reveal only the status needed for access and should not expose personal identity.

Event collection, AI processing, retention, and deletion should also follow [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

## 12. Reporting

An event report may include:

- objective and format;
- agenda delivered;
- attendance with stated counting method;
- speaker and session summary;
- sponsor deliverables;
- booth, campaign, or game activity;
- questions and feedback;
- issues and corrections;
- content produced;
- follow-up status;
- Platform Credit usage; and
- lessons for future events.

Reports should separate observed results from estimates, targets, impressions, and organizer interpretation. Public versions should remove private attendee identity, confidential commercial terms, staff notes, and investor-specific material.

Event participation alone does not demonstrate revenue, adoption, partnership, investment demand, or token performance.

---

## 13. Product Status and Boundary

AIE is presented as a developing product. Discovery sources, relevance ranking, watchlists, alerts, planning, partner workflows, integrations, and reporting may reach availability in stages.

The product supports event awareness and coordination. It is not a venue operator, ticketing guarantee, travel provider, emergency service, contracting authority, sponsor, or independent verifier of every event claim.

Users should confirm event status and material commitments with the authoritative organizer or counterparty.

---

## 14. Conclusion

AIE connects the event lifecycle from discovery to learning. It helps users decide what matters, coordinate the work, preserve evidence, report appropriately, and improve the next event.

Its practical value comes from source-aware discovery, clear ownership, controlled people data, accurate commitments, and reports that distinguish evidence from expectation.
