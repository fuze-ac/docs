# FUZE Product Revenue Readiness

## Executive Summary

This paper defines the commercial operating gate a FUZE product should pass before its revenue path is described as ready for controlled use.

Revenue readiness means the named product offer can:

- identify the buyer, user, payer, approver, and beneficiary;
- describe the exact product service being offered;
- present a clear price, quote, subscription, allowance, Platform Credit, service-fee, or contract basis;
- obtain the required authorization;
- receive or reconcile payment, allowance, grant, or credit availability;
- deliver the agreed product output or access;
- handle failure, cancellation, partial completion, refund, correction, and dispute;
- protect customer and operational data;
- support the customer;
- connect transaction, fulfillment, usage, cost, and accounting records; and
- review whether the offer should expand, change, pause, narrow, or retire.

The common lifecycle is:

```text
customer problem -> offer -> quote or plan -> authorization
-> payment, allowance, grant, or credit availability
-> fulfillment -> acceptance or completion
-> support, refund, correction, or dispute
-> reconciliation -> accounting treatment
-> review -> expand, revise, hold, or stop
```

Revenue readiness is not revenue confirmation.

The following remain different states:

- commercial idea;
- identified revenue path;
- designed offer;
- testable commercial flow;
- limited paid operation;
- completed paid delivery;
- reconciled payment;
- revenue recognition;
- repeat paid use;
- retention;
- positive contribution margin;
- profitability; and
- scalable commercial operation.

A pricing page, checkout mockup, invoice, pilot agreement, signed contract, payment authorization, Platform Credit grant, wallet transfer, product usage event, or public announcement does not independently establish revenue.

A payment does not independently establish completed fulfillment, accounting recognition, retention, margin, profitability, or future demand.

FUZE products may use different commercial patterns, but each named offer should pass the same operating controls.

Examples include:

- subscriptions and allowances;
- metered product actions;
- bounded service packages;
- workspace plans;
- enterprise agreements;
- event and organizer packages;
- sponsored inventory;
- reviewed listings;
- research and reporting services;
- operational monitoring services;
- defined desktop-work packages;
- game-administration or community services; and
- partner-funded or sponsor-funded programs.

Platform Credits, payments, stablecoins, wallets, FUZE token, token utility, claims, payouts, revenue, and profit remain separate records and concepts.

## Revenue-Readiness Standard

A product is revenue-ready for a named scope when FUZE can answer the following with current evidence.

1. Who is the customer?
2. Who uses the product?
3. Who pays?
4. Who approves the purchase or Platform Credit use?
5. Who receives the product benefit?
6. What exact service, access, output, inventory, period, or package is being offered?
7. What does successful fulfillment mean?
8. What limitations and customer responsibilities apply?
9. How is the price, quote, allowance, subscription, usage amount, or service fee determined?
10. Which payment, invoice, grant, allowance, sponsor, partner, or Platform Credit route applies?
11. Which costs and dependencies affect delivery?
12. What capacity can the product support?
13. How are failure, cancellation, partial completion, refund, chargeback, dispute, and correction handled?
14. Which transaction, fulfillment, usage, support, cost, and accounting records are connected?
15. What evidence determines whether the offer expands, changes, pauses, narrows, or stops?

Readiness belongs to the exact:

- product;
- module;
- offer;
- audience;
- plan;
- period;
- geography;
- platform;
- device;
- provider;
- event;
- campaign;
- workspace;
- partner arrangement;
- or other commercial scope.

One revenue-ready SheetLayer AI package does not establish readiness for every HerHelp product.

One completed ToolGrid campaign does not establish repeatable campaign revenue.

One paid Botmad task does not establish enterprise-service readiness.

## Commercial Readiness Levels

| Level | Evidence-backed meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue path identified | Customer, problem, possible paid service, and charging route are documented. | A complete offer, payment flow, delivery, or demand |
| Offer designed | Scope, unit, price or quote method, terms, responsibilities, and delivery are defined. | Working checkout, fulfillment, or revenue |
| Commercial flow testable | Quote or checkout, payment or credit path, fulfillment, support, and records can be tested. | External paid use or repeatability |
| Limited paid operation | Authorized customers can transact in a controlled scope with support and reconciliation. | General availability, retention, profitability, or scale |
| Paid delivery confirmed | Payment and the defined product service are both evidenced for the stated transaction. | Accounting recognition, repeat use, or margin |
| Revenue confirmed | Reconciled payment, completed service, period, and applicable accounting treatment are evidenced. | Profitability, growth, or future revenue |
| Repeat paid use | A defined customer or cohort has completed more than one paid use under a stated method and period. | Retention quality, positive margin, or scale |
| Retention evidenced | Continued paid or active use by a defined cohort is evidenced over a stated period. | Profitability or future growth |
| Contribution margin reviewed | Direct revenue and directly attributable delivery costs are reviewed under a stated method. | Company profitability or scalability |
| Repeatable commercial operation | The offer can be sold, delivered, supported, reconciled, and reviewed across its intended operating scope. | Unlimited scale, positive margin, profitability, or permanent demand |
| Scalable commercial operation | Capacity, support, provider, quality, cost, and controls are evidenced for an expanded scope. | Guaranteed growth or profitability |
| Paused | Commercial access is temporarily stopped. | Retirement |
| Narrowed | Commercial scope is reduced. | Failure of the entire product |
| Retired | The offer is no longer active. | Continued availability or support beyond the stated transition |

These levels are not interchangeable.

## Commercial Roles

The commercial workflow may involve several distinct roles.

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Prospect | Person or organization evaluating the offer |
| Customer | Person or organization receiving the product or service under the stated relationship |
| Buyer | Person or role selecting or negotiating the offer |
| Payer | Person, organization, sponsor, partner, or account providing payment or funded access |
| Approver | Authorized role approving purchase, budget, contract, invoice, or Platform Credit use |
| User | Person or role operating the product |
| Beneficiary | Person, team, community, shop, event, campaign, or organization receiving the intended benefit |
| Product owner | Role responsible for product value, scope, quality, status, and correction |
| Delivery owner | Role responsible for fulfillment of the offer |
| Support owner | Role responsible for customer issues and service recovery |
| Finance and accounting reviewer | Role responsible for payment, reconciliation, refund, accounting, and period treatment |
| Partner or sponsor | External organization funding, distributing, supporting, or receiving a defined service under an approved scope |

These roles may be the same person in a small purchase or different parties in a team, shop, event, enterprise, partner, sponsor, or community arrangement.

The offer should identify who has authority for:

- selecting the offer;
- approving the amount;
- accepting terms;
- paying;
- authorizing Platform Credit use;
- providing inputs;
- reviewing outputs;
- accepting fulfillment;
- requesting correction;
- requesting refund;
- disputing a transaction;
- renewing;
- and terminating.

## Commercial Flow

### 1. Customer Problem and Evidence

The product team identifies:

- customer segment;
- buyer;
- user;
- payer;
- approver;
- beneficiary;
- current problem;
- current workaround;
- urgency;
- frequency;
- cost of the existing process;
- willingness to test;
- willingness to pay;
- constraints;
- and evidence source.

Possible evidence includes:

- user interviews;
- observed workflows;
- support requests;
- inbound requests;
- pilot requests;
- letters of intent;
- signed orders;
- completed trials;
- usage records;
- paid transactions;
- renewal behavior;
- and customer feedback.

General interest, social engagement, community size, event attendance, wallet count, or token activity should not be treated as equivalent to willingness to pay for the named product service.

### 2. Offer Definition

The offer should define:

- offer name;
- product and module;
- intended customer;
- user;
- included product action or access;
- source scope;
- output or deliverable;
- quantity;
- duration;
- service level where applicable;
- customer responsibilities;
- required approvals;
- excluded work;
- support;
- correction;
- renewal;
- cancellation;
- refund;
- current limitations;
- and current availability.

A customer should understand what is being purchased without needing to interpret internal architecture or token mechanics.

### 3. Pricing and Quote

The offer may use:

- fixed price;
- subscription;
- included allowance;
- per-user price;
- per-workspace price;
- per-shop price;
- per-event price;
- per-campaign price;
- per-listing price;
- per-report price;
- per-task price;
- per-session budget;
- per-unit metering;
- Platform Credits;
- enterprise quote;
- partner-funded access;
- sponsor-funded access;
- revenue-share arrangement where separately approved;
- or another documented commercial basis.

The price or quote record should identify:

- offer version;
- amount;
- currency or Platform Credit basis;
- included scope;
- excluded scope;
- quantity or unit;
- period;
- discount;
- grant or promotion;
- tax treatment where applicable;
- payment timing;
- expiry;
- renewal;
- refund and cancellation conditions;
- provider or pass-through charges where relevant;
- approval;
- and current status.

A later price change should not make earlier transactions impossible to explain.

### 4. Authorization and Terms

Before payment or Platform Credit use, the commercial flow should confirm:

- customer or workspace;
- buyer and approver authority;
- offer version;
- amount;
- currency or credit basis;
- scope;
- terms;
- privacy and data handling;
- support;
- cancellation;
- refund;
- renewal;
- dispute route;
- geographic or user restriction where relevant;
- and acceptance record.

Commercial authorization does not automatically authorize:

- public disclosure;
- access to unrelated customer data;
- external communication beyond the service;
- file deletion;
- payment from another account;
- token transfer;
- wallet signing;
- trade execution;
- liquidity action;
- employment action;
- or another consequential action outside the offer.

### 5. Payment, Allowance, Grant, or Credit Availability

The commercial route may use:

- card or bank payment;
- invoice;
- stablecoin payment;
- Platform Credit purchase;
- Platform Credit allowance;
- trial grant;
- promotional grant;
- partner-funded grant;
- sponsor-funded access;
- enterprise allowance;
- purchase order;
- or another approved route.

The record should distinguish:

- quoted;
- authorized;
- pending;
- confirmed;
- failed;
- expired;
- refunded;
- reversed;
- disputed;
- charged back;
- reconciled;
- and corrected.

Payment confirmation, Platform Credit grant, product fulfillment, and revenue treatment remain separate records.

### 6. Fulfillment

The product fulfills the named offer.

The fulfillment record may include:

- start time;
- completion time;
- source scope;
- output or access;
- quantity;
- period;
- reviewer;
- provider dependency;
- completion state;
- partial completion;
- error;
- customer acceptance where applicable;
- correction;
- and support state.

For AI-assisted products, fulfillment includes appropriate treatment of:

- source quality;
- model and provider selection;
- output review;
- hallucination or unsupported inference;
- privacy;
- prompt injection;
- provider failure;
- human approval;
- and correction.

For partner-dependent or sponsor-dependent services, fulfillment should distinguish:

- FUZE obligations;
- partner obligations;
- sponsor obligations;
- customer obligations;
- attempted delivery;
- delivered work;
- accepted work;
- measured work;
- and disputed work.

### 7. Acceptance, Completion, or Service Period

The offer should define what closes fulfillment.

Possible closure conditions include:

- deliverable accepted;
- artifact delivered;
- access period completed;
- report generated and made available;
- campaign inventory delivered;
- event service completed;
- monitoring period completed;
- task completed;
- selected records processed;
- correction window closed;
- or another documented event.

A product-generated output does not automatically establish customer acceptance.

Customer acceptance does not automatically establish revenue recognition.

### 8. Support, Correction, Refund, and Dispute

Customers should have a route for:

- missing output;
- incorrect scope;
- incorrect result;
- access failure;
- delayed fulfillment;
- duplicate charge;
- duplicate Platform Credit consumption;
- incorrect quantity;
- incorrect price;
- failed payment;
- failed refund;
- provider issue;
- billing discrepancy;
- cancellation;
- renewal issue;
- privacy concern;
- security concern;
- and another material service issue.

The product should define:

- support owner;
- response path;
- evidence required;
- response target where applicable;
- correction authority;
- refund authority;
- escalation;
- dispute review;
- communication;
- and closure state.

### 9. Reconciliation

The commercial flow should connect, where applicable:

- quote;
- order;
- terms acceptance;
- invoice;
- payment authorization;
- payment confirmation;
- Platform Credit grant;
- Platform Credit reservation;
- Platform Credit consumption;
- fulfillment;
- customer acceptance;
- refund;
- chargeback;
- adjustment;
- provider cost;
- partner cost;
- tax treatment;
- accounting record;
- correction;
- and period close.

Unmatched or unresolved records should not be represented as reconciled.

### 10. Review and Decision

The product team reviews:

- demand;
- qualified interest;
- conversion under a stated method;
- fulfillment completion;
- reliability;
- quality;
- support load;
- refund and dispute rate;
- repeat paid use;
- retention;
- direct cost;
- provider dependency;
- gross or contribution margin under the stated method;
- capacity;
- privacy and security incidents;
- customer feedback;
- and public-reporting accuracy.

The decision may be:

- expand;
- continue;
- change offer;
- change price;
- change usage unit;
- improve delivery;
- narrow scope;
- pause;
- redesign;
- transition customers;
- or retire.

## Platform Credits in a Revenue-Ready Flow

Platform Credits may package or meter a supported product service.

A typical route is:

```text
offer selected -> payment, allowance, or grant confirmed
-> Platform Credits available -> product action quoted
-> Platform Credit use authorized -> product action fulfilled
-> consumption finalized -> support and correction available
-> payment, credit, fulfillment, and accounting records reconciled separately
```

The product should define:

- action consuming Platform Credits;
- usage unit;
- amount or calculation basis;
- purchased, included, granted, promotional, partner-funded, or sponsor-funded source;
- reservation rule;
- completion event;
- failure and retry behavior;
- refund, release, adjustment, or reversal treatment;
- expiry or scope restriction;
- user-facing record;
- finance and accounting record;
- and support route.

Platform Credit consumption is product-service evidence.

It is not revenue evidence by itself.

The following can create Platform Credit records without establishing recognized product revenue:

- promotional grant;
- trial grant;
- internal test;
- partner-funded grant;
- sponsor-funded grant;
- failed payment;
- unused balance;
- reserved balance;
- reversed action;
- support adjustment;
- migration;
- and correction.

Relevant cost categories may include:

- AI model usage;
- data provider fees;
- API and connector fees;
- payment and settlement fees;
- network fees;
- cloud computing;
- storage;
- monitoring;
- content production;
- moderation;
- reviewer time;
- support time;
- refunds;
- chargebacks;
- dispute handling;
- partner or sponsor obligations;
- venue or event costs;
- sales and campaign delivery;
- security and compliance operations;
- taxes and regional operating requirements where applicable;
- and incident recovery.

The product owner should be able to estimate or review:

- direct delivery cost;
- provider cost;
- variable cost;
- fixed operating cost;
- expected support cost;
- refund and dispute cost;
- gross margin under the stated method;
- contribution margin under the stated method;
- capacity limit;
- break-even assumptions where used;
- and sensitivity to volume or provider price changes.

The public paper does not publish cost or margin values unless separately approved.

An attractive price with unknown delivery effort, uncontrolled provider cost, or unbounded support demand is not a ready commercial model.

“Revenue” should not be used as a substitute for payment volume, invoice value, Platform Credit balance, token proceeds, treasury assets, or customer intent.

“Profitability” should not be inferred from revenue, gross margin, contribution margin, or cash receipt alone.

## Go, Hold, Revise, or Stop Decision

Before widening commercial access, the responsible team should record one of the following.

### Go

The offer, delivery, payment or Platform Credit route, records, support, cost review, and controls are adequate for the next defined scope.

Remaining limitations are disclosed and monitored.

### Hold

The customer problem remains relevant, but one or more of the following needs more work:

- price;
- offer scope;
- product quality;
- permissions;
- privacy;
- security;
- provider dependency;
- support;
- cost;
- capacity;
- reconciliation;
- legal review;
- or reporting.

### Revise

The evidence supports the product problem but not the current offer design.

The team changes:

- scope;
- price;
- usage unit;
- customer segment;
- delivery method;
- provider;
- support model;
- or commercial route.

### Stop or Retire

Evidence does not support the offer in its current form, or the operating burden and risk outweigh demonstrated value.

Existing customers receive the applicable:

- completion;
- refund;
- transition;
- data export;
- migration;
- support;
- correction;
- or closure treatment.

This decision process keeps monetization tied to product evidence rather than document publication or fundraising narratives.

- token demand;
- approved distributable value;

- payout eligibility;

## Revenue-Readiness Checklist

Before describing an offer as revenue-ready, confirm that:

1. customer, buyer, payer, approver, user, and beneficiary are identified;
2. the paid service and successful fulfillment are defined;
3. the release and commercial scope are current;

6. payment, grant, allowance, or Platform Credit states are testable;
7. fulfillment, partial completion, cancellation, failure, and retry rules exist;
8. support, correction, refund, chargeback, and dispute have owners;
9. delivery costs and provider dependencies are understood;
10. capacity and support limits are known;
11. transaction, usage, fulfillment, support, cost, and accounting records can be linked;
12. data remains within its authorized audience;
13. privacy and security controls are operating;
14. trials, grants, promotional activity, partner funding, and sponsor funding are separated from ordinary paid use;
15. payment, paid delivery, revenue recognition, margin, profitability, and repeatability remain separate;
16. public reporting has a defined period, source, method, exclusions, and correction state;
17. pause, transition, narrowing, and retirement routes exist;
18. token utility, wallet participation, claims, payouts, and market access remain separate; and
19. evidence supports the exact readiness level claimed.

## Key Takeaways

- Revenue readiness is an operating gate for a named product offer, customer, and scope.
- Customer interest, offer design, testable flow, limited paid use, paid delivery, revenue, repeat use, retention, margin, profitability, and scale are different states.
- Every offer should define buyer, payer, approver, user, beneficiary, scope, delivery, price or metering, support, refund, dispute, cost, and reconciliation.
- Payment, Platform Credit grant, product fulfillment, revenue recognition, and profitability remain separate.
- Platform Credit usage is service evidence and does not independently prove revenue.
- Stablecoin payment is an operational payment route and does not create FUZE-token utility.
- Product teams should understand direct cost, provider dependency, support demand, capacity, and margin before expanding paid access.
- Trials, grants, partner-funded activity, sponsor-funded activity, and ordinary customer-paid use must be labeled separately.
- Product revenue is not automatically approved distributable value, token demand, claim eligibility, payout eligibility, liquidity, or financial return.
- This paper defines the readiness framework and does not prove that any FUZE offer is currently paid, revenue-confirmed, repeatable, profitable, or scalable.
