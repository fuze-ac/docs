# HerHelp AI SaaS

## Executive Summary

HerHelp is the FUZE AI SaaS family for individuals, small businesses, operating teams, trainers, and communities that need practical, repeatable, and reviewable AI-supported work.

Rather than treating every task as an isolated prompt, HerHelp organizes work around:

- workspaces;
- approved source material;
- reusable instructions;
- module-specific permissions;
- clear task scope;
- human review;
- Platform Credit usage where applicable;
- output history;
- exports and handoffs; and
- product-specific reporting.

The HerHelp family includes five focused products:

- SheetLayer AI;
- ShopOS AI;
- SpeakShop AI;
- TrainLayer AI; and
- CommunityLayer AI.

Each product owns a distinct workflow, audience, data boundary, permission model, review process, status, and public limitation.

HerHelp provides the family-level workspace and navigation model. It does not turn all five products into one unrestricted general-purpose AI system, and it does not give every module automatic access to all workspace data.

HerHelp assists with preparation, organization, transformation, and coordination. It does not replace human judgment, professional review, organizational authority, or responsibility for external actions.

## Product Purpose

Many individuals, SMEs, and operating teams use separate tools for:

- spreadsheets;
- shop operations;
- promotional content;
- customer communication;
- training;
- onboarding;
- community administration;
- moderation support;
- recurring reports; and
- reusable business knowledge.

This fragmentation creates common problems:

- source material is copied repeatedly;
- different tools use inconsistent context;
- tone and terminology drift;
- approvals are difficult to trace;
- useful outputs remain trapped in one task;
- access is broader than necessary;
- task history is incomplete;
- teams cannot easily see what changed; and
- people spend time moving information instead of completing work.

HerHelp is designed to reduce that friction through a shared but controlled workspace.

Its purpose is operational:

- organize approved context;
- route work to the correct product;
- reduce repeated preparation;
- preserve task and review history;
- support reusable outputs;
- maintain role and module boundaries; and
- keep people responsible for final decisions.

## Intended Users

HerHelp may serve:

- individuals managing recurring administrative work;
- founders and small teams organizing business information;
- shops and local businesses coordinating customer and back-office operations;
- spreadsheet users preparing structured business outputs;
- trainers and employers creating onboarding and learning material;
- community owners, moderators, and support teams;
- project teams coordinating several related outputs;
- agencies or approved service partners supporting client work; and
- organizations that need role-based preparation, review, and approval.

The relevant HerHelp product depends on the task.

A user should be able to use only the modules, workspaces, data, and actions needed for their role.

- a campaign;

- a training program;
- a community;

A workspace can contain:

- purpose and description;

- approved source material;

- templates;
- module access;

## Standard HerHelp Workflow

### 1. Create or Enter a Workspace

The user creates or enters a workspace for an approved purpose.

The workspace establishes:

- organization or project context;
- members;
- roles;
- available modules;
- data sources;
- task permissions;
- budget or usage rules; and
- review responsibilities.

### 2. Add Approved Context

The user selects the source material needed for the task.

Possible sources include:

- spreadsheet ranges;
- product records;
- menu information;
- operating instructions;
- brand guidance;
- training notes;
- policies;
- community rules;
- approved templates;
- event material; or
- uploaded documents.

The product should not require broad access where a specific field, tab, document, record type, or time period is sufficient.

### 3. Choose a Product and Task

The user selects or confirms the product responsible for the work.

Examples:

- spreadsheet mapping belongs in SheetLayer AI;
- shop operations belong in ShopOS AI;
- voice or announcement creation belongs in SpeakShop AI;
- learning-content generation belongs in TrainLayer AI; and
- moderation or member-support assistance belongs in CommunityLayer AI.

The user should be able to understand why a task is routed to a particular product.

### 4. Review Scope and Usage

Before a metered or consequential task begins, the user may review:

- task purpose;
- selected source;
- permitted tools;
- output type;
- Platform Credit usage or estimate;
- maximum authorization;
- review requirement;
- destination; and
- any important limitation.

### 5. Generate or Process

The selected product performs the approved task.

Possible actions include:

- drafting;
- mapping;
- classifying;
- transforming;
- summarizing;
- generating;
- validating;
- organizing;
- comparing; or
- preparing a structured workflow output.

The product should preserve a task reference connecting the request, source, output, status, and usage record.

### 6. Review and Approve

Human review remains part of the workflow.

Depending on the task, a reviewer may:

- edit the output;
- request another version;
- reject the result;
- approve internal use;
- approve export;
- approve publication;
- approve an operational action; or
- escalate for specialist review.

Preparation authority and final approval authority may belong to different roles.

### 7. Reuse or Handoff

An approved output may support another HerHelp product when the user or workspace authorizes the handoff.

The handoff should identify:

- source product;
- destination product;
- approved data or output;
- purpose;
- responsible user or role;
- review status;
- effective time; and
- any restriction.

A handoff does not grant the destination product access to the full source workspace.

### 8. Export, Publish, or Complete

Where supported, an authorized user may export, publish, send, or otherwise use the approved output.

The destination action should preserve:

- authority;
- format;
- current version;
- final review status;
- permission; and
- applicable records.

An export does not remove the user's responsibility to confirm accuracy, rights, suitability, and destination requirements.

### 9. Record and Report

HerHelp may retain operational records such as:

- task request;
- product used;
- source reference;
- output status;
- reviewer;
- approval;
- Platform Credit event;
- export or handoff;
- correction;
- support event; and
- completion time.

Retention depends on product, workspace, support, security, financial, contractual, and applicable legal requirements.

## Cross-Product Workflows

The family creates value when one operating activity needs several specialist outputs.

### Commerce Campaign

A shop team may:

1. use SheetLayer AI to review and organize product data;
2. use ShopOS AI to prepare selected menu or catalog content;
3. use SpeakShop AI to create approved promotional scripts or announcements;
4. use TrainLayer AI to prepare staff guidance; and
5. use CommunityLayer AI to support a related member or customer community.

Each product receives only the context required for its task.

The campaign is not one automatic workflow unless the workspace explicitly defines and approves the handoffs.

### Staff Onboarding

An operator may:

1. organize roles and procedures in SheetLayer AI;
2. prepare onboarding material in TrainLayer AI;
3. create shop-operation checklists in ShopOS AI;
4. prepare announcement content in SpeakShop AI; and
5. coordinate staff or volunteer updates through CommunityLayer AI.

Changes to the source should be reviewed before revised training or announcements are issued.

### Community and Event Activity

A community team may:

1. organize participant or activity data in SheetLayer AI;
2. prepare guidance in TrainLayer AI;
3. support moderation and member communication in CommunityLayer AI;
4. use SpeakShop AI for approved public announcements; and
5. use ShopOS AI where a related shop, booth, order, or operational workflow exists.

Private community, participant, or moderation records should not be exposed to unrelated commerce or promotional tasks.

### Small-Team Content Workflow

A small team may define a campaign brief once and then:

- organize source information;
- prepare shop content;
- create scripts or announcements;
- prepare internal instructions;
- review outputs independently; and
- publish only approved versions.

The team should not be forced to approve every output as one undifferentiated package.

## Cross-Module Handoff Controls

A cross-module handoff should follow these principles:

| Control | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Minimum necessary data | Share only what the destination task needs |
| Explicit purpose | Record why the handoff occurs |
| Source authority | Confirm the source product and workspace may share the material |
| Destination authority | Confirm the receiving product and user may use it |
| Review status | Distinguish draft, approved, corrected, or withdrawn source material |
| Version control | Prevent outdated material from being reused silently |
| Visibility | Show the handoff in relevant task history |
| Revocation or correction | Stop or update downstream use where supported |

Cross-module reuse should not become hidden cross-product profiling.

The presence of data in one HerHelp workspace does not automatically make it appropriate for every product, user, model, provider, or destination.

## Platform Credit Use

Platform Credits may support clearly defined HerHelp actions.

Examples include:

- processing a spreadsheet or document;
- generating or transforming approved product content;
- preparing communication drafts;
- creating voice content;
- creating learning material;
- running a community-support or moderation-assistance workflow;
- producing a report;
- performing a premium processing task; or
- using an approved export or connected-service function.

Before a metered task, the user should be able to see, where applicable:

- product;
- action;
- usage unit;
- fixed amount, estimate, range, or maximum;
- available balance;
- approval requirement;
- completion condition; and
- failure or reversal treatment.

A standard usage lifecycle may be:

```text
quote -> authorize -> reserve if needed -> process
-> complete or fail -> consume or release -> record
```

Reserved credits are not final consumption.

A failed, cancelled, duplicated, or corrected task should follow the product's release, reversal, or correction rule.

Platform Credits are product usage credits.

They are separate from:

- FUZE token;
- stablecoins;
- wallets;
- token allocation;
- token circulation;
- wallet-based participation;
- claims;
- payouts; and
- investments.

The [FUZE Platform Credits Usage Examples](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/06-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_USAGE_EXAMPLES_PUBLIC.md) provides the wider usage model.

HerHelp should apply purpose-based access across the family and each product.

Relevant controls may include:

- account and role permissions;
- workspace separation;
- product-specific access;
- task-specific source selection;
- approval requirements;
- source and output visibility;
- AI-provider routing restrictions;
- export authorization;
- integration permissions;
- retention and deletion settings;
- activity records;
- correction history; and
- minimization or redaction before processing.

Users should provide only information they are authorized to use.

Sensitive information may require additional controls or may be unsuitable for a particular workflow.

- customer records;
- employee records;
- payment details;
- private community messages;
- moderation or safety records;
- learner assessments;
- credentials;
- private agreements;
- tax or accounting records; and

Public reporting should use reviewed, aggregated, redacted, or otherwise public-safe information.

## AI Provider and Integration Boundaries

- data sent;
- purpose;
- provider retention;

- access and authentication;
- processing location where relevant;
- connected tools;
