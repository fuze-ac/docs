# HerHelp SpeakShop AI

## Executive Summary

SpeakShop AI helps shops, stalls, cafés, service counters, event booths, community operators, and customer-facing teams prepare short spoken messages for real operating environments.

The product turns approved business, queue, service, campaign, event, or community context into reviewed communication assets such as:

- greetings;
- product or menu highlights;
- promotional announcements;
- queue and pickup notices;
- delay and service updates;
- location and direction messages;
- staff reminders;
- multilingual variants;
- pronunciation guidance;
- campaign message sets; and
- references to separately produced audio where supported.

SpeakShop AI is designed around practical constraints. A message should be:

- accurate;
- short enough for the setting;
- easy to hear;
- suitable for repetition;
- clear about prices, timing, eligibility, and location where relevant;
- appropriate for the audience;
- reviewed before delivery; and
- retired when its source facts expire or change.

SpeakShop AI prepares communication material. The operator remains responsible for product facts, prices, offers, pronunciation, translation review, voice or recording rights, public suitability, scheduling, device use, and the final decision to speak, display, record, publish, or play a message.

The product does not independently verify every claim, clear advertising rights, guarantee translation quality, guarantee commercial results, or establish that a message is lawful or appropriate in every venue.

## Product Purpose

Small operators often have a phone, speaker, display, staff member, or event microphone available but no repeatable process for preparing announcements.

Messages are frequently:

- improvised;
- inconsistent;
- too long;
- difficult to hear;
- based on outdated prices or availability;
- unclear about queue or pickup instructions;
- translated without review;
- reused after a promotion expires;
- mixed with private customer information; or
- played without a controlled approval process.

SpeakShop AI provides a focused workflow for turning ordinary operating information into concise spoken communication.

It may support:

- customer greetings;
- product or menu highlights;
- limited-period promotions;
- queue and pickup announcements;
- delay or service-status messages;
- location and direction guidance;
- closing-time notices;
- staff reminders;
- event-booth scripts;
- community activity notices;
- safety or operational notices;
- multilingual variants;
- customer-service recovery messages; and
- coordinated message sets for a campaign or service period.

The objective is not to replace direct human service.

The objective is to help operators prepare communication that staff can deliver consistently and customers can understand.

## Message Types

### Greetings

A greeting introduces the business, service point, event, or activity and may set expectations for the interaction.

Variants may reflect:

- time of day;
- venue;
- language;
- audience;
- formality;
- service mode; or
- campaign period.

A greeting should not make unsupported claims about popularity, quality, health, safety, price, availability, or exclusivity.

### Product and Offer Messages

The operator may supply approved:

- item or service name;
- price;
- option;
- availability;
- offer terms;
- valid date or time;
- eligibility;
- location;
- stock limitation; and
- required disclaimer.

SpeakShop AI may prepare short promotional variants while keeping source facts visible for review.

The product should not invent:

- discounts;
- availability;
- health claims;
- scarcity;
- popularity;
- warranties;
- performance claims;
- legal claims; or
- eligibility conditions.

### Queue and Pickup Notices

Queue messages may use:

- queue number;
- order reference;
- pickup code;
- table reference;
- appointment code; or
- another approved service identifier.

They may cover:

- order acceptance;
- waiting;
- readiness;
- delay;
- changed pickup point;
- service-counter call;
- missed pickup; or
- final reminder.

Public messages should use a suitable service reference rather than a full customer identity.

### Service and Issue Updates

These messages may explain:

- temporary delay;
- unavailable item;
- changed location;
- changed service flow;
- payment-system interruption;
- delivery delay;
- closing time;
- temporary pause;
- crowd or queue guidance; or
- another operating condition.

The wording should provide useful direction without exposing private customer, staff, supplier, payment, or incident information.

### Staff Reminders

Internal announcements may support:

- opening;
- stock checks;
- station changes;
- preparation;
- cleaning;
- device checks;
- handover;
- closing;
- event timing; or
- safety routines.

Staff-only messages should remain separate from customer playback lists and public displays.

### Event and Community Notices

Public event or community messages may include:

- schedule reminders;
- session start times;
- venue directions;
- registration guidance;
- activity updates;
- sponsor acknowledgments;
- safety notices;
- accessibility guidance;
- closing announcements; and
- public follow-up instructions.

Private participant, moderation, identity, safety, or organizer records should not be included in public scripts unless specifically authorized and appropriate.

### Campaign Sets

A campaign set groups approved messages for different moments, such as:

- opening;
- morning;
- lunch;
- afternoon;
- event peak;
- low-stock period;
- final call;
- service recovery; and
- closing.

Rotation may reduce repetition while preserving the same approved offer, facts, and brand direction.

A campaign set should not silently mix drafts with approved scripts.

## Brief-to-Announcement Workflow

### 1. Define the Purpose

The user selects the message type and intended result.

A useful purpose is specific, such as:

- announce pickup reference 24;
- promote the approved lunch set until 2:00 PM;
- explain a 15-minute delay;
- guide customers to a changed booth location; or
- remind staff of the closing checklist.

The purpose determines the appropriate source, audience, review, length, and destination.

### 2. Select the Audience and Setting

The user identifies:

- customer or staff audience;
- venue;
- expected noise level;
- language;
- delivery channel;
- time of use;
- repetition level;
- public or internal classification; and
- accessibility or localization needs where relevant.

A message suitable for a quiet service desk may not work in a noisy market or crowded event.

### 3. Supply Verified Facts

The brief may include:

- business, event, or community name;
- item or service;
- price and offer terms;
- valid dates or times;
- stock or availability condition;
- queue or location details;
- intended audience;
- language;
- preferred tone;
- maximum length;
- required wording;
- prohibited wording;
- pronunciation notes;
- channel; and
- expiry condition.

Source information may be:

- entered directly;
- selected from an approved ShopOS AI record;
- selected from an approved SheetLayer AI range;
- selected from an approved event or community source; or
- supplied through another authorized connection.

The source should have an owner, version, and review status where practical.

### 4. Generate Variants

SpeakShop AI may prepare variants that differ by:

- length;
- energy;
- formality;
- language;
- delivery style;
- time of day;
- audience;
- channel; or
- repetition pattern.

A speaker version may use shorter sentences and simpler structure than written advertising.

Each variant should preserve the approved facts.

### 5. Review Accuracy and Suitability

The operator checks:

- names;
- prices;
- dates;
- times;
- availability;
- eligibility;
- location;
- queue reference;
- pronunciation;
- language quality;
- tone;
- claims;
- required notices;
- audience suitability;
- repetition burden; and
- destination.

The user may:

- edit;
- reject;
- request another version;
- request a shorter or calmer version;
- request specialist review; or
- approve the script.

### 6. Approve the Script

Only an approved version should move into:

- staff guidance;
- active sound pack;
- campaign schedule;
- recording workflow;
- voice-generation workflow;
- public display;
- speaker playlist; or
- connected device service.

Approval should identify:

- owner;
- version;
- audience;
- delivery channel;
- language;
- validity period;
- reviewer;
- approval time; and
- expiry or retirement condition.

### 7. Deliver Through the Selected Channel

An approved script may be:

- read by staff;
- shown as speaking guidance;
- recorded through an approved process;
- converted to voice through a supported service;
- displayed as supporting text;
- scheduled for a supported device; or
- included in a training module.

SpeakShop AI does not imply that every audio-generation, scheduling, or device option is available in every release.

### 8. Monitor and Correct

The operator should be able to:

- pause a message;
- correct a script;
- replace a version;
- remove an obsolete message;
- change a schedule;
- stop playback;
- review an incident; and
- record a correction.

A correction should not silently overwrite the previous approved version without history.

### 9. Retire or Archive

Messages tied to price, date, stock, event, location, queue, or campaign should expire or return for review when source facts change.

Possible states include:

- draft;
- under review;
- approved;
- active;
- paused;
- expired;
- superseded;
- corrected;
- withdrawn; and
- archived.

## Verified-Fact and Claim Controls

SpeakShop AI should distinguish:

- user-supplied fact;
- connected-source fact;
- approved campaign claim;
- AI-generated wording;
- unresolved statement;
- required disclaimer;
- prohibited claim; and
- expired fact.

Claims that may require stronger review include:

- health or nutrition claims;
- medical claims;
- safety claims;
- financial claims;
- performance claims;
- “best,” “number one,” or similar superiority claims;
- limited-stock claims;
- discount claims;
- urgency claims;
- prize or reward claims;
- environmental claims;
- sponsorship claims;
- legal guarantees; and
- statements about customer eligibility.

A well-written sentence does not make a claim true.

The operator remains responsible for evidence and lawful use.

## Script Controls

### Length

Users may request:

- short callout;
- standard announcement;
- extended explanation;
- staff instruction;
- event notice; or
- customer-service recovery message.

Repeated public messages should be proportionate to the venue and customer experience.

### Tone

Tone settings may include:

- friendly;
- calm;
- energetic;
- formal;
- premium;
- playful;
- direct;
- reassuring; or
- urgent where justified.

Tone should match the product, audience, situation, venue, and issue severity.

A promotional tone should not be used for a safety incident or sensitive customer issue.

### Timing and Frequency

A campaign plan may identify:

- start time;
- end time;
- days;
- venue;
- channel;
- maximum frequency;
- quiet period;
- event trigger;
- stock condition;
- queue condition;
- manual pause; and
- automatic expiry where supported.

Frequency decisions remain with the operator or approved connected system.

Excessive playback can reduce customer experience and may violate venue, platform, or local requirements.

### Delivery Notes

Delivery notes may include:

- pause;
- emphasis;
- pace;
- pronunciation;
- volume guidance;
- staff instruction;
- device instruction; or
- location-specific direction.

Delivery notes should remain separate from spoken text so they are not accidentally read aloud or played.

- product names;
- proper names;
- numbers;
- currency;
- dates;
- times;
- location names;
- local phrasing;
- cultural meaning;
- formality;
- pronunciation;
- required notices;

Pronunciation guidance may include:

- phonetic spelling;

- approved transliteration;

Generated pronunciation should be reviewed for names, brands, locations, foreign-language terms, and numbers.

- queue number;
- order code;
- pickup code;
- table number;
- appointment reference; or
- another approved non-identifying label.

- full customer name;
- phone number;
- payment method;
- order contents where sensitive;
- delivery address;
- loyalty status;

## Practical Examples

### Lunch Promotion

A café enters the current set name, price, availability period, and desired friendly tone.

SpeakShop AI prepares:

- a short counter version;
- a slightly longer speaker version; and
- a closing reminder.

The owner verifies price, timing, availability, and wording.

Approved versions expire at the end of lunch service.

### Queue Handoff

[ShopOS AI](04-HERHELP_SHOPOS_AI_PUBLIC.md) supplies an authorized queue reference and readiness status.

SpeakShop AI applies an approved pickup format.

The public message uses the service reference rather than full customer identity.

### Weekend Market Booth

A vendor prepares a set covering:

- welcome;
- best-selling item;
- limited approved offer;
- payment guidance;
- location reminder; and
- closing time.

The scripts are shortened for a noisy environment and organized in a campaign pack for staff use.

### Tourist-Area Service

An operator requests two language versions of a location and pickup message.

A fluent reviewer checks wording, pronunciation, currency, and local phrasing before approval.

### Staff Communication Practice

A shop manager approves greeting and issue-response scripts, then sends those materials to [TrainLayer AI](06-HERHELP_TRAINLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) for a staff practice module.

Customer records are excluded from the training task.

### Community Event

An organizer supplies an approved activity schedule, venue instructions, sponsor wording, and safety text.

SpeakShop AI prepares short public notices while [CommunityLayer AI](07-HERHELP_COMMUNITYLAYER_AI_PUBLIC.md) manages the separate community workflow.

### Temporary Service Delay

A shop experiences a preparation delay.

The manager selects the approved delay category and estimated period.

SpeakShop AI prepares a calm public message without exposing staff details or internal incident notes.

The message is paused when normal service resumes.

## Key Takeaways

- SpeakShop AI turns approved operating context into short, reviewable spoken-message assets.
- Verified facts, audience, setting, timing, language, rights, approval, delivery, and expiry are all part of the workflow.
- Queue and pickup messages should use service references rather than unnecessary customer identity.

- Sound packs and schedules should distinguish drafts, approved versions, active messages, corrections, expiries, and withdrawals.

- SpeakShop AI succeeds only when it helps operators communicate clearly without weakening factual accuracy, privacy, rights, customer experience, or human control.
