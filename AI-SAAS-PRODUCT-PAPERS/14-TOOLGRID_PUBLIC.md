# ToolGrid AI

## Executive Summary

ToolGrid AI is the FUZE discovery and distribution product for AI tools, SaaS utilities, workflow helpers, mini-tools, and selected ecosystem resources. Users can search by the job they need to complete, review structured listings, compare relevant options, and identify which details still require verification.

Tool owners can submit listings and, where supported, purchase clearly labeled sponsored placement. ToolGrid separates neutral discovery from paid visibility and applies destination, creative, policy, and trust review before activation.

The product helps people find and evaluate tools; it does not certify that every listed tool is secure, suitable, lawful, or effective for every user. Campaign reporting describes observed visibility and interaction rather than guaranteed adoption, sales, or revenue.

---

## 1. Product Purpose

Tool discovery is fragmented across search, social posts, app stores, newsletters, communities, and advertisements. Product descriptions are inconsistent, sponsored results can be unclear, and users often need to visit many sites before understanding basic workflow fit.

ToolGrid AI provides a structured route to:

- search by problem, task, audience, or category;
- read a concise tool profile;
- compare selected options;
- browse curated utility collections;
- see when a placement is sponsored;
- save or share a shortlist where supported;
- find relevant FUZE products; and
- follow the tool owner's official destination.

For publishers and tool owners, the product provides a controlled submission, review, publication, sponsorship, and reporting workflow.

## 3. User Discovery Workflow

### Step 1: Describe the job

The user enters a task such as preparing a menu, summarizing a community, creating training material, reviewing a spreadsheet, or organizing an event.

### Step 2: Select context

Filters can include audience, category, platform, price direction, complexity, language, integration, deployment type, or another supported attribute.

### Step 3: Review results

ToolGrid presents matching listings with sponsored status visible. Ranking factors should be explainable and should not silently treat payment as neutral relevance.

### Step 4: Compare

The user can compare selected tools across:

- intended use;
- key functions;
- supported platforms;
- integration claims;
- pricing information supplied by the owner;
- current status;
- limitations;
- data or permission considerations; and
- questions to verify.

### Step 5: Visit or save

The user can follow the official destination, save a shortlist, or route to a related FUZE workflow where supported.

ToolGrid should record when listing information was reviewed because external products and pricing can change.

---

## 4. Listing Model

A public tool listing may include:

- name and owner;
- official destination;
- short description;
- category and tags;
- intended users;
- practical use cases;
- key functions;
- platform or deployment information;
- pricing direction where supplied;
- screenshots or approved media;
- integration status;
- sponsorship disclosure;
- review or update date; and
- relevant limitations.

AI can help draft or normalize the description, tags, and use cases. The tool owner or authorized reviewer remains responsible for factual claims.

Listings should distinguish:

- owner-supplied information;
- ToolGrid-generated summary;
- verified technical connection;
- editorial collection;
- sponsored placement; and
- user or third-party material where supported.

---

## 5. Tool Owner Workflow

### Submit

The owner supplies identity or authority information appropriate to the listing, tool details, destination, media, and required disclosures.

### Draft

ToolGrid AI can prepare a listing draft in the standard structure. Unsupported superlatives, guarantees, or ambiguous claims should return for clarification.

### Review

The destination, creative, category, claims, and applicable policy state are reviewed. Higher-risk tools or unclear ownership may require manual review or rejection.

### Publish

An approved listing receives its public status and update date. Material changes to ownership, destination, pricing, product function, or risk can trigger another review.

### Maintain

The owner can submit corrections and current information. ToolGrid can mark stale, suspended, removed, or unavailable listings according to the product process.

## 6. Sponsored Placement

Supported sponsored inventory may include:

- featured card;
- category position;
- collection placement;
- launch highlight;
- campaign zone;
- Telegram-linked surface;
- event or newsletter placement; or
- another defined promotional location.

The campaign record should identify:

- advertiser;
- promoted tool;
- inventory and location;
- campaign period;
- audience or category;
- creative and destination;
- budget or Platform Credit allocation;
- approval state; and
- reporting method.

Sponsored content must be visibly labeled. Payment can determine access to defined inventory, but it should not rewrite neutral comparison facts or create an undisclosed editorial ranking.

ToolGrid can decline, pause, or remove placement for destination changes, policy issues, deceptive content, security concerns, unavailable inventory, or other stated reasons.

---

## 7. Trust and Moderation

Review may address:

- ownership or submission authority;
- deceptive or impersonating destinations;
- phishing or credential theft;
- malware or harmful downloads;
- misleading redirects;
- unsupported product or performance claims;
- prohibited or restricted categories;
- inappropriate creative;
- disclosure accuracy; and
- repeated abuse.

AI and automated rules can help classify risk and prioritize review. Human reviewers control consequential approval, rejection, suspension, and appeal decisions where required.

A published listing means it passed the applicable ToolGrid process at a point in time. Users should still evaluate permissions, security, terms, pricing, and suitability before using an external tool.

## 8. Curated Collections and FUZE Routing

Collections can organize tools for:

- shops and local businesses;
- creators and marketers;
- spreadsheet work;
- training and education;
- community operations;
- events;
- research;
- Web3 workflows;
- productivity; and
- game communities.

Collections may be editorial, rule-based, personalized, or sponsored. The presentation should identify the basis where it materially affects selection.

ToolGrid can also route users to FUZE products such as HerHelp, ShopOS AI, CommunityLayer AI, AIE, QTB, or AIMM when the user's task matches their documented purpose. This route should not imply that every FUZE product is currently available or appropriate.

---

## 9. Platform Credit Use

ToolGrid may use Platform Credits for metered actions such as:

- generating a comparison;
- preparing a listing draft;
- creating a utility collection;
- producing a listing-improvement review;
- setting up supported sponsored inventory;
- processing a campaign report;
- performing selected AI-assisted destination or creative review; or
- using a premium discovery function.

Sponsored placement can also use Platform Credits where product rules permit. The booking should show inventory, duration, amount, and refund or adjustment treatment before confirmation.

Within ToolGrid, credits settle defined processing or placement charges; FUZE-token functions remain governed by their separate ecosystem rules.

## 10. Data and Permission Controls

ToolGrid may process:

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| User | Search, shortlist, comparison, and preference data |
| Listing | Owner submission, description, media, destination, and status |
| Campaign | Advertiser, creative, placement, dates, spend, and metrics |
| Moderation | Risk findings, decisions, appeals, and blocked entities |
| Reporting | Impressions, clicks, referrals, corrections, and usage |

User discovery history should follow appropriate consent, access, and retention settings. Tool-owner, advertiser, sponsor, commercial, and moderation records require role-based access.

Public reports should not reveal private user behavior or sensitive advertiser information. Wallet records are generally unnecessary; any activated wallet-based entitlement should remain separate from personal identity.

ToolGrid data handling should follow [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md).

---

## 11. Reporting

Listing reporting may cover publication status, update date, destination health, category, and user interaction under the stated method.

Campaign reporting may include:

- campaign period and inventory;
- impressions;
- clicks or referrals;
- click-through rate;
- spend or Platform Credit use;
- delivery status;
- invalid or filtered activity;
- creative or destination changes;
- moderation actions; and
- corrections.

Metrics should define counting, deduplication, bots, retries, and attribution windows. A click, referral, or impression does not prove product adoption, customer acquisition, revenue, or campaign profitability.

---

## 12. Product Status and Boundary

ToolGrid AI is presented as a developing product. Listings, comparisons, personalization, sponsored inventory, Telegram surfaces, trust review, analytics, and external-publisher participation may reach availability in stages.

ToolGrid is a discovery and distribution surface. It is not an app store certification, cybersecurity audit, legal review, procurement approval, or guarantee of tool quality or campaign performance.

Sponsored content remains separate from neutral discovery, and placement does not create a FUZE partnership or investment relationship.

---

## 13. Conclusion

ToolGrid AI helps users start with a job and reach a smaller, clearer set of tools. For tool owners, it provides a structured path from submission through publication and optional sponsored visibility.

The product's credibility depends on current listings, visible sponsorship, governed moderation, explainable ranking, and reports that describe observed activity without promising commercial outcomes.
