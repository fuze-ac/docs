# FUZE Product Language Dictionary

## Executive Summary

This dictionary gives FUZE writers and reviewers a compact reference for public product terminology. Its purpose is consistency: a reader should encounter the same product names, status signals, and distinctions across product pages, announcements, partner material, and supporting papers.

The entries below define how important terms are used in FUZE documentation. They do not replace the detailed product, platform, tokenomics, or disclosure papers. When a topic needs more than a short definition, this dictionary points to its primary source instead of repeating the full explanation.

FUZE communication should begin with the user, the task, and the product workflow. Platform infrastructure is described when it helps explain delivery. Token, wallet, market, and participation concepts appear only when they are material to the subject.

---

## 1. How to Use This Dictionary

Use this paper when drafting or reviewing:

- product pages and feature summaries
- public announcements and community updates
- partner and investor descriptions
- product demonstrations and presentations
- FAQs, reports, and support material
- AI-assisted documentation drafts

Each entry identifies a preferred public term, its intended meaning, and a usage note. The wording can be adapted to the surrounding sentence; consistency does not require copying a standard paragraph into every paper.

Product-specific facts should be verified against the relevant product paper. Detailed boundaries belong in [FUZE Product Risk Boundaries](16-FUZE_PRODUCT_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md), while broader terminology and document routing are covered by the [FUZE Public Language and Boundary Index](../PUBLIC-INDEX/03-FUZE_PUBLIC_LANGUAGE_AND_BOUNDARY_INDEX.md).

## 2. Naming Conventions

### 2.1 FUZE

**Preferred form:** FUZE

**Meaning:** The product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem that develops practical products and connects them through shared platform capabilities.

**Usage note:** Introduce the relevant product or user benefit before expanding into ecosystem architecture. Avoid treating FUZE as a synonym for its token.

### 2.2 Product names

Use the approved capitalization shown in Section 5. Do not shorten a product name in its first appearance unless the approved name already includes an abbreviation. Later references may use an unambiguous short form such as “Arena” or “Districts” within a product-specific document.

### 2.3 Abbreviations

Spell out these names on first use:

- **QTB:** Quant AI Trade Brain
- **AIMM:** AI Market Maker
- **AIE:** Event Intelligence

After the first definition, use the abbreviation consistently. Do not create new expansions or alternate initials.

### 2.4 Descriptive capitalization

Capitalize formal names such as Platform Credits and FUZE token. Use lower case for general concepts such as stablecoins, wallet records, product revenue, market access, and public reporting unless they begin a sentence or appear in a heading.

## 3. Core FUZE Terms

| Term | Public meaning | Usage guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Product-first | FUZE begins with useful products and real workflows. | Show the user problem and product action rather than repeating a slogan. |
| Shared platform rails | Common capabilities that products can use, including identity, permissions, credits, payments, AI orchestration, records, and reporting. | Mention only the rails that matter to the workflow being described. |
| Product ecosystem | The connected set of FUZE products and supporting infrastructure. | Use when cross-product relationships are material; use the product name for a single-product discussion. |
| Platform Credits | Usage credits for supported FUZE product actions and services. | Keep them distinct from FUZE token and describe the specific action they enable. |
| FUZE token | The single ecosystem token used for defined utility and participation functions. | Route supply, allocation, eligibility, and market detail to the tokenomics papers. |
| Stablecoins | External digital payment instruments that may support payment, settlement, treasury, or compensation workflows. | State the operational use in context; do not present a stablecoin balance as product usage or token utility. |
| Wallet record | A record associated with a blockchain address, such as an eligible activity, status, or transaction reference. | Public wallet visibility must remain separate from personal identity and private verification data. |
| Wallet-based participation ability | A controlled framework under which eligible FUZE-holding wallets could participate if the framework is activated and its requirements are met. | Use only in papers where eligibility, activation, or participation is directly relevant. |
| Approved distributable value | A specifically approved amount available under an activated participation process after applicable controls and review. | Do not substitute gross revenue, token-sale proceeds, or a general treasury balance for this term. |
| Public-safe reporting | Reporting designed for public release after sensitive or identifying information has been removed or withheld. | Name the report type and intended audience where possible. |
| Permission-aware workflow | A process in which access and actions follow defined roles, approvals, or scopes. | Identify the decision or data boundary instead of implying unrestricted automation. |
| Product-connected utility | A defined use that connects a FUZE product or platform function with FUZE token. | Explain the actual connection; do not infer utility from product proximity alone. |
| DEX-first | A cautious market-access direction that may begin with decentralized exchange access. | Pair with status language and avoid implying a launch date, available liquidity, or assured access. |
| CEX-later | The possibility of later centralized exchange consideration if readiness and external requirements support it. | Never describe a possible venue as confirmed or promised. |

## 4. Status and Claim Language

### 4.1 Product status

Public documents should distinguish current capability from direction. Use the most accurate status available:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Available | Users can access the described capability under its current operating conditions. |
| Supported | The product is designed to handle the stated workflow in an identified scope. |
| In development | Work is underway, but public availability should not be inferred. |
| Planned | The capability is part of current direction and remains subject to execution. |
| Under review | Product, technical, legal, operational, or market assessment is incomplete. |
| Possible | The item is an option rather than an approved commitment. |
| If activated | The framework has prerequisites and is not currently represented as active. |

Dates, launch windows, integrations, and commercial arrangements should appear only when approved for public release.

### 4.2 Support-based verbs

Preferred verbs describe what a product helps a user do:

- organize
- prepare
- generate
- summarize
- route
- compare
- review
- monitor
- discover
- coordinate
- record
- report

Choose a verb that matches the actual workflow. “Supports” is useful when scope varies, but repeating it in every sentence makes product copy vague.

### 4.3 Outcome claims

Separate product capability from user outcome. A tool can prepare a campaign summary, compare market information, or structure a training module without assuring conversion, profit, accuracy, attendance, learning, or business performance.

Public copy should identify material dependencies when they affect interpretation. Examples include user inputs, data quality, permissions, human review, third-party systems, venue requirements, and activation controls.

### 4.4 AI output

Describe AI material according to its role:

- draft
- suggestion
- classification
- summary
- analysis support
- workflow recommendation
- generated content

Where an output may affect sensitive, financial, safety, employment, legal, or public decisions, state the appropriate human or professional review boundary. Do not describe generated material as inherently complete or authoritative.

## 6. Context Rules for Financial and Web3 Terms

Product papers should mention financial or Web3 concepts only when they explain a real feature, dependency, or boundary.

**Platform Credits:** Name the supported product action, such as generation, reporting, premium workflow access, or another defined service. Avoid turning a credit explanation into tokenomics.

**FUZE token:** Explain the relevant utility or participation connection and refer readers to the dedicated token paper for mechanics. Product availability should not be implied to depend on token ownership unless that requirement is explicitly defined.

**Stablecoins:** Identify whether the context is customer payment, operational settlement, treasury handling, or contributor compensation. Apply the relevant accounting and permission controls.

**Wallets:** Discuss addresses and wallet records without publishing personal identity. Self-custody, exchange custody, eligibility, snapshots, claims, and corrections belong in the dedicated wallet-participation papers when those details are needed.

**Market access:** Use conditional status wording. DEX-first direction and possible later CEX consideration do not establish a venue, listing, date, liquidity level, price, or outcome.

## 7. Privacy, Permissions, and Reporting

Use “public” only for material approved for public release. Wallet transparency does not make customer files, partner terms, credentials, identity documents, private agreements, or internal operational records public.

When describing product data, identify:

1. who supplies or controls it
2. what the product needs for the task
3. which roles can access it
4. what action requires approval
5. what may appear in a public report

Reporting labels should match the underlying evidence. An activity summary records activity; it does not prove commercial success. A campaign report can describe delivery and engagement measures without predicting future performance. A market or liquidity report can document observations and operations without promising price behavior or access.

For the platform-wide treatment of these issues, see [FUZE Data, Privacy and AI Data Handling](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/07-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_AI_DATA_HANDLING_PUBLIC.md) and [FUZE Transparency and Reporting Rails](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/09-FUZE_TRANSPARENCY_AND_REPORTING_RAILS_PUBLIC.md).

## 8. Common Corrections

| Confusing formulation | Preferred correction |
|---|---|
| FUZE is the token. | FUZE is the ecosystem; FUZE token is its ecosystem token. |
| Credits or FUZE Credits | Platform Credits, when referring to FUZE product usage credits. |
| Platform Credits are token rewards. | State the product action for which Platform Credits are used. |
| All wallet information is transparent. | Specify which wallet records may be public and which identity data remains permissioned. |
| ZAGA Arena includes ZAGA Districts. | Present Arena and Districts as separate products within ZAGA. |
| QTB trades for users. | Describe QTB as market-intelligence and interpretation support. |
| AIMM guarantees market liquidity. | Describe the planning, coordination, monitoring, or reporting workflow. |
| Sponsored placement delivers customers. | Identify the placement, labeling, campaign period, and available reporting. |
| AI completes the decision. | Identify the generated output and the required reviewer or approval step. |
| A planned feature will launch. | Use planned, in development, under review, or available according to current approved status. |

## 9. Review Checklist

Before publishing product language, confirm that:

- the approved product name is used
- the opening describes a user, problem, or workflow
- current and future capabilities are clearly distinguished
- AI output is framed according to its actual role
- Platform Credits and FUZE token remain separate
- stablecoin language identifies an operational purpose
- wallet transparency does not reveal personal identity
- market-access language is conditional and current
- product capability is not presented as an assured outcome
- sensitive data and approval boundaries are stated where material
- detailed mechanics are linked to their primary paper
- repeated master-position paragraphs and long disclaimer lists have been removed

This dictionary should help different FUZE papers speak consistently while preserving each paper’s own reader value.
