# FUZE Product Language Dictionary

## Executive Summary

This dictionary is the public terminology reference for FUZE products, shared platform capabilities, product status, evidence, reporting, payments, wallets, token utility, market access, and cross-product relationships.

Its purpose is consistency without duplication.

A reader should encounter the same meaning for the same term across:

- product papers;
- public pages;
- launch material;
- investor and partner communication;
- community updates;
- product demonstrations;
- FAQs;
- support material;
- reports;
- event material;
- sponsored content; and
- AI-assisted drafts.

The dictionary follows five rules:

1. Lead with the user, problem, and product workflow.
2. Use approved product names and descriptors.
3. Distinguish current status from direction.
4. Distinguish product capability from outcome.
5. Route detailed mechanics to the applicable primary paper.

The dictionary does not replace product papers, tokenomics papers, privacy papers, market papers, or legal review.

It standardizes short public definitions and usage boundaries.

## Naming Rules

### FUZE

**Preferred form:** FUZE

**Meaning:** The product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem that develops practical products and connects them through shared platform capabilities.

**Usage guidance:**

- Introduce the relevant user or product workflow before explaining ecosystem architecture.
- Do not use FUZE as a synonym for FUZE token.
- Do not present every product as token-dependent.
- Use “FUZE ecosystem” when the connected set of products and platform capabilities is material.

### FUZE Token

**Preferred form:** FUZE token

**Meaning:** The single ecosystem token used only for approved and activated utility or participation functions.

**Usage guidance:**

- Use “FUZE token” in prose when distinguishing the token from the ecosystem.
- Use “FUZE” only where the ticker or exact token label is required.
- Route supply, allocation, release, circulation, participation, claim, payout, market, and risk detail to the token papers.

### Product Names

Use the approved capitalization in this paper.

Do not:

- invent alternate names;

- merge separate products;
- treat a family name as a single feature;
- create a new abbreviation without approval; or
- infer an integration from product proximity.

A short form may be used after the approved name is introduced and the reference remains unambiguous.

## Product Status Dictionary

Public material should use the most accurate evidence-backed status.

| Status | Public meaning | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | A product or capability idea has been defined. | Design, implementation, release, or availability |
| Designed | Purpose, users, workflow, controls, and boundaries are documented. | Working software or active service |
| Prototype | A reviewable demonstration or limited workflow exists. | Production readiness or general availability |
| Implemented | The cited capability works in a stated environment. | Public access, scale, or continuous availability |
| Internally tested | Defined internal tests have been completed with recorded results. | External validation or production readiness |
| Limited test | Controlled external users or environments are involved. | Public beta or general availability |
| In development | Work is underway. | Completion or release date |
| Planned | The capability is part of current direction. | Approved delivery date or commitment |
| Under review | Product, technical, legal, operational, commercial, or market assessment is incomplete. | Approval |
| Possible | The item is an option rather than a commitment. | Planned or approved status |
| Public beta | Public access exists under beta scope, terms, support, and known limitations. | Final quality or general release |
| Live | Production access and current operating support exist for the stated scope. | Universal access, scale, adoption, revenue, or permanence |
| Available | Users can access the capability under stated conditions. | Availability in every region, plan, platform, or environment |
| Supported | The product is designed or implemented to handle the stated workflow in an identified scope. | Universal compatibility or successful outcome |
| Actively used | Current activity is evidenced under a stated method and period. | Adoption quality, retention, paid use, or profitability |
| Paid delivery | A paid service has been delivered under current terms and fulfillment evidence. | Revenue recognition, profitability, or recurring demand |
| Revenue confirmed | Reconciled payment, completed service, period, and accounting treatment are evidenced. | Profitability, retention, or future revenue |
| Paused | Use or operation is temporarily stopped. | Retirement |
| Suspended | Access or operation is restricted because of a defined issue or decision. | Permanent removal |
| Retired | The capability or service is no longer active. | Continued support or availability |
| Archived | The record is retained for history and is not current. | Current product status |
| If activated | The framework has unmet prerequisites and is not represented as active. | Current utility, access, claim, payout, or operation |

A paper, mockup, screenshot, repository, local build, code, demo, sample report, proposal, announcement, provider discussion, contract discussion, roadmap date, token reference, or wallet reference does not independently prove a stronger status.

## Evidence and Reporting Terms

| Term | Public meaning | Usage guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Source observation | A value or state directly received from a named source. | Include source, time, scope, and known delay. |
| Calculated value | A value produced from stated inputs and a method. | Identify formula, inputs, units, period, and exclusions where material. |
| Target | A desired result used for planning or review. | Do not present as achieved. |
| Threshold | A configured condition that triggers review or routing. | A threshold event does not prove cause or required action. |
| Estimate | A value derived from incomplete information or assumptions. | Label method and uncertainty. |
| Forecast | A forward-looking estimate based on a method and assumptions. | Do not present as a guarantee. |
| Projection | A forward-looking result based on stated conditions or assumptions. | Distinguish from observed or forecast data. |
| Scenario | A conditional interpretation used to explore possible outcomes. | State assumptions and invalidation conditions. |
| Example | An illustrative case showing how a workflow may operate. | Do not present as live customer evidence. |
| Self-reported | Information supplied by a user, owner, organizer, provider, partner, or participant. | Identify the reporting party and verification state. |
| Verified | Checked through the stated review or evidence process. | State what was verified and what was not. |
| Reconciled | Compared against the applicable authoritative records and resolved under the stated process. | Do not treat an unreconciled value as final. |
| Corrected | Changed because an earlier record or claim was inaccurate or incomplete. | Preserve correction history. |
| Superseded | Replaced by a later approved version or record. | Do not present the older record as current. |
| Withdrawn | Removed from current use or publication. | Preserve historical state where required. |

| Profitability | Revenue exceeds applicable costs under a stated method and period. | Do not infer from revenue alone. |

## Product-Family Distinctions

### HerHelp Family

**HerHelp AI SaaS** is the family name.

The following are distinct products within the family:

- SheetLayer AI;
- ShopOS AI;
- SpeakShop AI;
- TrainLayer AI; and
- CommunityLayer AI.

Do not:

- call every HerHelp product “HerHelp” when the specific product matters;
- describe one product's capability as available across the whole family without evidence; or
- imply that every product uses the same data, pricing, integrations, or release status.

### ZAGA Family

**ZAGA** is the game ecosystem.

**ZAGA Arena** and **ZAGA Districts** are separate game products.

Do not describe Districts as a mode inside Arena or Arena as a feature inside Districts.

### QTB and AIMM

**QTB** supports market research and interpretation.

**AIMM** supports authorized liquidity-operations intelligence, monitoring, review, and reporting.

Do not describe QTB as executing trades or AIMM as guaranteeing liquidity or price support.

A QTB output does not become an AIMM instruction without an authorized handoff and operational approval.

### AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad

**AIE** concerns event intelligence and event operations.

**ToolGrid AI** concerns tool discovery, listings, comparisons, sponsored visibility, and campaign reporting.

**Botmad** concerns permission-controlled desktop work and supported actions.

These products may exchange approved records, but they remain authoritative only for their own workflows.

## Key Takeaways

- Use approved product names and abbreviations consistently.
- Begin with the user, problem, and product workflow.
- Distinguish design, prototype, implementation, testing, release, live use, paid delivery, and revenue.
- Distinguish source fact, AI interpretation, draft, review, approval, execution, confirmation, reconciliation, publication, and correction.
- Keep Platform Credits, stablecoins, wallets, FUZE token utility, claims, payouts, market access, liquidity, and price separate.
- Define game, market, event, sponsored-discovery, and desktop-action terms in their actual context.
- Use relationship terms only when the current evidence supports the exact scope.
- Use metrics only with method, period, source, exclusions, and limitations.
- Avoid guarantees, unsupported superlatives, implied availability, and overbroad AI claims.
- Route detailed mechanics to the relevant product, privacy, token, market, or disclosure paper.
