# FUZE AI SaaS Product Index

## Executive Summary

FUZE is a product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem built around distinct products for business operations, community work, games, market research, liquidity-operations intelligence, events, utility discovery, and permission-controlled AI assistance.

This index helps readers choose the correct product paper before moving into shared platform, Platform Credit, wallet, token, reporting, or risk documentation.

The portfolio includes:

- the HerHelp AI SaaS family;
- the ZAGA game family;
- QTB;
- AIMM;
- AIE;
- ToolGrid AI; and
- Botmad.

Each product owns its own:

- audience;
- problem;
- workflow;
- data and permission model;
- AI role;
- human-review requirements;
- Platform Credit use;
- reporting model;
- operational controls;
- status; and
- public boundary.

Shared platform capabilities may support several products, but they do not replace product-specific design or evidence.

An entry in this index confirms the approved product name and public scope. It does not establish that the product is implemented, released, live, adopted, revenue-generating, connected to an active wallet mechanism, or integrated with live market access.

## Purpose of This Index

Use this index to:

- find the correct product paper;
- understand how product scopes differ;
- identify the main user and workflow for each product;
- avoid merging products with different purposes;
- navigate to cross-product control papers;
- separate product usage from token, wallet, payment, and market claims; and
- verify where operational status must be checked.

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) controls current public status language. The [FUZE Product Language Dictionary](17-FUZE_PRODUCT_LANGUAGE_DICTIONARY_PUBLIC.md) controls approved product terminology.

## HerHelp AI SaaS Family

HerHelp groups focused products that turn recurring business and community tasks into guided AI-supported workflows.

### HerHelp

HerHelp is the family-level entry point.

It may provide access to supported modules while preserving each module's separate:

- workflow;
- data boundary;
- permission model;
- product status;
- pricing or Platform Credit treatment;
- support model; and
- reporting.

HerHelp should not be described as one unrestricted general-purpose AI system.

### SheetLayer AI

SheetLayer AI focuses on spreadsheet and business-data workflows.

Typical work may include:

- field mapping;
- formula generation;
- data validation;
- transformation;
- dashboards;
- reporting; and
- business workflow support.

Access should remain limited to approved workbooks, tabs, ranges, fields, or connected data sets.

### ShopOS AI

ShopOS AI focuses on operational workflows for shops and local businesses.

Its scope may include:

- menus;
- orders;
- queues;
- customer instructions;
- staff tasks;
- stock;
- loyalty;
- payment references;
- devices;
- delivery context; and
- reporting.

AI-supported functions should remain separate from ordinary operational actions where different controls or usage models apply.

### SpeakShop AI

SpeakShop AI focuses on promotional scripts, announcements, localized variants, and voice-output workflows.

It should preserve boundaries around:

- approved source content;
- voice and sound permissions;
- private drafts;
- generated outputs;
- final human review; and
- publication authority.

### TrainLayer AI

TrainLayer AI focuses on converting approved source knowledge into reusable learning material.

Typical outputs include:

- guides;
- lessons;
- quizzes;
- onboarding modules;
- training plans; and
- trainer-support material.

Learner records and private source material remain permissioned.

### CommunityLayer AI

CommunityLayer AI focuses on community administration, member support, moderation assistance, safety workflows, verification support, and reporting.

AI assistance should remain distinct from final human decisions where actions affect members, access, safety, moderation, or appeals.

### HerHelp Family Relationship

The HerHelp products may work independently.

They may also connect where a workflow benefits, such as:

- SheetLayer AI analyzing ShopOS AI business data;
- TrainLayer AI preparing staff onboarding from approved business material;
- SpeakShop AI preparing promotional content from approved shop information; or
- CommunityLayer AI supporting a business or member community.

A connection between products should not create automatic cross-product access to private data.

## ZAGA Game Family

ZAGA is the game-family and wider token-utility context for two distinct products.

### ZAGA Arena

ZAGA Arena is designed around shorter battle sessions, survival, bosses, progression, competition, events, and shareable community activity.

Its gameplay, progression, account, community, economy, Platform Credit, wallet, and FUZE token records should remain separately defined.

### ZAGA Districts

ZAGA Districts is a persistent Telegram-native cyberpunk MMORPG where players and communities may develop districts, organize participants, manage game systems, progress, and compete over time.

Its persistent-world, district, community, progression, economy, and governance concepts are distinct from Arena.

### Required Separation

ZAGA Districts is not a mode inside ZAGA Arena.

The two products should retain separate:

- user journeys;
- gameplay loops;
- progression;
- social systems;
- economy design;
- community model;
- release status;
- reporting; and
- risk boundaries.

ZAGA provides the shared family route and ecosystem context rather than merging both products into one game.

Game design, progression, or wallet support does not establish live token rewards, stablecoin rewards, earnings, withdrawals, claims, liquidity, or market access.

## Market Research and Liquidity Operations

QTB and AIMM support different professional workflows and must remain clearly separated.

| Product | Main purpose | Typical user decision | Prohibited implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| QTB | Organize market information, narratives, watchlists, sources, and research | How to interpret and review available market context | Autonomous trading, guaranteed predictions, or assured investment outcomes |
| AIMM | Monitor and report on authorized liquidity-operations information | How an authorized operator reviews operational conditions, controls, incidents, and records | Price protection, guaranteed liquidity, assured spread, or guaranteed market results |

### QTB Boundary

QTB is a research and interpretation product.

It may support:

- source organization;
- market narratives;
- watchlists;
- comparisons;
- scenario review;
- report generation; and
- human decision support.

QTB should not be presented as an autonomous trading agent or a guarantee of market accuracy or returns.

### AIMM Boundary

AIMM is an authorized liquidity-operations intelligence, monitoring, and reporting product.

It may support:

- venue and market monitoring;
- operational observations;
- incident records;
- treasury or liquidity-operation reporting;
- authorized review; and
- public-safe reporting where appropriate.

AIMM should not be presented as a mechanism that guarantees price, depth, spread, volume, liquidity, listing, or investment performance.

## Events, Discovery, and Work Assistance

### AIE

AIE supports event intelligence workflows such as:

- discovery;
- planning;
- briefings;
- organizer coordination;
- participant or partner context;
- sponsor support;
- recaps; and
- reporting.

Public event information, organizer workspaces, participant data, sponsor records, private partner terms, and public reports should remain separately governed.

### ToolGrid AI

ToolGrid AI supports AI-utility discovery and approved sponsored visibility.

Its product model should preserve:

- neutral discovery information;
- tool-owner submissions;
- destination review;
- trust and safety records;
- sponsor-management information;
- campaign records; and
- clearly labeled sponsored placement.

A sponsored placement should not be presented as an independent ranking or guarantee impressions, clicks, customers, conversion, or revenue.

### Botmad

Botmad is a permission-controlled desktop work assistant.

It may assist with bounded work involving:

- research;
- documents;
- reports;
- files;
- selected applications;
- connectors;
- task preparation; and
- approved tool actions.

The user or workspace must define:

- task scope;
- allowed files and sources;
- allowed tools;
- credentials and access method;
- usage or spending limits;
- destinations;
- action limits;
- review requirements; and
- final approval.

Botmad does not receive unrestricted authority merely because it can access one file, application, workspace, or account.

## Product-to-Platform Relationship

FUZE products may use shared capabilities where those capabilities improve delivery, consistency, control, or evidence.

Possible shared services include:

- accounts;
- organizations and workspaces;
- roles and permissions;
- AI task routing and review;
- Platform Credit records;
- approved payment and settlement routes;
- product data and retention controls;
- wallet-aware references;
- service monitoring;
- support records;
- evidence lineage; and
- reporting.

The [FUZE Platform Overview](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/01-FUZE_PLATFORM_OVERVIEW_PUBLIC.md) explains the platform model. The [FUZE Core Platform Rails](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/04-FUZE_CORE_PLATFORM_RAILS_PUBLIC.md) defines the shared-service layer.

Not every product needs every rail.

A shared service should be adopted only when:

- the product has a real dependency;
- the interface is defined;
- authority is clear;
- failure behavior exists;
- privacy and security controls are appropriate;
- service ownership exists; and
- the integration can be supported.

A shared architecture diagram does not prove that every product is integrated with every platform rail.

## Platform Credits

Platform Credits may support clearly defined product services.

Examples include:

- generating a report;
- processing an AI task;
- preparing training material;
- creating promotional voice content;
- running a research workflow;
- producing an operations summary;
- preparing an event brief;
- using a sponsored-placement service; or
- completing a bounded Botmad work session.

Each product paper should identify:

- the user-visible action;
- usage basis;
- quotation or estimate;
- authorization;
- reservation where needed;
- completion condition;
- failure and reversal treatment;
- user history; and
- support path.

Actual prices, amounts, packages, subscriptions, grants, expiry, refunds, and transfer rules depend on approved product policies and current terms.

Platform Credits are product usage credits.

They are separate from:

- FUZE token;
- stablecoins;
- wallets;
- token allocation;
- token circulation;
- wallet-based participation;
- claims;
- payouts; and
- investments.

The [FUZE Platform Credits Usage Examples](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/06-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_USAGE_EXAMPLES_PUBLIC.md) provides practical patterns.

## Payments and Stablecoins

Products may use approved payment routes for product or service delivery.

Stablecoins may serve as operational rails for:

- payment;
- settlement;
- treasury movement;
- refunds;
- partner payments; or
- contributor compensation

where the product and operating controls allow.

A payment event does not independently prove:

- product delivery;
- customer acceptance;
- reconciled revenue;
- profit;
- distributable value;
- token-holder payout; or
- financial return.

Stablecoin use does not turn Platform Credits into FUZE token or create token-related rights.

## Wallet-Aware Product Features

A product may use a supported wallet for a defined purpose, such as:

- address association;
- proof of control;
- product-scoped access;
- transaction evidence;
- public report reference;
- governance context; or
- a specialist mechanism status.

A wallet is not automatically:

- a complete identity;
- a universal account;
- a product role;
- beneficial-ownership proof;
- an eligibility decision;
- a claim right;
- a participation right; or
- administrative authority.

Product accounts, workspace permissions, public-chain evidence, private verification, and specialist mechanism rules remain distinct.

The [FUZE Wallet-Based Platform Model](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/08-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PLATFORM_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) defines the shared wallet integration model.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE is product-first: readers should begin with the product matching their task.
- Each product owns its own users, workflow, data, permissions, AI role, review, credits, reporting, status, and boundary.
- HerHelp contains distinct AI SaaS products that may connect without sharing unrestricted access.
- ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts are separate games under the wider ZAGA family.
- QTB is a research and interpretation product; AIMM is an authorized liquidity-operations intelligence and reporting product.
- ToolGrid AI must distinguish neutral discovery from sponsored visibility.
- Botmad operates only within explicit permissions and human authority.
- Platform Credits, stablecoins, wallets, FUZE token, product utility, participation, claims, and market access remain separate systems.
- An approved paper or index entry does not prove implementation, release, adoption, revenue, activation, or market availability.
- Product status and evidence must be checked separately from product definition.
