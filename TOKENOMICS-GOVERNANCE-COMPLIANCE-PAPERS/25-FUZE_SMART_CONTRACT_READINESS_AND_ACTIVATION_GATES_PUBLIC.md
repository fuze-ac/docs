# FUZE Smart Contract Readiness and Activation Gates

## Executive Summary

FUZE Smart Contract Readiness and Activation Gates explains how FUZE may use smart contracts for transparency infrastructure, vault records, report hashes, conversion proof, token release records, public vault visibility, migration claim readiness, eligibility records, snapshot records, emergency pause controls, governance controls, and possible future claim logic.

FUZE uses one ecosystem token only: **FUZE token**.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, community participation structures, and wallet-based participation ability where activated under final policy.

Smart-contract readiness does not mean live payout activation.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean legal approval.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean accounting approval.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean audit completion.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean every wallet is eligible.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean claim logic is active.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean participation distribution is available.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean approved distributable value exists.

FUZE may build smart-contract rails early for transparency, records, vault structure, report hashes, conversion proof, treasury controls, token release clarity, audit records, migration claim readiness, public vault visibility, governance references, emergency pause controls, and future compatibility.

FUZE activates live eligible-wallet claim logic only after legal, accounting, treasury, audit, reporting, smart-contract, privacy, eligibility, operator, jurisdiction, approved distributable value, and product revenue pool gates are ready.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and operational compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Governance, multisig, timelock, and smart-contract controls improve structure, reviewability, and accountability. They do not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, approved distributable value, wallet eligibility, smart-contract security, AI accuracy, or investment return.

## 1. Purpose of This Paper

This paper explains FUZE’s public smart-contract readiness and activation-gate model.

It defines:

- why FUZE may use smart contracts from early stages;
- which smart-contract modules can support transparency without live payout activation;
- which modules belong to readiness infrastructure;
- which modules belong to possible future participation activation;
- which gates are required before live eligible-wallet claim logic becomes active;
- how smart contracts connect to vaults, report hashes, conversion proof, audits, eligibility, snapshots, public vault visibility, migration claims, and emergency pause controls;
- why smart-contract readiness does not equal claim availability;
- how governance, multisig, timelock, and role separation apply to smart-contract systems;
- how smart-contract readiness connects to Platform Credits, stablecoins, token release, and wallet-based participation ability;
- how investors and community members can understand readiness versus activation;
- what public wording FUZE avoids.

This paper is not a smart-contract audit, legal opinion, accounting policy, tax opinion, securities opinion, claim instruction, token sale document, public investment offer, public solicitation, investment recommendation, exchange listing announcement, payout policy, or financial-return promise.

It is a public smart-contract readiness, activation-gate, governance, and boundary paper.

## 4. FUZE Smart Contract Readiness Model

### 4.1 Readiness Contracts vs Activation Contracts

FUZE separates readiness contracts from activation contracts.

| Category | Purpose | Public Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness contracts | Support transparency, records, vaults, report hashes, conversion proof, audit references, token release clarity, public vault visibility, and governance references | Not payout systems |
| Activation contracts | Support eligibility, snapshots, distribution vaults, claims, restrictions, and dispute processes if activated | Not active until all gates are ready |

This separation is critical.

It allows FUZE to build Web3 transparency early while avoiding premature payout promises.

A readiness contract is not described as an active distribution or claim system.

A testnet contract is not described as live public participation.

An inactive claim module is not described as claim availability.

An eligibility registry concept is not described as final wallet eligibility.

### 4.2 Readiness Contract Modules

Readiness modules may be built early to support transparency and future compatibility.

| Module | Purpose | Public Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Vault Record Module | Tracks or references product revenue-related wallet activity where appropriate | Not approved distributable value by default |
| Credit Balance Record Module | Records Platform Credit purchase or usage references where appropriate without making credits tokens | Not token balance or participation right |
| Treasury Vault | Separates operating treasury assets by purpose | Not automatic payout supply |
| Reserve Vault | Separates reserve assets for obligations and risk coverage | Not automatic approved distributable value |
| Report Hash Registry | Publishes hashes of selected reports | Not a full audit by itself |
| Conversion Proof Registry | Records references for asset conversion and stablecoin conversion proof | Not participation claim approval |
| Audit Record Registry | Records references to audit or review records where appropriate | Not zero-risk guarantee |
| Token Release Registry | Records release, unlock, circulation, claim, and vault movement references | Not market outcome promise |
| Public Vault Directory | Labels public-safe vaults and wallet categories | Not public treasury control |
| Public Vault Visibility Registry | Shows public-safe vault labels, reports, release records, and status | Not Public Vault Access |
| Migration Claim Readiness Module | Prepares legacy-holder migration claim references where applicable | Not active claim unless final rules are live |
| Multisig Controller | Requires multiple approvals for sensitive actions | Not zero-risk governance |
| Timelock Controller | Delays sensitive actions after approval | Not risk elimination |
| Emergency Pause Registry | Records pause status for sensitive systems where appropriate | Not permanent safety guarantee |

These modules can support public trust without activating claim rights.

Readiness modules do not create automatic eligibility, claim rights, payout, income, yield, profit, approved distributable value, or investment return.

### 4.3 Possible Future Activation Modules

Future activation modules may exist only if wallet-based participation ability becomes active.

| Module | Purpose | Public Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility Registry | Stores or references eligible wallet status under defined rules | Not active eligibility until final rules apply |
| Snapshot Module | Records eligible wallet balances or statuses at defined record dates | Not payout guarantee |
| Distribution Vault | Holds approved participation distribution value if the framework is active | Not active without approved distributable value |
| Claim Contract | Allows eligible wallets to claim only under activated rules | Not active before all gates are ready |
| Transfer Restriction Module | Restricts transfers where required by legal, jurisdiction, custody, or eligibility rules | Not unrestricted transfer guarantee |
| Excluded Wallet Registry | Records excluded wallet categories where appropriate | Not public identity exposure |
| Claim Status Registry | Records claim availability, completion, pause, dispute, or expiration status | Not guaranteed claim availability |
| Dispute Window Module | Allows review or dispute before or after claim events where appropriate | Not zero-dispute guarantee |
| Emergency Pause Module | Pauses claims or sensitive functions during critical issues | Not risk elimination |
| Jurisdiction Restriction Module | Applies jurisdiction restrictions where required and legally reviewed | Not availability in every country |

Activation modules are sensitive.

They are not live for public payout until all activation gates are ready.

Building an activation module on testnet, in inactive mode, or as an internal prototype does not mean participation claims are active.

### 4.4 Smart-Contract Status Language

FUZE uses clear status language for smart-contract systems.

| Status | Public Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concept defined | The public model is explained but not deployed |
| Readiness design | The system architecture is being defined for transparency or compatibility |
| Prototype | Internal or test implementation exists without live public activation |
| Testnet | Contract logic is tested in a non-production environment |
| Deployed readiness module | A live contract supports records, transparency, or references without claim activation |
| Audited / reviewed | A contract or process has audit or review references where available |
| Gate-ready | Required gates are ready for activation review |
| Activated | Live function becomes active under final rules |
| Paused | Function is paused due to legal, technical, treasury, audit, reporting, security, or policy issue |
| Retired / archived | Function is no longer active and remains documented for historical clarity |

Status language prevents confusion between development, deployment, readiness, and activation.

## 6. Activation Gates

### 6.1 Activation Gate Framework

Live eligible-wallet claim logic requires all relevant gates to be ready.

| Gate | Public Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legal gate | Public wording, legal status, eligibility, transfer rules, and jurisdiction treatment are reviewed |
| Accounting gate | Revenue, costs, reserves, refunds, fees, taxes, and approved distributable value policy are defined |
| Treasury gate | Vaults, reserves, multisig, timelock, approvals, and payment rails are ready |
| Audit gate | Review, audit, verification, dispute window, and evidence process are ready |
| Reporting gate | Public-safe reports, report hashes, dashboards, and records are ready |
| Smart-contract gate | Readiness contracts and activation contracts are reviewed and ready where applicable |
| Eligibility gate | Eligible wallet rules, custody treatment, transfer treatment, and verification requirements are defined |
| Privacy gate | Public wallet records are separated from private identity and off-chain verification |
| Jurisdiction gate | Country, residency, securities, tax, exchange, custody, and distribution rules are reviewed |
| Operator gate | No single person controls sensitive approval, treasury movement, claim activation, or distribution activation alone |
| Product revenue pool gate | Defined product revenue pools exist and are reconciled where applicable |
| Approved distributable value gate | Approved distributable value exists only after calculation, review, approval, and reporting readiness |

Activation-gated means controlled, reviewed, and conditional.

It does not mean guaranteed future activation.
