# FUZE Community Participation Round

## Executive Summary

The FUZE Community Participation Round is the public operating framework for approved community and product-user access to the **110,000,000 FUZE Community Participation Allocation**, representing **22.00%** of the fixed **500,000,000 FUZE** supply.

The round is designed to support structured participation by eligible:

- product users;
- testers;
- contributors;
- educators;
- translators;
- community organizers;
- moderators;
- event participants;
- ecosystem supporters;
- approved organizations; and
- other participant classes defined in an activated round notice.

The full 110,000,000 FUZE allocation is a category limit.

It does not mean:

- one round must use the full allocation;
- the full allocation is available immediately;
- any person is automatically eligible;
- any price, date, jurisdiction, wallet, contract, release schedule, or claim process is active;
- approvals equal token release;
- released tokens are necessarily circulating;
- or participation creates market access, liquidity, price support, revenue share, payout, or financial return.

Each round, cohort, window, product-user route, contributor route, event route, or other approved use should have its own:

- identifier;
- purpose;
- budget;
- participant classes;
- eligibility rules;
- application or qualification method;
- decision method;
- pricing or contribution treatment where applicable;
- payment route where applicable;
- vesting, lockup, claim, or delivery treatment;
- custody and release controls;
- privacy notice;
- support and appeal route;
- anti-abuse controls;
- reconciliation;
- closure; and
- current status.

The round becomes available only when FUZE publishes an approved and activated round notice for the exact scope that is open.

The Community Participation Allocation remains separate from:

- the **25,000,000 FUZE BOARD / Surfboard Migration** allocation;
- private strategic fundraising;
- Team Allocation;
- Holder Incentives;
- Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships;
- ordinary product payments;
- Platform Credit purchases and grants;
- wallet-based participation;
- approved distributable value;
- claims;
- payouts;
- DEX and CEX access;
- and market operations.

Participation quality, product connection, consistent review, controlled release, privacy-preserving records, and accurate status language are more important than price-led promotion or speculative urgency.

## Round Principles

### Participation Purpose Before Token Promotion

The round should begin with a defined participation purpose.

The purpose may involve:

- product use;
- product testing;
- product feedback;
- contribution;
- education;
- translation;
- moderation;
- event participation;
- community support;
- ecosystem onboarding;
- or another approved community objective.

The round should not be framed primarily around:

- expected token price;
- anticipated exchange listing;
- discount-to-market claims;
- guaranteed liquidity;
- guaranteed future access;
- urgency based on speculative upside;
- or financial return.

### Route-Specific Eligibility

Different participant routes may use different evidence.

A product-user route should not rely on the same criteria as:

- a contributor route;
- an event route;
- an education route;
- a moderation route;
- an organization route;
- or a long-term ecosystem-support route.

The approved notice should identify the route and its evidence before review begins.

### Minimum Necessary Data

FUZE should collect only the information required for:

- eligibility;
- jurisdictional treatment;
- duplicate prevention;
- decision-making;
- wallet or delivery processing;
- payment or refund where applicable;
- support;
- correction;
- legal and compliance obligations;
- and reporting.

Public reporting should remain aggregate or address-based where appropriate and should not expose private identity or wallet-person mappings.

### Decision Before Release

The following remain separate:

- application;
- qualification;
- validation;
- review;
- approval;
- allocation record;
- participant acceptance;
- payment confirmation where applicable;
- vesting or lockup commencement;
- claim eligibility;
- claim submission;
- release authorization;
- transfer submission;
- transfer confirmation;
- settlement;
- reconciliation;
- and circulation classification.

### Evidence Before Public Status

A waiting list, survey, form draft, community message, partner discussion, or expression of interest does not establish an open round.

A round should be described as open only when the approved notice, application or qualification channel, support route, operating owner, and relevant controls are active.

### Controlled Use of the Allocation

FUZE may divide the Community Participation Allocation into multiple:

- rounds;
- windows;
- cohorts;
- routes;
- product-user programs;
- contributor programs;
- event programs;
- reserves;
- or future community uses.

Each use should remain within the controlling category and reconcile to its ledger.

### Market Outcomes Remain Separate

Participation does not guarantee:

- DEX access;
- CEX access;
- listing;
- liquidity;
- depth;
- spread;
- volume;
- token demand;
- price support;
- price appreciation;
- payout;
- revenue share;
- or financial return.

## Activation Gates

A round should not become open until the applicable activation gates are satisfied.

### Purpose and Allocation Gate

- round purpose is approved;
- source allocation is confirmed;
- program budget is approved;
- budget does not exceed available category capacity;
- unused-allocation treatment is defined;
- and the program ledger is ready.

### Participant-Route Gate

- participant classes are defined;
- route-specific eligibility is documented;
- evidence requirements are proportionate;
- duplicate and overlapping-program treatment is defined;
- and route ownership is assigned.

### Legal and Compliance Gate

- supported and restricted jurisdictions are reviewed;
- participant terms are prepared;
- eligibility and verification requirements are defined;
- sanctions or prohibited-person controls are addressed where applicable;
- consumer and financial-promotion language is reviewed;
- payment, token, gaming, and data implications are reviewed where applicable;
- and unresolved legal limitations are documented.

### Pricing, Contribution, and Payment Gate

Where the round includes payment or financial contribution:

- price or allocation method is approved;
- accepted assets and networks are approved;
- quote validity is defined;
- payment destination is verified;
- overpayment, underpayment, duplicate payment, wrong-network payment, and refund treatment are defined;
- fees are disclosed;
- settlement and finality rules are defined;
- accounting and tax treatment is reviewed;
- and payment support is available.

A round may also be non-purchase-based or use multiple routes.

The notice should state the exact treatment.

### Custody and Release Gate

- source custody is confirmed;
- release authority is defined;
- vesting, lockup, claim, or delivery method is documented;
- wallet and custody support are defined;
- address checks and transaction controls are ready;
- failed-transfer and correction procedures exist;
- release batches can be reconciled;
- and pause authority is assigned.

### Technical Gate

- application or qualification system is ready;
- duplicate and abuse controls are tested;
- wallet collection and validation are tested where applicable;
- claim or delivery systems are tested where applicable;
- data export and audit records are available;
- provider and infrastructure dependencies are monitored;
- and rollback or pause procedures are ready.

### Privacy and Data Gate

- privacy notice is approved;
- collected data is minimized;
- purpose and retention are defined;
- public and permissioned records are separated;
- wallet-person mapping access is restricted;
- correction and deletion routes are defined where applicable;
- and incident response is ready.

### Operations and Support Gate

- round owner is assigned;
- route reviewers are assigned;
- decision authority is assigned;
- support channels are active;
- service expectations are defined;
- correction and appeal routes are active;
- escalation and incident handling are documented;
- and closure ownership is assigned.

### Communication and Reporting Gate

- round notice is approved;
- all public and partner materials use the same operative terms;
- status vocabulary is defined;
- public FAQs and support guidance are available where needed;
- reporting methodology is defined;
- and corrections can be published without exposing private data.

Passing one gate does not satisfy the others.

## Participant Routes

An activated round may define one or more routes.

### Product-User Route

This route may recognize meaningful use of an approved FUZE product.

Evidence may include:

- completion of a supported workflow;
- use during a defined pilot or beta;
- recurring product activity;
- verified feedback linked to actual use;
- approved product research participation;
- or another product-specific measure.

Account creation, page visits, raw click counts, or screenshots alone may be insufficient.

Product-use evidence should respect customer privacy and should not expose unrelated business data.

### Tester and Feedback Route

This route may recognize approved testing, issue reproduction, usability feedback, security-safe testing, accessibility feedback, language review, or another quality contribution.

The record should connect:

- defined test need;
- submitted evidence;
- reviewer;
- result;
- usefulness;
- duplicate treatment;
- and participation decision.

Testing that requires authorization should not be performed outside the approved scope.

### Contributor Route

This route may include approved:

- documentation;
- translation;
- education;
- moderation;
- community support;
- technical work;
- research;
- content;
- event support;
- design;
- or another contribution.

Contributor participation should distinguish community recognition from:

- employment;
- contractor work;
- vendor services;
- advisor grants;
- strategic-contributor grants;
- partner consideration;
- and other compensated services.

### Education and Translation Route

This route may recognize approved educational or language work that helps users understand products, token boundaries, safety, privacy, or participation.

Evidence should identify:

- approved topic or material;
- intended audience;
- language;
- source accuracy;
- publication or delivery state;
- reviewer;
- and correction process.

### Community Support and Moderation Route

This route may recognize approved community support, onboarding, moderation, reporting, or conflict-handling work.

The route should define:

- authority;
- period;
- evidence;
- privacy boundaries;
- incident handling;
- conflict-of-interest controls;
- and whether the activity is voluntary, contracted, or otherwise compensated.

### Event and Program Route

This route may support approved:

- onboarding sessions;
- workshops;
- hackathons;
- product demonstrations;
- exhibitions;
- community events;
- game events;
- partner programs;
- or other bounded activities.

Registration, attendance, verified attendance, contribution, completion, winning, and allocation eligibility are separate states.

### Long-Term Ecosystem-Support Route

This route may support eligible long-term ecosystem supporters where the criteria are specific, evidence-backed, and consistently reviewable.

Criteria should not rely only on:

- social following;
- promotional reach;
- message count;
- token-price commentary;
- speculative enthusiasm;
- or informal personal relationships.

### Organization Route

An approved organization may participate through a defined:

- product-user;
- education;
- event;
- contributor;
- community;
- or ecosystem-support route.

Where the primary purpose is a commercial partnership, strategic allocation, investment, or service agreement, the appropriate partnership, treasury, advisor, team, or fundraising process should be used instead.

## Participation Workflow

### 1. Preparation

FUZE confirms:

- round purpose;
- program budget;
- participant routes;
- eligibility;
- evidence requirements;
- decision authority;
- pricing or contribution treatment where applicable;
- payment route where applicable;
- participant terms;
- data requirements;
- technical process;
- custody;
- vesting, lockup, claim, or delivery treatment;
- support;
- appeals;
- reporting;
- pause;
- and closure.

### 2. Notice and Activation

The approved notice is published through the designated source.

Public pages, community posts, forms, partner materials, support scripts, and operational systems should use the same current terms and version.

The round moves from announced to open only when the application or qualification route and support process are active.

### 3. Application or Qualification

Participants provide only the information required for the selected route.

Possible records include:

- route selection;
- product-use evidence;
- contribution evidence;
- event or community evidence;
- organization details;
- wallet address where applicable;
- wallet-control proof where applicable;
- jurisdiction or eligibility confirmation;
- conflict disclosure;
- terms acceptance;
- and payment information where applicable.

Submission creates an application or qualification record.

It does not create approval or allocation entitlement.

### 4. Validation

FUZE checks:

- completeness;
- route fit;
- authenticity;
- duplicate identity or wallet where applicable;
- duplicate activity;
- conflicting programs;
- jurisdictional or eligibility restrictions;
- prohibited conduct;
- payment status where applicable;
- and wallet or delivery readiness.

Product or contribution evidence should be reviewed by the responsible owner rather than inferred only from social activity.

### 5. Review and Decision

The authorized reviewer records one of the following:

- approved;
- partially approved;
- approved subject to conditions;
- waitlisted;
- correction requested;
- rejected;
- withdrawn;
- expired;
- suspended;
- or cancelled.

The decision record should identify:

- route;
- criteria version;
- reviewer;
- decision time;
- reason category;
- amount or allocation method where approved;
- outstanding conditions;
- acceptance deadline;
- and appeal eligibility.

### 6. Participant Acceptance

An approved participant may need to:

- accept terms;
- confirm wallet or custody information;
- complete verification;
- complete payment or contribution where applicable;
- acknowledge vesting, lockup, claim, or delivery treatment;
- and meet the stated deadline.

Approval may expire if acceptance conditions are not completed.

### 7. Allocation Record

A final allocation record should identify:

- participant or permissioned participant identifier;
- public-safe reference where applicable;
- route;
- source program;
- approved amount;
- accepted amount;
- price or contribution basis where applicable;
- payment state where applicable;
- custody or destination;
- vesting or restriction;
- claim or delivery method;
- release schedule or condition;
- decision and acceptance references;
- correction state;
- and current status.

### 8. Payment and Settlement Where Applicable

Payment states may include:

- quoted;
- pending;
- submitted;
- detected;
- under confirmation;
- confirmed;
- settled;
- under review;
- underpaid;
- overpaid;
- wrong network;
- wrong asset;
- duplicate;
- refunded;
- reversed;
- disputed;
- or corrected.

Payment confirmation does not itself establish token release or circulation.

### 9. Vesting, Claim, Release, or Delivery

Depending on the approved notice, tokens may be:

- delivered directly;
- held in controlled custody;
- subject to vesting;
- subject to lockup;
- released in stages;
- made claimable under an approved process;
- subject to milestone conditions;
- or handled through another approved method.

The release process should distinguish:

- allocation approved;
- allocation accepted;
- payment settled where applicable;
- vesting commenced;
- vested amount;
- claimable amount;
- claim submitted;
- claim approved;
- release authorized;
- transaction submitted;
- transaction confirmed;
- participant received;
- restricted after release;
- and circulating classification.

### 10. Closure and Reporting

FUZE closes the window by:

- stopping new applications or qualifications;
- completing or closing decisions;
- handling outstanding acceptance and payment states;
- completing approved release batches or documenting remaining schedules;
- resolving corrections and appeals;
- reconciling the program budget;
- returning unused capacity to the Community Participation Allocation unless another approved treatment applies;
- publishing a public-safe report;
- retaining required records;
- and assigning the final status.

## Qualification and Decision Standard

The round should use route-specific criteria rather than one vague concept of community value.

| Review area | Example question |
|---|---|
| Route fit | Does the participant satisfy the purpose of the selected route? |
| Evidence | Is product use, contribution, participation, or support activity backed by an approved record? |
| Quality | Did the activity create useful product, community, or ecosystem value? |
| Relevance | Is the activity relevant to the published round purpose? |
| Recency and period | Did the activity occur within the stated review period? |
| Completion | Was the required workflow, contribution, event, or program completed? |
| Consistency | Can the criterion be applied consistently across the route? |
| Duplication | Has the same identity, wallet, activity, or evidence been submitted elsewhere? |
| Conflicts | Does employment, vendor, advisor, partner, investor, or other status require separate treatment? |
| Restrictions | Do jurisdiction, policy, sanctions, abuse, or prohibited-conduct rules affect participation? |
| Capacity | Is budget available within the approved program? |
| Terms | Has the participant accepted the applicable conditions? |
| Delivery readiness | Can the approved amount be processed through the supported wallet or custody route? |

Possible decision methods include:

- threshold qualification;
- weighted scoring;
- ranked review;
- cohort selection;
- equal treatment within an approved qualified class;
- formula-based allocation;
- capped first-complete treatment where lawful and approved;
- lottery where lawful, approved, and transparently controlled;
- committee review;
- or another documented method.

The method should be approved before substantive decisions begin.

Criteria that are easy to manipulate should not carry controlling weight without verification.

Examples include:

- raw message count;
- automated referral count;
- follower count;
- unverified screenshots;
- duplicate accounts;
- paid engagement;
- copied content;
- or token-price promotion.

## Pricing, Contribution, and Payment Boundaries

A round may include:

- a purchase route;
- a non-purchase contribution route;
- a product-user route;
- an event route;
- a recognition route;
- or multiple routes with separate treatment.

The notice should state the exact structure.

Where a token price applies, the notice should identify:

- price or pricing method;
- unit of account;
- accepted payment assets;
- network;
- quote validity;
- minimum and maximum;
- fees;
- exchange-rate treatment where applicable;
- refund treatment;
- and current status.

A price in an activated notice is a round term.

It is not a market-price guarantee, valuation guarantee, listing-price promise, or future-return statement.

### Payment

Payment should identify:

- payer;
- payee;
- approved asset;
- network;
- destination;
- amount;
- fees;
- submission;
- confirmation;
- finality;
- settlement;
- refund;
- correction;
- and reconciliation.

Stablecoins are operational payment rails where approved.

They remain separate from FUZE token and Platform Credits.

### Non-Purchase Participation

Where allocation is based on approved contribution, product use, event participation, education, testing, or another non-purchase route, the notice should explain:

- qualification basis;
- decision method;
- allocation method;
- tax or reporting considerations where applicable;
- vesting or release treatment;
- and whether the route overlaps with compensation or another program.

### No Automatic Rights

The following do not automatically create round access:

- product use;
- community membership;
- contribution;
- employment;
- partnership;
- Platform Credit activity;
- stablecoin payment;
- FUZE holding;
- legacy-token holding;
- social following;
- event attendance;
- or previous participation.

Access depends on the activated notice and route-specific decision.

- time;
- milestone;
- contribution continuation;
- product or program condition;
- staged schedule;

The notice and participant record should identify:

- commencement;
- cliff where applicable;
- schedule;
- calculation method;
- rounding;
- transfer effect;
- cancellation or forfeiture where applicable;
- departure or disqualification treatment;
- pause;
- correction;

- eligibility source;
- claim window;
- wallet and network;
- contract or delivery method;
- fees;
- duplicate prevention;
- failed claims;
- unclaimed treatment;

## Wallet and Custody Treatment

The notice should state which custody models are supported.

Possible models include:

- self-custody wallet;
- smart account;
- multisig wallet;
- institutional custody;
- exchange custody;
- omnibus custody;
- direct contract custody;
- or another approved method.

### Self-Custody

Participants are responsible for:

- correct address;
- correct network;
- wallet security;
- transaction review;
- fees where applicable;
- phishing avoidance;
- and recovery limitations.

FUZE should never request:

- seed phrases;
- private keys;
- recovery codes;
- passwords;
- or unrelated wallet credentials.

### Exchange and Omnibus Custody

Exchange or omnibus custody may not provide a unique public address for each participant.

The notice should state whether this custody model is:

- supported;
- supported through another verification or delivery process;
- unsupported;
- or under review.

### Address Changes

An address-change process should define:

- identity or control verification;
- cutoff time;
- approval authority;
- old-address treatment;
- duplicate prevention;
- incident handling;
- and audit record.

### Compromised Wallets

A compromised-wallet process should define:

- reporting route;
- evidence;
- pause ability;
- decision authority;
- replacement-address treatment;
- already-submitted transaction treatment;
- privacy;
- and finality.

FUZE may be unable to reverse a confirmed blockchain transaction.

| Under validation | Completeness, eligibility, authenticity, duplicates, and restrictions are being checked |
| Under review | Authorized decisions are being prepared |
| Approved pending acceptance | A decision exists, but participant acceptance remains incomplete |
| Approved pending payment | An allocation exists, but required payment is incomplete |
| Approved pending release | Decision and applicable acceptance or payment conditions are complete, but release has not occurred |
| Releasing | Approved claim or delivery processing is underway |
| Released | Approved delivery is confirmed for the stated amount and scope |
| Closed | Applications, decisions, reconciliation, and closure actions are complete or controlled under remaining schedules |
| Paused | New or continuing processing is temporarily stopped while obligations are managed |
| Cancelled | The round or affected scope ended before normal completion under an approved decision |
| Archived | Historical records remain available but the round is not current |

“Sold out,” “fully subscribed,” “distributed,” or “completed” should not be used without defining whether the statement refers to:

- applications;
- qualifications;
- approvals;
- accepted allocations;
- payments;
- claims;
- releases;
- transfers;
- or circulating tokens.

## Corrections, Appeals, Disputes, and Abuse

### Corrections

A correction request may address:

- incorrect application data;
- wrong participant route;
- omitted evidence;
- duplicate classification;
- wallet or custody information;
- payment discrepancy;
- allocation discrepancy;
- vesting discrepancy;
- claim discrepancy;
- release or transaction discrepancy;
- or inaccurate public aggregate reporting.

The correction record should identify:

- original record;
- requested change;
- evidence;
- reviewer;
- decision;
- effect on allocation or release;
- report effect;
- and closure.

### Appeals

An appeal should be limited to published grounds, such as:

- material factual error;
- process error;
- incorrect duplicate finding;
- omitted valid evidence;
- incorrect application of route criteria;
- or another stated ground.

The notice should define:

- appeal window;
- eligible grounds;
- required evidence;
- reviewer;
- independence or escalation where applicable;
- allocation-capacity treatment;
- finality;
- and reporting.

An appeal should not become unlimited reconsideration of discretionary criteria.

- wrong asset;

- duplicate payment;
- delayed confirmation;

- fees;
- fraud review;
- and finality.

Each release batch should reconcile:

```text

approved program budget
```

## Key Takeaways

- The Community Participation Round is the operating framework for approved use of the **110,000,000 FUZE Community Participation Allocation**, representing **22.00%** of supply.
- The allocation is a category limit and does not mean one round must use the full amount or that access is automatically available.
- A round becomes open only through an approved and activated notice with active application or qualification, support, ownership, and controls.
- Product users, testers, contributors, educators, translators, moderators, event participants, ecosystem supporters, and organizations may use separate routes with separate evidence.
- Application, validation, approval, acceptance, payment, allocation, vesting, claim, release, transfer, receipt, and circulation are different states.
- Pricing and payment, where applicable, are round terms and do not guarantee market price, listing price, liquidity, or return.
- The Community Participation Allocation remains separate from BOARD / Surfboard Migration, fundraising, team and advisor grants, incentives, partnerships, Platform Credits, wallet-based participation, claims, payouts, and market operations.
- Each program should maintain a reconciled ledger for approved, accepted, paid, vested, claimable, released, cancelled, returned, corrected, unresolved, and remaining amounts.
- Public reporting should remain aggregate or public-safe and should not expose private identity, customer data, wallet-person mappings, payment details, or confidential records.
- A round can be paused, cancelled, corrected, closed, or archived while preserving outstanding participant, vesting, claim, release, refund, support, and reporting obligations.
