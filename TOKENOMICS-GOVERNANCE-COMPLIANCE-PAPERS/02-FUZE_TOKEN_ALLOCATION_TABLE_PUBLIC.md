# FUZE Token Allocation Table

## Executive Summary

The FUZE token supply is fixed at **500,000,000 FUZE** and assigned across ten approved allocation categories. This paper is the authoritative public reference for those category names, amounts, percentages, and intended mandates.

Allocation defines why supply is reserved. It does not establish release, circulation, eligibility, ownership by a future recipient, or availability through a market venue. Those later states depend on the applicable vault, release, vesting, access, governance, and reporting rules.

The largest allocation is the **Treasury Reserve** at 120,000,000 FUZE, or 24.00%. The **Community Participation Allocation** contains 110,000,000 FUZE, or 22.00%. The remaining supply supports migration continuity, team and contributor alignment, foundation stewardship, holder programs, ecosystem growth, market operations, and transparency or stability needs.

Every operational record should reconcile to this table. A category movement should identify its approved purpose, source balance, authorization, destination, status, and reporting treatment. Reclassification between categories requires formal approval and a visible update to the controlling allocation record.

---

## 1. Allocation Objective

The allocation table gives the fixed FUZE supply an explicit purpose structure.

It allows readers and operators to answer:

- how much FUZE exists in total;
- which categories make up that total;
- what mandate belongs to each category;
- how category balances should be reconciled;
- which controls apply before release or use;
- where detailed mechanism rules are maintained.

This paper controls the public allocation values. It does not replace the policies that govern circulation, vesting, vaults, community access, migration, liquidity operations, or other category-specific execution.

## 4. Category Mandates

### 4.1 Community Participation Allocation

**Approved allocation:** 110,000,000 FUZE, representing 22.00% of supply.

This category supports the Community Participation Round and other approved community programs. Potential participant classes can include eligible community members, product users, contributors, and long-term ecosystem supporters.

The category is governed through eligibility, access, jurisdiction, release, and reporting controls. It is separate from migration, seed financing, team compensation, holder incentives, and conditional wallet-based participation.

Detailed access design belongs in the [FUZE Community Participation Round](06-FUZE_COMMUNITY_PARTICIPATION_ROUND_PUBLIC.md).

### 4.2 BOARD / Surfboard Migration

**BOARD / Surfboard Migration allocation:** 25,000,000 FUZE, representing 5.00% of supply.

This category is reserved for eligible legacy-holder continuity from the earlier BOARD / Surfboard context. Migration records should connect an approved legacy position to the applicable eligibility decision, conversion or claim method, release status, and resulting FUZE record.

Unused migration supply remains governed by the migration allocation unless an approved policy changes its treatment. It does not become community-round or treasury inventory merely because it has not been claimed.

The [FUZE BOARD Surfboard Migration](20-FUZE_BOARD_SURFBOARD_MIGRATION_PUBLIC.md) is the primary mechanism paper.

### 4.3 Team Allocation

**Approved allocation:** 45,000,000 FUZE, representing 9.00% of supply.

The Team Allocation supports long-term alignment for approved founders, employees, builders, operators, product owners, engineers, and future key team members. Individual grants should be documented, approved, and linked to vesting or other release conditions.

An internal grant record should identify the recipient class, grant amount, effective date, vesting terms, cancellation treatment, custody state, and remaining balance. Public reporting can aggregate these records to avoid exposing personal compensation details.

### 4.4 Foundation Reserve

**Approved allocation:** 35,000,000 FUZE, representing 7.00% of supply.

This reserve supports long-horizon stewardship, continuity, governance readiness, and mission-aligned responsibilities. It is intended to remain distinct from ordinary operating inventory.

Foundation use should document the purpose, authority, amount, destination, continuing restrictions, and effect on the reserve balance. The designation “reserve” indicates retained capacity, not an assumption that the allocation must be spent.

### 4.5 Treasury Reserve

**Approved allocation:** 120,000,000 FUZE, representing 24.00% of supply.

The Treasury Reserve is the largest category. It provides strategic capacity for approved platform, infrastructure, product, operating, ecosystem, or other treasury needs.

Its size makes governance and reporting especially important. Treasury actions should follow a defined approval path and distinguish a reservation, commitment, transfer, expenditure, conversion, or reallocation request. Treasury balances should not be treated as circulating supply solely because the tokens are visible on-chain.

### 4.6 Holder Incentives

**Approved allocation:** 55,000,000 FUZE, representing 11.00% of supply.

This category can support approved product-use incentives, loyalty, referrals, campaigns, recognition, or other holder programs. Each program should define its target behavior, eligibility, budget, release method, duration, abuse controls, and success measure.

Program records should separate authorized budget, earned or qualified amount, distributed amount, expired amount, recovered amount, and remaining category balance.

### 4.7 Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships

**Approved allocation:** 40,000,000 FUZE, representing 8.00% of supply.

This allocation supports approved partnerships, integrations, grants, co-marketing, distribution, product adoption, and ecosystem-development work. Release can be tied to milestones, delivery evidence, vesting, continuing obligations, or other documented conditions.

Partner records should preserve commercial confidentiality while still allowing authorized reviewers to confirm the allocation source, approval, consideration, milestone status, and remaining obligations.

### 4.8 Liquidity & Market Operations

**Approved allocation:** 30,000,000 FUZE, representing 6.00% of supply.

This category supports controlled market-structure preparation and approved liquidity or venue-related operations. Records should distinguish tokens reserved for planning, committed to a service provider or venue, transferred into an operational wallet, deployed in a pool, withdrawn, or otherwise returned.

The allocation does not determine whether a venue will support FUZE or what market conditions will result. DEX-first and possible CEX-later treatment is maintained in the [FUZE Liquidity and Listing Policy](21-FUZE_LIQUIDITY_AND_LISTING_POLICY_PUBLIC.md).

### 4.9 Advisors / Strategic Contributors

**Approved allocation:** 15,000,000 FUZE, representing 3.00% of supply.

This category supports approved advisors, experts, operators, and strategic contributors whose work falls outside ordinary team allocation treatment. Grants should connect the allocation to a documented contribution, role, milestone, service period, or continuing obligation.

The [FUZE Team Advisor Partner Vesting](19-FUZE_TEAM_ADVISOR_PARTNER_VESTING_PUBLIC.md) paper defines the relevant alignment and release principles.

### 4.10 Transparency / Stability Reserve

**Transparency / Stability Reserve allocation:** 25,000,000 FUZE, representing 5.00% of supply.

This reserve provides controlled capacity for exceptional transparency, trust, or stability-related needs. The mandate is intentionally bounded: a proposed use should state the event or objective, why another allocation is unsuitable, the approving authority, safeguards, reporting method, and expected effect.

The label does not create a standing price-support commitment. Any use should remain consistent with applicable market, treasury, legal, and governance controls.

## 5. Allocation Ledger

FUZE should maintain an allocation ledger that reconciles the fixed supply over time.

Each category record can include:

1. approved category name and identifier;
2. original allocation amount;
3. custody addresses, contracts, or controlled accounts;
4. opening balance for the reporting period;
5. authorized releases or transfers;
6. reversals, recoveries, returns, or corrections;
7. restricted, vested, committed, or deployed amounts;
8. circulating classification where supportable;
9. closing balance;
10. approval and transaction references;
11. report version and responsible owner.

The basic reconciliation is:

`opening category balance - authorized outbound movement + authorized returns or corrections = closing category balance`

The category ledger should also reconcile to custody records. A difference should be investigated and documented rather than absorbed into another category.

## 6. Allocation State Model

Tokens within one allocation can occupy different states.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reserved | Assigned to the category and retained under its control |
| Planned | Included in an approved or proposed program budget but not committed |
| Committed | Subject to an approved obligation or agreement |
| Locked or vesting | Assigned under time, milestone, or service conditions |
| Released | Moved under an approved release instruction |
| Deployed | Used in an operating program, contract, pool, or other approved mechanism |
| Circulating | Reasonably classified as available in circulation under the reporting method |
| Returned | Moved back into approved category custody |
| Cancelled | Removed from a proposed or contingent assignment |
| Disputed or suspended | Held pending correction, investigation, or decision |

These states prevent a common reporting error: treating every token outside an original vault as freely circulating.

The [FUZE Token Release and Circulation Clarity](13-FUZE_TOKEN_RELEASE_AND_CIRCULATION_CLARITY_PUBLIC.md) defines the detailed classification standard.

## 7. Changes and Reclassification

The approved total supply and category values should remain stable unless FUZE formally approves and publishes a revised allocation model.

An operational transfer between wallets within the same category is not a reclassification when the mandate remains unchanged. A movement that changes the token’s allocation purpose is a reclassification and should require:

- a written rationale;
- identification of source and destination categories;
- amount and percentage impact;
- governance and specialist review appropriate to the change;
- updated ledger and custody mapping;
- assessment of existing commitments;
- effective date and public communication where required.

The revised table must still reconcile to 500,000,000 FUZE and 100.00%.

Silent category substitution would weaken the purpose controls established by this paper.

## 8. Vault and Release Ownership

Allocation, custody, and release are related but separate responsibilities.

| Responsibility | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Allocation owner | Confirms the proposed use fits the category mandate |
| Treasury or custody owner | Confirms source balance, destination, execution method, and reconciliation |
| Governance approver | Provides the required authorization |
| Legal, accounting, or compliance reviewer | Reviews matters within the relevant professional scope |
| Technical operator | Executes approved contract or wallet actions |
| Reporting owner | Updates the ledger, evidence references, and public or controlled report |
| Reviewer or auditor | Tests records and identifies exceptions where applicable |

No single table can replace this operating separation.

The [FUZE Vault and Reserve Policy](14-FUZE_VAULT_AND_RESERVE_POLICY_PUBLIC.md) and [FUZE Vault-by-Vault Release Rules](15-FUZE_VAULT_BY_VAULT_RELEASE_RULES_PUBLIC.md) provide the primary custody and release frameworks.

## 9. Public Reporting

A public allocation report should be useful without exposing private identity, confidential agreements, credentials, or security-sensitive procedures.

It can include:

- the controlling allocation table;
- period and report version;
- category opening and closing balances;
- aggregate releases and returns;
- relevant public wallet or transaction references;
- vesting, migration, community, or program status at an appropriate level;
- material reclassifications;
- methodology for circulating-supply treatment;
- exceptions, corrections, and unresolved reconciliation items.

Public totals should use the same category names and values as this paper. When a shortened label is used in a dashboard, its mapping should be available.

Reports should also distinguish a token allocation from Platform Credit balances, stablecoin assets, product revenue, investor funds, and approved distributable value. Those are separate records even when a business process connects them.

---

## 10. Public Boundary

This allocation table is a supply-governance record. It is not a sale schedule, entitlement register, market forecast, or statement that an allocation is currently active.

Participation, migration, incentive, team, advisor, partner, reserve, and market-operation uses remain subject to their own eligibility, vesting, approval, technical, legal, accounting, jurisdiction, and reporting requirements.

Market availability and liquidity depend on external conditions and third-party decisions as well as FUZE readiness. Detailed token risks are maintained in [FUZE Token Risk Boundaries](29-FUZE_TOKEN_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md) and the [FUZE Risk and Disclosure Appendix](../WHITEPAPER-PAPERS/05-FUZE_RISK_AND_DISCLOSURE_APPENDIX_PUBLIC.md).

---

## Conclusion

The FUZE allocation model assigns the complete fixed supply of 500,000,000 FUZE across ten approved purposes.

Its value as a control depends on consistent category names, reconciled balances, documented state changes, accountable approval, and reporting that distinguishes allocation from release and circulation.

This paper supplies the authoritative public ledger. Dedicated policies govern how each category can move from reserved supply into an approved use.
