# FUZE Stablecoin Compensation Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE can use approved stablecoins as operational payment rails for documented work, services, vendors, contractors, contributors, advisors, partners, and other authorized counterparties.

Stablecoin compensation follows the same core discipline as another business payment: an approved engagement, defined deliverable or period, verified payee and payment details, budget authority, review evidence, treasury authorization, execution record, reconciliation, and applicable accounting, tax, legal, and jurisdiction treatment.

The policy separates compensation from token allocation. A stablecoin payment settles an approved obligation; a FUZE token grant or allocation follows its own purpose, vesting, release, and reporting rules. Platform Credits remain product-consumption units and are not a compensation asset.

This paper defines the public operating standard. It does not publish individual compensation, payroll records, private contracts, personal wallet associations, or payment instructions.

---

## 1. Policy Objective

The policy provides a controlled route for stablecoin compensation while preserving:

- clear work and payment evidence;
- treasury and approval separation;
- accurate asset and network details;
- accounting and tax records;
- privacy for workers and counterparties;
- correction and incident procedures;
- separation from token incentives and public participation.

Stablecoin use should solve an operational payment need. It should not be used merely to make ordinary compensation appear token-related.

---

## 2. Covered Payments

The policy can apply to:

| Payment category | Example basis |
|---|---|
| Contractor work | Project, task, period, or milestone |
| Contributor work | Approved community, product, technical, documentation, event, or support contribution |
| Advisor service | Defined advisory scope and period |
| Vendor invoice | Infrastructure, software, agency, specialist, or operating service |
| Partner settlement | Approved delivery, integration, campaign, event, or service obligation |
| Professional service | Legal, accounting, audit, security, design, research, or other specialist work |
| Expense reimbursement | Approved and documented business expense |
| Other operating obligation | Authorized payment with a defined business purpose |

Employment compensation can require additional payroll, labor, tax, benefits, and local payment treatment. FUZE should use stablecoins for such payments only where the operating entity and applicable review support the method.

---

## 3. Engagement Record

Compensation begins with an approved engagement or obligation.

The record should identify:

1. counterparty or authorized payee;
2. role, service, or business purpose;
3. scope and deliverables;
4. period or milestone;
5. amount or calculation basis;
6. currency of account;
7. proposed payment asset and network;
8. invoice, agreement, purchase order, or approval reference;
9. reviewer and budget owner;
10. confidentiality, tax, and jurisdiction requirements;
11. dispute, correction, or termination treatment.

Public community activity or an informal request does not establish a payment obligation without the applicable approval.

---

## 4. Stablecoin and Network Selection

The payment instruction should identify the exact asset and network.

Selection should consider:

- asset support by FUZE and the recipient;
- network compatibility;
- treasury availability;
- transaction fees and settlement time;
- custody and wallet capability;
- counterparty, issuer, bridge, and network risks;
- jurisdiction and compliance treatment;
- accounting and conversion records;
- operational support and incident handling.

Symbols alone can be ambiguous across networks. The internal instruction should use the verified contract or native asset reference where applicable.

FUZE should avoid sending a bridged, wrapped, or unsupported representation when the recipient expects another asset or network.

---

## 5. Currency and Amount

The engagement should state whether the obligation is denominated in:

- a stablecoin unit;
- a fiat currency settled through stablecoin;
- another approved currency or asset basis.

If conversion is required, the payment record should capture:

| Conversion field | Required detail |
|---|---|
| Currency of account | Amount owed under the engagement |
| Settlement asset | Stablecoin used for payment |
| Rate source | Approved provider, venue, or method |
| Rate time | Timestamp or defined pricing window |
| Fees | Network, conversion, provider, or other charges |
| Rounding | Approved precision and treatment |
| Final amount | Stablecoin quantity authorized for transfer |

The policy should state whether fees are paid by FUZE, deducted, shared, or handled through another approved arrangement.

---

## 6. Payee and Address Verification

Stablecoin transfers can be difficult or impossible to reverse. Payee verification is therefore a required control.

The process can include:

- confirming the legal or contractual counterparty through permissioned records;
- obtaining the address through an approved channel;
- confirming asset and network support;
- validating address format and contract restrictions;
- checking for duplicate, replaced, or suspicious instructions;
- completing required restricted-party or compliance review;
- obtaining a signed message, portal confirmation, test transfer, or another verification where proportionate;
- applying dual review before treasury execution.

The personal identity behind an address remains private. Public reports should not publish a named person and wallet association.

An address change should be treated as a new payment instruction and reverified.

---

## 7. Work and Delivery Evidence

Before payment approval, the responsible owner confirms the obligation.

Evidence can include:

- accepted deliverable;
- milestone completion;
- approved timesheet or service period;
- product, support, moderation, or event record;
- invoice matched to contract or purchase order;
- partner acceptance;
- expense receipt and business-purpose approval;
- documented exception or advance-payment authority.

Evidence should be proportionate. A recurring infrastructure invoice needs different proof from a community contribution or milestone-based software project.

The reviewer should identify partial completion, disputed work, offsets, prior advances, and duplicate invoices before treasury approval.

---

## 8. Approval Workflow

The compensation workflow is:

```text
Engagement approved -> evidence submitted -> obligation reviewed
-> asset and address verified -> budget and treasury authorized
-> transaction executed -> receipt confirmed -> records reconciled
```

Roles should be separated where practical.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Engagement owner | Confirms scope, delivery, and business purpose |
| Budget owner | Confirms available and permitted budget |
| Finance or accounting reviewer | Confirms classification and required records |
| Compliance or specialist reviewer | Reviews applicable identity, jurisdiction, tax, legal, or policy issues |
| Treasury approver | Authorizes the exact asset, network, amount, and destination |
| Transaction executor | Executes according to the approved instruction |
| Reconciler | Matches the transaction to obligation, budget, and ledger |

One person should not independently create the obligation, change the destination, approve, execute, and reconcile a sensitive payment.

---

## 9. Treasury Execution

The execution record should include:

- payment identifier;
- approved obligation and invoice reference;
- source treasury account or wallet;
- destination address;
- asset, network, and amount;
- fees;
- approval references;
- transaction hash or provider reference;
- execution time;
- confirmation status;
- exceptions and operator notes.

Treasury operators should compare the final transaction against the authorized instruction immediately before signing.

Batch payments require an itemized manifest and total reconciliation. A batch total alone is insufficient evidence for individual obligations.

---

## 10. Confirmation and Reconciliation

Execution is not the final step.

FUZE should confirm:

1. the transaction reached the intended network and address;
2. required confirmations or provider status were obtained;
3. the recipient or service process acknowledged settlement where appropriate;
4. fees and conversion agree with the record;
5. the invoice or obligation is marked accurately;
6. treasury and accounting ledgers reflect the movement;
7. duplicate or remaining balances are resolved;
8. applicable reports are updated.

Stablecoin settlement can have timing differences between on-chain confirmation, internal treasury records, recipient recognition, and formal accounting close. The reconciliation should preserve those distinctions.

---

## 11. Advances, Milestones, and Recurring Payments

### Advances

An advance requires explicit authority, purpose, amount, recovery or reconciliation method, and treatment if work is delayed or cancelled.

### Milestones

Milestone payments should state the acceptance criteria and reviewer. A milestone can be partially accepted only when the agreement or approved change defines the resulting payment.

### Recurring payments

Recurring compensation requires a current engagement, approved period, updated address confirmation where policy requires, and review of changes in amount, role, jurisdiction, or payment treatment.

Automation should not bypass approval or address-change controls.

---

## 12. Corrections and Incidents

Potential incidents include:

- wrong address or network;
- unsupported or incorrect asset;
- duplicate payment;
- incorrect amount or rate;
- compromised payee or treasury wallet;
- fraudulent address-change request;
- failed or delayed transaction;
- contract or token restrictions;
- disputed delivery;
- sanctions, jurisdiction, or compliance concern;
- accounting or classification error.

The incident process should preserve evidence, stop related payments where possible, notify responsible roles, assess recovery options, correct ledgers, communicate with the counterparty through approved channels, and document the outcome.

Recovery may be unavailable. Controls before execution are therefore more reliable than relying on reversal.

---

## 13. Relationship to FUZE Token and Credits

Stablecoin compensation, token alignment, and product consumption are separate.

| System | Primary role |
|---|---|
| Stablecoin compensation | Settles approved work, service, vendor, partner, or operating obligations |
| FUZE token allocation | Supports approved ecosystem utility and long-term alignment under token policies |
| Platform Credits | Authorize and record supported product consumption |

A counterparty can receive both stablecoin compensation and an approved token grant only when each has its own purpose, agreement, approval, vesting or release treatment, and records.

Stablecoin compensation does not establish FUZE token ownership, community-round access, or wallet-based participation eligibility.

Using stablecoins to purchase Platform Credits follows the product-payment and credit-ledger process rather than this compensation workflow.

---

## 14. Funding and Classification

Compensation can be funded from an approved operating or treasury source. The payment record should identify the source category and avoid implying that a visible stablecoin balance is product revenue.

Possible sources can include:

- operating treasury;
- product receipts after appropriate treatment;
- project or partner budget;
- financing proceeds used under an approved plan;
- reserve released for its documented purpose.

Source classification remains separate from the compensation obligation. Paying an invoice from product receipts does not by itself determine formal revenue, cost, tax, or approved-value treatment.

---

## 15. Privacy and Record Access

Permissioned records can contain names, contact details, agreements, invoices, tax information, wallet ownership evidence, payment instructions, and dispute material.

Access should follow role and purpose. Public reporting should use categories, periods, aggregate amounts, and transaction references only where appropriate and privacy-reviewed.

FUZE should not publish:

- personal identity linked to a wallet;
- private compensation amounts by named individual;
- invoices or agreements containing confidential terms;
- credentials, signer information, or security procedures;
- tax, employment, banking, or identity documents.

Retention and deletion should follow the applicable record and legal requirements.

---

## 16. Public Reporting

A public-safe compensation report can show:

- reporting period;
- compensation categories;
- aggregate amount by approved asset or unit;
- product, project, partner, or operating categories supported;
- transaction count;
- treasury source category;
- reconciliation status;
- corrections or incidents at an aggregate level;
- report version and review status.

Public transaction hashes should be included only when they do not expose a protected identity or confidential relationship.

The report should distinguish compensation from product revenue, treasury transfers, token releases, community allocations, and wallet-participation activity.

---

## 17. Public Boundary

This paper is a public operating policy, not a payment instruction, employment or contractor agreement, payroll standard, tax opinion, or announcement that every FUZE counterparty can receive stablecoins.

Supported assets, networks, counterparties, jurisdictions, payment methods, and controls depend on the operating entity, engagement, treasury capability, and current review.

Payment settles an approved obligation. It does not promise future work, token allocation, investment results, or market access.

---

## Conclusion

Stablecoin compensation gives FUZE an operational payment route for approved work and services while token allocations remain governed by their own long-term purposes.

The policy depends on precise engagement records, asset and network selection, verified payment details, separated approval roles, treasury evidence, reconciliation, privacy, and incident handling.

These controls make stablecoin use a traceable business payment process rather than an extension of token participation language.
