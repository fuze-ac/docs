# FUZE Stablecoin Compensation Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE may use approved stablecoins as operational settlement rails for documented work, services, vendors, contractors, contributors, advisors, partners, professional firms, expense reimbursements, and other authorized business obligations.

Stablecoin compensation is a business-payment process.

It should follow the same core discipline as any controlled compensation or vendor-payment process:

```text
approved engagement or obligation
-> defined scope, period, milestone, invoice, or expense
-> work or delivery evidence
-> payee and payment-instruction verification
-> asset, network, amount, rate, and fee review
-> budget, finance, compliance, and treasury approval
-> transaction preparation and final pre-sign review
-> execution
-> network or provider confirmation
-> recipient acknowledgment where appropriate
-> treasury, accounting, tax, and obligation reconciliation
-> correction, dispute, recovery, closure, and reporting
```

Each state is separate.

An engagement does not create an approved payment until the required evidence and approvals are complete.

Payment approval does not equal transaction execution.

Transaction submission does not equal network confirmation.

Network confirmation does not by itself establish recipient acceptance, payroll completion, accounting close, tax completion, or final settlement.

Stablecoin compensation remains separate from:

- FUZE token grants and allocations;
- Team Allocation;
- Advisors / Strategic Contributors Allocation;
- Community Participation Allocation;
- Holder Incentives;
- Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships Allocation;
- BOARD / Surfboard Migration;
- Platform Credits;
- customer payments;
- product revenue;
- approved distributable value;
- wallet-based participation;
- claims;
- payouts under a participation framework;
- token release;
- token circulation;
- DEX or CEX access;
- liquidity;
- market price;
- and financial return.

A person or organization may receive both stablecoin compensation and a separately approved FUZE token grant only when each has its own:

- purpose;
- agreement or authority;
- valuation or amount basis;
- approval;
- tax and accounting treatment;
- vesting or release treatment where applicable;
- custody and wallet records;
- reporting;
- and correction process.

The payment policy should use exact asset and network references.

A symbol such as `USDT` or `USDC` is not sufficient by itself because multiple native, bridged, wrapped, or unsupported representations may exist across networks.

The operating record should identify:

- legal or contractual counterparty;
- authorized payee;
- business purpose;
- engagement, invoice, milestone, period, or expense;
- currency of account;
- settlement asset;
- network;
- verified contract or native-asset reference;
- payment address or custody destination;
- amount;
- rate source and rate time where conversion applies;
- fees and fee responsibility;
- budget source;
- approvals;
- transaction reference;
- confirmation and finality;
- recipient acknowledgment where applicable;
- accounting and tax classification;
- correction and dispute state;
- and current-as-of date.

Stablecoin use should solve an operational settlement need.

It should not be used to:

- avoid required payroll, employment, tax, invoice, sanctions, or reporting controls;
- hide compensation or beneficial ownership;
- convert an informal community interaction into an undocumented obligation;
- make ordinary compensation appear token-related;
- or create implied FUZE token rights.

This paper owns the public operating model for stablecoin compensation, including covered obligations, engagement records, payee verification, asset and network selection, amount and rate treatment, approval, treasury execution, recurring and milestone payments, reimbursement, payroll boundaries, incidents, corrections, privacy, reconciliation, reporting, closure, and separation from token and product systems.

## Public Position

Stablecoins are external settlement assets that may be used for approved business obligations when the operating entity, counterparty, jurisdiction, treasury, custody, accounting, tax, and compliance treatment support their use.

Stablecoin compensation should not be described as:

- FUZE token compensation;
- a token allocation;
- a token sale;
- a community-participation reward by default;
- a product credit;
- a wallet-based participation payout;
- a guaranteed salary method;
- a guaranteed future payment method;
- a guaranteed fiat-equivalent outcome;
- or a financial return.

Stablecoin settlement remains exposed to risks including:

- issuer risk;
- reserve and redemption risk;
- depegging;
- network congestion;
- network fees;
- smart-contract risk;
- bridge and wrapped-asset risk;
- custody risk;
- wallet compromise;
- transaction finality;
- wrong-address or wrong-network loss;
- provider failure;
- jurisdictional restrictions;
- and market-conversion differences.

FUZE should use stablecoin settlement only when those risks and operating controls are proportionate to the obligation.

## Covered Payment Categories

The policy may apply to the following approved obligations.

| Payment category | Typical basis | Additional considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor work | Project, task, sprint, period, deliverable, or milestone | Contract status, acceptance, invoice, tax, jurisdiction, and worker-classification review |
| Contributor work | Approved product, technical, documentation, education, translation, moderation, event, research, community, or support contribution | Separation from informal participation, employment, grants, token incentives, and duplicate compensation |
| Advisor service | Defined advisory scope, period, deliverable, or retainer | Conflict, authority, service evidence, token-grant separation, and tax treatment |
| Vendor invoice | Infrastructure, software, agency, production, security, design, research, or operating service | Purchase authority, invoice matching, service period, vendor verification, and withholding or tax treatment |
| Partner settlement | Approved integration, campaign, event, distribution, delivery, or service obligation | Agreement, milestone, revenue-share or fee treatment, dispute process, and beneficial ownership |
| Professional service | Legal, accounting, audit, tax, security, compliance, engineering, research, or other specialist service | Confidentiality, professional authority, engagement letter, invoice, and restricted workpapers |
| Expense reimbursement | Approved business expense | Original receipt, business purpose, approval, exchange-rate method, duplicate prevention, and local tax treatment |
| Advance or deposit | Explicitly approved prepayment | Recovery, milestone offset, cancellation, refund, and credit-risk treatment |
| Retainer | Defined service availability or recurring scope | Period, renewal, unused-service treatment, scope changes, and termination |
| Employment-related payment | Salary, bonus, allowance, reimbursement, or other employment obligation where supported | Payroll, labor law, benefits, withholding, social contributions, local currency rules, consent, and payslip treatment |
| Other operating obligation | Documented and authorized business purpose | Clear classification, authority, evidence, and reconciliation |

Coverage under this policy does not mean every counterparty, jurisdiction, or obligation is eligible for stablecoin settlement.

## Excluded or Separately Governed Transfers

The following should not enter this compensation workflow unless separately classified and approved:

| Transfer type | Primary treatment |
|---|---|
| FUZE token grant or allocation | Token allocation, vesting, release, custody, circulation, and reporting policy |
| Community Participation Round allocation | Community-round terms, eligibility, allocation, vesting, release, and reporting |
| BOARD / Surfboard Migration | Legacy-holder eligibility, allocation, claim, release, and reporting |
| Holder incentive | Incentive-program purpose, budget, qualification, claim, and reporting |
| Ecosystem or partner token allocation | Partnership, grant, milestone, vesting, and release process |
| Customer refund | Product-payment and refund process |
| Platform Credit purchase or refund | Platform Credit payment, issuance, usage, refund, and ledger process |
| Wallet-based participation payout | Activated participation, claim, payout, and approved-value process |
| Investor or fundraising receipt | Fundraising, transaction documents, compliance, custody, and investor records |
| Internal treasury transfer | Treasury movement and reconciliation |
| Liquidity or market-operations transfer | Market-operations, custody, venue, and liquidity policy |
| Personal or undocumented transfer | Prohibited unless a valid business obligation and full approval are established |

A payment should not be reclassified as compensation merely because it goes to a worker, advisor, partner, or community member.

The controlling purpose and evidence determine the process.

## Compensation Status Model

| Status | Evidence-backed meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed | A possible obligation or payment is being prepared. | Approved engagement or amount |
| Engagement approved | Scope, counterparty, authority, and terms are approved. | Delivery or payment approval |
| Work in progress | Approved work or service period is underway. | Accepted deliverable |
| Evidence submitted | Invoice, milestone, timesheet, expense, or delivery record is submitted. | Reviewer acceptance |
| Under review | Responsible owners are verifying obligation and evidence. | Payment authorization |
| Approved for payment | Exact obligation, asset or currency basis, and amount are approved subject to final payment controls. | Treasury submission |
| Payment instruction verified | Payee, destination, asset, network, and details are verified. | Treasury authorization |
| Treasury authorized | Exact source, asset, network, amount, destination, fees, and approvers are authorized. | Transaction submission |
| Submitted | Transaction or provider instruction has been sent. | Confirmation or finality |
| Confirmed | Required network or provider confirmations exist. | Recipient acceptance or accounting close |
| Acknowledged | Recipient or service process confirms receipt where required. | Final reconciliation |
| Settled | Obligation, treasury, accounting, tax, and recipient records are reconciled for the stated scope. | Future compensation or token rights |
| Partially settled | Part of the approved obligation is settled. | Full completion |
| Under dispute | Work, amount, destination, asset, rate, or settlement is contested. | Automatic reversal or repayment |
| Corrected | A later approved entry changes the record while preserving history. | Erasure of the original record |
| Reversed or returned | Value returned or was reversed where operationally possible. | Complete recovery of all costs or effects |
| Cancelled | Approved obligation or payment is withdrawn before normal completion. | Removal of historical records |
| Closed | Obligation and remaining actions are complete or controlled. | Future engagement |
| Archived | Historical records remain retained but are not current. | Active payment status |

“Paid” should not be used without clarifying whether it means:

- authorized;
- submitted;
- confirmed;
- acknowledged;
- or settled.

15. engagement owner;
16. budget owner;

The following do not independently establish a compensation obligation:

- community participation;
- product use;
- a chat message;
- a public post;
- volunteer activity;
- an informal request;
- a referral;
- an introduction;
- an event appearance;
- a token-holding record;
- a wallet address;
- a submitted invoice without an approved engagement;
- or work performed outside an approved scope.

An authorized exception requires a documented business basis and review.

## Counterparty and Payee Verification

The legal or contractual counterparty, the authorized payee, the beneficial owner, and the payment destination may be different records.

The process should define which evidence is required for each.

### Counterparty Verification

Possible evidence includes:

- legal name;
- entity registration;
- authorized representative;
- contract or engagement;
- tax information;
- contact details;
- invoice details;
- beneficial ownership where required;
- and jurisdiction.

### Payee Verification

The payee record should identify:

- counterparty relationship;
- authority to receive payment;
- account or wallet ownership or control;
- custody model;
- payment-instruction source;
- and verification date.

### Third-Party Payee

Payment to a third party should require explicit approval and evidence addressing:

- why the payee differs from the counterparty;
- authority;
- legal and tax treatment;
- beneficial ownership;
- fraud risk;
- sanctions and restricted-party review where applicable;
- invoice or contract consistency;
- and dispute treatment.

A third-party instruction should not be accepted merely because it is communicated by chat or email.

## Payment-Address and Custody Verification

Stablecoin transfers may be difficult or impossible to reverse.

Address and custody verification are required before treasury authorization.

### Verification Controls

The process may include:

- obtaining the address through an approved portal or authenticated channel;
- confirming the exact asset and network;
- verifying the native or contract asset reference;
- validating address format;
- confirming whether the destination is self-custody, exchange, institutional custody, omnibus custody, or contract custody;
- confirming memo, tag, destination code, or other routing requirement;
- checking for duplicate, replaced, or suspicious instructions;
- completing required sanctions or restricted-party screening;
- using signed-message verification where supported;
- using a test transfer where proportionate;
- confirming receipt of a test transfer;
- applying dual review;
- and recording the verification method and date.

### Address Change

An address change should be treated as a new payment instruction.

The record should identify:

- prior address;
- new address;
- request channel;
- request time;
- identity or authority re-verification;
- asset and network re-verification;
- hold period where appropriate;
- reviewer;
- approver;
- and effective time.

### Compromised Address or Account

A compromised-address process should define:

- reporting route;
- evidence;
- payment hold;
- new-destination verification;
- treatment of already-submitted transactions;
- recipient communication;
- incident response;
- and finality limitations.

### Public Privacy

The identity behind a payment address remains private unless a separate lawful and approved disclosure basis exists.

Public reporting should not link a named person to a wallet merely because the transaction is visible onchain.

## Currency of Account, Amount, and Rate Treatment

The engagement should define the currency of account separately from the settlement asset.

Possible structures include:

- stablecoin-denominated obligation;
- fiat-denominated obligation settled in stablecoin;
- another approved asset or unit converted to stablecoin;
- fixed amount;
- hourly or daily rate;
- milestone amount;
- retainer;
- recurring period amount;
- expense reimbursement;
- revenue-share or commission formula under a separate approved agreement;
- or another documented basis.

### Conversion Record

Where conversion applies, the payment record should identify:

| Field | Required detail |
|---|---|
| Currency of account | Amount owed under the engagement |
| Settlement asset | Approved stablecoin used for payment |
| Network | Approved settlement network |
| Rate source | Approved provider, venue, index, treasury method, or agreement method |
| Rate time | Timestamp, pricing window, invoice date, approval date, execution date, or other approved point |
| Quote validity | Time or condition under which the calculation remains valid |
| Fees | Network, conversion, custody, provider, or other charges |
| Slippage or spread | Approved treatment where conversion occurs |
| Rounding | Precision and residual treatment |
| Final amount | Stablecoin quantity authorized |
| Reviewer | Person or role confirming the calculation |
| Correction rule | Treatment if the rate, amount, or execution differs |

### Fee Responsibility

The agreement or instruction should state whether fees are:

- paid by FUZE;
- deducted from the payment;
- paid by the recipient;
- shared;
- reimbursed;
- or handled through another approved method.

The recipient should not discover an undisclosed deduction after settlement.

### Rate Changes and Delayed Payment

The policy should define whether delayed payment requires:

- a new quote;
- a new rate time;
- the original currency-of-account obligation;
- a fixed stablecoin amount;
- interest or late-payment treatment where lawful and agreed;
- or another approved method.

## Work, Delivery, Milestone, Period, and Expense Evidence

Before payment approval, the responsible owner should confirm that the obligation exists and is payable.

### Evidence Types

Evidence may include:

- accepted deliverable;
- milestone acceptance;
- approved timesheet;
- approved service period;
- product or technical work record;
- support or moderation record;
- event delivery record;
- advisor activity record;
- partner acceptance;
- invoice matched to agreement or purchase order;
- expense receipt;
- business-purpose approval;
- recurring-service confirmation;
- or authorized advance-payment record.

### Evidence Standard

Evidence should be proportionate to the obligation.

A recurring cloud-infrastructure invoice requires different evidence from:

- a community contribution;
- a software milestone;
- a legal engagement;
- an advisor retainer;
- a reimbursable expense;
- or an event deliverable.

### Review Questions

The reviewer should identify:

- whether the scope was approved;
- whether the work or service was delivered;
- whether the period or milestone is complete;
- whether the invoice or amount matches the agreement;
- whether partial acceptance applies;
- whether prior advances or offsets apply;
- whether the obligation is disputed;
- whether the same work is invoiced elsewhere;
- whether the payment duplicates a token grant, community allocation, incentive, or reimbursement;
- and whether the payee is authorized.

Possible sources include:

- operating treasury;
- department or project budget;
- product-delivery budget;
- partner or campaign budget;
- financing proceeds used under an approved plan;
- reserve released for its documented purpose;

The payment obligation and source classification are different.

Paying compensation from product receipts does not by itself determine:

- revenue recognition;
- cost classification;
- tax treatment;
- approved distributable value;
- or treasury availability for another purpose.

A visible stablecoin balance does not prove that the balance is free for compensation.

## Approval Workflow

The controlled workflow is:

```text
engagement or obligation approved
-> work, milestone, invoice, period, or expense evidence submitted
-> obligation and amount reviewed
-> counterparty and payee verified
-> asset, network, address, rate, and fees verified
-> budget and finance review completed
-> legal, tax, payroll, compliance, or specialist review completed where applicable
-> treasury authorization recorded
-> final pre-sign check completed
-> transaction submitted
-> confirmation and finality recorded
-> recipient acknowledgment obtained where appropriate
-> treasury, accounting, tax, and obligation records reconciled
-> payment closed or corrected
```

### Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Engagement owner | Confirms business purpose, scope, delivery, milestone, period, or expense |
| Counterparty owner | Maintains the approved relationship and authorized-payee record |
| Budget owner | Confirms available and permitted budget |
| Finance or accounting reviewer | Confirms amount, classification, currency, records, and reconciliation requirements |
| Legal, tax, payroll, or compliance reviewer | Reviews applicable worker, jurisdiction, tax, sanctions, payroll, benefits, or policy issues |
| Payment-instruction verifier | Confirms payee, destination, asset, network, and routing details |
| Treasury approver | Authorizes the exact source, asset, network, amount, destination, and fee treatment |
| Transaction preparer | Prepares the transaction or batch manifest |
| Signer or executor | Executes only the approved instruction |
| Reconciler | Matches submission, confirmation, recipient, treasury, accounting, tax, and obligation records |
| Reporter | Prepares approved private or public-safe reports without changing source records |

### Separation of Duties

Where proportionate, one person should not independently:

- create the engagement;
- certify delivery;
- alter the payee destination;
- approve the amount;
- prepare the transaction;
- sign or execute;
- and reconcile

for a sensitive payment.

When staffing limits require combined roles, the record should document:

- combined responsibilities;
- compensating controls;
- dual review;
- transaction limits;
- and decision authority.

## Treasury Preparation and Final Pre-Sign Review

Treasury should compare the prepared transaction against the approved instruction immediately before signing or execution.

The final review should confirm:

- source wallet or provider account;
- destination address;
- memo, tag, or routing code;
- asset;
- network;
- contract or native-asset reference;
- amount;
- decimal precision;
- fees;
- nonce or sequence where relevant;
- batch total where relevant;
- authorization threshold;
- signer roles;
- destination risk or warning indicators;
- quote validity;
- and no unauthorized change since approval.

A discrepancy should stop execution and return the payment to verification or authorization.

## Confirmation, Finality, Acknowledgment, and Settlement

### Confirmation

FUZE should record the required:

- block confirmations;
- finality state;
- provider status;
- custody status;
- or bank or exchange settlement state

for the selected network and route.

### Recipient Acknowledgment

Where appropriate, the recipient should acknowledge:

- asset;
- network;
- amount;
- transaction reference;
- date;
- invoice or period;
- and any discrepancy.

Recipient acknowledgment may be unnecessary for automated vendor settlement where provider and invoice records are sufficient.

### Settlement Reconciliation

FUZE should confirm:

1. the transaction reached the intended destination;
2. the asset and network match the instruction;
3. required confirmation or provider finality exists;
4. the recipient or service process recognized the payment where applicable;
5. fees and conversion match the record;
6. the invoice or obligation status is updated;
7. prior advances, partial payments, credits, and offsets are reflected;
8. treasury balances are updated;
9. accounting records are updated;
10. payroll and tax records are updated where applicable;
11. duplicate or remaining obligations are resolved;
12. reports are updated; and
13. the payment is closed or assigned an open exception.

Onchain confirmation, recipient acknowledgment, payroll completion, and accounting close may occur at different times.

The records should preserve those distinctions.

## Key Takeaways

- Stablecoin compensation is a controlled business-payment process for approved work, services, vendors, contractors, contributors, advisors, partners, reimbursements, and other obligations.
- Engagement, evidence, payment approval, address verification, treasury authorization, submission, confirmation, acknowledgment, settlement, payroll completion, and accounting close are separate states.
- The exact asset, network, native or contract reference, amount, rate, fees, destination, and source custody should be verified before execution.
- A symbol alone is insufficient because native, bridged, wrapped, or unsupported stablecoin representations may exist.
- Payee, wallet, custody, and beneficial-ownership evidence should be proportionate to the counterparty and risk.
- Stablecoin settlement should not bypass employment, payroll, labor, tax, benefits, sanctions, invoice, procurement, or reporting controls.
- Batch payments require itemized manifests and item-level reconciliation rather than only a batch total.
- Advances, retainers, milestones, recurring payments, commissions, and reimbursements require their own calculation and recovery treatment.
- Corrections, disputes, fraud, wrong-network transfers, depegging, custody issues, and failed payments require durable incident and recovery records.
- Stablecoin compensation remains separate from FUZE token grants, Platform Credits, product revenue, approved distributable value, wallet-based participation, claims, payouts, market access, liquidity, price support, and financial return.
