# FUZE Governance Multisig Timelock Model

## Executive Summary

FUZE Governance Multisig Timelock Model explains how FUZE treats governance direction, multisig controls, timelock controls, treasury authorization, emergency pause boundaries, smart-contract administration, and public reporting.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Governance direction does not mean unrestricted control over FUZE operations. Governance direction may support community signaling, product feedback, ecosystem feedback, reporting feedback, and proposal direction where defined. It does not automatically give token holders control over company operations, treasury movements, private agreements, legal decisions, payroll, investor terms, exchange relationships, confidential partner terms, product code, or emergency controls.

Multisig controls and timelock controls are governance and treasury safeguards. They help reduce one-person control, improve review discipline, create delay windows for sensitive actions, and support public-safe transparency where appropriate.

A multisig can require more than one authorized signer before an action is executed.

A timelock can delay execution after approval, giving time for review, monitoring, public notice where appropriate, or emergency intervention.

Multisig and timelock controls may apply to treasury vaults, reserve vaults, ecosystem vaults, product utility vaults, liquidity-related vaults, smart-contract admin roles, report registries, eligibility modules, snapshot modules, distribution modules if activated, and emergency pause functions where appropriate.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Governance, multisig, and timelock controls improve structure and accountability, but they do not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, or investment return.

## 6. Multisig Controls

A multisig is a control structure requiring more than one authorized signer to approve sensitive actions.

Multisig controls may be useful for:

- treasury vault transfers
- reserve vault transfers
- ecosystem vault transfers
- liquidity-related vault transfers
- team, advisor, partner, or contributor allocation releases
- smart-contract admin actions
- report registry administration
- token release administration
- emergency pause actions
- distribution vault actions if activated
- claim contract actions if activated
- eligibility registry updates if activated
- snapshot module updates if activated
- public vault access settings where applicable

A multisig reduces one-person control.

It can create stronger review discipline.

Boundary:

A multisig reduces risk, but it does not guarantee zero error, zero collusion, zero compromise, zero exploit, or perfect governance.

## 7. Timelock Controls

A timelock is a delay mechanism between approval and execution.

Timelock controls may support:

- review time
- public notice where appropriate
- signer review
- technical monitoring
- treasury monitoring
- community awareness where public-safe
- emergency response
- dispute detection
- incorrect transaction prevention
- malicious proposal detection
- contract upgrade review
- activation-gate review
- pause window before sensitive execution

Timelocks are especially useful for high-impact changes.

Examples include smart-contract upgrades, treasury movement, vault policy changes, token release schedule changes, claim module activation, or distribution vault activity if activated.

Boundary:

A timelock creates a delay and review window, but it does not guarantee that every risk will be detected or prevented.

---

## 8. Multisig and Timelock Together

Multisig and timelock controls are stronger together.

A multisig can require multiple approvals.

A timelock can delay execution after approval.

Together, they can support:

- separation of authority
- review before execution
- emergency intervention
- public-safe monitoring
- reduction of rushed decisions
- protection against one-person control
- stronger treasury governance
- clearer smart-contract administration
- safer activation-gate management
- better public confidence

Not every action needs both controls.

The level of control should match the risk level.

Boundary:

Multisig and timelock controls improve governance discipline, but they do not guarantee market outcome, product success, payout, or investment return.

## 33. Governance Development Direction

FUZE governance, multisig, and timelock controls can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Governance Scope Definition

Define what governance direction can influence and what remains outside public governance scope.

### Phase 2: Vault and Treasury Control Mapping

Map treasury vaults, reserve vaults, ecosystem vaults, product utility vaults, liquidity-related vaults, and participation-related readiness vaults where applicable.

### Phase 3: Multisig Setup

Apply multisig controls to sensitive vaults, smart-contract admin roles, treasury movement, and emergency functions where appropriate.

### Phase 4: Timelock Setup

Apply timelock controls to high-impact actions such as contract upgrades, vault policy changes, token release changes, distribution activation if applicable, and large treasury movements where appropriate.

### Phase 5: Emergency Pause Framework

Define pause reasons, pause authority, unpause process, reporting, and abuse-prevention rules.

### Phase 6: Reporting and Audit Trail

Publish public-safe governance reports, transaction summaries, report hashes, correction notices, and action logs where appropriate.

### Phase 7: Wallet Privacy Framework

Maintain wallet-level transparency while keeping private identity, legal records, tax records, and private agreements off-chain and permissioned.

### Phase 8: Participation Gate Governance

Govern eligibility, snapshot, approved distributable value, claim module, distribution vault, and jurisdiction rules only if the participation framework becomes active.

### Phase 9: Ongoing Governance Review

Continue reviewing signer roles, thresholds, timelock delays, emergency powers, reporting practices, and public language as the platform matures.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.
