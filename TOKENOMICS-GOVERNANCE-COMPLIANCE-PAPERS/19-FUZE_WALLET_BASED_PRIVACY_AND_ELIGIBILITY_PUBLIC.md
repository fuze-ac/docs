# FUZE Wallet-Based Privacy and Eligibility

## Executive Summary

FUZE Wallet-Based Privacy and Eligibility explains how FUZE uses wallet-level transparency without public identity exposure, how eligibility may be handled for wallet-based participation ability, and how public records remain separated from private verification records.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Wallet-based privacy means FUZE can use wallet addresses, vault records, report hashes, audit records, token release records, snapshot records, eligibility status, and claim status where applicable without publishing personal identity publicly.

Wallet-based eligibility means a wallet may need to meet defined conditions before it can participate in a specific framework, feature, claim process, governance direction, vault record, product utility surface, or reporting status.

Holding FUZE token does not automatically make every wallet eligible for every future feature.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Eligibility may depend on wallet status, custody type, snapshot timing, transfer status, lockup status, vesting status, jurisdiction review, private verification where required, legal review, accounting review, treasury readiness, audit readiness, reporting readiness, smart-contract readiness, and activation status.

FUZE does not publish personal names, emails, phone numbers, ID documents, passports, home addresses, investor identities, contributor identities, customer identities, private agreements, private legal records, private tax records, or private verification records publicly by default.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, or investment return.

## 2. FUZE Public Position on Wallet-Based Privacy

FUZE’s public position is:

FUZE uses wallet-level transparency without public identity exposure.

This means FUZE may use public-safe wallet records, vault labels, report hashes, token release records, snapshot records, eligibility status, and claim status where applicable, while keeping personal identity and private verification records off-chain, permissioned, access-controlled, and separated from public blockchain records where required.

The position has five core parts.

| Area | FUZE Position |
|---|---|
| Wallet-level transparency | Public records may show wallet addresses, vault activity, report hashes, and status records where appropriate. |
| No public identity exposure | FUZE does not publish personal identity publicly by default. |
| Private verification separation | Verification, if required, stays off-chain and permissioned. |
| Eligibility control | Wallet eligibility may depend on defined rules and activation status. |
| No automatic claims | Holding FUZE token does not automatically guarantee participation, payout, or claim rights. |

This model respects the Web3 preference for wallet-based participation while avoiding unnecessary public exposure of personal identity.

## 3. Why Wallet-Based Privacy Matters

Wallet-based privacy matters because many Web3 users, investors, contributors, community members, builders, partners, and token holders prefer to interact through wallets rather than public personal identity.

FUZE recognizes that public identity exposure can create concerns around:

- personal safety
- privacy
- harassment
- phishing
- social engineering
- unwanted public attention
- investor confidentiality
- contributor confidentiality
- private agreement exposure
- legal and tax record exposure
- business relationship exposure
- unnecessary doxxing risk
- public misunderstanding of wallet ownership

At the same time, FUZE also needs transparency for vaults, token release records, report hashes, treasury controls, public reporting, and possible wallet-based participation readiness.

The wallet-based model balances these needs.

It allows public-safe wallet visibility without exposing personal identity publicly.

Boundary:

Wallet-based privacy supports privacy and transparency, but it does not remove legal, tax, accounting, custody, eligibility, or jurisdiction requirements.

Define self-custody, exchange custody, contract wallet, multisig wallet, and custody-provider treatment.

### Phase 6: OTC and Transfer Treatment

Define how OTC transfers, transfer timing, lockup status, vesting status, snapshot timing, and claim status affect eligibility.

Define how jurisdiction, residency, sanctions screening, KYC or AML where applicable, tax treatment, and legal review affect eligibility.

### Phase 8: Smart-Contract Readiness

Prepare eligibility registry, snapshot module, report hash registry, pause module, and claim-related modules only if required and only under activation gates.

### Phase 9: Public Reporting and Community Education

Publish public-safe explanations that prevent confusion around wallet privacy, eligibility, exchange custody, OTC transfers, and no automatic payout claims.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.

Public records may show wallet addresses, vault activity, report hashes, audit records, snapshot records, eligibility status, claim status where applicable, token release records, and circulating supply records where appropriate.

FUZE does not publish personal names, emails, phone numbers, ID documents, passports, home addresses, investor identities, contributor identities, customer identities, private agreements, private legal records, private tax records, or private verification records publicly by default.

Private verification, if required, stays off-chain, permissioned, access-controlled, and separated from public blockchain records.

Wallet-based eligibility is rule-based.

Holding FUZE token does not automatically make every wallet eligible for every future feature or framework.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Eligibility may depend on token holding, wallet status, snapshot timing, custody, transfer history, lockup, vesting, jurisdiction, private verification, legal review, accounting review, treasury readiness, audit readiness, reporting readiness, smart-contract readiness, and activation status.

Exchange custody may not support user-level eligibility or claim records automatically.

Self-custody may support direct wallet records but does not guarantee eligibility.

OTC transfer does not guarantee eligibility transfer, claim transfer, liquidity, exit, or participation continuity.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and do not create wallet-based participation eligibility.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails and do not create wallet-based participation eligibility.

Product revenue does not create wallet eligibility by itself.

Approved distributable value and wallet eligibility are separate controls.

FUZE uses privacy-aware public reporting to support transparency without unnecessary public doxxing.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, or investment return.
