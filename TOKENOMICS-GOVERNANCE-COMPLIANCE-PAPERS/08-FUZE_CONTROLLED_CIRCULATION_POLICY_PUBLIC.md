# FUZE Controlled Circulation Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE Controlled Circulation Policy explains how FUZE treats token release, circulation, vault separation, supply clarity, and market-boundary communication.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Controlled circulation means FUZE manages token release, allocation movement, vault separation, vesting, unlocks, ecosystem distribution, liquidity-related movement, team and partner allocation, and public circulation reporting with discipline.

Controlled circulation does not mean price control.

Controlled circulation does not mean price support.

Controlled circulation does not mean guaranteed liquidity.

Controlled circulation does not mean guaranteed listing.

Controlled circulation does not mean guaranteed exit.

The purpose of controlled circulation is to reduce public confusion, avoid uncontrolled supply shocks where possible, separate allocation purposes, support public reporting, and help the community understand what is circulating, what is locked, what is reserved, what is vested, and what is not yet active.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, or investment return.

## 3. Why Controlled Circulation Matters

Controlled circulation matters because token supply movement can affect community trust, investor understanding, exchange discussions, market perception, treasury planning, product utility, and public reporting.

Without a clear policy, public readers may not understand:

- how much supply is circulating
- how much supply is locked
- how much supply is reserved
- which wallets are treasury-related
- which wallets are team-related
- which wallets are ecosystem-related
- which tokens are vested
- which tokens are unlocked
- which tokens are reserved for future utility
- whether a transfer is operational, treasury-related, liquidity-related, or distribution-related
- whether tokens are being released faster than expected
- whether a wallet movement changes circulating supply

Controlled circulation helps reduce confusion.

It also helps FUZE avoid unsafe claims such as price protection, guaranteed liquidity, guaranteed listing, or guaranteed market support.

Boundary:

A controlled circulation policy can improve transparency, but it does not guarantee token price, trading volume, demand, liquidity, listing, exit, or investment return.

## 5. Total Supply, Circulating Supply, and Locked Supply

FUZE public communication should clearly separate supply categories.

| Supply Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Total supply | The full token supply created or defined for FUZE token. |
| Circulating supply | Tokens available in market or user circulation under the reporting method used. |
| Locked supply | Tokens restricted by lockup, vesting, contract, policy, or custody rule. |
| Reserved supply | Tokens held for future ecosystem, treasury, product, liquidity, partner, or other defined purpose. |
| Vested supply | Tokens that have passed vesting rules but may still require policy, transfer, or release control. |
| Unvested supply | Tokens not yet available under vesting rules. |
| Treasury supply | Tokens held for treasury, reserve, ecosystem, or operational purposes. |
| Liquidity-related supply | Tokens allocated or used for liquidity-related purposes, subject to policy and boundary controls. |

Public reporting should explain which category is being discussed.

Boundary:

A supply category label does not guarantee market outcome, price, liquidity, or future availability.

## 30. Controlled Circulation Development Direction

Controlled circulation can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Define Supply Categories

Define total supply, circulating supply, locked supply, reserved supply, treasury supply, vested supply, and unvested supply.

### Phase 2: Label Allocation Purposes

Label token allocations by purpose, including ecosystem, treasury, reserve, team, advisor, partner, community, product utility, and liquidity-related categories.

### Phase 3: Establish Vault Separation

Separate token vaults or wallets by purpose where practical and public-safe.

### Phase 4: Define Vesting and Lockup Schedules

Define applicable vesting, lockup, release, and transfer rules.

### Phase 5: Build Reporting Rails

Publish public-safe token release, supply, vault, and circulation updates where appropriate.

### Phase 6: Add Multisig and Timelock Controls

Use multisig and timelock controls for sensitive vaults where appropriate.

### Phase 7: Explain Exchange and OTC Treatment

Clarify how exchange wallets, custody, OTC transfers, and eligibility implications are treated.

### Phase 8: Connect to Product Utility

Align token circulation with product readiness, Platform Credit separation, reporting, and token utility surfaces.

### Phase 9: Continue Risk Boundary Review

Keep public language away from price support, guaranteed liquidity, guaranteed listing, guaranteed payout, or investment return claims.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.
