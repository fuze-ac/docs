# FUZE BOARD / Surfboard Migration

## Executive Summary

FUZE reserves **25,000,000 FUZE**, equal to **5.00%** of the fixed **500,000,000 FUZE** supply, for continuity with eligible legacy BOARD / Surfboard positions.

This allocation creates a bounded source for approved migration claims while keeping legacy continuity separate from:

- current community participation;
- holder incentives;
- team or advisor vesting;
- treasury activity;
- ecosystem grants;
- liquidity and market operations;
- approved distributable value;
- and future public-access programs.

Migration depends on evidence.

The controlling lifecycle is:

```text
approved migration allocation and process
-> active migration notice
-> source-scope, snapshot, cutoff, and custody rules
-> application or claim submission
-> source-position and authority verification
-> prior-action and duplicate review
-> conversion calculation
-> allocation-capacity check
-> decision and participant notification
-> claim funding, lock, vesting, custody, or release
-> allocation, vault, claim, custody, and circulation reconciliation
-> correction, dispute, recovery, closure, and archive
```

Each state is separate.

A legacy balance does not automatically create migration eligibility.

Eligibility does not automatically create an approved FUZE amount.

An approved amount does not automatically become claimable.

Claimable does not mean claimed.

Claimed does not automatically mean released.

Released does not automatically mean unrestricted or circulating.

A screenshot, wallet address, social-media statement, historical market price, prior token ownership, or unsupported account record does not independently establish an approved migration position.

Every active migration process should identify:

- migration-process identifier and version;
- eligible legacy asset, contract, platform, pool, staking, or ledger scope;
- snapshot time, cutoff, or history period;
- supported custody classes;
- accepted evidence types;
- prior-action and duplicate treatment;
- conversion or allocation formula;
- caps, scaling, precision, and rounding;
- destination verification;
- claim, lock, vesting, staged-release, or custody treatment;
- dispute, correction, recovery, and appeal routes;
- opening and closing conditions;
- public-reporting method;
- current status;
- and current-as-of date.

The migration process should distinguish:

- submitted source positions;
- complete applications;
- verified source positions;
- eligible legacy quantities;
- disputed positions;
- rejected positions;
- approved FUZE amounts;
- claim-funded amounts;
- claimable amounts;
- claimed amounts;
- released amounts;
- locked or vesting amounts;
- returned or recovered amounts;
- expired or cancelled amounts;
- unused migration capacity;
- and circulation classification.

Self-custody positions may be supported through wallet signatures, transaction history, balances, contract events, snapshots, or other chain evidence.

Exchange, custodian, staking, contract, pool, bridge, or off-chain ledger positions may require intermediary records and different calculations.

Public reporting can show aggregate progress without publishing a holder's identity, private account data, wallet-person mapping, or support evidence.

This paper defines the public migration framework.

It does not activate a migration window, publish a conversion ratio, approve an individual claim, create a token-release instruction, or promise market value.

Allocation amounts and mandates remain governed by [FUZE Token Allocation Table](02-FUZE_TOKEN_ALLOCATION_TABLE_PUBLIC.md).

Source-vault release authority remains governed by [FUZE Vault-by-Vault Release Rules](15-FUZE_VAULT_BY_VAULT_RELEASE_RULES_PUBLIC.md).

Supply and circulation terminology remain governed by [FUZE Token Release and Circulation Clarity](13-FUZE_TOKEN_RELEASE_AND_CIRCULATION_CLARITY_PUBLIC.md).

## Public Position

The migration allocation exists for evidence-backed continuity, not as a general distribution pool.

FUZE should not approve a migration claim merely because:

- a claimant once held BOARD or Surfboard;
- a wallet currently holds a legacy asset;
- an exchange screenshot shows a balance;
- a person participated in an earlier community;
- a public username matches a wallet label;
- a claimant provides an unsupported spreadsheet;
- a prior migration was discussed;
- or a historical market price suggests a value.

Every migration decision should rely on:

- the active process version;
- supported source scope;
- applicable snapshot, cutoff, or history period;
- custody-specific evidence;
- source-position authority or beneficial-position support;
- prior-action review;
- duplicate review;
- deterministic calculation;
- remaining migration capacity;
- verified destination;
- and required approval.

The process should apply the same active method to similarly situated claims.

A private exception should not create an undisclosed conversion method for one applicant.

## Supported Source-Position Classes

### Direct Token Position

A supported position held in a claimant-controlled wallet at the applicable cutoff.

### Exchange or Custodian Position

A customer or beneficial position recorded by an exchange, custodian, broker, or institutional provider.

A pooled onchain wallet does not identify the customer's amount by itself.

### Staking Position

A legacy position deposited into a supported staking contract or service.

The method should define whether it recognizes:

- deposited principal;
- receipt token;
- staking share;
- accrued amount;
- or another approved quantity.

### Liquidity-Pool Position

A legacy position represented by LP units, pool shares, or another pool claim.

The method should define:

- valuation time;
- pool composition;
- ownership share;
- eligible underlying asset;
- fees or rewards treatment;
- and duplicate avoidance.

### Smart-Contract Position

A position held through a contract whose ownership, beneficiary, withdrawal, or claim logic can be supported.

### Bridge Position

A position represented across a supported bridge or wrapped-asset route.

The method should define canonical backing, lock-and-mint treatment, and duplicate controls.

### Off-Chain Platform or Ledger Position

A position recorded in an approved platform database, ledger export, signed statement, or reconciled record.

### Prior Conversion or Settlement Position

A position affected by an earlier burn, claim, conversion, settlement, refund, withdrawal, or migration action.

These prior actions should be considered before approving a new claim.

### Lost-Access Position

A position for which the claimant cannot use the original wallet, account, or key.

Such claims require a separate supported evidence route if one exists.

Support should never be assumed merely because the claimant describes lost access.

## Migration Calculation

The active notice must define the formula and every parameter.

A general structure is:

```text
eligible legacy quantity
x approved conversion factor
+/- approved adjustments
= preliminary FUZE amount
```

The preliminary amount may then be subject to:

- claimant cap;
- category capacity;
- scaling;
- minimum amount;
- precision;
- rounding;
- and duplicate or prior-action adjustment.

### Eligible Legacy Quantity

The eligible quantity should be derived from the supported source position after applying:

- snapshot or cutoff rules;
- custody treatment;
- staking or pool treatment;
- bridge treatment;
- prior-action treatment;
- duplicate removal;
- and corrections.

### Conversion Factor

The conversion factor should identify:

- unit;
- numerator and denominator;
- process version;
- approval;
- effective scope;
- and whether it is fixed, formula-based, or otherwise calculated.

### Adjustments

Possible adjustments include:

- prior claims;
- prior conversions;
- duplicate representations;
- staking or pool treatment;
- exchange or custody adjustment;
- bridge adjustment;
- cap or scaling;
- precision and rounding;
- corrected source records;
- and returned or recovered amounts.

### Capacity Constraint

```text
final approved FUZE amount
<= remaining uncommitted migration capacity
```

### Scaling

If total approved demand exceeds available migration capacity, the active process should define the approved method, such as:

- pro-rata scaling;
- claimant caps;
- tiering;
- queueing;
- cutoff priority;
- or another disclosed method.

### Price Boundary

No conversion factor should be inferred from historical or current market price unless the approved migration method expressly uses that source and calculation.

A migration conversion rule is a continuity mechanism, not a forecast, valuation guarantee, price floor, or compensation for market loss.

### Calculation Record

## Reconciliation

### Migration Allocation Reconciliation

```text
opening migration allocation balance
- confirmed releases
- active claim-funded or committed amounts
+ expired, cancelled, rejected, or returned commitments
+ verified returns and recoveries
+/- corrections
= uncommitted closing migration balance
```

### Claim-State Reconciliation

```text
approved FUZE amount
= awaiting claim funding
+ claimable
+ claimed pending transfer
+ released
+ locked or vesting under the approved method

+ returned or recovered
+/- corrections
```

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE reserves 25,000,000 FUZE, or 5.00% of fixed supply, for evidence-backed BOARD / Surfboard continuity.
- The migration allocation is a bounded category limit, not an automatic entitlement.
- Legacy position, application, eligibility, approved amount, claim funding, claimability, claim completion, release, lock, and circulation are separate states.
- Every active migration process should publish its source scope, snapshot or cutoff, custody rules, evidence hierarchy, conversion method, claim boundaries, release treatment, dispute route, and current status.
- Self-custody, exchange, custodian, smart-contract, staking, pool, bridge, and off-chain ledger positions require different evidence and duplicate controls.
- Wallet control can support a self-custody position but does not independently establish natural-person identity or authority over another account.
- Prior migrations, conversions, burns, withdrawals, settlements, LP units, staking receipts, pool underlying, and bridge representations should be reviewed to prevent double counting.
- The conversion formula should be deterministic, versioned, capacity-bounded, and independent from unsupported market-price assumptions.
- Public reporting should show aggregate migration progress while protecting claimant identity, account data, wallet-person mappings, support evidence, and credentials.
- Disputes, appeals, corrections, supplemental releases, returns, and recoveries should preserve the original case history.
- Closing a migration process does not decide the future use of unused migration capacity.
- Migration eligibility or release does not guarantee listing, liquidity, depth, spread, volume, demand, price support, income, revenue share, or financial return.
