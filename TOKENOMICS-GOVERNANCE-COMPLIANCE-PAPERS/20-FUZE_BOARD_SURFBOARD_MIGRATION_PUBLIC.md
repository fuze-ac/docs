# FUZE BOARD / Surfboard Migration

## Executive Summary

FUZE reserves 25,000,000 FUZE, or 5.00% of its fixed supply, for continuity with eligible BOARD / Surfboard positions. The allocation gives FUZE a bounded source for migration claims while keeping legacy continuity separate from current community participation, treasury, incentive, and market-operation programs.

Migration depends on evidence. An active process must define supported source systems, snapshot or record cutoffs, eligible position types, conversion method, custody treatment, destination verification, duplicate controls, claim timing, release conditions, and correction procedures.

Self-custody positions can often be reviewed through wallet or on-chain evidence. Exchange, custodian, staking, contract, or pool positions may require intermediary records and a different calculation method. Public reports can show aggregate progress without publishing a holder's identity or private account evidence.

This paper defines the public migration lifecycle. It does not activate a claim window or publish a migration ratio. Those details require an approved migration notice and technical readiness.

---

## 1. Migration Objective

The migration framework should provide eligible legacy holders with a consistent route from a supportable BOARD / Surfboard position to an approved FUZE record.

It should answer:

1. Which legacy positions are supported?
2. What evidence establishes the position?
3. Which cutoff, snapshot, or history applies?
4. How is the FUZE amount calculated?
5. How are direct and intermediary custody handled?
6. Which claim and release conditions apply?
7. How are duplicates, disputes, and corrections resolved?
8. What aggregate information is reported?

The objective is continuity with traceable evidence, not a recreation of the earlier ecosystem or a general public-access program.

## 3. Separation from Other Routes

Migration has its own evidence, purpose, and ledger.

| Route | Controlling basis |
|---|---|
| BOARD / Surfboard Migration | Eligible legacy position under the active migration method |
| Community Participation | Approved community, product-user, or contributor route |
| Holder Incentives | Earned qualification under a defined incentive program |
| Team or advisor vesting | Approved grant and vesting conditions |
| Wallet-based participation | Separate activation, eligibility, and value framework where active |

Qualifying for one route creates no automatic status in another. Records should preserve that separation even when the same wallet participates in multiple FUZE systems.

---

## 4. Active Migration Notice

Before claims open, FUZE should publish an approved notice containing:

- eligible legacy asset, contract, platform, or record scope;
- snapshot time, record cutoff, or history period;
- supported custody types;
- conversion or allocation method;
- maximum migration allocation;
- application and claim boundaries;
- destination-wallet requirements;
- lock, vesting, or staged-release treatment;
- jurisdiction and participant requirements;
- evidence, support, dispute, and correction routes;
- current contract or system references where approved.

The notice should state whether the process is upcoming, active, paused, processing, closed, or archived.

---

## 5. Eligibility Record

Each applicant should have a permissioned eligibility record.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Applicant reference | Internal identifier separated from the public report |
| Source position | Legacy wallet, account, contract, pool, or custody record |
| Evidence type | Signature, snapshot, statement, history, or intermediary confirmation |
| Eligible quantity | Supported legacy amount after applicable adjustments |
| Custody class | Self-custody, exchange, institutional, contract, staking, or pool |
| Prior action | Earlier claim, conversion, withdrawal, burn, transfer, or migration history |
| Destination | Verified FUZE-compatible wallet or approved custody route |
| Calculation version | Migration method and parameters applied |
| Status | Submitted, under review, eligible, rejected, disputed, approved, claimed, or corrected |
| Reason code | Basis for the decision or required follow-up |

Public reporting should use aggregate status counts and amounts instead of applicant identity.

## 6. Evidence Sources

### On-chain position

Evidence can include a wallet signature, token balance, transaction history, contract event, snapshot proof, or other verifiable chain record.

### Platform or ledger position

Where the legacy system used an off-chain ledger, FUZE can rely on an approved export, signed record, reconciliation, or other authoritative evidence.

### Exchange or custodian position

Evidence can include a statement, transaction history, withdrawal record, account confirmation, or a supported intermediary process. Documents should show the relevant asset, quantity, owner or account reference, and applicable date.

### Contract, staking, or pool position

Evidence can include contract balances, staking shares, pool units, underlying reserves, reward treatment, and ownership logic at the applicable cutoff.

### Prior migration or conversion

Earlier claims, burns, conversions, withdrawals, or settlements should be considered to prevent duplicate recognition.

Evidence quality can differ. The active method should define acceptable combinations and escalation routes rather than treating every screenshot or unsupported statement as equivalent.

---

## 7. Snapshot and Cutoff

The active process should define whether eligibility uses:

- a single on-chain snapshot;
- a range of historical records;
- a platform ledger cutoff;
- a final eligible balance after specified adjustments;
- an application-date verification;
- a combination of these methods.

The snapshot record should identify network, contract, block or timestamp, source, methodology, and any exclusions.

A snapshot captures a position under its stated method. It may require adjustments for pending transactions, bridged assets, pool positions, staking, custody delays, prior claims, or duplicated representations.

Changes after the cutoff should follow the published treatment. Reviewers should avoid moving the cutoff informally for individual applicants.

---

## 8. Custody Treatment

### Self-custody

The applicant can demonstrate control through a supported signature or transaction and connect the source position to a destination wallet.

### Exchange custody

The exchange can hold pooled assets while users hold account claims. FUZE may require exchange cooperation or account-level records because a shared on-chain wallet cannot identify each customer.

### Institutional custody

The custodian can provide account or beneficial-position evidence under its supported process. Public reports can identify the custody class without naming the customer.

### Smart-contract custody

Eligibility depends on the contract's ownership and withdrawal logic. The process should identify whether the user, contract, protocol, or operator controlled the relevant position.

### Staking and liquidity positions

The method should state whether it recognizes the deposited amount, receipt token, pool share, accrued amount, or another defined quantity. Underlying calculations should avoid counting both a base asset and its representation.

### Lost access

Claims involving unavailable keys or inaccessible accounts require a separate evidence route if FUZE supports one. Support availability should never be assumed.

---

## 9. Migration Calculation

The active notice must define the formula and its parameters.

A general structure is:

```text
Eligible legacy quantity
x approved conversion factor
+/- documented adjustments
= preliminary FUZE amount
```

Adjustments can address:

- prior claims or conversions;
- duplicate representations;
- contract, pool, or staking treatment;
- eligible caps or allocation scaling;
- precision and rounding;
- corrected source records.

The final approved amount is also limited by remaining migration capacity.

No conversion factor should be inferred from historical or current market prices unless an approved migration method expressly uses such data. The calculation is a continuity rule rather than a market-value promise.

---

## 10. Claim Lifecycle

### Notice

FUZE publishes the active method, evidence requirements, timing, and support route.

### Submission

The applicant provides source, custody, destination, acknowledgement, and evidence fields.

### Verification

The process tests source position, custody, duplicate history, destination control, and evidence completeness.

### Calculation

The approved method produces a preliminary amount and records all adjustments.

### Review

Jurisdiction, abuse, exception, and allocation-capacity checks are completed as applicable.

### Approval

FUZE records the approved amount, release conditions, and claim status.

### Claim or release

FUZE transfers the approved amount, makes it claimable, or places it into the stated lock or staged-release structure.

### Reconciliation

The source migration balance, claim ledger, custody evidence, and public aggregate report are updated.

---

## 11. Destination Verification

A destination should be verified before release.

Verification can include:

- supported network and address format;
- proof of control where appropriate;
- custody-provider confirmation;
- duplicate destination review;
- contract compatibility;
- sanctions, fraud, or restricted-activity review where required;
- acknowledgement of lock or claim conditions.

Changing a destination after approval should require re-verification and an auditable update. Public records can label the destination by migration function while keeping the holder's identity private.

---

## 12. Duplicate and Abuse Controls

The process should detect:

- repeated applications for the same source position;
- one position represented through multiple custody records;
- prior migration, conversion, or settlement;
- altered statements or unsupported screenshots;
- attempts to claim both pool units and underlying assets;
- related accounts used to bypass limits;
- destination substitution or compromised credentials.

A flagged record should move to review rather than automatic release. The reason, evidence, reviewer, and final decision should remain in the permissioned case file.

---

## 13. Claims, Appeals, and Corrections

An applicant can use the published support route for missing evidence, calculation questions, custody issues, or status corrections.

The case record should include:

1. disputed field or decision;
2. original evidence and calculation;
3. new evidence;
4. reviewer findings;
5. revised status or amount;
6. approval and effective date;
7. release or recovery action;
8. public aggregate effect.

An appeal process should apply the active method consistently. It should not create a private conversion rule for one applicant.

If excess FUZE was released through error, the correction route can include recovery, offset, suspension, or another approved treatment. Under-release can use a supplemental claim where supported.

---

## 14. Privacy

Migration review can involve wallet ownership, exchange statements, transaction history, identity checks, support communications, and jurisdiction records.

These materials should remain permissioned according to their purpose and retention requirements.

Public reports can show:

- total migration allocation;
- approved method version;
- claim-window status;
- aggregate submitted, eligible, approved, claimed, released, disputed, and remaining amounts;
- custody categories;
- correction and report history;
- approved contract or vault references.

Public reporting must keep names, account identifiers, identity documents, private wallet ownership mappings, support files, and exchange credentials private.

---

## 15. Reconciliation

The migration allocation should reconcile as:

```text
Opening migration balance
- approved released amount
- approved claimable commitment
+ expired or cancelled commitments
+ verified returns
+/- corrections
= uncommitted closing balance
```

The claim ledger should separately reconcile application statuses so one position cannot appear in multiple final categories.

Reports should distinguish approved, claimable, released, locked, and circulating amounts. The [FUZE Token Release and Circulation Clarity](13-FUZE_TOKEN_RELEASE_AND_CIRCULATION_CLARITY_PUBLIC.md) provides the classification vocabulary.

---

## 16. Pause and Closure

FUZE can pause processing for a source-system issue, disputed snapshot, intermediary delay, contract problem, security event, allocation difference, legal concern, or material calculation error.

The public status should explain the affected stage and treatment of existing submissions at an appropriate level.

Closure requires:

- final submission and claim cutoffs;
- completion or disposition of open cases;
- final allocation reconciliation;
- treatment of expired or unclaimed approvals;
- archive of the method and evidence references;
- final public report.

Closing one process does not determine the future use of remaining migration capacity. That decision requires separate approval.

---

## 17. Boundaries

This paper provides no active claim instruction, conversion ratio, listing commitment, liquidity assurance, payout right, or investment outcome.

Eligibility and amount depend on the active migration notice and supportable records. Market access and token value remain separate from continuity recognition.

Detailed token risks are maintained in [FUZE Token Risk Boundaries](29-FUZE_TOKEN_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md).

---

## Conclusion

BOARD / Surfboard migration gives FUZE a dedicated, capped route for eligible legacy continuity.

A published method, supportable source evidence, custody-specific review, deterministic calculation, verified destination, duplicate controls, private case records, and aggregate reconciliation make each claim traceable while preserving the separation between migration and current FUZE participation programs.
