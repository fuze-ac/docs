# FUZE Smart Contract Readiness and Activation Gates

## Executive Summary

FUZE Smart Contract Readiness and Activation Gates explains how FUZE may use smart contracts for transparency infrastructure, vault records, report hashes, conversion proof, token release records, eligibility records, snapshot records, emergency pause controls, and possible future claim logic.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Smart-contract readiness does not mean live payout activation.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean legal approval.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean accounting approval.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean audit completion.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean every wallet is eligible.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean claim logic is active.

Smart-contract deployment does not mean participation distribution is available.

FUZE may build smart-contract rails early for transparency, records, vault structure, report hashes, conversion proof, treasury controls, token release clarity, audit records, and future compatibility.

FUZE should activate live eligible-wallet claim logic only after legal, accounting, treasury, audit, reporting, smart-contract, privacy, eligibility, operator, jurisdiction, approved distributable value, and product revenue pool gates are ready.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, smart-contract security, or investment return.

## 1. Purpose of This Paper

This paper explains FUZE’s public smart-contract readiness and activation-gate model.

It answers:

- Why may FUZE use smart contracts from early stages?
- Which smart-contract modules can support transparency without live payout activation?
- Which modules belong to readiness infrastructure?
- Which modules belong to possible future participation activation?
- What gates must be ready before any live eligible-wallet claim system activates?
- How do smart contracts connect to vaults, report hashes, conversion proof, audits, eligibility, snapshots, and emergency pause?
- Why does smart-contract readiness not equal claim availability?
- How should investors and community members understand smart-contract status?
- What public wording should FUZE avoid?

This paper is not a smart-contract audit, legal opinion, accounting policy, tax opinion, securities opinion, claim instruction, token sale document, investment recommendation, exchange listing announcement, payout policy, or financial return promise.

It is a public smart-contract readiness and activation-boundary paper.

Smart contracts may support transparency, records, vault structure, reporting integrity, controlled circulation, and future compatibility, but live participation claim activation requires separate readiness gates.

The position has seven parts.

| Area | FUZE Position |

Smart contracts can support trust.

They do not replace legal, accounting, tax, audit, treasury, privacy, or jurisdiction review.

## 4. Readiness Contracts vs Activation Contracts

FUZE separates readiness contracts from activation contracts.

Readiness contracts support proof, transparency, reporting, vault structure, and control before live claim logic exists.

Activation contracts support live eligible-wallet claim logic only if the framework becomes active and all required gates are ready.

| Category | Purpose | Public Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness contracts | Support transparency, records, vaults, report hashes, conversion proof, and audit references | Not payout systems |
| Activation contracts | Support eligibility, snapshots, distribution vaults, and claims if activated | Not active until all gates are ready |

This separation is critical.

It allows FUZE to build Web3 transparency early while avoiding premature payout promises.

Boundary:

A readiness contract should not be described as an active distribution or claim system.

## 30. Activation Decision Model

A live eligible-wallet claim system should activate only when all required gates are ready.

A simplified activation model:

| Gate | Required Before Live Claim Activation |
|---|---|
| Legal | Reviewed and cleared for applicable structure and jurisdictions |
| Accounting | Revenue, costs, reserves, and approved value method reviewed |
| Treasury | Vaults, reserves, funding source, and approvals ready |
| Audit | Records, controls, and calculations reviewed where required |
| Reporting | Public-safe reports, hashes, and correction process ready |
| Smart Contract | Tested, reviewed, configured, and controlled |
| Privacy | Public and private data separation ready |
| Eligibility | Eligible and excluded wallet rules defined |
| Operator | Multisig, timelock, role separation, and emergency process ready |
| Jurisdiction | Availability and restrictions defined |
| Approved Value | Value reviewed, approved, and not excluded |
| Product Pool | Included product revenue pools defined |

Activation is not a marketing decision.

Activation is a controlled governance, legal, accounting, treasury, audit, reporting, technical, privacy, and jurisdiction decision.

Boundary:

No single gate should activate the system alone.
