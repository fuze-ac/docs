# FUZE Exchange Custody and Wallet Participation

## Executive Summary

FUZE Exchange Custody and Wallet Participation explains how FUZE treats self-custody, exchange custody, OTC transfer, wallet-level transparency, wallet-based participation eligibility, snapshots, claim support, transfer boundaries, and public communication around custody risk.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Custody matters because wallet-based participation depends on wallet-level records, eligibility rules, snapshots, claim status, private verification where required, jurisdiction review, and activation status.

A self-custody wallet may provide direct wallet-level visibility because the user controls the blockchain wallet directly.

An exchange-custody wallet may not provide direct user-level visibility because the visible blockchain wallet usually belongs to the exchange, custodian, or omnibus account rather than the individual user.

Exchange-held FUZE token may require separate treatment.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not automatically support wallet-based participation eligibility.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not automatically support user-level snapshots.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not automatically support user-level claims.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not automatically support eligibility records.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not guarantee payout, claim rights, liquidity, exit, or participation continuity.

OTC transfers may also affect eligibility assumptions. Buying FUZE token through OTC does not automatically transfer historical eligibility, snapshot status, claim status, private agreement rights, or participation status unless the active rules define that treatment.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

FUZE uses wallet-level transparency without public identity exposure.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, smart-contract security, or investment return.

## 4. Self-Custody

Self-custody means the user controls the wallet directly.

Self-custody may include:

- browser wallet
- mobile wallet
- hardware wallet
- multisig wallet controlled by the user or group
- smart contract wallet controlled by the user or group

Self-custody may make wallet-level participation easier to support because the wallet holder can directly:

- hold FUZE token
- sign messages
- connect to supported applications
- appear in on-chain snapshots
- prove wallet control
- interact with supported contracts if activated
- receive wallet-level status records
- claim directly if a claim framework becomes active and the wallet is eligible

Self-custody also creates user responsibility.

Self-custody users must protect:

- private keys
- seed phrases
- wallet approvals
- device security
- transaction signing
- phishing risk
- malicious links
- fake token contracts
- fake claim sites
- wrong-address transfers

Boundary:

Self-custody may support direct wallet records, but it does not guarantee eligibility, claim rights, payout, asset safety, liquidity, or exit.

## 8. Self-Custody vs Exchange Custody

The difference between self-custody and exchange custody is central to wallet-based participation.

| Topic | Self-Custody | Exchange Custody |
|---|---|---|
| Wallet control | User controls wallet directly | Exchange or custodian controls wallet |
| On-chain visibility | User wallet appears directly | Exchange wallet may appear instead |
| Message signing | Usually possible | Usually not possible directly through exchange account |
| Snapshot clarity | More direct | May require exchange cooperation |
| Claim interaction | More direct if activated | May be unsupported or indirect |
| Private verification | May still be required | May require exchange or custodian records |
| Eligibility clarity | Easier to define | More complex |
| Transfer timing | User controls timing | Exchange controls deposit and withdrawal timing |
| Custody risk | User key risk | Exchange or custodian risk |
| Participation support | Possible if rules allow | Not automatic |

Boundary:

Neither custody type guarantees payout, claim rights, liquidity, listing, exit, or investment return.

## 38. Development Direction

FUZE exchange custody and wallet participation treatment can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Custody Category Definition

Define self-custody, exchange custody, institutional custody, multisig custody, smart-contract wallet custody, OTC transfer, treasury wallet, reserve wallet, team wallet, advisor wallet, partner wallet, contributor wallet, and liquidity-related wallet categories.

### Phase 2: Public Custody Boundary

Explain that exchange custody may not support user-level wallet participation records and that self-custody does not guarantee eligibility or safety.

### Phase 3: Snapshot Rule Design

Define how snapshots treat self-custody, exchange custody, custody providers, contract wallets, excluded wallets, transfers, and timing.

### Phase 4: Eligibility Rule Design

Define eligible, not eligible, pending verification, custody unsupported, jurisdiction restricted, excluded, claim available, claim completed, claim paused, and disputed statuses where applicable.

### Phase 5: OTC and Transfer Treatment

Define how OTC transfers, transfer timing, lockups, vesting, private agreements, claim status, and snapshot timing affect eligibility.

### Phase 6: Exchange and Custodian Review

Review whether any exchange or custodian can support user-level records, claims, distributions, or verification if the framework becomes active.

### Phase 7: Privacy and Verification Design

Keep personal identity, private verification, KYC or AML where applicable, tax records, and account records off-chain, permissioned, access-controlled, and separated from public wallet records.

### Phase 8: Smart-Contract Readiness

Prepare eligibility registry, snapshot module, claim status registry, excluded wallet registry, report hash registry, and emergency pause module only under activation gates.

### Phase 9: Public Education

Educate holders that custody choice affects records and that neither self-custody nor exchange custody guarantees payout, eligibility, liquidity, listing, or exit.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.

## 39. Key Takeaways

FUZE Exchange Custody and Wallet Participation explains exchange custody, self-custody, OTC transfer, wallet participation eligibility, snapshots, claim support, transfer boundaries, and why exchange-held tokens may require separate treatment.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE.

Self-custody means the user controls the wallet directly.

Exchange custody means the exchange or custodian controls the wallet for the user.

Self-custody may support more direct wallet-level records, but it does not guarantee eligibility, claim rights, asset safety, liquidity, or exit.

Exchange-held FUZE token may not support user-level wallet participation records because the visible blockchain wallet may belong to the exchange or custodian.

Exchange-held FUZE token does not automatically support snapshots, eligibility records, direct claims, payout, or participation continuity.

Exchange listing does not guarantee wallet participation support.

Exchange liquidity does not guarantee wallet participation eligibility.

OTC transfer does not automatically transfer historical eligibility, snapshot status, claim status, private agreement rights, or participation continuity.

Transfer timing can affect eligibility if a framework becomes active.

Holding FUZE token does not automatically make every wallet eligible for every future feature or framework.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Product revenue custody does not automatically create approved distributable value or wallet eligibility.

Private verification, if required, stays off-chain, permissioned, access-controlled, and separated from public blockchain records.

FUZE uses wallet-level transparency without public identity exposure.

Smart-contract readiness does not guarantee exchange custody support, user-level claims, legal approval, or participation activation.

Custody choice does not remove market risk.
