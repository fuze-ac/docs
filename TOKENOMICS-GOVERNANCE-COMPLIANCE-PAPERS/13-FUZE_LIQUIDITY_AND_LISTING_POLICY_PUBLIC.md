# FUZE Liquidity and Listing Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE Liquidity and Listing Policy explains how FUZE treats liquidity, exchange access, listing communication, market integrity, liquidity-related allocations, AIMM support boundaries, and public no-guarantee language.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Liquidity and listing are important topics for public market access, user transferability, token visibility, investor review, community confidence, and ecosystem participation. However, liquidity and listing must be communicated carefully because they can easily be misunderstood as promises of price, exit, trading volume, exchange access, or market support.

FUZE does not guarantee listing.

FUZE does not guarantee liquidity.

FUZE does not guarantee trading volume.

FUZE does not guarantee exchange access.

FUZE does not guarantee price support.

FUZE does not guarantee market support.

FUZE does not guarantee exit liquidity.

Liquidity-related planning is operational readiness. It is not a market outcome promise.

AIMM may support liquidity operations reporting, venue comparison notes, exchange communication summaries, market maker communication records, liquidity risk reports, and public-safe liquidity updates. AIMM does not guarantee liquidity, price support, listing, market outcome, trading volume, trading profit, payout, income, yield, profit, or investment return.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

This policy explains how FUZE can discuss liquidity and listing without creating unsafe investor or community expectations.

## 3. Why Liquidity and Listing Clarity Matters

Liquidity and listing clarity matters because token communities often misunderstand market access.

Public readers may assume:

- listing is guaranteed
- liquidity is guaranteed
- exchange access creates price growth
- liquidity allocation means price support
- market makers guarantee price stability
- trading volume can be promised
- every holder can exit easily
- OTC buyers will always exist
- exchange custody supports all wallet-based participation rights
- liquidity tools remove market risk

These assumptions are unsafe.

FUZE separates market access from guaranteed outcomes.

Liquidity and listing can support access, visibility, trading pathways, and ecosystem participation, but they do not eliminate market risk.

Boundary:

Liquidity and listing clarity does not guarantee token price, demand, trading volume, market depth, exchange support, payout, income, yield, profit, exit, or investment return.

## 4. What Liquidity Means

Liquidity means the ability to buy or sell a token with reasonable market depth, available counterparties, and acceptable execution conditions.

Liquidity may be affected by:

- trading venue availability
- DEX liquidity pool depth
- CEX order book depth
- market maker participation where applicable
- buyer and seller demand
- spread
- volatility
- token circulation
- unlock schedules
- market sentiment
- community trust
- product progress
- regulatory environment
- broader crypto market cycles
- stablecoin and payment rail access
- exchange custody rules
- technical availability
- bridge or chain support where applicable

Liquidity is dynamic.

Liquidity can increase, decrease, disappear, or move between venues.

Boundary:

FUZE does not guarantee that holders can buy, sell, transfer, or exit at a desired price, time, size, or venue.

## 5. What Listing Means

Listing means a token becomes available on a trading venue, exchange, DEX interface, aggregator, marketplace, or supported platform where trading or discovery can occur.

Listing may involve:

- DEX pool creation
- DEX interface visibility
- token list inclusion
- aggregator visibility
- CEX application
- CEX review
- CEX approval
- CEX integration
- deposit and withdrawal setup
- market pair setup
- compliance review
- technical integration
- liquidity preparation
- market maker coordination where applicable
- custody review
- user support process
- jurisdiction review
- listing announcement only after approval where appropriate

Listing depends on the venue.

Each venue may have its own rules, review process, technical standards, legal requirements, market criteria, liquidity requirements, jurisdiction limits, and risk policies.

Boundary:

FUZE should not say a listing is active or confirmed unless the listing is actually approved and live or officially announced under appropriate terms.

## 18. Liquidity and Wallet-Based Participation

Liquidity and wallet-based participation are separate topics.

Wallet-based participation ability is a possible FUZE token-related framework where eligible FUZE-holding wallets may participate in approved distributable value from defined FUZE product revenue pools if the framework becomes active.

Liquidity refers to market access and trading conditions.

The two can interact because selling FUZE token may affect future wallet status if participation rules are active.

For example, if an activated framework uses snapshots, eligibility records, holding status, lock status, or claim status, then selling through exchange, OTC, or self-custody transfer may affect participation status under the rules in effect.

However:

- liquidity does not guarantee participation
- participation does not guarantee liquidity
- exchange access does not guarantee eligibility
- OTC sale does not guarantee eligibility transfer
- holding FUZE does not guarantee claim rights
- wallet-based participation is not active as a guaranteed public payout right

Boundary:

Liquidity access does not guarantee wallet-based participation eligibility, payout, claim support, or continued participation status after sale.

## 30. Liquidity and Listing Development Direction

FUZE liquidity and listing policy can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Public Boundary Definition

Define no listing guarantee, no liquidity guarantee, no price-support promise, and no exit guarantee.

### Phase 2: Official Token and Venue Clarity

Publish official token contract details, official venue references, and public-safe market access notes where appropriate.

### Phase 3: DEX Readiness

Support DEX liquidity only where technically, operationally, and publicly appropriate, with clear risk language.

### Phase 4: CEX Review

Explore CEX opportunities only where appropriate, subject to venue review, technical review, legal review, and operational readiness.

### Phase 5: Liquidity-Related Vault Separation

Separate liquidity-related allocation from treasury, reserves, team allocation, community allocation, and approved distributable value.

### Phase 6: AIMM Reporting Support

Use AIMM for liquidity operation summaries, risk notes, venue notes, exchange communication summaries, and public-safe liquidity reporting.

### Phase 7: Market Integrity Controls

Avoid fake volume, wash trading, manipulation, undisclosed price support, misleading market-maker claims, and unsafe public claims.

### Phase 8: Custody and Participation Clarity

Clarify exchange custody, self-custody, OTC transfer, wallet-based participation, snapshot, and eligibility boundaries.

### Phase 9: Ongoing Public Reporting

Publish public-safe liquidity, circulation, venue, and risk updates where appropriate.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.
