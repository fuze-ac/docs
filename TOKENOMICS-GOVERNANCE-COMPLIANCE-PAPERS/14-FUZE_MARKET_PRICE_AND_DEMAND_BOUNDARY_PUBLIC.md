# FUZE Market Price and Demand Boundary

## Executive Summary

FUZE Market Price and Demand Boundary explains how FUZE communicates token market price, demand, product performance, market cycles, liquidity, exchange access, and public expectation limits.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

FUZE token market price may move independently from FUZE product performance.

A stronger product ecosystem can support utility, public trust, user interest, community attention, and long-term platform alignment, but it does not guarantee token demand, token price, trading volume, liquidity, listing, exit, payout, income, yield, profit, or investment return.

FUZE does not claim that market price decline does not matter because holders may receive participation.

FUZE does not claim that product revenue protects token price.

FUZE does not claim that wallet-based participation removes market risk.

FUZE does not claim that team performance alone controls token demand.

FUZE token demand may be affected by product utility, user adoption, market cycles, liquidity, exchange access, circulating supply, unlock schedules, regulation, community trust, investor behavior, public communication, macro conditions, and broader crypto sentiment.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

This paper keeps FUZE market language clear, disciplined, and safe for investors and the community.

## 31. Market Boundary Development Direction

FUZE market price and demand boundary can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Public No-Price-Promise Language

Define that FUZE does not provide price targets, price predictions, guaranteed returns, liquidity guarantees, listing guarantees, or exit promises.

### Phase 2: Product Utility Separation

Explain product utility without making market price claims.

### Phase 3: Platform Credit Separation

Keep Platform Credits as product usage credits, not token-demand promises.

### Phase 4: Wallet Participation Boundary

Explain that wallet-based participation ability is activation-gated and does not remove market risk.

### Phase 5: Liquidity and Listing Boundary

Explain DEX, CEX, OTC, liquidity allocation, and exchange custody risks without promising access or exit.

### Phase 6: Controlled Circulation Reporting

Use supply, vesting, release, and vault reporting to improve clarity without promising price stability.

### Phase 7: AIMM and QTB Boundary

Keep AIMM as liquidity operations support and QTB as market interpretation support, without trading or price guarantees.

### Phase 8: Community Language Review

Educate community members to avoid unsafe claims around price, demand, payout, listing, liquidity, and return.

### Phase 9: Public Reporting

Publish public-safe reports that support transparency while avoiding market outcome promises.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.
