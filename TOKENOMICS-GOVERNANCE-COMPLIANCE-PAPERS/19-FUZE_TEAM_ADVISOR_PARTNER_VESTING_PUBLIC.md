# FUZE Team Advisor Partner Vesting

## Executive Summary

FUZE uses grants, vesting, service conditions, milestone controls, custody restrictions, and release approvals to connect long-term token alignment with verified work, responsibility, contribution, or partner outcomes.

This paper governs four recipient classes:

1. team members and operators;
2. advisors;
3. strategic contributors; and
4. partners, integrators, grantees, and ecosystem counterparties receiving approved token-based alignment.

The controlling lifecycle is:

```text
approved relationship and allocation source
-> complete grant proposal
-> role, scope, milestone, schedule, custody, and termination review
-> grant approval
-> grant activation
-> vesting, service, contribution, or milestone measurement
-> evidence review and acceptance
-> vested-amount calculation
-> release approval
-> claim, custody transfer, lock, or staged release
-> allocation, grant, vault, recipient, and circulation reconciliation
-> amendment, suspension, termination, forfeiture, return, correction, closure, or archive
```

Each state is separate.

A relationship does not create a grant.

A grant does not automatically become active.

An active grant does not mean the full amount is vested.

Vested does not mean released.

Released does not automatically mean unrestricted, transferable, or circulating.

A public announcement, title, introduction, partnership discussion, informal promise, invoice, salary payment, or completed task does not independently authorize FUZE token release.

Every grant should identify:

- stable grant identifier and version;
- recipient class;
- source allocation and source vault;
- maximum approved FUZE amount;
- relationship and purpose;
- effective date;
- vesting method;
- schedule or milestone tranches;
- service, role, contribution, performance, or partner conditions;
- acceptance evidence;
- custody and destination treatment;
- continuing restrictions;
- release authority;
- termination, forfeiture, cancellation, return, and dispute treatment;
- amendment and correction rules;
- public-reporting treatment;
- current status;
- and current-as-of date.

The approved allocation categories relevant to this paper are:

- **Team Allocation:** 45,000,000 FUZE — 9.00% of fixed supply;
- **Advisors / Strategic Contributors:** 15,000,000 FUZE — 3.00% of fixed supply; and
- **Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships:** 40,000,000 FUZE — 8.00% of fixed supply, only for the approved portion assigned to a partner, integration, grant, or ecosystem relationship.

These allocations remain separate.

A partner grant should not be funded from Team Allocation merely because a person works closely with the team.

An advisor grant should not be funded from Ecosystem Growth & Partnerships merely because the advisor introduced a partner.

Classification should follow the substance of the relationship and the approved allocation mandate.

Token alignment is separate from current compensation.

Stablecoin or fiat-equivalent compensation can support salary, fees, expenses, bonuses, invoices, or completed work under the applicable compensation process.

A FUZE token grant supports longer-term alignment under documented vesting, service, contribution, milestone, custody, and release conditions.

Platform Credits remain product-consumption units and belong to neither the compensation ledger nor the token-vesting ledger.

This paper owns the grant, vesting, milestone, service, custody, release, amendment, termination, forfeiture, dispute, correction, reconciliation, public-reporting, and archive framework for the covered recipient classes.

Allocation amounts and mandates remain governed by [FUZE Token Allocation Table](02-FUZE_TOKEN_ALLOCATION_TABLE_PUBLIC.md).

Source-vault release authority remains governed by [FUZE Vault-by-Vault Release Rules](15-FUZE_VAULT_BY_VAULT_RELEASE_RULES_PUBLIC.md).

Vault custody and reserve administration remain governed by [FUZE Vault and Reserve Policy](14-FUZE_VAULT_AND_RESERVE_POLICY_PUBLIC.md).

Stablecoin compensation remains governed by [FUZE Stablecoin Compensation Policy](11-FUZE_STABLECOIN_COMPENSATION_POLICY_PUBLIC.md).

## Public Position

FUZE grants should be approved before vesting begins and should use conditions that can be measured, reviewed, and reconciled.

No universal vesting schedule is established by this paper.

Each grant should use a schedule and condition set appropriate to:

- the source allocation;
- recipient class;
- role or relationship;
- expected duration;
- measurable contribution;
- milestone risk;
- retention objective;
- custody structure;
- and termination treatment.

FUZE should not approve or release a token grant merely because:

- a person holds a title;
- a person previously worked with the team;
- an advisor attended meetings;
- a contributor submitted informal work;
- a partner signed a non-binding memorandum;
- a partnership was announced;
- a service invoice was paid;
- a stablecoin payment was completed;
- a product was launched;
- or token price changed.

Every vesting and release event should be supported by the active grant version, measurable conditions, current relationship status, accepted evidence, reconciled prior events, valid custody, and required authority.

## Vesting Methods

### Time-Based Vesting

FUZE becomes vested in defined portions over an approved period.

The schedule should state:

- commencement date;
- cliff where applicable;
- vesting frequency;
- tranche size or formula;
- final vesting date;
- partial-period treatment;
- and termination-date calculation.

### Cliff with Periodic Vesting

No portion vests before the cliff condition is satisfied.

After the cliff, vesting follows the approved schedule.

The record should state whether the cliff tranche includes the period before the cliff or begins only after it.

### Service-Based Vesting

Vesting depends on continued service or an approved role through each measurement date.

The grant should define:

- what counts as active service;
- leave treatment;
- part-time or role-change treatment;
- suspension treatment;
- and effective departure date.

### Milestone-Based Vesting

A tranche vests only when a defined deliverable or outcome is completed and accepted.

The grant should define:

- milestone;
- owner;
- evidence;
- acceptance criteria;
- reviewer;
- deadline;
- cure period;
- partial-completion treatment;
- and continuing obligations.

### Contribution-Based Vesting

Vesting depends on measurable advisory, strategic, technical, community, ecosystem, or operating contribution.

The record should avoid vague language such as `support the project` without measurable expectations.

### Performance-Based Vesting

Vesting depends on approved, measurable performance criteria.

The method should identify:

- metric;
- source data;
- period;
- threshold;
- calculation;
- reviewer;
- reversal treatment;
- and anti-manipulation controls.

### Hybrid Vesting

A grant combines two or more of:

- time;
- service;
- role;
- milestone;
- contribution;
- performance;
- continuing support;
- or another measurable condition.

The record should state whether the conditions are:

- cumulative;
- alternative;
- sequential;
- weighted;
- or tranche-specific.

### Staged Grant

Separate stages receive separate review or activation.

Approval of an initial stage does not automatically approve later stages.

### Immediate Vesting

Immediate vesting requires a specific documented basis and does not bypass release, custody, tax, legal, or reporting controls.

## Vesting Calculation

### Time-Based Calculation

The grant should define the exact formula.

A generic linear form is:

```text
vested amount at measurement time
= grant amount x completed qualified vesting time / total vesting time
```

The active grant should define:

- cliff treatment;
- day-count or interval method;
- start and end timestamps;
- timezone;
- partial interval treatment;
- precision;
- and rounding.

### Tranche Calculation

```text
vested amount
= sum(accepted tranche amounts)
```

### Hybrid Calculation

The grant should define the order and interaction of time, service, role, milestone, contribution, and performance conditions.

### Current Vested-Unreleased Amount

```text
vested unreleased
= cumulative vested
- cumulative released
- vested amount cancelled, returned, or otherwise resolved
+/- approved corrections
```

### Current Unvested Amount

```text
unvested
= active grant amount
- cumulative vested
- unvested amount cancelled or forfeited
+/- approved corrections
```

### Calculation Record

Each vesting calculation should identify:

- grant identifier and version;
- measurement time;
- active grant amount;
- applicable condition;
- evidence;
- prior cumulative vested amount;
- newly vested amount;
- cumulative vested amount;
- cumulative released amount;
- vested-unreleased amount;
- unvested amount;
- cancelled, forfeited, returned, disputed, and corrected amounts;
- precision and rounding;
- reviewer;
- approval;
- status;
- and current-as-of date.

## Vesting Ledger

FUZE should maintain a recipient-level permissioned ledger and an aggregate public-safe ledger.

### Mutually Exclusive Grant Buckets

For reconciliation, a grant may use:

- unvested;
- vested unreleased;
- released;
- cancelled or forfeited;
- returned;
- and disputed or pending final classification.

The grant reconciliation is:

```text
original grant
+ approved grant increases
- approved grant reductions before vesting
+/- corrections
= unvested
+ vested unreleased
+ released
+ cancelled or forfeited
+ returned
+ disputed or pending final classification
```

The exact treatment should avoid double counting.

### Overlapping Attributes

The following may be attributes rather than additive buckets:

- locked;
- continuing restricted;
- claimable;
- recipient-custodied;
- externally custodied;
- and circulating.

For example, released FUZE can also remain locked.

## Custody, Claim, Lock, and Release

Unvested FUZE should remain in:

- source-allocation custody;
- an approved vesting contract;
- a controlled sub-account;
- or another approved structure.

A grant ledger entry alone should not be treated as an onchain transfer.

### Vested-Unreleased Custody

Vested FUZE may remain controlled until:

- a scheduled release;
- participant claim;
- destination verification;
- tax or legal condition;

- claim opening;
- claim closing;

- employment or service basis;
- invoice, payroll, milestone, reimbursement, bonus, or payment period;
- payment asset;

- settlement;

A token-grant record identifies:

- source allocation;
- maximum amount;
- long-term purpose;
- earning or vesting conditions;
- custody;
- release;
- cancellation;
- and reporting.

### No Silent Substitution

A completed stablecoin payment should not:

- activate a token grant;
- increase a grant;
- accelerate vesting;
- satisfy a milestone;
- or authorize token release

unless the active grant expressly defines that relationship and receives the required evidence and approval.

Likewise, token vesting should not replace a missing salary, invoice, reimbursement, tax, or accounting record.

### Platform Credits

Platform Credits remain product-consumption units and should not be recorded as:

- salary;
- stablecoin compensation;
- FUZE grant;
- vested FUZE;
- or token release.

### Standard Departure

Possible treatment includes:

- vesting stops at the defined effective date;
- vested amounts remain subject to applicable restrictions;
- vested-unreleased amounts follow the grant terms;
- unvested amounts are cancelled or returned;
- open milestones close or enter review;
- and disputes remain restricted.

### Termination for Cause or Material Breach

Treatment should follow the governing terms and applicable law.

The record should identify:

- reason class;
- effective date;
- affected unvested amount;
- affected vested-unreleased amount;
- released amount;
- return or clawback right where legally supported;
- dispute route;
- authority;
- and final disposition.

### Partner Relationship Termination

The record should address:

- completed milestones;
- accepted deliverables;
- unaccepted milestones;
- continuing support obligations;
- previously released amount;
- unused grant capacity;
- return rights;
- and public-reporting treatment.

### No Automatic Treatment Beyond the Grant

Departure or termination should not be used to impose a result that the active grant, applicable agreement, and required review do not support.

## Cancellation, Forfeiture, Return, and Recovery

### Cancellation

Cancellation removes future grant capacity before it vests.

### Forfeiture

Forfeiture applies only when supported by the active terms and applicable review.

It should identify:

- amount;
- condition;
- effective date;
- authority;
- dispute status;
- and destination.

### Return

A return restores FUZE to approved allocation or recovery custody.

The return record should identify:

- original grant;
- original release;
- reason;
- amount;
- source and destination;
- transaction or custody evidence;
- revised grant ledger;
- revised allocation balance;
- circulation effect;
- and current status.

### Recovery

Recovery after an erroneous release, duplicate release, wrong destination, contract issue, or other incident depends on technical, legal, contractual, and practical feasibility.

A recovery right does not guarantee recovery.

### Reuse

Cancelled, forfeited, returned, or recovered FUZE should not be reused until:

- source allocation is confirmed;
- dispute and legal treatment are resolved;
- custody reconciles;
- public reports are corrected where necessary;
- and the amount is restored to an available category state.

## Disputes and Cure Periods

### Dispute Types

Possible disputes include:

- service status;
- milestone completion;
- contribution attribution;
- acceptance decision;
- vesting calculation;
- termination date;
- duplicate grant;
- destination control;
- release status;
- return obligation;
- or public-reporting error.

- affected amount;
- issue class;
- evidence;
- interim restriction;
- reviewer;
- escalation route;
- decision;
- correction or amendment effect;
- allocation and custody effect;

### Cure Period

Where a cure period applies, the grant should define:

- deficiency;
- required remediation;
- deadline;
- evidence;
- reviewer;
- effect on vesting;
- effect on release;
- and outcome if not cured.

### Public Treatment

Public reporting should use aggregate categories and should not publish private allegations, performance details, legal advice, or personal identity without authority.

## Death, Incapacity, Assignment, and Succession Boundaries

Where relevant and legally supported, the grant should define treatment for:

- death;
- incapacity;
- dissolved entity;
- merger or acquisition of a partner;
- assignment;
- successor entity;
- estate or representative authority;
- and destination change.

No third party should receive or control a grant solely through an informal request.

The process should require appropriate legal, identity, authority, custody, tax, and destination review.

Public reporting should remain aggregate and privacy-protective.

## Corrections and Restatements

### Correction

A correction addresses an error in:

- recipient reference;
- source allocation;
- grant amount;
- effective date;
- schedule;
- milestone status;
- service status;
- calculation;
- rounding;
- custody;
- destination;
- release;
- return;
- or reporting.

### Correction Record

The record should identify:

1. affected grant and version;
2. affected vesting periods or milestones;
3. prior entry;
4. corrected entry;
5. cause;
6. affected vested amount;
7. affected released amount;

9. affected source-vault balance;
10. affected recipient treatment;
11. supplemental release, cancellation, refund, return, or recovery treatment;
12. authority;
13. publication effect;

### Amendment Versus Correction

A correction fixes an error.

An amendment changes approved terms.

The two event types should remain separate.

### Restatement

A public restatement may be required when a material correction changes aggregate vesting, release, return, or allocation reports.

Prior public versions should remain traceable.

## Reconciliation

### Grant Reconciliation

```text
opening active grant amount
+ approved grant increases
- approved grant reductions before vesting
+/- corrections
= unvested
+ vested unreleased
+ released
+ cancelled or forfeited
+ returned
+ disputed or pending final classification
```

### Vesting-Period Reconciliation

```text
opening cumulative vested amount
+ newly vested amount
- vested amount reversed or corrected
= closing cumulative vested amount
```

### Release Reconciliation

```text
opening vested-unreleased amount
+ newly vested amount
- confirmed releases
- vested cancellations, returns, or recoveries
+/- corrections
= closing vested-unreleased amount
```

### Allocation Reconciliation

For each covered allocation:

```text
opening allocation balance
- confirmed grant releases or grant-funding transfers
+ verified returns and recoveries
+/- approved reclassifications and corrections
= closing allocation balance
```

### Custody Reconciliation

The grant ledger should reconcile with:

- source vault;
- vesting contract;
- claim contract;
- recipient custody where tracked;
- return or recovery vault;
- and public aggregate reporting.

### No Double Counting

Released FUZE should not remain in the additive vested-unreleased bucket.

Continuing restrictions should be tracked as attributes of vested or released amounts rather than counted as extra FUZE.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE team, advisor, strategic-contributor, and partner grants should originate from the correct allocation and use measurable time, service, role, contribution, performance, or milestone conditions.
- A relationship, title, announcement, invoice, or stablecoin payment does not independently create a token grant or vesting right.
- Grant approval, activation, vesting, vested-unreleased status, release approval, claimability, release, lock, and circulation are separate states.
- Every grant should define source allocation, amount, purpose, effective date, vesting method, evidence, custody, release, amendment, termination, dispute, return, reporting, and closure before activation.
- Team grants, advisor or strategic-contributor grants, and partner grants should remain separately classified and reported.
- Milestones should use objective acceptance criteria, evidence, reviewer authority, partial-completion treatment, cure periods, and continuing obligations.
- Vesting ledgers should use mutually exclusive additive buckets and track continuing restrictions as attributes to avoid double counting.
- Stablecoin or fiat-equivalent compensation and FUZE token alignment require separate records and should not silently modify each other.
- Role changes, scope changes, acceleration, termination, forfeiture, and return require explicit treatment and approval.
- Batch vesting reviews can improve operations, but every grant still requires item-level evidence and calculation.
- Corrections fix errors; amendments change terms. Both should preserve history.
- A grant, vested amount, release, or partner milestone does not guarantee listing, liquidity, depth, spread, volume, demand, price support, income, revenue share, or financial return.
