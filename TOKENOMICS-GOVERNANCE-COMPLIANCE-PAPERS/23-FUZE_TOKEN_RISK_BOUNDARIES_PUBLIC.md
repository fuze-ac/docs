# FUZE Token Risk Boundaries

## Executive Summary

FUZE Token Risk Boundaries explains token-specific risks, market risks, liquidity risks, wallet-based participation risks, custody risks, smart-contract risks, regulatory risks, public communication limits, and no-guarantee boundaries for FUZE token.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Token risk boundaries matter because FUZE token connects to a product-first AI SaaS ecosystem, Platform Credits, stablecoin rails, wallet-level records, public reporting, vault transparency, controlled circulation, governance direction, ZAGA utility surfaces, QTB and AIMM boundaries, and possible future wallet-based participation ability.

These connections can create public interest, but they also create risks and possible misunderstanding.

FUZE token does not guarantee payout.

FUZE token does not guarantee income.

FUZE token does not guarantee dividend.

FUZE token does not guarantee yield.

FUZE token does not guarantee profit.

FUZE token does not guarantee price appreciation.

FUZE token does not guarantee listing.

FUZE token does not guarantee liquidity.

FUZE token does not guarantee trading volume.

FUZE token does not guarantee market support.

FUZE token does not guarantee exit.

FUZE token does not guarantee approved distributable value.

FUZE token does not guarantee wallet-based participation eligibility.

FUZE token does not guarantee business revenue, game earnings, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, or investment return.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

This paper gives investors, community members, partners, contributors, and public readers a clear risk boundary for understanding FUZE token without unsafe return expectations.

## 3. Why Token Risk Boundaries Matter

Token risk boundaries matter because public crypto language can easily create unsafe expectations.

Public readers may assume:

- token utility means token price will rise
- product progress means token demand is assured
- product revenue means holders will receive payout
- wallet-based participation means every holder is eligible
- vault balances are claimable value
- stablecoin balances will be distributed
- Platform Credit usage creates token demand
- exchange listing is expected
- liquidity will be available
- AIMM can protect price
- QTB can predict market direction
- ZAGA game values create financial earnings
- governance gives token holders company control
- smart contracts make participation legally approved

These assumptions are not FUZE’s public position.

FUZE separates product utility from market outcome, product revenue from approved distributable value, wallet records from public identity, and participation readiness from live claim activation.

Boundary:

FUZE token risk cannot be removed by public language, product ambition, smart contracts, AI systems, or community belief.

FUZE token risk boundaries can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Public Risk Language Alignment

Align all public papers around one-token model, no guaranteed-return language, Platform Credit separation, stablecoin rail separation, and activation-gated wallet participation.

### Phase 2: Token Market Risk Education

Explain token price, demand, liquidity, listing, OTC, custody, controlled circulation, and exchange risks in investor and community language.

### Phase 3: Product and Revenue Boundary Education

Explain product usage, Platform Credits, product revenue, approved distributable value, and wallet-based participation boundaries.

### Phase 4: Wallet and Eligibility Risk Education

Explain self-custody, exchange custody, OTC transfer, eligibility status, snapshots, private verification, jurisdiction, and claim status boundaries.

### Phase 5: Smart-Contract and Security Review

Document smart-contract readiness, security review, audit limits, emergency pause, multisig, timelock, and incident response boundaries.

### Phase 6: Product-Specific Risk Review

Maintain separate risk boundaries for HerHelp, SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, CommunityLayer AI, ZAGA, QTB, AIMM, AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad.

### Phase 7: Public Reporting

Use public-safe reports, vault labels, report hashes, token release summaries, and risk boundary updates to reduce misunderstanding.

### Phase 8: Community Communication Control

Help the community avoid unsafe claims around payout, price, listing, liquidity, AI accuracy, game earnings, and participation eligibility.

### Phase 9: Ongoing Risk Review

Update risk boundaries as product status, market status, legal status, exchange status, smart-contract status, and public reporting maturity evolve.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.

## 39. Key Takeaways

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE.

FUZE token may support product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Token utility does not guarantee token demand, token price, liquidity, listing, payout, income, yield, profit, exit, or investment return.

FUZE token market price may rise, fall, or become volatile.

Liquidity is not guaranteed.

Listing is not guaranteed.

OTC buyer availability is not guaranteed.

Exit is not guaranteed.

Controlled circulation supports supply clarity, but it does not guarantee price stability.

Vault balances are not automatically approved distributable value.

Reserve balances are not automatic payout supply.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Product revenue is not guaranteed and does not automatically become approved distributable value.

Approved distributable value is conditional and not guaranteed.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Holding FUZE token does not automatically make every wallet eligible for every feature or framework.

Self-custody and exchange custody carry different risks.

Smart contracts can support transparency and automation, but they do not remove legal, accounting, tax, audit, treasury, privacy, jurisdiction, technical, or user risks.

QTB is not financial advice, investment advice, trading instruction, guaranteed market prediction, or autonomous trading execution.
