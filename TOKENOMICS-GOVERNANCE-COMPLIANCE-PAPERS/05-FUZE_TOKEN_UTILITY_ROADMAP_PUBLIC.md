
# FUZE Token Utility Roadmap

## Executive Summary

The FUZE Token Utility Roadmap sequences token-connected functions from product evidence to controlled activation, measurement, expansion, correction, pause, withdrawal, or retirement.

The roadmap is evidence-led rather than date-led.

Its governing sequence is:

```text
useful product or ecosystem function
-> utility candidate
-> qualification
-> specification
-> build
-> internal test
-> required review
-> activation approval
-> limited pilot
-> public beta where appropriate
-> active for stated scope
-> measured continuation, expansion, narrowing, pause, withdrawal, or retirement
```

Later-stage work should not outrun the evidence required by earlier stages.

A product may remain useful without FUZE-token utility.

A utility may remain narrow rather than expand across the whole ecosystem.

Different products may occupy different stages at the same time.

Parallel work does not mean simultaneous launch, shared status, or automatic approval.

The roadmap begins with product and shared-platform readiness. It then permits narrow token-utility pilots where token relevance is clear. Community, game, partner, reporting, and cross-product functions may expand only after bounded operating evidence exists. Governance, wallet-based participation, approved distributable value, claims, payouts, liquidity, listings, and other higher-impact mechanisms remain subject to their own specialist gates.

This roadmap does not establish:

- launch dates;
- active utility;
- product availability;
- wallet eligibility;
- participation rights;
- governance authority;
- claim eligibility;
- payout eligibility;
- DEX activation;
- CEX approval;
- liquidity;
- token demand;
- token price;
- revenue;
- profitability; or
- financial return.

Every public status should identify the exact product, utility, audience, network, contract, partner, period, geography, custody model, and release scope to which it applies.

The controlling utility taxonomy and lifecycle vocabulary appear in [FUZE Token Utility Model](04-FUZE_TOKEN_UTILITY_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

## Roadmap Principles

### Product Value Before Token Utility

A token connection should follow a useful product or ecosystem function.

The roadmap should not use token interaction to compensate for:

- an unclear user problem;
- incomplete product design;
- unreliable delivery;
- missing support;
- weak permissions;
- unclear commercial treatment;
- missing data controls;
- or unavailable operating ownership.

### Dependency Before Date

Roadmap advancement depends on completed prerequisites rather than target dates alone.

Dates may support planning, but they do not override unresolved:

- product;
- technical;
- security;
- privacy;
- legal;
- compliance;
- accounting;
- treasury;
- governance;
- partner;
- custody;
- support;
- reporting;
- or market dependencies.

### Scope Before Portfolio-Wide Claims

A roadmap stage belongs to the exact:

- utility;
- product;
- module;
- audience;
- network;
- contract;
- partner;
- geography;
- period;
- custody model;
- and release level.

One active pilot does not establish broad FUZE-token utility.

### Evidence Before Expansion

Expansion should follow evidence that the current scope is:

- understandable;
- useful;
- reliable;
- supportable;
- secure;
- privacy-preserving;
- compliant with applicable requirements;
- measurable;
- and reversible.

### Minimum Necessary Token Interaction

Utility work should use the least consequential token or wallet interaction that satisfies the use case.

A read-only check should not become a transfer or token lock without a separate justification and review.

### Status Before Marketing Strength

Roadmap language should match evidence.

The following remain different:

- candidate;
- qualified;
- specified;
- planned;
- in development;
- internally testable;
- under review;
- approved for activation;
- limited pilot;
- public beta;
- active for stated scope;
- paused;
- narrowed;
- withdrawn;
- retired;
- and archived.

### Reversibility Before Expansion

Every utility should have a route to:

- pause;
- narrow;
- correct;
- migrate;
- withdraw;
- replace;
- or retire.

### Market Outcomes Remain Separate

Utility sequencing does not establish:

- market access;
- listing;
- liquidity;
- depth;
- spread;
- volume;
- token demand;
- price support;
- price appreciation;
- or financial return.

## Roadmap Control Model

Each stage should use the same control structure.

| Control element | Required question |
|---|---|
| Objective | What capability or evidence should this stage create? |
| Entry conditions | What must already be true before work begins? |
| Deliverables | What should be built, documented, reviewed, or operated? |
| Exit evidence | What proves the stage is complete for the stated scope? |
| Dependencies | Which systems, teams, contracts, providers, partners, or reviews can block advancement? |
| Pause criteria | Which result requires correction, narrowing, or reassessment? |
| Public status | What exact language can be supported? |
| Next decision | Continue, expand, hold, revise, narrow, pause, withdraw, or retire? |

A stage can be complete for one utility and incomplete for another.

The roadmap should be maintained at the utility-entry level rather than only as a single portfolio percentage.

## Roadmap at a Glance

| Stage | Primary objective | Typical public status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Product Foundation | Establish useful, supportable product or ecosystem behavior before token dependence | Product status only; utility candidate at most |
| 2. Shared Platform Readiness | Build reusable identity, permission, wallet, data, audit, payment, credit, support, and reporting rails | Shared capability designed, tested, or active for stated product scope |
| 3. Qualified Utility and Controlled Pilots | Specify and test narrow FUZE-token functions | Candidate, qualified, specified, internally testable, under review, or limited pilot |
| 4. Community, Game, Partner, and Reporting Expansion | Extend evidence-backed utility into bounded ecosystem contexts | Pilot, beta, or active for a named program and scope |
| 5. Portfolio Utility and Interoperability | Reuse approved utility patterns across products without duplicating token identity or rights | Active for stated cross-product scope |
| 6. Advanced Governance and Specialist Mechanisms | Prepare higher-impact mechanisms through separate specialist gates | Designed, under review, approved for activation, or active only under the specialist process |

These stages describe maturity and sequencing.

They are not guaranteed release waves or calendar commitments.

## Stage 1: Product Foundation

### Objective

Establish products and ecosystem workflows that create recognizable user value without depending on token interaction.

### Entry Conditions

- target user and user problem are defined;
- product or ecosystem owner exists;
- the core journey is documented;
- current product status can be stated accurately;
- data, permission, support, and provider needs are understood at a basic level;
- product-specific risks are identified;
- and the product can be evaluated independently from token price or market activity.

### Deliverables

- defined product or ecosystem outcome;
- usable workflow appropriate to current status;
- product interface or operating process;
- source and destination boundaries;
- account and permission model;
- data handling;
- support and correction route;
- reliability and provider controls;
- Platform Credit treatment where product consumption uses credits;
- payment and revenue classification where commercial activity exists;
- product status record;
- product-specific risk boundaries;
- and a candidate utility hypothesis only where token relevance may exist.

### Exit Evidence

- intended users can complete the named workflow in the stated scope;
- product results and failures can be observed;
- support ownership exists;
- product data remains within approved boundaries;
- product status matches current evidence;
- unresolved risks and dependencies are recorded;
- and any proposed token connection passes initial utility qualification.

### Dependencies

- product design;
- AI and data providers;
- architecture;
- privacy;
- security;
- identity and permissions;
- infrastructure;
- customer or participant access;
- support capacity;
- and commercial ownership.

### Pause Criteria

Pause token-related work when:

- the product lacks a clear user problem;
- users cannot complete the intended journey;
- token involvement substitutes for missing product value;
- product reliability is inadequate;
- permissions or data boundaries are unclear;
- support cannot manage the current scope;
- or public descriptions exceed demonstrable product status.

### Supported Public Status

At this stage, public language may describe:

- product concept;
- product design;
- prototype;
- internal testing;
- limited pilot;
- beta;
- or active product scope

when supported by evidence.

It should not describe token utility as active merely because a future connection is being considered.

## Stage 2: Shared Platform Readiness

### Objective

Provide reusable platform capabilities that allow products and later utility surfaces to operate consistently, securely, and audibly.

### Entry Conditions

- at least one product has a current need for each proposed shared capability;
- platform ownership and product boundaries are defined;
- teams agree on the required behavior;
- records and correction requirements are known;
- and reuse creates more value than local product implementation.

### Deliverables

Shared capabilities may include:

- account and identity integration;
- role and permission controls;
- wallet discovery and connection;
- network and canonical-contract configuration;
- read-only token data;
- wallet-control signatures;
- transaction-state handling;
- custody-model handling;
- Platform Credit metering;
- payment and reconciliation interfaces;
- event and audit records;
- privacy and data controls;
- provider failover;
- incident handling;
- support tooling;
- correction workflows;
- public-status reporting;
- utility-registry infrastructure;
- and versioned technical documentation.

### Exit Evidence

- each shared capability works in its approved product scope;
- records can connect product, wallet, token, credit, payment, support, and correction events where applicable;
- permissions remain scoped;
- public and private data remain separated;
- failures and incidents have owners;
- support can identify the affected product and utility;
- a second product can reuse selected capabilities where justified;
- and status reporting distinguishes design, implementation, test, deployment, activation, and availability.

### Dependencies

- architecture;
- security;
- privacy;
- wallet and node providers;
- indexers;
- product integration;
- payment and credit systems;
- support;
- incident response;
- and reporting systems.

### Pause Criteria

Pause or narrow the shared-platform effort when:

- no current product needs the capability;
- reuse creates more complexity than value;
- permissions become broader than the product requires;
- wallet-person identity linkage is unnecessary or unsafe;
- records cannot be reconciled;
- provider dependency is uncontrolled;
- or a platform failure can affect products without adequate isolation.

### Supported Public Status

Shared capabilities may be described as:

- designed;
- implemented;
- internally testable;
- deployed;
- or active for stated product scope.

Deployment does not establish product or utility activation.

## Stage 3: Qualified Utility and Controlled Pilots

### Objective

Move narrow FUZE-token functions from candidate status through qualification, specification, testing, review, approval, and limited pilot.

### Entry Conditions

- the underlying product or ecosystem action is usable;
- a defined user or ecosystem problem exists;
- the token role is materially relevant;
- a simpler non-token alternative has been evaluated;
- the utility registry entry exists;
- the canonical token identity is known for the test environment;
- wallet, network, custody, permission, privacy, support, and reporting requirements are understood;
- and required reviewers have identified the conditions for a controlled test or pilot.

### Deliverables

- utility qualification record;
- full utility specification;
- utility-registry entry;
- token-condition logic;
- wallet and custody support matrix;
- supported network and contract scope;
- product or ecosystem response;
- user instructions;
- consent and transaction language where applicable;
- error and fallback behavior;
- support and correction route;
- monitoring;
- abuse controls;
- incident and pause route;
- test scenarios and evidence;
- required review records;
- activation decision;
- pilot scope, audience, period, and owner;
- and measurement plan.

Candidate pilots may include approved:

- access;
- event registration;
- community participation;
- recognition;
- contribution;
- game access or profile linkage;
- partner program;
- reporting or verification;
- or another bounded utility.

### Exit Evidence

- eligible users complete the intended token and product action;
- system responses match the specification;
- wallet and custody behavior is understood;
- failures, retries, corrections, and support demand are measurable;
- security and privacy controls operate within the pilot scope;
- users understand the distinction among FUZE token, Platform Credits, payments, points, game values, eligibility, claims, and payouts;
- product or ecosystem value remains clear;
- and the responsible reviewers approve continuation, revision, expansion, pause, withdrawal, or closure.

### Dependencies

- canonical token identity;
- token-contract status;
- wallet interfaces;
- network providers;
- indexers;
- product integration;
- security;
- privacy;
- legal and compliance review;
- accounting and treasury review where applicable;
- support;
- reporting;
- and pilot participants.

### Pause Criteria

Pause or redesign when:

- users cannot understand the token condition;
- the token role adds no meaningful product or ecosystem value;
- wallet failure or abandonment is material;
- unsupported custody creates unfair or misleading treatment;
- privacy controls are inadequate;
- security findings remain open;
- support cannot resolve common failures;
- public language would overstate the scope;
- or the pilot produces no useful outcome.

### Supported Public Status

Use the exact status:

- candidate;
- qualified;
- specified;
- in development;
- internally testable;
- under review;
- approved for activation;
- or limited pilot.

A pilot is not public availability.

## Stage 4: Community, Game, Partner, and Reporting Expansion

### Objective

Extend proven token utility into bounded community, ZAGA, event, campaign, partner, contribution, recognition, and reporting contexts.

### Entry Conditions

- a relevant pilot or comparable utility has operating evidence;
- the underlying product or ecosystem function remains useful;
- community, game, event, campaign, or partner responsibilities are documented;
- game and product values use clear labels;
- moderation, anti-abuse, support, and reporting capacity exist;
- data and permission boundaries are clear;
- and expansion remains within approved token-identity and utility rules.

### Deliverables

- defined utility program and version;
- exact utility class;
- eligible audience;
- token condition or action;
- product or ecosystem result;
- role and authority mapping;
- game, product, payment, credit, and token naming boundaries;
- campaign, event, or partner scope;
- duration and budget where applicable;
- moderation and anti-abuse controls;
- dispute, appeal, correction, and support routes;
- partner integration and offboarding terms;
- public instructions;
- aggregate reporting;
- pause and retirement treatment;
- and updated registry status.

### Exit Evidence

- participation and utility completion can be measured;
- users understand what FUZE token does and does not do;
- partner obligations are met and reviewable;
- game values remain distinct from FUZE token and financial assets;
- community safety and moderation remain supportable;
- data remains within approved audiences;
- abuse and duplicate treatment are controlled;
- support and correction operate effectively;
- and continuation is justified by product or ecosystem value.

### Dependencies

- community operations;
- game design;
- event operations;
- partner delivery;
- campaign systems;
- moderation;
- customer support;
- identity and permissions;
- wallet infrastructure;
- data governance;
- local requirements;
- and public reporting.

### Pause Criteria

Pause or narrow when:

- activity is dominated by speculation rather than product or ecosystem use;
- game or product labels create financial confusion;
- partner performance cannot be verified;
- abuse exceeds available controls;
- community harm or moderation burden is material;
- data use exceeds consent or authority;
- support cannot meet the expanded scope;
- or operating effort is disproportionate to user value.

### Supported Public Status

The program may be described as:

- limited pilot;
- public beta;
- active for the named audience and scope;
- paused;
- narrowed;
- withdrawn;
- or retired.

It should not be described as ecosystem-wide utility unless the evidence and registry scope support that statement.

## Stage 5: Portfolio Utility and Interoperability

### Objective

Reuse approved token-utility patterns across products and partners without creating inconsistent rights, uncontrolled identity linkage, duplicated token systems, or unsupported cross-product behavior.

### Entry Conditions

- multiple utility surfaces have stable definitions and operating evidence;
- the canonical FUZE-token identity is used consistently;
- product and utility records reconcile;
- shared platform capabilities can support cross-product behavior;
- product owners agree on responsibilities;
- wallet and custody differences are understood;
- privacy impact is reviewed;
- and no product-specific unit is being presented as a second FUZE ecosystem token.

### Deliverables

- common utility identifiers;
- shared registry standards;
- consistent lifecycle vocabulary;
- reusable wallet and network patterns;
- interoperable access or recognition where appropriate;
- cross-product permission rules;
- product-specific opt-in and disclosure;
- cross-product data contracts;
- privacy and identity-linkage controls;
- partner integration standards;
- migration and backward-compatibility rules;
- common support routes;
- common incident handling;
- cross-product reporting;
- and scope-specific pause or withdrawal controls.

### Exit Evidence

- users can understand where a token status applies;
- status does not silently create rights in another product;
- product and platform records agree;
- permissions remain product-scoped;
- identity linkage is limited to the approved purpose;
- support can identify and resolve cross-product failures;
- partners use the correct canonical token identity;
- no credit, point, badge, game value, receipt, or partner unit is presented as a second FUZE token;
- and cross-product utility improves measurable user or operating outcomes.

### Dependencies

- platform architecture;
- product ownership;
- canonical token registry;
- wallet and custody support;
- data governance;
- product APIs;
- partner APIs;
- migration tooling;
- support;
- change management;
- and public documentation.

### Pause Criteria

Pause or redesign when:

- interoperability expands rights unintentionally;
- product teams cannot support shared behavior;
- identity linkage threatens privacy;
- one product can expose another product’s data or permissions;
- records disagree;
- partner mappings are unreliable;
- legacy behavior cannot be migrated safely;
- or complexity exceeds the user and operating benefit.

### Supported Public Status

Use scope-specific language such as:

- active across the named products;
- active for the named partner integration;
- supported for the named wallet and network scope;
- limited pilot;
- public beta;
- paused;
- or retired.

“Interoperable across FUZE” should be used only when the documented scope supports it.

## Stage 6: Advanced Governance and Specialist Mechanisms

### Objective

Prepare higher-impact utility and token mechanisms only after the required governance, technical, financial, privacy, legal, operational, and reporting controls are mature.

### Entry Conditions

- the relevant product and utility evidence is current;
- authority and legal effect are defined;
- wallet and identity boundaries are understood;
- custody and treasury responsibilities are defined;
- specialist policies exist;
- eligibility and reporting methods are defined;
- contracts and operators can be tested without implying activation;
- and the mechanism cannot be safely represented as an ordinary product, account, payment, credit, badge, or low-risk utility.

### Deliverables

Depending on the mechanism, deliverables may include:

- proposal, signaling, voting, or delegation specification;
- governance authority map;
- multisig and timelock controls;
- smart-contract specifications;
- audit and security evidence;
- wallet and custody support;
- eligibility and snapshot rules;
- approved distributable value records;
- claim and payout records;
- treasury and accounting treatment;
- privacy-preserving reporting;
- legal and compliance review;
- market or venue preparation under the applicable policy;
- emergency pause and correction controls;
- migration and retirement plan;
- user instructions;
- support;
- and activation record.

### Exit Evidence

- every applicable activation gate has an accountable approval or documented unresolved status;
- technical behavior reconciles to policy, treasury, custody, and accounting records;
- users can identify their rights, responsibilities, costs, limitations, and support route;
- private identity and wallet-person mappings remain protected;
- claim, payout, governance, custody, and market states remain separately classified;
- public reporting can be maintained accurately;
- and pause, correction, migration, and retirement procedures have been tested.

### Dependencies

- governance;
- treasury;
- accounting;
- audit;
- legal;
- compliance;
- smart contracts;
- custody;
- jurisdiction;
- security;
- privacy;
- support;
- reporting;
- market providers;
- and other third-party operators.

### Pause Criteria

Pause advancement when:

- a material gate remains unresolved;
- authority is unclear;
- eligibility cannot be supported fairly;
- custody or treasury records do not reconcile;
- privacy-preserving reporting is unavailable;
- a third party cannot meet the required controls;
- users may mistake discussion or testing for an active right;
- or public language would overstate governance, participation, claims, payouts, liquidity, or market access.

## Portfolio Prioritization

FUZE should prioritize utility work using evidence rather than product count or marketing visibility.

| Factor | Review question |
|---|---|
| User value | Does the utility improve a real product or ecosystem action? |
| Token relevance | Is FUZE token better than a simpler non-token mechanism? |
| Product readiness | Is the underlying product journey usable and supportable? |
| Shared readiness | Are identity, permissions, wallet, data, support, and reporting capabilities available? |
| Evidence | Can success, failure, correction, and current status be measured? |
| Risk | What custody, privacy, security, legal, governance, market, or financial interpretation may arise? |
| Effort | Can FUZE build and support the function with available resources? |
| Reuse | Can work benefit more than one approved surface without creating harmful coupling? |
| Reversibility | Can the utility be paused, narrowed, migrated, or retired safely? |
| Communication | Can the current state be described without overstating rights, availability, demand, or market effect? |

Priority should generally favor utility that is:

- narrow;
- understandable;
- product-connected;
- low-friction;
- evidence-generating;
- supportable;
- reusable where justified;
- and reversible.

A large number of utility candidates is not a measure of utility quality.

## Pause, Rollback, Withdrawal, and Retirement

A utility may return to an earlier stage or leave the roadmap.

### Pause

Pause new or continuing use while preserving support, records, and user obligations.

### Rollback

Return to a prior implementation, contract, configuration, token condition, or product behavior under an approved process.

### Narrowing

Reduce the audience, product scope, network, contract, geography, partner, period, or token condition.

### Withdrawal

Remove the utility from current use pending redesign, replacement, or closure.

### Retirement

End new use after completing migration, user treatment, reconciliation, support, reporting, and archive requirements.

The decision record should identify:

- utility and version;
- affected stage and status;
- reason;
- effective time;
- affected users, wallets, products, and partners;
- wallet and token treatment;
- product-access treatment;
- pending transactions;
- claims or payouts where applicable;
- data treatment;
- migration or replacement;
- support;
- public communication;
- retained records;
- reactivation conditions if paused;
- reviewer;
- and final status.

A paused, withdrawn, or retired utility should not remain represented as active.

## Key Takeaways

- The FUZE Token Utility Roadmap is evidence-led rather than date-led.
- The sequence begins with useful products, then shared platform readiness, qualified utility pilots, measured ecosystem expansion, cross-product interoperability, and specialist advanced mechanisms.
- Different utilities and products may occupy different stages at the same time.
- Parallel development does not mean simultaneous activation or shared status.
- Product value, token relevance, wallet and custody behavior, security, privacy, legal review, support, and reporting should be proven before expansion.
- A pilot, deployed contract, wallet screen, partner discussion, roadmap item, or target date does not independently establish active utility.
- Product use, Platform Credits, payments, token holding, utility, participation, governance, claims, payouts, market access, liquidity, price, revenue, and financial return remain separate.
- Roadmap priority should favor narrow, understandable, product-connected, supportable, measurable, and reversible utility.
- Public updates should identify exact stage, lifecycle status, scope, evidence, dependencies, limitations, next decision, and current-as-of date.
- Utilities can be reprioritized, narrowed, paused, rolled back, withdrawn, or retired when evidence no longer supports advancement.
