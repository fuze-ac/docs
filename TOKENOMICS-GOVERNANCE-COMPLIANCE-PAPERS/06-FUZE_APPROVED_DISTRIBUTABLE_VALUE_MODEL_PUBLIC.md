# FUZE Approved Distributable Value Model

## Executive Summary

FUZE Approved Distributable Value Model explains the difference between product revenue and approved distributable value.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

Wallet-based participation ability is a possible FUZE token-related framework where eligible FUZE-holding wallets may participate in approved distributable value from defined FUZE product revenue pools if the framework becomes active.

Approved distributable value is not gross revenue. It is not token sale proceeds. It is not seed-round funds. It is not general treasury balance. It is not game rewards. It is not unrealized token value. It is not exchange trading volume. It is not a public payout promise.

Approved distributable value can exist only after product revenue is confirmed, reconciled, reviewed, reduced by applicable deductions, separated from excluded categories, placed under treasury controls, checked against legal and jurisdiction limits, reviewed through accounting and audit processes where required, and approved under an active participation framework.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails. Product revenue, if generated, requires reconciliation and does not automatically become approved distributable value.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

FUZE does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, or investment return.

## 2. FUZE Public Position on Approved Distributable Value

FUZE’s public position is:

Approved distributable value is the portion of defined FUZE product revenue that may be considered for eligible-wallet participation only if the participation framework becomes active and after required reconciliation, deductions, reserves, review, reporting, approval, and legal or jurisdiction checks.

This position has several important parts.

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Defined product revenue | Only product revenue from defined FUZE product revenue pools may be considered. |
| Reconciliation | Revenue must be confirmed, matched, and reviewed. |
| Deductions | Refunds, chargebacks, fees, taxes, costs, and other required deductions may apply. |
| Reserves | FUZE may reserve funds for operations, risk, support, treasury stability, and future obligations. |
| Approval | No value becomes approved distributable value without defined approval. |
| Activation | Participation must be active before any claim logic can exist. |
| Eligibility | Only eligible FUZE-holding wallets may be considered if the framework is active. |
| No guarantee | Product revenue does not guarantee payout, income, yield, profit, or investment return. |

Approved distributable value is a controlled output of a process.

It is not the same as money received.

## 3. Why This Model Is Necessary

FUZE needs an approved distributable value model because public readers may confuse several different things:

- gross revenue
- product revenue
- Platform Credit purchases
- stablecoin payments
- treasury balances
- token sale proceeds
- seed-round funds
- game rewards
- product usage
- approved distributable value
- wallet-based participation claims

These are not the same.

Without a clear model, community members may wrongly assume that every payment to FUZE automatically becomes claimable value for token holders.

That is not FUZE’s model.

FUZE separates product revenue from approved distributable value because revenue must first pass through accounting, treasury, legal, audit, reserve, reporting, and approval controls.

This protects the platform, users, investors, community members, product customers, contributors, and eligible wallets if any participation framework becomes active.

Boundary:

The existence of a revenue model does not guarantee revenue, approved distributable value, participation activation, or claim availability.

## 5. Gross Revenue

Gross revenue is the total amount received or recorded before deductions, adjustments, reserves, refunds, taxes, fees, and costs.

Gross revenue is not approved distributable value.

Gross revenue may need to be adjusted for:

- refunds
- chargebacks
- payment processor fees
- platform fees
- gas fees
- exchange or conversion fees
- taxes
- cost of service delivery
- AI inference costs
- infrastructure costs
- support costs
- partner shares
- vendor costs
- staff or contributor compensation
- reserves
- accounting adjustments
- failed or disputed payments
- fraud or abuse adjustments
- jurisdiction restrictions

Gross revenue is only an early input.

It is not the final value that can be considered for participation.

Boundary:

FUZE should not state or imply that gross revenue goes directly to eligible wallets.

## 6. Confirmed Product Revenue

Confirmed product revenue is product revenue that passes basic confirmation.

Confirmation may include:

- payment received
- product or service identified
- invoice or usage record matched
- payment method recorded
- user or workspace record linked where appropriate
- Platform Credit purchase matched where applicable
- refund period considered where applicable
- chargeback risk considered where applicable
- stablecoin transaction matched where applicable
- fiat payment matched where applicable
- product revenue pool identified where applicable

Confirmed product revenue is stronger than gross receipt.

However, confirmed product revenue is still not approved distributable value.

It must pass additional reconciliation, deduction, reserve, review, and approval steps.

Boundary:

Confirmed revenue does not guarantee approved distributable value or wallet-based participation claim.

## 7. Reconciled Product Revenue

Reconciled product revenue is product revenue that has been reviewed and adjusted.

Reconciliation may include:

- matching payment records to product usage
- matching Platform Credit usage to product service
- matching stablecoin transaction records to invoices or product records
- deducting refunds
- deducting chargebacks
- deducting payment fees
- excluding failed payments
- excluding promotional credits
- excluding unpaid invoices
- identifying product category
- identifying revenue period
- checking customer records where appropriate
- checking tax treatment
- checking accounting classification
- checking whether the revenue belongs to a defined product revenue pool

Reconciled product revenue is a stronger input for review.

It is still not automatically approved distributable value.

Boundary:

Reconciled product revenue may still be excluded, reserved, reduced, delayed, or rejected under the approved distributable value policy.

## 8. Approved Distributable Value

Approved distributable value is the value that remains after the defined process is completed.

It may require:

1. Product revenue is generated.
2. Product revenue is confirmed.
3. Product revenue is reconciled.
4. Excluded categories are removed.
5. Refunds and chargebacks are handled.
6. Fees and taxes are considered.
7. Operating costs are considered.
8. Product-specific costs are considered.
9. AI usage costs are considered.
10. Infrastructure costs are considered.
11. Reserves are applied.
12. Treasury controls are followed.
13. Accounting review is completed.
14. Audit or review process is completed where required.
15. Legal and jurisdiction review is completed.
16. Product revenue pool rules are applied.
17. Approval process is completed.
18. Participation framework is active.
19. Eligibility rules are applied.
20. Reporting and claim readiness are available where applicable.

Approved distributable value is therefore not a simple revenue number.

It is a controlled and reviewed amount.

Boundary:

Approved distributable value exists only after the required process and does not exist as a guaranteed public payout promise.

## 25. Approved Distributable Value Calculation Concept

FUZE can describe the calculation concept at a high level.

A public-safe concept is:

Confirmed product revenue  
minus exclusions  
minus refunds and chargebacks  
minus fees and taxes  
minus product costs  
minus operating and infrastructure costs  
minus reserves  
subject to accounting review  
subject to treasury approval  
subject to audit or review where required  
subject to legal and jurisdiction review  
subject to participation framework activation  
equals possible approved distributable value for a defined period and product revenue pool.

This concept is not a fixed formula.

The actual calculation may vary by product, jurisdiction, accounting treatment, treasury policy, reserve policy, legal review, and activation status.

Boundary:

A public concept does not create a claim, payout, income right, yield, dividend, or guaranteed distribution.

## 30. Development Direction

The approved distributable value model can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Define Revenue Categories

Define which FUZE products and services can generate product revenue.

### Phase 2: Define Exclusions

Clearly exclude token sale proceeds, seed-round funds, general treasury balance, game rewards, market price movement, and unreconciled values.

### Phase 3: Build Product Revenue Records

Record product usage, Platform Credit purchases, product payments, stablecoin payments, and service activity where supported.

### Phase 4: Build Reconciliation Process

Match payments, usage, refunds, chargebacks, fees, taxes, costs, and product revenue categories.

### Phase 5: Define Reserve Policy

Define operating reserves, refund reserves, tax reserves, AI cost reserves, infrastructure reserves, legal reserves, security reserves, and treasury stability reserves.

### Phase 6: Build Reporting Rails

Create public-safe reports, report hashes, audit records, conversion proof records, and product revenue pool summaries where appropriate.

### Phase 7: Review Legal and Jurisdiction Rules

Review eligibility, jurisdictions, securities considerations, tax treatment, payment treatment, custody issues, and public communication.

### Phase 8: Connect to Activation Gates

Allow approved distributable value to matter for wallet-based participation only if the participation framework becomes active and all required gates are ready.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.

## 31. Key Takeaways

FUZE Approved Distributable Value Model explains the difference between product revenue and approved distributable value.

Approved distributable value is not gross revenue.

Approved distributable value is not token sale proceeds.

Approved distributable value is not seed-round funds.

Approved distributable value is not general treasury balance.

Approved distributable value is not game rewards.

Approved distributable value is not market price movement, unrealized token value, exchange trading volume, or liquidity pool value.

Product revenue, if generated, must be confirmed, reconciled, adjusted, reviewed, and approved before any value can be considered approved distributable value.

Costs, fees, refunds, chargebacks, taxes, reserves, operating expenses, AI costs, infrastructure costs, legal costs, audit costs, and treasury controls may apply.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

Eligible FUZE-holding wallets may participate only if the participation framework becomes active and all required gates are ready.

Holding FUZE token does not automatically create eligibility, payout, income, yield, profit, or claim rights.

FUZE uses wallet-level transparency without public identity exposure.

Approved distributable value modeling supports clarity and discipline, but it does not guarantee payout, income, dividend, yield, profit, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, exit, game earnings, business revenue, user growth, community growth, or investment return.
