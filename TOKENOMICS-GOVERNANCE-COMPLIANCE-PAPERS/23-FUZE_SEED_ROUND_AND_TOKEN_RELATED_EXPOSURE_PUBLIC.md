# FUZE Seed Round and Token-Related Exposure

## Executive Summary

FUZE describes its current fundraising direction as a Private Strategic Seed Round for strategic investors, operator-angels, early-stage partners, and other qualified interested parties. Detailed structure and terms belong in controlled private review rather than public participation materials.

The round is intended to support product-first execution, shared platform capabilities, operating capacity, market entry, security, reporting, and professional readiness. Fundraising receipts remain separate from product revenue, Platform Credit activity, community participation allocations, and any approved distributable value.

Private terms may include token-related exposure where the reviewed structure permits it. Any such exposure requires its own amount, source, vesting or lockup, custody, transfer, release, jurisdiction, and risk records. Participation in the financing does not itself activate a public token route or wallet-based participation framework.

This paper explains the process and records surrounding that private exposure. It is a public boundary document, not the round's offering materials or a statement that financing has closed.

---

## 1. Paper Objective

The paper should let a public reader understand:

1. what kind of fundraising process FUZE is describing;
2. which information can be public;
3. which information requires qualified private review;
4. how financing records differ from token records;
5. how optional token-related exposure would be documented;
6. how confidentiality, custody, vesting, and reporting apply.

Private legal, commercial, financial, tax, and suitability terms are determined through the applicable reviewed documents. This paper does not supply those terms.

## 3. Process Stages

### Preparation

FUZE prepares the round structure, company and product materials, use-of-funds framework, diligence records, risk information, data-room controls, and review responsibilities.

### Introduction

FUZE and an interested party exchange enough information to assess strategic fit and determine whether private review should proceed.

### Qualification

The applicable process considers investor or partner type, jurisdiction, authority, confidentiality, eligibility, and other required factors.

### Diligence

Authorized parties review relevant company, product, technical, financial, legal, token, governance, market, and risk materials.

### Terms

The parties review proposed financing and any associated rights, conditions, restrictions, or token-related exposure in private documents.

### Approval and execution

Required company and investor approvals are completed, documents are executed, and settlement instructions are verified.

### Closing and post-closing

FUZE reconciles receipts, updates ownership or contractual records, records any token-related schedule, and completes required reporting and ongoing obligations.

Moving through one stage provides no assurance that a later stage or closing will occur.

## 4. Qualification Record

A permissioned qualification record can include:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Party identifier | Controlled reference for the interested person or entity |
| Participant class | Strategic investor, operator-angel, partner, or another approved class |
| Jurisdiction | Applicable location and review status |
| Authority | Confirmation that the representative can act for the party |
| Confidentiality | Agreement and information-access level |
| Eligibility | Required qualification, suitability, or policy result where applicable |
| Source and settlement review | Required payment and treasury checks |
| Diligence access | Approved data-room folders and expiration |
| Status | Introduced, qualified, reviewing, declined, withdrawn, committed, or closed |

The public record should use aggregate categories. Identity and underlying evidence remain permissioned unless disclosure is authorized.

## 5. Private Materials

Private review can include materials such as:

- company and ownership information;
- product evidence and roadmap;
- business model and operating plan;
- financial history, budget, or model where available;
- use-of-funds framework;
- team and contractor information;
- legal and compliance records;
- technical architecture and security information;
- token allocation, custody, vesting, and market-boundary context;
- material risks and dependencies;
- proposed financing and token-related terms.

Access should follow need, stage, and confidentiality. Sensitive credentials, private keys, customer data, source secrets, privileged advice, and unrelated personal records should remain outside ordinary investor access.

## 6. Financing Record

Each completed investment should have a controlled financing record.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Transaction identifier | Stable reference |
| Instrument | Approved financing structure |
| Party | Qualified investor or entity record |
| Commitment | Amount and currency under executed terms |
| Settlement | Payment asset, account or wallet route, date, and evidence |
| Closing conditions | Required approvals, documents, and completion status |
| Company record | Ownership, contractual, accounting, and governance treatment |
| Token schedule | Separate reference where token-related exposure exists |
| Restrictions | Confidentiality, transfer, lockup, or other continuing terms |
| Reporting | Internal owner and permitted public status |

Fundraising receipts should remain classified as financing according to the applicable accounting and legal treatment. They should not be recorded as product revenue or Platform Credit receipts.

The round can support approved execution categories such as:

- product engineering and launch;
- shared identity, payments, credits, AI, reporting, and administration capabilities;
- infrastructure, security, and technical review;
- product operations and user support;
- distribution, partnerships, and market entry;

## 8. Token-Related Exposure

Token-related exposure is an optional private component rather than an assumed feature of every investment.

Where included, the record should identify:

| Field | Required definition |
|---|---|
| Source | Approved allocation or other controlling token record |
| Maximum amount | FUZE covered by the private terms |
| Basis | Contractual relationship between financing and exposure |
| Effective event | Closing, vesting start, milestone, or another defined trigger |
| Schedule | Lock, vesting, claim, or staged-release conditions |
| Custody | Vault, contract, custodian, or verified destination |
| Transfer treatment | Restrictions, approvals, and permitted transfers |
| Cancellation | Treatment when closing conditions fail or rights terminate |
| Jurisdiction | Applicable restrictions and review |
| Reporting | Private detail and public aggregate category |

The financing instrument and token schedule should cross-reference each other without merging their ledgers.

---

## 9. Source and Allocation Control

Any FUZE amount connected to private terms must map to an approved token source.

The source review should confirm:

- allocation mandate;
- available and uncommitted balance;
- authority to grant the exposure;
- consistency with other commitments;
- vesting and release route;
- custody and reporting treatment.

The private round does not create a new token allocation by itself. A source outside the existing mandate would require the appropriate allocation and governance process.

Token release follows the [FUZE Vault-by-Vault Release Rules](15-FUZE_VAULT_BY_VAULT_RELEASE_RULES_PUBLIC.md).

---

## 10. Vesting and Lockups

A token schedule can use:

- a closing-based cliff;
- time-based vesting;
- milestone-based vesting;
- staged claimability;
- a post-release transfer lock;
- a hybrid of approved conditions.

The schedule should define dates or events, calculation precision, partial periods, termination, acceleration where expressly approved, dispute handling, and remaining unvested amounts.

Vested, released, transferable, and circulating are separate states. Private terms should use the same supply vocabulary as public reports.

No schedule should be inferred from the public existence of the round.

---

## 11. Custody and Settlement

Financing settlement and token custody require separate controls.

### Financing settlement

Can use an approved banking, stablecoin, or other supported route. The record should verify asset, amount, account or wallet, payer, transaction, fees, and treasury receipt.

### Token custody

Can use an allocation vault, vesting contract, custodian, or verified recipient wallet according to the private schedule.

Stablecoin settlement does not make the stablecoin amount product revenue or approved distributable value. Likewise, payment completion does not by itself release FUZE.

Credentials, wallet ownership mappings, banking information, and settlement evidence remain permissioned.

---

## 12. Transfer Treatment

Private financing rights and token-related exposure can have different transfer rules.

A proposed transfer should identify:

1. the right or asset being transferred;
2. contractual permission and required consent;
3. transferee qualification and jurisdiction;
4. remaining lock or vesting;
5. custody and destination verification;
6. effect on related rights and records;
7. reporting and effective date.

Receiving FUZE through a permitted transfer does not automatically transfer company rights, private information access, historical wallet status, or eligibility in another FUZE program.

| System | Relationship to the seed round |
|---|---|
| Community Participation Round | Separate allocation, audience, rules, and public process |
| Public Vault Access Window | Separate source, eligibility, pricing, and activation record |
| Platform Credits | Product-usage units purchased or granted under product terms |
| Product revenue | Revenue from products and services under its reporting basis |
| Approved distributable value | Separate reviewed value category where an active framework exists |
| Wallet-based participation | Separate activation and eligibility model |
| Liquidity and listing | Market-structure work independent of private financing terms |

A person can interact with more than one system, but each status should be established through its own evidence.

---

## 14. Amendments

Changes to financing or token terms should be documented through the authority and consent required by the governing agreements.

An amendment record should show:

- prior and revised term;
- reason and effective date;
- affected amount or schedule;
- investor and company approvals;
- legal, accounting, tax, custody, or token-ledger effect;
- downstream documents and reports updated.

An administrative correction should remain distinguishable from a commercial amendment.

---

## 15. Confidentiality and Privacy

Private round records can include investor identity, beneficial ownership, qualification evidence, financial information, negotiations, valuations, legal advice, tax information, signatures, bank details, and wallet ownership.

Access should be role-based, logged, time-bounded where appropriate, and revoked when no longer required.

Public reporting can use:

- round name and general purpose;
- current public status;
- aggregate financing progress where approved;
- broad use-of-funds categories;
- general investor or partner classes;
- aggregate token-related category information where appropriate.

Names, terms, allocations, or closing details should be published only when accurate, authorized, and suitable for public release.

## 16. Public Status Vocabulary

| Status | Public meaning |
|---|---|
| Preparing | Structure and materials are under development |
| Open to private discussions | FUZE is engaging selected qualified parties |
| In diligence | Authorized private review is underway |
| Commitments in process | Some parties are completing required decisions or documents |
| Initial closing completed | A completed closing can be publicly confirmed |
| Additional closing in process | Further private participation remains under review |
| Closed | The round has ended under the approved structure |
| Paused | FUZE has suspended new activity or completion |

Public status should avoid implying commitments, investors, amounts, or closing until those facts are supportable and approved for release.

---

## 17. Reporting and Reconciliation

Internal reporting should reconcile:

```text
Executed commitments
- unsettled or failed amounts
- refunds or reversals
= verified financing receipts
```

Token-related exposure should reconcile separately:

```text
Approved private exposure
= unvested
+ vested unreleased
+ released
+ cancelled or returned
+/- corrections
```

Reports should preserve currency and token units rather than combining them into one headline value without a stated valuation method.

---

## 18. Public Boundary

This paper announces no investment terms, valuation, allocation, closing amount, investor, token schedule, listing, liquidity, or return.

The Private Strategic Seed Round remains a qualified private process. Any token-related exposure depends on the final reviewed documents, source allocation, vesting, custody, transfer, jurisdiction, and release conditions.

Investor-specific risks are addressed in the [FUZE Investor Risk Disclosure](../INVESTOR-PARTNER-PAPERS/17-FUZE_INVESTOR_RISK_DISCLOSURE_PUBLIC.md), with token risks consolidated in [FUZE Token Risk Boundaries](29-FUZE_TOKEN_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md).

---

## Conclusion

FUZE keeps private financing, token-related exposure, product revenue, and public participation in separate records.

Qualification, controlled diligence, executed terms, verified settlement, allocation-backed token schedules, vesting, custody, transfer review, and public-safe status reporting allow strategic fundraising to support product execution without turning private terms into public token marketing.
