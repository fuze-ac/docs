# FUZE Liquidity and Listing Policy

## Executive Summary

FUZE treats liquidity and venue access as controlled market-structure work. The approved Liquidity & Market Operations allocation contains 30,000,000 FUZE, or 6.00% of the fixed supply, for authorized liquidity, venue, custody, and related operating needs.

The public direction is DEX-first where technical, legal, treasury, security, and operational readiness support deployment. Centralized exchange access may be evaluated later, but discussions, applications, technical review, and due diligence are preparation states rather than listing confirmation.

Token inventory and pairing capital are separate. Deploying FUZE to a pool or venue requires an approved amount from the liquidity allocation; stablecoin, ETH, or other paired assets require their own treasury source, budget, custody, and reconciliation.

This policy defines readiness, venue evaluation, deployment records, monitoring, market-integrity controls, incident response, withdrawal, and public status language. It does not announce a venue, pool, date, pair, market maker, or listing.

---

## 1. Policy Objective

The policy should make each market-structure action answer:

1. What venue or access function is being considered?
2. Which token and pairing assets are required?
3. Who approves and controls deployment?
4. Which technical, legal, custody, and security conditions apply?
5. How will depth, spread, slippage, concentration, and incidents be monitored?
6. What can be communicated publicly at each stage?
7. How are assets withdrawn, returned, and reconciled?

The objective is operational readiness and accurate communication. Market conditions and third-party venue decisions remain outside FUZE's direct control.

## 2. Approved Allocation

| Field | Approved value |
|---|---:|
| Allocation | Liquidity & Market Operations |
| FUZE amount | 30,000,000 |
| Share of fixed supply | 6.00% |
| Mandate | Controlled liquidity and market-structure preparation or operations |

The [FUZE Token Allocation Table](02-FUZE_TOKEN_ALLOCATION_TABLE_PUBLIC.md) controls the category amount and mandate.

Holding tokens in the allocation represents capacity. It does not mean those tokens have been committed, transferred, paired, deployed, listed, or classified as circulating.

---

## 3. Asset Separation

Market operations can involve several asset records.

| Component | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| FUZE inventory | Drawn from the approved liquidity allocation |
| Stablecoin pairing capital | Separately budgeted and approved treasury asset |
| ETH or native-asset pairing capital | Separately budgeted with network and volatility treatment |
| Venue fees | Treasury expense or deducted asset with explicit accounting |
| Service-provider funds | Governed by an approved agreement and custody record |
| Withdrawn or recovered assets | Returned to the appropriate liquidity or treasury custody |

A visible FUZE allocation balance provides no evidence that matching capital exists. Reports should identify token-side and pairing-side amounts separately.

Platform Credits remain product-usage records and never serve as liquidity-pool inventory.

## 4. Readiness Framework

### Token readiness

The verified contract, supply, decimals, metadata, allocation, custody, and official publication routes should be consistent.

### Technical readiness

Wallets, contracts, pools, integrations, deposit and withdrawal paths, monitoring, and recovery procedures should be tested for the intended scope.

### Security readiness

Key management, signers, contract review, destination verification, incident response, and authority replacement should be ready.

### Treasury readiness

FUZE inventory, pairing capital, fees, service costs, custody, limits, and accounting treatment should be approved.

### Legal and compliance readiness

The venue, jurisdiction, communications, counterparties, custody, and operating model should receive the required review.

### Documentation readiness

Token information, public links, risk materials, allocation records, technical references, and due-diligence responses should be current.

### Operations readiness

Named owners should cover deployment, monitoring, reconciliation, reporting, support, escalation, and withdrawal.

Readiness should be evaluated for each venue or deployment. Passing one venue's requirements does not establish readiness everywhere.

---

## 5. DEX-First Direction

FUZE can use decentralized exchange infrastructure as its first public market-access path when an approved deployment is ready.

A DEX plan should specify:

- chain and exchange protocol;
- official token contract and pair;
- pool type and fee tier where applicable;
- initial FUZE and pairing amounts;
- price-range or concentration parameters where applicable;
- custody and liquidity-position ownership;
- router, interface, and aggregator considerations;
- monitoring and rebalancing authority;
- withdrawal and emergency procedure;
- public verification links.

DEX deployment offers on-chain inspectability, but pool existence and usable liquidity are different facts. Depth, slippage, routing, interface visibility, arbitrage, volatility, gas costs, and smart-contract conditions affect execution.

FUZE should communicate DEX status only after the contract, pool, pair, and official links have been verified.

---

## 6. Possible Later CEX Evaluation

Centralized exchange access may be considered after product, technical, legal, compliance, custody, market, and operational factors support evaluation.

A venue review can cover:

- jurisdiction and customer reach;
- licensing and compliance posture;
- custody and wallet integration;
- deposit and withdrawal support;
- supported networks and confirmation rules;
- market pair options;
- technical and security requirements;
- liquidity expectations;
- fees and ongoing obligations;
- reporting, incident, and delisting procedures;
- strategic value and operating burden.

FUZE may prepare information, enter discussions, submit materials, answer due diligence, or complete integration work. Final acceptance, timing, pairs, custody availability, and continued support remain venue decisions.

Public language should use the exact current state rather than compressing these steps into “listed.”

## 7. Venue Status Vocabulary

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Researching | FUZE is assessing venue fit and requirements |
| Preparing | Documentation, technical, custody, or operating work is underway |
| In discussion | Confidential or public communication with a venue has begun |
| Submitted | Required materials have been delivered |
| Under review | The venue is evaluating the request or integration |
| Technically ready | FUZE-side integration conditions are complete |
| Approved | The venue has provided an approval that can be publicly confirmed |
| Scheduled | A venue-approved activation time can be announced |
| Live | Supported trading or market access is operational and verified |
| Paused | Trading, deposits, withdrawals, or liquidity support is interrupted |
| Ended | Venue or deployment support has closed |

Confidentiality can limit disclosure of intermediate states. Silence should not be interpreted as confirmation or rejection.

## 8. Deployment Proposal

Every liquidity deployment should have an approved proposal containing:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Deployment ID | Stable operating reference |
| Purpose | DEX pool, venue support, routing, custody, or another approved function |
| Venue and network | Protocol, exchange, custodian, chain, and contract references |
| FUZE amount | Source-vault amount and remaining allocation capacity |
| Pairing capital | Asset, amount, treasury source, and conversion basis |
| Parameters | Pair, range, fee, term, limits, or service conditions |
| Custody | Wallet, contract, position token, exchange account, or provider |
| Authority | Proposal, approval, signing, monitoring, and withdrawal roles |
| Risks | Technical, custody, counterparty, market, legal, and operational issues |
| Reporting | Public and permissioned fields, cadence, and evidence |
| Exit | Pause, withdrawal, termination, and recovery procedure |

Approval should occur before material assets move.

---

## 9. Execution

The operator should:

1. verify token, network, venue, pair, and destination;
2. confirm approved FUZE and pairing balances;
3. test the execution path where appropriate;
4. execute through the authorized wallet, contract, custodian, or venue;
5. capture transactions, position identifiers, and custody evidence;
6. update allocation and treasury ledgers;
7. record deployed, committed, returned, and fee amounts;
8. verify public links before publication.

Execution records should identify whether FUZE remains controlled, sits in a pool, is held by a service provider, is available in an order book, or has returned to treasury custody.

The [FUZE Vault-by-Vault Release Rules](15-FUZE_VAULT_BY_VAULT_RELEASE_RULES_PUBLIC.md) governs the allocation release packet.

---

## 10. Monitoring

Monitoring should match the deployment type.

### DEX measures

Can include pool reserves, depth, slippage, price range, fee accrual, concentration, impermanent-loss exposure, router access, contract events, and abnormal transactions.

### CEX measures

Can include deposit and withdrawal health, order-book depth, spread, volume quality, custody status, service incidents, pair availability, and venue notices.

### Cross-venue measures

Can include price dispersion, routing, fragmentation, arbitrage conditions, source reliability, stablecoin conditions, and network congestion.

### Operational measures

Can include signer availability, reconciliation status, provider obligations, alert response, reporting freshness, and open incidents.

Monitoring informs decisions; it does not ensure a specific depth, spread, price, or volume.

---

## 11. Market Integrity

FUZE market operations should avoid conduct intended to create a false appearance of demand, volume, depth, or price.

Controls should address:

- wash or self-dealing patterns;
- spoofed or deceptive orders;
- artificial volume;
- undisclosed price-support arrangements;
- misleading listing or venue statements;
- misuse of confidential venue information;
- promotional pressure tied to unconfirmed events;
- conflicts among treasury, operators, providers, and communicators.

Service-provider agreements should define permitted activity, reporting, conflicts, custody, termination, and compliance obligations.

AIMM can assist with market-operation analysis and reporting, and QTB can assist with market interpretation. Their outputs remain decision support and require accountable human review.

---

## 12. Custody

Market assets can be held through:

- FUZE-controlled wallets;
- multisignature custody;
- liquidity-pool contracts;
- centralized exchange accounts;
- qualified custodians;
- approved service-provider structures.

Each arrangement should document legal or beneficial ownership, transaction authority, withdrawal rights, account permissions, reconciliation evidence, and incident contacts.

Exchange custody can combine customer or project balances in omnibus structures. On-chain visibility may therefore be insufficient to determine beneficial ownership or venue liabilities.

Private keys, credentials, recovery material, detailed signer identity, and sensitive security procedures remain restricted.

---

## 13. Rebalancing and Additional Deployment

Changing a pool range, pair, venue, provider, amount, or custody arrangement can alter risk and reporting.

The operator should document:

- reason for the change;
- assets withdrawn and received;
- fees, conversion, or value differences;
- new parameters and destination;
- authority and effective time;
- resulting allocation, custody, and circulation treatment.

Additional deployment requires available approved capacity. Prior deployment performance does not create automatic authority for expansion.

---

## 14. Pause, Withdrawal, and Termination

FUZE can pause or withdraw activity after:

- a contract, custody, venue, oracle, bridge, or network incident;
- loss of reliable monitoring or reconciliation;
- a legal, compliance, market-integrity, or counterparty concern;
- abnormal market conditions beyond approved operating limits;
- expiry or breach of a provider arrangement;
- a governance or treasury decision.

The response can include suspending new deployment, withdrawing liquidity, cancelling orders, restricting authority, moving assets to recovery custody, or ending support.

Withdrawal records should reconcile FUZE, pairing capital, fees, losses, recovered assets, and outstanding provider balances. Returned FUZE retains the Liquidity & Market Operations mandate unless formally reclassified.

---

## 15. Public Reporting

Public reporting can include:

- allocation and deployed FUZE amounts;
- pairing-asset class and amount where approved;
- verified DEX pool or live venue references;
- deployment, withdrawal, and status dates;
- custody or contract class;
- aggregate depth, reserve, or position information;
- governance and transaction references;
- pauses, incidents, and corrections at a public-safe level;
- report methodology and cutoff.

Reports should protect confidential exchange discussions, applications, fee terms, service-provider agreements, treasury strategy, credentials, and security procedures.

Only a venue-approved and verified status should be described as approved, scheduled, or live.

---

## 16. Communication Boundaries

FUZE's market-access direction is DEX-first where ready, with possible later CEX evaluation. Neither path establishes timing, approval, continuous liquidity, market depth, resale capacity, or a particular price.

A liquidity allocation is operational inventory rather than a promise to support the market. Pairing capital, venue participation, and provider support each require separate evidence.

Price and demand communication is governed by [FUZE Market Price and Demand Boundary](22-FUZE_MARKET_PRICE_AND_DEMAND_BOUNDARY_PUBLIC.md). Consolidated token risks remain in [FUZE Token Risk Boundaries](29-FUZE_TOKEN_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md).

---

## Conclusion

FUZE liquidity and listing work begins with verified readiness and ends with reconciled assets and accurate status reporting.

Separating token inventory from pairing capital, using venue-specific approvals, preserving custody evidence, monitoring deployed positions, enforcing market integrity, and using careful DEX-first / possible-CEX-later language support credible market-structure operations without converting preparation into a public promise.
