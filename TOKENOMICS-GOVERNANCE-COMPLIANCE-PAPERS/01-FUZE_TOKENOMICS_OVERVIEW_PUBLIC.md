- allocation;

## Tokenomics Principles

### Product Utility Leads

FUZE tokenomics should connect with useful products and ecosystem functions rather than rely on token activity alone.

Before stronger token utility is represented as active, the relevant product or ecosystem function should have:

- a defined user;
- a defined action;
- a defined token condition;
- a clear product or ecosystem result;
- a supported network and contract;
- permission and authority controls;
- security and privacy review;
- operating support;
- reporting;
- correction; and
- an activation record.

Product activity does not automatically create token utility.

Token utility does not automatically create token demand, liquidity, price support, revenue, payout, or financial return.

### One Token, Distinct Functions

FUZE uses one ecosystem token for approved token functions.

A one-token model does not mean every product uses the token in the same way or that every product requires a wallet.

Each utility or participation surface should define its own:

- purpose;
- eligible users;
- token condition;
- wallet behavior;
- duration;
- authority;
- records;
- risks;
- status; and
- correction route.

The [FUZE One Token Model](03-FUZE_ONE_TOKEN_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) is the primary paper for this architecture.

### Supply Has Approved Purposes

The full fixed supply is assigned across ten approved categories.

Each category has a purpose and should be governed according to that purpose rather than treated as interchangeable inventory.

A movement from one category, vault, reserve, recipient class, or program to another requires the applicable authority and record.

### Allocation and Circulation Are Different

The following are different states:

- allocated;
- assigned to custody;
- reserved;
- subject to release conditions;
- authorized;
- released;
- technically unlocked;
- transferred;
- distributed;
- restricted after distribution;
- circulating;
- held for liquidity operations;
- used in a product or program;
- claimed;
- paid out;
- returned;
- corrected;
- suspended; and
- retired.

A headline allocation table should not be read as a circulating-supply table.

### Controls Precede Sensitive Actions

More consequential token actions require stronger controls.

Controls may include:

- purpose verification;
- recipient-class verification;
- governance approval;
- multisig approval;
- timelock;
- legal and compliance review;
- accounting and tax review;
- treasury review;
- technical review;
- contract review;
- security review;
- sanctions or eligibility review where applicable;
- reporting readiness;
- reconciliation;
- incident handling;
- and public communication.

### Records Should Explain Movement

A token movement should be understandable through its records.

Where applicable, records should identify:

- allocation category;
- source vault or address;
- destination;
- network;
- contract;
- amount;
- authority;
- purpose;
- release condition;
- transaction reference;
- restriction or vesting state;
- accounting or program classification;
- reporting period;
- correction state;
- and current status.

A transaction hash proves that a blockchain transaction occurred.

It does not by itself explain the business purpose, authority, legal treatment, accounting treatment, recipient eligibility, circulation status, or product result.

### Market Outcomes Remain External

Tokenomics can define supply, purpose, utility, controls, reporting, and readiness.

It cannot guarantee:

- market demand;
- exchange approval;
- market access;
- liquidity;
- depth;
- spread;
- execution;
- price stability;
- price appreciation;
- trading volume;
- holder return;
- or financial outcome.

## Allocation-State Model

Each allocation may move through several states.

| State | Meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Approved allocation | Supply is assigned to a named purpose. | Release, ownership, or circulation |
| Custody assigned | Supply is held in an approved vault, reserve, contract, or address. | Recipient entitlement or release |
| Conditions defined | Release, vesting, eligibility, or program requirements are documented. | Satisfaction of those conditions |
| Ready for authorization | Required reviews and records are prepared. | Approval |
| Authorized | The applicable authority approves the stated action. | Execution |
| Released | The approved amount is made available under the stated process. | Free circulation or end-user ownership |
| Unlocked | A technical or contractual restriction is removed or expires. | Transfer, distribution, sale, or circulation |
| Transferred | Tokens move between addresses. | Purpose, eligibility, or circulation classification |
| Distributed | Tokens are delivered to an intended recipient or program. | Free transferability, market activity, or active utility |
| Restricted after distribution | The recipient has tokens subject to remaining restrictions. | Circulating status |
| Circulating | Tokens meet the stated circulation methodology. | Liquidity, demand, price, or market depth |
| Program-active | Tokens are being used in an approved utility, participation, migration, incentive, partner, or market-operations program. | Product success or financial return |
| Suspended | Further action is temporarily stopped. | Cancellation or retirement |
| Corrected | A prior classification, transfer, amount, or record has been corrected. | Erasure of the original history |
| Closed or retired | The program or mechanism has ended under the applicable process. | Removal of required historical records |

The [FUZE Token Release and Circulation Clarity](13-FUZE_TOKEN_RELEASE_AND_CIRCULATION_CLARITY_PUBLIC.md) is the primary paper for these distinctions.

## Token Lifecycle

### 1. Allocation

Supply is assigned to one approved category.

The allocation record should identify:

- category;
- approved amount;
- approved share;
- purpose;
- owner or responsible authority;
- custody plan;
- release principles;
- reporting method;
- and current status.

### 2. Custody

Allocated supply is held through an approved:

- vault;
- reserve;
- multisig;
- timelock;
- contract;
- custodian;
- or other controlled arrangement.

Custody records should identify, where appropriate:

- network;
- address;
- contract;
- signers or roles in permissioned records;
- approval threshold;
- timelock;
- key-management responsibility;
- monitoring;
- incident route;
- and public label.

Public reporting should not expose private keys, recovery material, exploitable signer details, or sensitive security procedures.

### 3. Readiness

Before movement, the responsible team confirms the applicable prerequisites.

These may include:

- documented purpose;
- recipient class;
- approved amount;
- release or vesting condition;
- eligibility method;
- legal and compliance review;
- accounting and tax review;
- treasury review;
- technical and security readiness;
- contract readiness;
- wallet or claim readiness;
- reporting readiness;
- reconciliation capability;
- support;
- and correction procedures.

Readiness is not authorization.

### 4. Authorization

The applicable governance, treasury, multisig, timelock, contract, or approved operational authority authorizes the stated action.

The authorization should identify:

- purpose;
- source allocation;
- source custody;
- amount;
- destination;
- recipient class;
- timing;
- restriction;
- required confirmations;
- reporting treatment;
- expiration where applicable;
- and approving authority.

Authorization is not execution.

### 5. Release or Execution

The approved action is executed.

The record may include:

- transaction hash;
- block or timestamp;
- network;
- source;
- destination;
- amount;
- contract event;
- release status;
- restriction status;
- program reference;
- and exception state.

Submitted, pending, confirmed, finalized, settled, reconciled, reversed, and corrected are different states.

### 6. Restricted Use, Distribution, or Circulation

After release, tokens may:

- remain in controlled custody;
- remain subject to vesting;
- move to a program contract;
- be distributed to an eligible recipient;
- support a utility surface;
- support a migration;
- support an incentive program;
- support a partnership program;
- support approved liquidity operations;
- or become circulating under the stated methodology.

The correct state should be reported rather than inferred.

### 7. Utility or Program Activity

Tokens may support approved:

- product access;
- ecosystem participation;
- community programs;
- game functions;
- contribution recognition;
- partner programs;
- governance input where defined;
- migration;
- incentive programs;
- claims;
- payouts;
- or market-operations functions.

Each mechanism requires its own rules, activation, controls, evidence, and current status.

### 8. Reconciliation and Reporting

Reports compare:

- approved allocation;
- opening balance;
- authorized movement;
- executed transaction;
- closing balance;
- restricted amount;
- released amount;
- distributed amount;
- circulating classification;
- program use;
- claim or payout state where applicable;
- correction;
- and unresolved exception.

### 9. Review, Correction, Suspension, or Closure

Review may result in:

- continued operation;
- correction;
- revised rule;
- changed custody;
- changed reporting classification;
- suspension;
- remediation;
- migration;
- closure;
- or retirement.

Historical records should remain traceable.

## Governance and Approval Architecture

Token operations should use proportionate authority and separation of duties.

| Control area | Core question |
|---|---|
| Allocation authority | Which approved category permits the action? |
| Purpose | What approved use is being performed? |
| Custody | Where are the tokens held and under whose authority? |
| Approval | Which role, multisig, timelock, governance process, or contract authorizes the action? |
| Timing | Are release, vesting, eligibility, or access conditions satisfied? |
| Recipient | Is the recipient or recipient class eligible? |
| Technical readiness | Are network, contract, wallet, monitoring, and recovery controls ready? |
| Security | Are signing, custody, contract, provider, and incident risks controlled? |
| Legal and compliance | Has the action received proportionate review? |
| Accounting and tax | Is the action classified and recorded appropriately? |
| Reconciliation | Do balances, transactions, and approved instructions agree? |
| Reporting | What can public and permissioned readers verify? |
| Exception handling | How are errors, disputes, incidents, reversals, and corrections handled? |

### Multisig

A multisig can reduce single-person control by requiring multiple approvals.

It does not replace:

- policy;
- purpose verification;
- signer accountability;
- transaction review;
- monitoring;

- accounting review;
- or incident response.

### Timelock

A timelock can provide review time before execution.

It does not make an action correct, lawful, secure, or appropriate by itself.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE has a fixed supply of **500,000,000 FUZE** across ten approved allocation categories totaling **100.00%**.
- Allocation identifies purpose and does not by itself establish release, unlock, distribution, circulation, liquidity, or ownership.
- FUZE token, Platform Credits, stablecoins, wallet records, claims, payouts, product revenue, and market access serve different functions.
- Every token movement should have purpose, authority, custody, transaction, classification, reconciliation, reporting, and correction evidence appropriate to the action.
- Product-connected utility should be separately specified, reviewed, tested, activated, measured, and capable of being paused or retired.
- Community participation, BOARD / Surfboard migration, and wallet-based participation are distinct mechanisms.
- Foundation Reserve, Treasury Reserve, and Transparency / Stability Reserve are separate categories with separate purposes and controls.
- Liquidity & Market Operations allocation does not prove active liquidity, listing, market depth, or price support.
- Public market direction is DEX-first, while any later CEX access remains conditional and externally dependent.
- Public transparency should provide useful address-based and aggregate evidence without exposing private identities, credentials, security details, or confidential terms.
- Fixed supply, allocation, custody, utility, participation, claims, payouts, market access, liquidity, price, revenue, and financial return must not be treated as interchangeable.
