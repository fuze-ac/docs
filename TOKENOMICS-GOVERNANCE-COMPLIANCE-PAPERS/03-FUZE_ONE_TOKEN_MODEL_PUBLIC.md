# FUZE One Token Model

## Executive Summary

FUZE maintains **FUZE token** as the sole public ecosystem token issued and governed under the FUZE tokenomics model.

The one-token model gives products, users, contributors, communities, partners, wallets, custody providers, bridges, reporting systems, and public communications one canonical token identity to reference.

It prevents token identity from becoming fragmented across:

- product-specific tokens;
- community tokens;
- participation tokens;
- claim tokens;
- governance tokens;
- reward tokens;
- partner-issued lookalikes;
- transferable product credits;
- wrapped or bridged forms described as new supply;
- receipts presented as independent FUZE assets; or
- game values presented as externally redeemable ecosystem tokens.

One token does not mean one undifferentiated system.

The following remain separate from FUZE token:

- Platform Credits used for defined product consumption;
- stablecoins used for payment, settlement, treasury, refund, and compensation workflows;
- product accounts and permissions;
- loyalty points and badges;
- game values and simulated economies;
- eligibility and claim records;
- invoices and revenue records;
- wallet addresses and identity records;
- liquidity-pool positions;
- third-party derivatives; and
- internal accounting entries.

A non-token record remains outside the FUZE token layer only when its behavior, rights, transferability, redemption, naming, supply, custody, and public communication remain consistent with that classification.

A label such as “credit,” “point,” “receipt,” “share,” “reward,” “badge,” or “access unit” does not prevent an instrument from functioning like a second token.

Every new digital unit, right, representation, receipt, wrapped form, partner unit, or product mechanism should pass a classification review before public release.

The governing rule is:

```text
map approved token functions to FUZE token
or keep the mechanism clearly non-token
or submit the proposed exception to full governance and specialist review
```

The one-token model does not require every FUZE product user to hold FUZE token.

It does not require every product feature to use a wallet.

It does not make every product action token utility.

It does not guarantee token demand, market access, liquidity, price, revenue, payout, or financial return.

## Core Policy

FUZE token is the only FUZE-issued public ecosystem token unless a future exception receives formal system-level approval and is published with complete migration, supply, rights, custody, risk, and communication treatment.

A product, program, community, event, game, campaign, participation mechanism, partner integration, claim process, or governance process should not create a second FUZE-issued public token merely because it needs:

- access control;
- usage accounting;
- loyalty;
- recognition;
- voting or signaling;
- game progression;
- contribution tracking;
- payment;
- settlement;
- claims;
- rewards;
- receipts;
- or reporting.

Those needs may be met through:

- FUZE token where an approved token function is appropriate;
- Platform Credits;
- stablecoins;
- product accounts;
- permissions;
- internal records;
- badges;
- non-transferable points;
- game-state values;
- eligibility records;
- claim records;
- invoices;
- or another clearly classified non-token mechanism.

The chosen mechanism should be the simplest one that satisfies the product and control requirements.

## Why One Token

### User Comprehension

Users can identify which asset represents approved FUZE token functions.

They do not need to compare multiple FUZE-issued public assets with overlapping:

- utility;
- participation;
- access;
- governance;
- claims;
- rewards;
- supply;
- market status;
- or risk.

### Product Coherence

Product teams can build against one token identity while deciding separately whether a product feature needs:

- no token interaction;
- read-only wallet verification;
- a signature;
- a token condition;
- an onchain transaction;
- a claim process;
- or another approved token action.

### Supply Integrity

One-token architecture allows all approved token supply to reconcile to the fixed supply of:

- **500,000,000 FUZE**; and
- the ten approved allocation categories totaling **100.00%**.

Product credits, stablecoins, loyalty points, receipts, game values, and internal records remain outside the fixed FUZE supply ledger.

### Custody and Infrastructure Clarity

Wallets, custodians, bridges, explorers, indexers, products, liquidity systems, and market venues can reference one canonical token identity and approved representations of that identity.

### Governance and Communication

A proposal can state clearly whether it affects:

- FUZE token;
- a non-token product system;
- an external payment asset;
- a third-party instrument;
- an approved representation of FUZE token;
- or a proposed exception requiring governance review.

### Reduced Hidden Fragmentation

A one-token model reduces the risk that multiple instruments compete for:

- user attention;
- product utility;
- wallet support;
- liquidity;
- public identity;
- governance meaning;
- partner integration;
- and reporting authority.

These benefits depend on disciplined implementation.

A second token can be created in substance even when it is not called a token.

The identity record should include, where current and approved:

1. token name;
2. token symbol;
3. canonical network;
4. canonical contract address;
5. technical standard;
6. decimals;
7. total supply relationship;
8. deployment status;
9. ownership or administrative status;
10. pause, upgrade, mint, burn, bridge, or migration capabilities where applicable;
11. authoritative publication source;
12. contract version;
13. superseded contract or representation references;
14. security and governance references;
15. current status; and
16. current-as-of date.

A contract address should not be published as canonical before the applicable deployment, verification, security, governance, and activation records are complete.

A token with a similar name or symbol is not FUZE token unless it appears in the authoritative token-identity record.

### Canonical Supply

The canonical economic supply is governed by the approved fixed-supply model.

A bridge, wrapped representation, exchange balance, custodial balance, omnibus balance, application balance, or receipt should not be represented as additional FUZE supply when it corresponds to FUZE held, locked, burned, custodied, or otherwise accounted for elsewhere.

The relationship should be reconcilable.

### Official and Supported Representations

A representation may be:

- official and active;
- official but paused;
- supported but third-party operated;
- recognized but not recommended;
- legacy;
- deprecated;
- unsupported;
- disputed;
- or fraudulent.

These states should not be treated as equivalent.

## Systems That Remain Separate

| System | Primary purpose | Why it is not FUZE token | Main control boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform Credits | Meter or package supported product consumption | Product-use ledger units rather than public transferable ecosystem supply | Purchase, grant, reservation, consumption, refund, expiry, and correction rules |
| Stablecoins | Payment, settlement, treasury, refund, vendor, sponsor, partner, or compensation activity | External payment assets with separate issuers, contracts, risks, and supply | Payment, network, custody, settlement, reconciliation, and risk controls |
| Product permissions | Control access to features, data, workspaces, or actions | Authorization state rather than transferable value | Role, scope, approval, expiry, revocation, and audit records |
| Loyalty points | Recognize bounded program activity | Program records with limited scope and no independent public-token claim | Earning, expiry, transfer, redemption, abuse, and closure rules |
| Badges and credentials | Represent recognition, completion, status, or contribution | Evidence or profile records rather than ecosystem supply | Issuer, evidence, revocation, correction, and public-label rules |
| Game values | Support score, progression, resources, property, simulated economies, or game decisions | Product mechanics defined by game rules | Persistence, transfer, redemption, anti-abuse, economy, and public-language controls |
| Eligibility records | Record status under an approved process | Evidence of eligibility rather than a transferable participation asset | Source, period, authority, expiry, appeal, and correction |
| Claim records | Record a request under an approved process | Workflow evidence rather than a second claim token | Eligibility, submission, review, approval, rejection, and correction |
| Invoices and revenue records | Document commercial activity | Accounting and transaction evidence rather than token ownership | Offer, payment, fulfillment, revenue, refund, and reconciliation |
| Wallet records | Identify addresses, signatures, transactions, or snapshots | Address-based evidence rather than legal identity or a new asset | Network, ownership proof, privacy, status, and correction |
| Internal accounting units | Support operational or financial ledgers | Internal classifications with no public transferable asset identity | Ledger authority, reconciliation, audit, and reporting |

A system remains separate only while its actual behavior matches its classification.

## Classification Test

Before FUZE introduces a new unit, right, receipt, credential, wrapped form, partner instrument, or digital representation, the responsible owner should complete the following classification test.

### 1. Identity Test

- Does the unit use FUZE branding, symbol, visual identity, or ecosystem language?
- Is it described as a token, coin, asset, share, reward, claim, receipt, governance unit, or ownership unit?
- Could a reasonable user mistake it for FUZE token?
- Could a partner, wallet, explorer, market venue, or media outlet list it as a FUZE ecosystem token?
- Does the public name imply official FUZE issuance or endorsement?

### 2. Transferability Test

- Can the unit move between users, accounts, contracts, or wallets?
- Can it leave the product environment?
- Can it be bridged, wrapped, pooled, deposited, or withdrawn?
- Can a secondary market support it?
- Can the issuer freeze, reverse, mint, burn, or restrict transfers?

### 3. Value and Redemption Test

- Can the unit be purchased or sold?
- Can it be redeemed for money, stablecoins, FUZE token, goods, services, claims, payouts, or financial value?
- Can its exchange value change independently?
- Is it linked to revenue, treasury assets, market price, profit, yield, or another financial result?
- Can holders reasonably expect external value from the efforts of FUZE or another party?

### 4. Rights Test

- Does the unit grant access?
- Does it grant governance, signaling, voting, participation, claim, distribution, compensation, redemption, ownership, or profit rights?
- Are those rights already associated with FUZE token or another approved record?
- Are the rights temporary, revocable, non-transferable, or jurisdiction-dependent?

### 5. Supply and Issuance Test

- Who creates or issues the unit?
- Is supply fixed, capped, expandable, algorithmic, user-generated, or provider-controlled?
- Which ledger or contract records supply?
- Is the unit backed by FUZE token or another asset?
- Does issuance create economic exposure beyond the approved FUZE supply?

### 6. Custody and Control Test

- Where is the unit held?
- Who controls issuance, transfer, redemption, pause, freeze, or recovery?
- Which contract, account, custodian, bridge, or provider is involved?
- What happens if the operator fails?

### 7. Market and External-Support Test

- Can wallets display it as an asset?
- Can explorers track it as a token?
- Can venues list or trade it?
- Can third parties build derivatives or pools around it?
- Could public markets interpret it as an official FUZE asset?

### 8. Communication Test

- How will the unit be described in product interfaces, public papers, support material, and partner communications?
- Is its issuer clear?
- Is its relationship to FUZE token clear?
- Are transfer, redemption, expiry, revocation, and market limitations clear?
- What prevents the unit from being presented as a competing FUZE token?

### 9. Legal, Accounting, Tax, and Compliance Test

- What legal or regulatory characterization may apply?
- How is it treated for accounting and tax purposes?
- Does it create payment, securities, gaming, consumer, data, sanctions, or financial-promotion considerations?
- Which jurisdictions and users are affected?

### 10. Exit and Correction Test

- Can the mechanism be paused, corrected, migrated, redeemed, deprecated, or retired?
- What happens to users and balances?
- How are disputes, lost access, duplicate records, and provider failures handled?
- How is public communication corrected?

## Classification Outcomes

After review, the proposed mechanism should be classified as one of the following.

| Outcome | Meaning | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| FUZE-token utility | The function belongs to FUZE token and passes the applicable utility and activation gates. | Use the canonical FUZE token identity and specialist utility controls |
| Approved FUZE representation | The unit represents underlying FUZE through a bridge, wrapper, custody, or another reconciled mechanism. | Publish backing, custody, mint and burn, redemption, risk, and status rules |
| Non-token product record | The mechanism is a bounded account, permission, credit, badge, point, game value, eligibility, or internal record. | Preserve non-transferability, bounded rights, naming, ledger, and public boundaries |
| External asset | The mechanism belongs to another issuer or protocol. | Identify the issuer and avoid implying official FUZE issuance or equivalence |
| Third-party derivative or receipt | The unit represents a position, exposure, pool, claim, or derivative created by another system. | State that it is not FUZE token and publish applicable risk and issuer information |
| Rejected or redesigned | The mechanism creates unnecessary fragmentation, risk, confusion, or duplication. | Remove, redesign, or return to a simpler non-token mechanism |
| Governance exception required | The mechanism may create a second FUZE-issued public token or materially change token identity. | Complete system-level governance, legal, supply, custody, migration, market, and public review before proceeding |

If classification remains uncertain, the mechanism should not be publicly launched as an official FUZE asset.

## Wrapped, Bridged, and Custodied Representations

Technical and custody systems may create representations of FUZE token.

- a wrapped representation backed by locked FUZE;
- a custody account balance;
- an exchange account balance;
- an omnibus application balance;
- a deposit receipt;
- a liquidity-pool receipt;
- or another tokenized position containing FUZE.

These representations require exact classification.

### Approved Representation Requirements

An approved FUZE representation should identify:

1. canonical underlying FUZE source;
2. network;
3. representation contract or custody system;
4. operator;
5. custody or locking method;
6. mint and burn, credit and debit, or deposit and withdrawal controls;
7. backing ratio;
8. reconciliation method;
9. redemption or withdrawal method;
10. fees;
11. confirmation and finality rules;
12. pause and emergency controls;
13. upgrade and migration controls;
14. security review;
15. bridge, custody, provider, counterparty, smart-contract, and liquidity risks;
16. public naming;
17. status;
18. deprecation treatment; and
19. current-as-of date.

### Supply Integrity

An approved representation should not be counted as additional economic supply when it corresponds to canonical FUZE locked, burned, custodied, or otherwise removed from independent circulation under the representation mechanism.

Reports should reconcile:

```text
underlying FUZE backing
<-> issued FUZE representation
<-> pending deposits and withdrawals
<-> fees, exceptions, corrections, and paused balances
```

A mismatch should be treated as an exception or incident.

### Wrapped and Bridged Naming

Public naming should state:

- that the representation is not a separate FUZE-issued ecosystem token;
- which network it uses;
- which operator or bridge is responsible;
- what backs it;
- how it is redeemed;
- and its current status.

### Custodial Balances

A custodial account balance is a record against the custodian or provider.

It is not necessarily an onchain token held directly by the user.

Custody, withdrawal, insolvency, access, verification, and counterparty risks should be disclosed appropriately.

### Liquidity-Pool Receipts and Derivatives

A liquidity-pool token, vault share, lending receipt, derivative, or third-party exposure that includes FUZE is not FUZE token.

Its issuer, underlying assets, rights, redemption, risks, and status should be stated separately.

FUZE should not describe a third-party derivative as an official FUZE token without explicit approval.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE token is the sole FUZE-issued public ecosystem token under the current architecture.
- One token does not mean every product requires a wallet or token interaction.
- Platform Credits, stablecoins, permissions, loyalty points, badges, game values, eligibility records, claims, invoices, and internal ledgers remain separate systems.
- Classification depends on behavior, rights, transferability, redemption, supply, custody, and communication—not the label alone.
- A new token-like mechanism should map to FUZE token, remain clearly non-token, be classified as an external or third-party asset, or undergo full governance review.
- Wrapped, bridged, and custodied representations require backing, reconciliation, redemption, risk, status, and naming controls.
- A representation of FUZE token should not be counted as additional economic supply when it is backed by canonical FUZE under an approved mechanism.
- Partner branding, wallet display, technical deployment, or market listing does not independently create an official FUZE token.
- Contract migration and representation deprecation require supply reconciliation, user treatment, support, public communication, and registry updates.
- Canonical token identity, product utility, Platform Credits, payments, claims, market access, liquidity, price, revenue, and financial return must not be treated as interchangeable.
