# FUZE Enterprise and SME Expansion

## Executive Summary

FUZE can serve small and medium-sized businesses and larger enterprises through the same product families, but the route to adoption differs. SMEs usually need a fast start, understandable packages, mobile-friendly workflows, practical templates, and accessible support. Enterprises require structured discovery, procurement, security and data review, integration ownership, governed workspaces, acceptance criteria, and accountable rollout.

Expansion should begin with one operational problem and one bounded product deployment. ShopOS AI can address shop administration; SheetLayer AI can organize existing spreadsheet work; TrainLayer AI can prepare learning material; CommunityLayer AI can support community operations; AIE can assist event teams; and Botmad can perform approved work tasks. Other products enter when their audience and controls fit the deployment.

This paper defines the expansion model from qualification through onboarding, pilot, evidence review, rollout, renewal, and offboarding. It also explains packaging, partner implementation, permissions, support, and the evidence investors should expect before a pilot is presented as a repeatable business channel.

---

## 1. Expansion Objective

The objective is to turn a useful FUZE workflow into an operating customer deployment that can be supported and repeated.

The expansion process should identify:

1. customer segment and buyer;
2. operational problem;
3. initial product and scope;
4. users, roles, and data;
5. implementation owner;
6. commercial package;
7. onboarding and support route;
8. acceptance and success evidence;
9. expansion or closure decision.

Expansion is product-specific. A successful shop pilot does not establish enterprise readiness for an unrelated market-intelligence workflow.

---

## 2. SME and Enterprise Profiles

| Area | SME pattern | Enterprise pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner, founder, operator, or small-team manager | Business sponsor, department owner, procurement, or executive |
| Decision process | Direct and relatively short | Multi-role review and formal approval |
| Initial scope | One shop, team, campaign, or workflow | Department, business unit, controlled user group, or integration |
| Setup | Guided self-service or assisted onboarding | Planned implementation with owners and acceptance tests |
| Packaging | Starter plan, credits, add-on, fixed package | Contracted workspace, seats, service scope, implementation |
| Data review | Practical permission and import checks | Data inventory, security, privacy, retention, and vendor review |
| Integration | Sheets, QR, devices, exports, simple connections | APIs, identity, internal systems, devices, and change control |
| Support | Tutorials, chat, setup assistance | Named contacts, escalation, incident and service process |
| Reporting | Operational summaries | Management, audit, service, usage, and control reporting |
| Expansion | More features, staff, or locations | More teams, business units, systems, regions, or products |

These patterns guide design; individual customers can require a mix. A regulated small business can have enterprise-level controls, while a limited enterprise team can begin with a simple package.

## 3. Product Entry Patterns

FUZE should lead with the product that best matches the customer’s immediate problem.

### Shop operations

ShopOS AI can provide the central operating workflow, with SheetLayer AI, SpeakShop AI, or TrainLayer AI added for data, announcements, and staff learning. The initial scope might be one menu, queue, reporting, or operator process rather than the full shop system.

### Spreadsheet-heavy operations

SheetLayer AI can enter through a current spreadsheet and a specific task such as mapping fields, preparing a dashboard draft, or organizing a report. This reduces migration pressure and makes the source and output easy to review.

### Training and knowledge

TrainLayer AI can start with one approved source set and one guide, quiz, onboarding module, or internal learning package. Version ownership and reviewer authority become more important as the audience grows.

### Community and support

CommunityLayer AI can begin with a recurring summary, support-draft set, admin report, or moderation-assistance workflow. The customer retains policy and member-impacting authority.

### Events and campaigns

AIE and SpeakShop AI can support planning, briefings, announcements, recaps, and sponsor reporting. ToolGrid AI can provide a separate reviewed discovery or sponsored-placement service.

### Work assistance

Botmad can enter through a bounded task with approved sources, output, reviewer, and action limits. Wider desktop authority follows only after permissions and support evidence mature.

### Specialized Web3 operations

QTB and AIMM can serve qualified research or operations teams through controlled reports and workspaces. Their market context, source discipline, access restrictions, and review requirements make broad self-service entry less suitable for early deployment.

ZAGA products use their own game and community onboarding path. Enterprise or SME involvement can occur through events, community programs, services, or approved integrations rather than treating game participation as a general business workflow.

## 4. SME Adoption Playbook

### 4.1 Discover

Use a short conversation, form, demonstration, or partner introduction to identify the business, current process, main friction, available data, devices, staff roles, and buyer.

### 4.2 Demonstrate

Show the selected workflow using representative or permissioned material. The demonstration should make setup, output, review, and daily use visible.

### 4.3 Package

Offer a simple product scope:

- included workflow or module;
- users or location;
- Platform Credit allowance or usage basis where relevant;
- setup or implementation;
- support route;
- price and period;
- cancellation, refund, or renewal terms.

### 4.4 Onboard

Configure the account or workspace, import only necessary data, assign roles, train the operator, and complete the first real task.

### 4.5 Support

Provide concise help for recurring issues, billing, data import, product output, and permission changes. Support findings should feed product improvement.

### 4.6 Review

Assess activation, repeated use, completion, reliability, support burden, payment behavior, and customer feedback. Decide whether to renew, expand, change the offer, or close the account.

SME adoption benefits from limited choice. A small operator should not need to compare the full FUZE portfolio before solving the first task.

---

## 5. Enterprise Deployment Lifecycle

### 5.1 Qualification

Confirm the sponsor, users, business problem, expected benefit, decision process, budget path, systems, data sensitivity, timeline, and implementation capacity.

### 5.2 Solution discovery

Map the current workflow, source systems, roles, approvals, exceptions, reporting, and support responsibilities. Define the smallest deployment that can produce useful evidence.

### 5.3 Review and procurement

Provide the materials required for product, security, privacy, legal, finance, procurement, and vendor review. Responses should match current product status and avoid commitments outside the approved scope.

### 5.4 Technical and data design

Document accounts, identity, roles, sources, interfaces, exports, retention, monitoring, error handling, and access removal. Acceptance tests should reflect the real operating workflow.

### 5.5 Pilot

Activate a controlled audience with named operators, training, support, monitoring, success measures, and pause or rollback procedures.

### 5.6 Acceptance

Review whether the product delivered the agreed workflow, users adopted it, controls operated, incidents were handled, and the commercial model remains supportable.

### 5.7 Rollout

Wider deployment can add users, teams, locations, integrations, products, or regions in planned stages. Each stage updates permissions, training, support, capacity, and reporting.

### 5.8 Renewal or exit

Renewal reviews value, reliability, usage, economics, support, security, and scope. Offboarding removes access, resolves open obligations, handles customer data, and preserves required evidence.

## 6. Packaging Model

### SME packages

Possible formats include:

- starter workflow
- product or module subscription
- Platform Credit bundle
- shop or community package
- setup and training package
- fixed report or campaign service
- partner-assisted onboarding

The package should be easy to compare with the customer’s current cost and effort.

### Enterprise packages

Possible formats include:

- governed workspace and user tiers
- product and usage allowance
- implementation and integration
- training and change management
- support and escalation service
- reporting and review cadence
- partner or regional delivery
- custom scope under approved terms

Enterprise packaging should separate recurring access, usage, implementation, external-provider costs, and optional services so responsibilities remain understandable.

Platform Credits can meter eligible actions in either segment. They should describe consumption rather than replace the customer agreement, invoice, or service definition.

## 7. Implementation Ownership

Every deployment needs named owners.

| Work area | FUZE | Customer | Partner where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product scope | Define supported behavior and current limits | Confirm use case and acceptance needs | Translate local or sector requirements |
| Data | Provide product controls | Supply authorized data and classifications | Assist mapping under approved access |
| Configuration | Provide supported settings | Approve workspace, roles, and workflow | Perform agreed setup or implementation |
| Training | Supply product guidance | Assign users and enforce internal policy | Deliver local or first-line education |
| Support | Resolve product and platform issues | Report evidence and manage user process | Handle agreed first-line assistance |
| Security and incidents | Investigate FUZE systems and coordinate response | Manage customer systems and contacts | Support affected integration or local process |
| Reporting | Define product measures and records | Review business outcome and internal use | Supply attributed delivery evidence |

Unclear ownership is a common source of failed pilots. Shared tasks should still have a lead, response time, and escalation path.

## 8. Data, Permissions, and Security

Expansion requires controls proportionate to the deployment.

### SME baseline

- distinct customer or workspace account
- owner and staff roles
- necessary data only
- protected payment and billing records
- export and deletion behavior
- support-access rules
- account recovery and access removal

### Enterprise controls

- identity and role integration where supported
- least-privilege administration
- source and destination authorization
- data classification and retention
- environment and customer separation
- approval for sensitive AI outputs or actions
- audit and operational records
- security testing and incident contacts
- vendor and subcontractor review where applicable
- controlled change, pause, and offboarding

Public wallet references, if a specific product utility uses them, remain separate from personal identity and customer records. Ordinary business adoption should not require token or wallet mechanics unless the workflow explicitly depends on them.

---

## 9. Partner-Led Expansion

Implementation partners can improve local reach and reduce onboarding friction. Useful roles include:

- product setup and workflow mapping
- device or data integration
- language and market adaptation
- customer training
- first-line support
- event or community delivery
- enterprise change management
- sector-specific requirements

FUZE should qualify the partner’s delivery capacity, data practices, support process, commercial role, public communication, and offboarding responsibilities.

Partner economics should reflect verified contribution. A referral, implementation project, reseller arrangement, integration, and managed service need different attribution and support rules.

The [FUZE Strategic Partner Model](04-FUZE_STRATEGIC_PARTNER_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) owns the complete partner lifecycle.

## 10. Pilot Evidence

A useful pilot report identifies:

- customer segment and deployment scope
- product and workflow
- users, locations, or teams
- onboarding and training completed
- product status and configuration
- activation and completion
- reliability and incidents
- support requests and resolution
- usage and Platform Credit activity where relevant
- customer feedback
- implementation effort and direct cost
- data and permission findings
- commercial status
- recommendation

The recommendation should be one of: expand, renew the same scope, revise, hold, pause, or offboard.

A pilot is evidence for the tested customer and scope. Its results should not be presented as proof of an entire market.

---

## 11. Expansion Ladder

A deployment can expand along several dimensions:

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| Users | Owner to staff, team to department |
| Locations | One shop to several branches |
| Workflow | One report to recurring operations |
| Product | SheetLayer AI to ShopOS AI or TrainLayer AI |
| Data | Manual input to approved integration |
| Service | Self-service to partner or managed support |
| Region | One local market to another reviewed market |
| Evidence | Internal pilot to qualified case study |

Each expansion step should confirm capacity, permissions, support, commercial terms, and reporting. Cross-product expansion should follow a related customer need rather than a portfolio sales target.

---

## 12. Commercial and Payment Operations

SMEs can use simple checkout, invoices, subscriptions, Platform Credit packages, or approved local payment routes. Enterprises can require purchase orders, invoicing, billing controls, contract milestones, tax documentation, and formal reconciliation.

Stablecoins can support an approved payment or settlement route for suitable customers or partners. The transaction still requires customer, purpose, fulfillment, fee, refund, and accounting context.

Product revenue evidence follows delivery and reconciliation. Investment proceeds, token activity, treasury transfers, and wallet balances remain outside ordinary customer revenue.

## 13. Measures for Investors and Operators

### Adoption

- qualified opportunities
- demonstrations and pilot starts
- onboarding completion
- time to first value

### Product use

- active customers, workspaces, users, or locations
- completed workflows
- frequency and depth of use
- product or module expansion

### Commercial

- paid conversion
- renewal and cancellation
- repeat purchases
- refunds and disputes
- customer and partner concentration

### Delivery

- implementation time
- product reliability
- support volume and resolution
- direct delivery and partner cost

### Control

- permission findings
- security or privacy incidents
- audit or reconciliation exceptions
- offboarding completion

Measures need consistent definitions, periods, cohorts, and sources. SME self-service, partner-led deployments, and enterprise pilots should not be blended without context.

---

## 14. Scale Decision

An SME motion can scale when onboarding is simple, product value appears quickly, support is manageable, payments reconcile, and retention fits the product cycle.

An enterprise motion can scale when qualification is consistent, pilots reach acceptance, integrations are supportable, procurement evidence is reusable, data controls operate, and rollout economics are understood.

FUZE should hold expansion when customer demand is weak, implementation is bespoke beyond capacity, product reliability remains insufficient, support overwhelms the team, or permissions and data controls are incomplete.

---

## 15. Public Boundary

This paper defines a customer expansion and deployment model. It does not claim current contracts, procurement approvals, customer growth, revenue, retention, market share, or investment outcomes.

Product availability, packaging, integrations, support, payments, and regions depend on current approved scope and customer terms. Private customer, partner, pricing, security, and commercial information remains within its authorized audience.

---

## Conclusion

FUZE can expand into SME and enterprise markets by solving one operational problem, delivering one controlled deployment, and widening the relationship only when evidence supports it.

SMEs need speed, clarity, and practical support. Enterprises need governance, integration, procurement, and accountable rollout. A shared product and platform foundation can serve both when packaging, permissions, implementation, and reporting match the customer’s actual operating environment.
