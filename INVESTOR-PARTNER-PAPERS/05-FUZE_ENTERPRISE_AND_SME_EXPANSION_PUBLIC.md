# FUZE Enterprise and SME Expansion

## Executive Summary

FUZE can serve small and medium-sized businesses and larger enterprises through the same product families, but the route to adoption differs. SMEs usually need a fast start, understandable packages, mobile-friendly workflows, practical templates, local payment options, and accessible support. Enterprises usually require structured discovery, procurement, security and privacy review, integration ownership, governed workspaces, acceptance criteria, change control, and accountable rollout.

Expansion should begin with one operational problem and one bounded product deployment. ShopOS AI can address shop administration; SheetLayer AI can organize spreadsheet-heavy work; SpeakShop AI can support approved announcements; TrainLayer AI can prepare learning material; CommunityLayer AI can assist community operations; AIE can support event teams; and Botmad can perform approved work tasks. QTB and AIMM should enter only where the customer, data, permissions, and review controls fit the intended professional workflow. ZAGA follows its own game and community onboarding path.

The goal is not to move every customer into the full FUZE portfolio. The goal is to deliver one useful workflow, establish reliable onboarding and support, confirm commercial and control evidence, and widen the relationship only when the next scope solves a related customer need.

This paper defines the expansion model from qualification through demonstration, packaging, onboarding, pilot, acceptance, rollout, renewal, and offboarding. It also explains implementation ownership, partner-led delivery, Platform Credits, payments, data controls, support, and the evidence investors should expect before a pilot is presented as a repeatable business channel.

This paper is a public operating model. It does not confirm current contracts, customers, procurement approvals, revenue, retention, market share, or expansion outcomes.

It is designed to answer:

1. How should FUZE enter SME and enterprise environments?
2. Which products fit which operational problems?
3. How should SME packaging differ from enterprise deployment?
4. What must be reviewed before onboarding or integration?
5. Which party owns product, data, implementation, support, and incident duties?
6. What evidence supports pilot, rollout, renewal, or expansion?
7. How should Platform Credits, payments, and revenue records be handled?
8. When should a deployment be narrowed, paused, or closed?

## 2. Current Public Position

FUZE's public papers define product concepts, target users, workflows, launch sequencing, packaging possibilities, partner roles, permissions, and evidence requirements.

They do not by themselves prove:

- that a product is available for purchase;
- that an SME or enterprise customer is active;
- that a pilot has started or completed;
- that procurement or security review has been approved;
- that an integration is implemented or live;
- that users have activated, retained, or expanded;
- that a payment or Platform Credit route is active;
- that paid delivery or recognized revenue exists;
- that a partner-led channel is repeatable;
- that a deployment can scale to another market, product, or customer type.

Stronger claims require dated product, customer, implementation, usage, support, commercial, reconciliation, and control evidence for the stated scope.

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) controls public status vocabulary.

## 3. Expansion Objective

The objective is to turn a useful FUZE workflow into a customer deployment that can be operated, supported, measured, and repeated.

The expansion process should identify:

1. customer segment and buyer;
2. operational problem;
3. initial product and scope;
4. users, roles, systems, and data;
5. implementation owner;
6. commercial package;
7. onboarding and support route;
8. acceptance and success evidence;
9. operating cost and capacity;
10. expansion, revision, renewal, or closure decision.

Expansion is product-specific. A successful shop pilot does not establish enterprise readiness for an unrelated market-intelligence workflow. A working integration does not prove user adoption. A signed contract does not prove delivery or retention.

## 4. SME and Enterprise Profiles

| Area | SME pattern | Enterprise pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner, founder, operator, or small-team manager | Business sponsor, department owner, procurement, finance, security, or executive |
| Decision process | Direct and relatively short | Multi-role review and formal approval |
| Initial scope | One shop, team, campaign, or workflow | Department, business unit, controlled user group, or integration |
| Setup | Guided self-service or assisted onboarding | Planned implementation with owners and acceptance tests |
| Packaging | Starter plan, Platform Credits, add-on, or fixed package | Contracted workspace, seats, usage, implementation, and support |
| Data review | Practical permission and import checks | Data inventory, privacy, security, retention, vendor, and legal review |
| Integration | Sheets, QR, devices, exports, and simple connections | APIs, identity, internal systems, devices, and change control |
| Support | Tutorials, chat, setup assistance | Named contacts, escalation, incident, service, and review process |
| Reporting | Operational summaries | Management, service, usage, control, and audit-oriented reporting |
| Expansion | More features, staff, or locations | More users, teams, systems, regions, or products |

These are patterns rather than rigid categories. A regulated small business may require enterprise-level controls, while a limited enterprise team may begin with a simple package.

## 5. Product Entry Patterns

FUZE should lead with the product that best matches the customer's immediate problem.

### 5.1 Shop operations

ShopOS AI can provide the central operating workflow, with SheetLayer AI, SpeakShop AI, or TrainLayer AI added for data, announcements, and staff learning.

The first scope may cover one menu, queue, reporting, stock, promotion, or staff process rather than the full shop system.

### 5.2 Spreadsheet-heavy operations

SheetLayer AI can enter through a current spreadsheet and one defined task such as mapping fields, preparing a dashboard draft, checking data structure, transforming records, or organizing a report.

This reduces migration pressure and makes source, permission, output, and review easier to verify.

### 5.3 Training and knowledge

TrainLayer AI can begin with one approved source set and one guide, quiz, onboarding module, or learning package.

Source authority, version control, reviewer responsibility, and learner access become more important as the audience grows.

### 5.4 Communications

SpeakShop AI can begin with a bounded set of scripts, announcements, or campaign material subject to customer review and approval.

Generated content should not be presented as approved customer communication until the responsible reviewer accepts it.

### 5.5 Community and support

CommunityLayer AI can begin with a recurring summary, support-draft set, admin report, moderation-assistance workflow, or knowledge route.

The customer retains policy, enforcement, and member-impacting authority.

### 5.6 Events and campaigns

AIE and SpeakShop AI can support planning, briefing, announcements, recap, and sponsor reporting. ToolGrid AI may provide a separate reviewed discovery or sponsored-visibility service.

Event attendance or campaign reach alone is not evidence of product adoption or commercial success.

### 5.7 Work assistance

Botmad can enter through a bounded task with approved sources, permitted tools, expected output, reviewer, action limits, and audit records.

Wider desktop authority should follow only after permission, reliability, support, and incident evidence mature.

### 5.8 Specialized Web3 operations

QTB and AIMM can serve qualified research or operations teams through controlled reports and workspaces.

QTB should remain market-research and interpretation support. AIMM should remain liquidity-operations analysis, planning, monitoring, controls, and reporting. Neither should be presented as autonomous trading, price protection, or guaranteed liquidity.

### 5.9 ZAGA

ZAGA products use game and community onboarding rather than a general business-software motion. SME or enterprise involvement may occur through events, communities, content, services, sponsorships, or approved integrations.

Game records, token-connected features, external value, and withdrawal status should remain clearly separated.

## 6. SME Adoption Playbook

### 6.1 Discover

Use a short conversation, form, demonstration, or partner introduction to identify:

- business type;
- current workflow;
- main friction;
- buyer and users;
- available data and devices;
- payment preference;
- support need;
- urgency;
- disqualifying constraints.

### 6.2 Demonstrate

Show the selected workflow using representative or permissioned material.

The demonstration should make setup, input, output, review, daily use, limitations, and support visible.

### 6.3 Package

Offer a simple scope that identifies:

- included workflow or module;
- users, team, or location;
- Platform Credit allowance or usage basis where relevant;
- setup or implementation;
- support route;
- price, period, and payment method;
- cancellation, refund, expiry, and renewal terms;
- current product status.

### 6.4 Onboard

Configure the account or workspace, import only necessary data, assign roles, train the operator, and complete the first real task.

### 6.5 Activate

Activation should mean the customer reaches first meaningful value, such as a completed shop workflow, approved announcement, reviewed report, training module, or community summary.

Account creation, login, page view, or credit grant alone is not meaningful activation.

### 6.6 Support

Provide concise assistance for billing, data import, permissions, product output, operational errors, and account changes.

Support evidence should feed product and packaging improvements.

### 6.7 Review

Assess:

- activation;
- repeated use;
- completion and reliability;
- support burden;
- payment behavior;
- refund or dispute activity;
- customer feedback;
- delivery cost;
- renewal or expansion fit.

Decide whether to renew, expand, revise, pause, or close.

SME adoption benefits from limited choice. A small operator should not need to evaluate the full FUZE portfolio before solving the first task.

## 7. Enterprise Deployment Lifecycle

### 7.1 Qualification

Confirm:

- business sponsor;
- users and decision-makers;
- operational problem;
- expected benefit;
- procurement path;
- budget authority;
- systems and integrations;
- data sensitivity;
- security and privacy review;
- implementation capacity;
- support requirements;
- timeline and decision criteria.

### 7.2 Solution discovery

Map the current workflow, systems, roles, approvals, exceptions, reporting, data movement, and support responsibilities.

Define the smallest deployment that can create useful evidence.

### 7.3 Product, security, and procurement review

Provide current materials required for product, architecture, security, privacy, legal, finance, procurement, vendor, and service review.

Responses should match actual product status and should not commit to unsupported controls, certifications, integrations, service levels, or delivery dates.

### 7.4 Technical and data design

Document:

- accounts and identity;
- users and roles;
- source and destination systems;
- data classification and authority;
- interfaces and exports;
- retention and deletion;
- monitoring and audit records;
- error and exception handling;
- environment separation;
- support access;
- credential rotation;
- access removal.

Acceptance tests should reflect the real operating workflow.

### 7.5 Pilot approval

Confirm scope, users, owners, data, access, product status, commercial treatment, support, monitoring, incident contacts, pause criteria, and rollback procedures.

### 7.6 Controlled pilot

Operate the approved scope with named users, training, support, monitoring, and evidence collection.

### 7.7 Acceptance review

Review whether:

- the product delivered the agreed workflow;
- users reached meaningful value;
- controls operated;
- incidents were handled;
- support was adequate;
- costs were understood;
- commercial operation remained supportable;
- open requirements were documented.

### 7.8 Rollout

Wider deployment may add users, teams, locations, integrations, products, or regions in controlled stages.

Each stage should update permissions, training, support, capacity, cost, monitoring, and reporting.

### 7.9 Renewal, revision, or exit

Renewal reviews value, reliability, usage, economics, support, security, data, and scope.

Offboarding removes access, resolves obligations, handles customer data, updates public status, and preserves required evidence.

## 8. Packaging Model

### 8.1 SME packages

Possible formats include:

- starter workflow;
- product or module subscription;
- Platform Credit bundle;
- shop, team, or community package;
- setup and training package;
- fixed report or campaign service;
- partner-assisted onboarding.

The package should be easy to compare with the customer's current cost, effort, and expected benefit.

### 8.2 Enterprise packages

Possible formats include:

- governed workspace and user tiers;
- product access and usage allowance;
- implementation and integration;
- training and change management;
- support and escalation service;
- reporting and review cadence;
- partner or regional delivery;
- custom scope under approved terms.

Enterprise packaging should separate recurring access, usage, implementation, integration, external-provider costs, support, and optional services.

### 8.3 Platform Credits

Platform Credits may meter eligible product actions in either segment.

They should identify consumption, balance, allowance, reservation, reversal, refund, and expiry treatment. They do not replace the customer agreement, invoice, service definition, payment evidence, or revenue classification.

Platform Credits remain separate from FUZE token.

## 9. Implementation Ownership

Every deployment needs named owners.

| Work area | FUZE | Customer | Partner where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product scope | Define supported behavior, dependencies, and current limits | Confirm use case, users, and acceptance needs | Translate local or sector requirements |
| Data | Provide product controls and approved handling | Supply authorized data, classifications, and consent | Assist mapping under approved access |
| Configuration | Provide supported settings and guidance | Approve workspace, roles, and workflow | Perform agreed setup or implementation |
| Integration | Define FUZE interface and test requirements | Approve customer systems and access | Build or support the agreed connection |
| Training | Supply product guidance | Assign users and enforce internal policy | Deliver local or first-line education |
| Support | Resolve FUZE product and platform issues | Report evidence and manage internal user process | Handle agreed first-line assistance |
| Security and incidents | Investigate FUZE-side systems and coordinate response | Manage customer systems, users, and contacts | Support affected integration or local process |
| Commercial | Operate FUZE billing, credits, and reconciliation duties | Approve purchase, payment, and internal controls | Perform agreed referral, resale, or service duties |
| Reporting | Define product measures and source records | Review business outcomes and internal use | Supply attributed delivery evidence |
| Offboarding | Remove FUZE-side access and complete obligations | Remove customer-side access and manage internal records | Close partner systems and support duties |

Shared responsibility should still have a lead owner, response expectation, and escalation path.

## 10. Data, Permissions, and Security

Expansion requires controls proportionate to the deployment.

### 10.1 SME baseline

- distinct customer or workspace account;
- owner and staff roles;
- necessary data only;
- protected payment and billing records;
- support-access rules;
- export, correction, and deletion behavior;
- account recovery;
- access removal at staff or account closure.

### 10.2 Enterprise controls

- identity and role integration where supported;
- least-privilege administration;
- source and destination authorization;
- data classification and retention;
- customer and environment separation;
- approval for sensitive AI outputs or actions;
- operational and audit records;
- security testing and incident contacts;
- vendor and subcontractor review where applicable;
- controlled change, pause, rollback, and offboarding.

### 10.3 AI and human authority

AI outputs should identify their source context, review state, and limitations where material.

The customer retains authority over business, employment, moderation, legal, financial, security, and other consequential decisions unless a separate approved process defines a narrower automated action.

### 10.4 Wallet and token boundary

Ordinary business adoption should not require token or wallet mechanics unless the workflow explicitly depends on an approved and active ecosystem function.

Public wallet references remain separate from personal identity, customer records, and commercial account data.

The [FUZE Data Privacy and Permission Model](08-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_PERMISSION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) governs the wider treatment.

## 11. Partner-Led Expansion

Implementation partners can improve local reach and reduce onboarding friction.

Useful roles include:

- product setup and workflow mapping;
- device or data integration;
- language and market adaptation;
- customer training;
- first-line support;
- event or community delivery;
- enterprise change management;
- sector-specific implementation.

FUZE should qualify the partner's delivery capacity, permissions, data practices, support process, commercial role, communication, incident handling, and offboarding responsibilities.

Partner economics should reflect verified contribution. Referral, resale, implementation, integration, managed service, and sponsorship models require different attribution and support rules.

The [FUZE Strategic Partner Model](04-FUZE_STRATEGIC_PARTNER_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) owns the complete partner lifecycle.

## 12. Pilot Design and Evidence

A useful pilot should define:

- customer segment and deployment scope;
- product and workflow;
- users, teams, locations, or systems;
- start and end dates;
- owners and responsibilities;
- onboarding and training;
- product status and configuration;
- data, permissions, and security controls;
- activation and completion events;
- reliability and incident handling;
- support routes;
- Platform Credit treatment where relevant;
- payment and commercial status;
- success, revision, pause, and stop criteria;
- offboarding procedure.

A pilot report should include:

- actual scope operated;
- onboarding and training completed;
- activation and usage evidence;
- product completion and reliability;
- support requests and resolution;
- incidents and corrections;
- customer feedback;
- implementation effort and direct cost;
- data and permission findings;
- commercial and reconciliation status;
- limitations;
- recommendation.

The recommendation should be one of:

- expand;
- renew the same scope;
- revise;
- hold;
- pause;
- offboard.

A pilot is evidence for the tested customer and scope. It is not proof of the entire SME, enterprise, industry, or geographic market.

## 13. Expansion Ladder

A deployment can expand along several dimensions.

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| Users | Owner to staff, team to department |
| Locations | One shop to several branches |
| Workflow | One report to recurring operations |
| Product | SheetLayer AI to ShopOS AI or TrainLayer AI |
| Data | Manual input to approved integration |
| Integration | One export to a governed API workflow |
| Service | Self-service to partner or managed support |
| Region | One local market to another reviewed market |
| Commercial | Trial to paid package or contracted deployment |
| Evidence | Internal pilot to qualified case study |

Each expansion step should confirm:

- customer need;
- product readiness;
- capacity;
- permissions;
- support;
- commercial terms;
- implementation ownership;
- reporting;
- pause and closure routes.

Cross-product expansion should follow a related customer need rather than a portfolio sales target.

## 14. Commercial and Payment Operations

SMEs may use checkout, invoice, subscription, Platform Credit package, or approved local payment routes.

Enterprises may require purchase orders, invoicing, billing controls, contract milestones, tax documentation, vendor onboarding, and formal reconciliation.

Stablecoins may support an approved payment or settlement route where appropriate. The transaction still requires:

- customer or contracting account;
- product and purpose;
- amount, asset, and network;
- fees and conversion;
- fulfillment;
- refund or reversal treatment;
- treasury context;
- reconciliation;
- accounting review.

Product revenue evidence follows delivery, adjustment, classification, and reconciliation.

Investment proceeds, token activity, treasury transfers, wallet balances, liquidity movements, and unrealized asset changes remain outside ordinary customer revenue.

## 15. Customer Success, Support, and Service Operations

Expansion requires more than initial onboarding.

A support model should define:

- support channels and hours;
- severity and priority;
- first-line and escalation ownership;
- product versus customer-system responsibility;
- incident and service communication;
- response and resolution expectations where agreed;
- customer training and knowledge material;
- correction and refund routes;
- change requests;
- renewal and offboarding support.

Support evidence can reveal product complexity, onboarding weakness, hidden delivery cost, and recurring customer value.

A product should not be scaled faster than the team or partner network can support it reliably.

### 16.1 Qualification and acquisition

- qualified opportunities;
- demonstrations;
- discovery completion;
- pilot approvals;
- source and partner attribution.

### 16.2 Activation

- onboarding completion;
- time to first meaningful value;
- workflow completion;
- activation rate by cohort;
- support contacts before activation.

### 16.3 Product use

- active customers, workspaces, users, or locations;
- completed workflows;
- frequency and depth of use;
- retained use by period;
- product or module expansion.

### 16.4 Commercial

- paid conversion;
- fulfilled paid delivery;
- renewal and cancellation;
- repeat purchases;
- refunds and disputes;
- customer and partner concentration;
- reconciliation status.

### 16.5 Delivery

- implementation time;
- product reliability;
- support volume and resolution;
- direct delivery and partner cost;
- incident rate;
- acceptance-test results.

### 16.6 Control

- permission findings;
- security or privacy incidents;
- audit or reconciliation exceptions;
- correction history;
- access-review and offboarding completion.

Measures need consistent definitions, source owners, periods, cohorts, exclusions, and correction methods.

SME self-service, partner-led deployment, and enterprise pilots should not be blended without context.

## 17. Scale Readiness

### 17.1 SME motion

An SME motion may be ready to scale when:

- the buyer and problem are clear;
- onboarding is simple;
- first value appears quickly;
- support is manageable;
- pricing and delivery cost are understood;
- payments and refunds reconcile;
- retention fits the product cycle;
- partner delivery is reliable where used;
- product reliability remains acceptable at higher volume.

### 17.2 Enterprise motion

An enterprise motion may be ready to scale when:

- qualification is consistent;
- pilots reach acceptance;
- procurement and review materials are reusable;
- integrations are supportable;
- permissions and data controls operate;
- support ownership is clear;
- rollout capacity and economics are understood;
- reporting explains usage, incidents, cost, and outcomes.

FUZE should hold expansion when demand is weak, implementation is too bespoke, reliability remains insufficient, support overwhelms capacity, commercial records do not reconcile, or permissions and data controls are incomplete.

## 18. Pause and Stop Criteria

A deployment should be narrowed, paused, or stopped when:

- users do not reach meaningful value;
- product reliability blocks normal use;
- support effort is disproportionate;
- the customer cannot provide authorized data or owners;
- security or privacy concerns remain unresolved;
- procurement or legal requirements cannot be met;
- commercial terms are not supportable;
- refunds, complaints, or disputes reveal product or expectation problems;
- integration cost exceeds the tested value;
- partner delivery is unreliable;
- the deployment depends on unsupported token, market, revenue, or outcome claims.

A pause or closure should record scope, authority, reason, user treatment, data handling, access changes, obligations, and next steps.

## 19. Renewal and Offboarding

Renewal is a new operating decision.

Review should cover:

- customer value;
- product usage;
- reliability;
- support;
- security and privacy;
- commercial performance;
- costs;
- open incidents;
- proposed expansion;
- customer and FUZE capacity.

Offboarding should:

1. stop new activity;
2. communicate with affected users;
3. remove permissions;
4. revoke credentials and disconnect integrations;
5. return, export, retain, or delete customer records as required;
6. reconcile invoices, payments, Platform Credits, refunds, and obligations;
7. update support and public status;
8. preserve required decision and incident records;
9. capture lessons for future product and expansion decisions.

## 20. Investor Review Questions

Investors should ask:

- Which SME or enterprise workflows have progressed beyond documentation?
- Which products have completed real customer onboarding?
- Which pilots reached meaningful activation and acceptance?
- Which offers have completed paid delivery and reconciliation?
- What implementation and support effort is required per customer?
- Which deployments depend on partners, devices, APIs, or providers?
- How concentrated is the channel by customer, partner, sector, or geography?
- Which controls are required for enterprise procurement and data review?
- Which customer evidence supports renewal or expansion?
- Which motions were paused or stopped, and what was learned?

## 21. Public Boundary

This paper defines an SME and enterprise expansion model.

It does not claim:

- current customers or contracts;
- procurement or security approval;
- live integrations;
- customer growth or market share;
- paid conversion or recognized revenue;
- renewal or retention;
- enterprise readiness for every FUZE product;
- repeatable channel economics;
- successful regional expansion;
- investor or token-holder outcomes.

Product availability, packaging, integrations, support, payments, regions, service levels, and commercial terms depend on current approved scope and customer agreements.

Private customer, partner, pricing, security, procurement, legal, and commercial information remains within its authorized audience.

## Key Takeaways

- SME and enterprise expansion should begin with one operational problem and one bounded product deployment.
- SMEs usually need speed, clarity, simple packaging, and practical support.
- Enterprises usually need discovery, procurement, integration, permissions, acceptance, and accountable rollout.
- A demonstration, pilot, rollout, paid delivery, renewal, and expansion are different evidence states.
- Platform Credits may support product usage but remain separate from FUZE token and revenue recognition.
- Stablecoin payments require customer, purpose, fulfillment, fee, refund, treasury, and accounting context.
- Expansion should follow customer need and operating evidence rather than portfolio breadth.
- A motion should scale only when product value, supportability, controls, payments, and economics are understood.
