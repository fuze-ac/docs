# FUZE Investor FAQ

## Executive Summary

This FAQ gives investors concise answers to the main questions that arise before detailed diligence. It covers the FUZE thesis, product status, commercial model, shared platform, token and credit separation, fundraising boundary, evidence, controls, market access, and principal uncertainties.

FUZE is developing practical AI SaaS, game, event, market-intelligence, utility-discovery, and work-assistance products supported by reusable services. The investment case depends on turning selected workflows into useful products, paid delivery, reliable operation, effective distribution, and evidence that shared services create leverage.

The public corpus currently establishes product definitions, workflows, architecture direction, policies, and review frameworks. It does not by itself establish that every product is live, generating revenue, or operating at scale. Investors should use the current status and evidence records rather than infer execution from document volume.

FUZE token, Platform Credits, stablecoin rails, and wallet records serve different purposes. Detailed token, wallet, market, and participation mechanics belong in their specialist papers. This FAQ is an orientation document, not an offer, forecast, legal opinion, or substitute for qualified diligence.

## 1. Business and Investment Thesis

### What is FUZE?

FUZE is a product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem. It combines a portfolio of focused products with shared services for accounts, workspaces, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, data controls, wallet-aware records, and reporting.

The [FUZE Investor Overview](01-FUZE_INVESTOR_OVERVIEW_PUBLIC.md) provides the full public thesis.

### What does product-first mean?

FUZE begins with a user, a recurring problem, and a complete workflow. Shared infrastructure is developed when products need the same controlled capability. Broader ecosystem functions follow where they support an approved purpose.

Investors should therefore look first for product completion, use, delivery economics, support, and learning rather than token or platform claims in isolation.

### Why use a portfolio rather than one product?

Different FUZE products address different audiences and distribution routes. Portfolio breadth can create optionality and shared-service leverage, but it also creates prioritization and operating risk.

The portfolio case becomes stronger when FUZE can identify a small number of priority workflows, complete them, and demonstrate that common services reduce repeated work or improve control.

### Which products are included?

The portfolio includes HerHelp, SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, CommunityLayer AI, ZAGA, ZAGA Arena, ZAGA Districts, QTB, AIMM, AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad.

The [FUZE AI SaaS Product Index](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/01-FUZE_AI_SAAS_PRODUCT_INDEX_PUBLIC.md) explains each product’s audience and workflow without requiring this FAQ to repeat the catalogue.

### What are the strongest potential advantages?

Potential advantages include:

- recognizable user problems across several markets;
- reusable product and control services;
- multiple commercial formats;
- direct, partner, community, enterprise, and event distribution routes;
- product-specific entry points into Web3 audiences;
- structured public and private evidence.

These are propositions to test, not completed advantages merely because they are documented.

### What is the main execution challenge?

FUZE must convert a broad portfolio into a focused sequence of complete, supportable products. It must also prove that shared rails are economically and operationally useful across products rather than becoming infrastructure built ahead of demand.

## 2. Product Status and Evidence

### Are all FUZE products live?

No. The current public baseline is primarily product or game design and public documentation. A stronger status requires evidence appropriate to the exact product, module, audience, and environment.

The authoritative public baseline appears in the [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md).

### What evidence should an investor request?

Evidence can include:

- a working demonstration of the core workflow;
- test scope, results, defects, and corrections;
- release, onboarding, monitoring, and support records;
- defined usage events and cohort reports;
- customer or partner acceptance where permissioned;
- paid-delivery and reconciliation records;
- implemented permissions, data, AI, security, and incident controls;
- current status, owner, limitations, and next milestone.

The [FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix](14-FUZE_PRODUCT_STATUS_AND_EVIDENCE_MATRIX_PUBLIC.md) provides the detailed claim-to-proof framework.

### Is a product paper evidence of a working product?

A product paper establishes the approved purpose, target user, workflow, features, controls, and boundary. It does not establish implementation or availability.

A prototype, limited release, live service, usage result, and commercial result each require their own records.

### How should demos be evaluated?

A useful demonstration identifies the product version, environment, source material, user role, steps, output, controls, manual intervention, failures, and disposition. Investors should distinguish a prepared example from a workflow executed in the product.

### How does FUZE report current progress?

FUZE uses scoped status labels and evidence appropriate to the claim. Public metrics should identify definition, period, cohort, source, review, limitations, and correction history.

See [FUZE Public Metrics and Transparency](09-FUZE_PUBLIC_METRICS_AND_TRANSPARENCY_PUBLIC.md).

## 3. Commercial Model

### How can FUZE earn product revenue?

Potential commercial formats include subscriptions, metered actions, Platform Credit packages, workspace plans, implementation services, reports, event or community packages, sponsored services, partner deployments, and approved game or ecosystem services.

Each offer needs a customer, scope, delivery obligation, price basis, payment route, fulfillment record, adjustment process, and support model.

### What is the difference between a revenue path and revenue evidence?

A revenue path is a designed method for charging for a product or service. Revenue readiness means that the offer and transaction flow can be operated. Revenue evidence requires confirmed activity, proper classification, reconciliation, and period review.

The [FUZE Product Revenue Model](02-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) owns these distinctions.

### Does a payment automatically count as revenue?

No. A transaction may be a customer payment, deposit, refund, dispute, treasury transfer, investor receipt, partner settlement, grant, compensation, or another category. Product revenue requires the relevant service, fulfillment, classification, adjustments, and review.

### What commercial evidence matters most?

Investors should examine:

- completed paid delivery;
- repeat purchase or continued use;
- pricing and discount behavior;
- refunds, disputes, and service corrections;
- AI, infrastructure, payment, partner, and support costs;
- onboarding and fulfillment effort;
- retention within the product’s natural frequency;
- period reconciliation and source records.

### How does FUZE plan to reach customers?

FUZE uses product-specific motions rather than one ecosystem campaign. Routes can include demonstrations, content, communities, direct sales, enterprise pilots, events, implementation partners, and Web3 channels.

The [FUZE Go-To-Market](03-FUZE_GO_TO_MARKET_PUBLIC.md) explains entry offers, funnels, channel ownership, experiments, and scale decisions.

### How do partnerships contribute?

Partners can provide distribution, integration, implementation, infrastructure, events, community access, local knowledge, or specialized operations. Investor evidence should distinguish a discussion or announcement from an accepted integration, active deployment, measured delivery, or renewal.

See [FUZE Strategic Partner Model](04-FUZE_STRATEGIC_PARTNER_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

## 4. Platform and Technology

### What shared capabilities can products reuse?

Potential shared capabilities include accounts, workspaces, roles, permissions, Platform Credit handling, payment status, AI routing, data controls, wallet-aware records, and reporting.

The platform thesis is demonstrated when more than one operating product uses the same capability with clear service ownership and measurable benefit.

### What should investors test about platform leverage?

Investors can ask:

- Which rails are implemented?
- Which products use them?
- What repeated product problem did each rail remove?
- Who owns availability, incidents, change control, and support?
- How are costs attributed?
- What migration or dependency risk does reuse introduce?

### How does FUZE approach AI safety?

Each AI workflow should have a bounded purpose, controlled inputs and tools, product-specific evaluation, meaningful human authority, monitoring, and a correction path. The required controls depend on the consequence of the output.

The full operating model appears in [FUZE AI Safety and Reliability](07-FUZE_AI_SAFETY_AND_RELIABILITY_PUBLIC.md).

### How are data and permissions handled?

FUZE’s model connects data purpose and classification to roles, actions, providers, retention, deletion, reporting, and incident response. Public reporting uses aggregation or other controls to avoid exposing private customer, partner, investor, contributor, or wallet-linked identity.

See [FUZE Data Privacy and Permission Model](08-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_PERMISSION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

### What are the principal technical dependencies?

Dependencies can include AI model providers, cloud and data services, payment providers, wallets, networks, Telegram or other distribution platforms, devices, external integrations, and specialist security or compliance services.

Investors should examine ownership, portability, failure behavior, provider concentration, costs, and continuity plans.

## 5. Credits, Payments, and Token

### What are Platform Credits?

Platform Credits are consumption units for eligible product actions. They can support quoting, granting, purchasing, reserving, consuming, reversing, and reporting usage within FUZE products.

They remain separate from FUZE token. Detailed examples appear in [FUZE Platform Credits Usage Examples](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/06-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_USAGE_EXAMPLES_PUBLIC.md).

### How are stablecoins used?

Stablecoins can provide operational payment, settlement, treasury, or compensation routes where suitable. Each transaction still needs its real business classification and reconciliation.

Stablecoin use does not make a product dependent on FUZE token or turn a treasury balance into a user or holder entitlement.

### What is FUZE token’s role?

FUZE token is the ecosystem token for approved utility and participation functions. A product should use it only when a defined action requires that role.

Product consumption through Platform Credits, ordinary payment activity, token utility, and wallet records should remain distinguishable in product design and reporting.

### Does every product require FUZE token?

No. Products can provide value through accounts, payments, subscriptions, services, or Platform Credits. Token functions should be added where they serve a material product or ecosystem purpose and the necessary controls are ready.

### What is wallet-based participation?

It is an activation-gated framework with separate treatment for eligibility, approved distributable value, snapshots, custody, privacy, claims, corrections, governance, and jurisdiction.

Ordinary token holding does not by itself establish every condition required by that framework. Investors should use the dedicated [FUZE Wallet-Based Participation Model](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/07-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PARTICIPATION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) for current detail.

### Does product revenue automatically flow to token holders?

No. Product revenue and any approved distributable value are separate concepts. Revenue requires normal commercial and financial classification. A participation mechanism, if activated for a defined scope, would require its own source pool, approvals, eligibility, custody, reporting, and claim records.

## 6. Token Launch and Market Access

### What does “token launch” mean?

FUZE separates contract deployment, token initialization, allocation funding, transfer activation, product utility, distribution, custody support, market access, and participation activation.

Completion of one event does not establish the others. The [FUZE Token Launch Mechanics](13-FUZE_TOKEN_LAUNCH_MECHANICS_PUBLIC.md) provides the launch runbook.

### Is FUZE pursuing DEX-first market access?

FUZE’s public direction is decentralized-first. A DEX route should be described as live only when its contract, network, pair, pool, and public status can be verified.

Possible later CEX consideration depends on FUZE readiness and the venue’s independent processes. Review, discussion, application, approval, integration, and live access are different statuses.

### Does a DEX pool or exchange discussion establish liquidity?

No. Availability, depth, volume, spread, continuity, custody, and user access depend on actual market and provider conditions. Public communication should identify the current verified status without predicting price or market performance.

### How are self-custody and exchange custody different?

Self-custody gives the user direct wallet control and responsibility. Exchange or platform custody operates through the custodian’s accounts, wallets, rules, and withdrawal support.

Custody treatment can affect address-level records and specialist participation mechanisms. It does not change the need to protect personal identity.

## 8. Diligence, Risk, and Outcomes

### What is publicly available versus private?

Public papers explain approved positioning, products, status, operating models, tokenomics policies, and boundaries. Qualified diligence can include greater product, commercial, financial, technical, governance, partner, and risk evidence.

Personal data, customer records, private agreements, credentials, security findings, detailed treasury controls, and professional advice remain restricted to their authorized audiences.

### How is the investor data room organized?

The room should route each investor question to a current evidence pack, owner, access class, limitations, and gap record. It should distinguish active evidence from superseded or draft files.

The [FUZE Investor Data Room Index](18-FUZE_INVESTOR_DATA_ROOM_INDEX_PUBLIC.md) owns the detailed structure.

### What are the main investor risks?

Principal areas include:

- product quality, prioritization, adoption, and support;
- portfolio and platform execution;
- commercial demand, pricing, costs, and financing;
- AI, data, privacy, security, and provider dependency;
- token, wallet, custody, contract, market, and liquidity conditions;
- legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, and jurisdiction change;
- team, partner, governance, and operational capacity;
- incomplete or changing evidence.

Investors should use [FUZE Investor Risk Disclosure](17-FUZE_INVESTOR_RISK_DISCLOSURE_PUBLIC.md) and the [FUZE Risk and Disclosure Appendix](../WHITEPAPER-PAPERS/05-FUZE_RISK_AND_DISCLOSURE_APPENDIX_PUBLIC.md) for the consolidated treatment.

### Can products or priorities change?

Yes. FUZE can narrow, pause, combine, replace, or retire products and mechanisms as evidence, costs, dependencies, controls, and priorities develop. Status changes should identify the affected scope and supporting decision.

### What creates long-term optionality?

Useful products, reusable capabilities, distribution knowledge, trustworthy records, strategic relationships, and modular commitments can create future choices. A long list of possible markets or outcomes is not sufficient.

See [FUZE Long-Term Optionality](10-FUZE_LONG_TERM_OPTIONALITY_PUBLIC.md).

### Is an acquisition, listing, liquidity event, or investor exit assured?

No. Those outcomes depend on future execution, counterparties, approvals, market conditions, transaction terms, and other factors. Public references to optionality or market direction should not be read as completed or assured outcomes.

## 9. Investor Review Checklist

Before advancing diligence, an investor can ask:

1. Which products and workflows have the strongest current evidence?
2. What status applies to each exact scope?
3. Which shared rails are implemented and reused?
4. Which offers have completed paid delivery?
5. What are the delivery and support economics?
6. Which channels have measurable activation and retention?
7. Which claims rely on design rather than operation?
8. How are AI, data, security, and incidents controlled?
9. How are credits, payments, token, wallet, and treasury records separated?
10. Which token or market functions are active, prepared, or gated?
11. What will strategic capital fund, and what evidence will prove delivery?
12. Which material gaps or external dependencies remain?
13. Which information is public, qualified, restricted, or transaction-specific?
14. How are status changes and corrections communicated?

The [FUZE Investor Readiness Summary](11-FUZE_INVESTOR_READINESS_SUMMARY_PUBLIC.md) provides the maturity and gap-management framework behind this checklist.

This FAQ provides the route into that review while leaving detailed product, token, wallet, market, funding, and risk mechanics in their primary documents.
