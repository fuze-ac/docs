# FUZE Investor FAQ

## Executive Summary

This FAQ gives investors concise, public-safe answers to the main questions that arise before detailed diligence. It covers the FUZE thesis, product status, commercial model, shared platform, AI and data controls, Platform Credits, stablecoin rails, FUZE token, wallet participation, fundraising, use of funds, market access, evidence, governance, and principal uncertainties.

FUZE is developing practical AI SaaS, game, event, market-intelligence, utility-discovery, and permission-controlled work-assistance products supported by reusable platform services. The investment case depends on turning selected workflows into useful products, completed paid delivery, reliable operation, effective distribution, supportable economics, and evidence that shared services create real leverage.

The current public corpus establishes product definitions, intended workflows, architecture direction, policies, status language, and review frameworks. It does not by itself establish that every product is built, live, generating revenue, operating at scale, using FUZE token, or ready for investment. Investors should use the latest scoped status and evidence records rather than infer execution from document volume.

FUZE token, Platform Credits, stablecoin transactions, product revenue, treasury movements, wallet records, and investment proceeds serve different purposes and retain separate classifications.

This FAQ is an orientation document. It is not an offer, forecast, valuation, audit opinion, token sale, exchange announcement, legal opinion, or substitute for qualified diligence and approved private transaction documents.

## 1. Business and Investment Thesis

### What is FUZE?

FUZE is a product-first AI SaaS and Web3 ecosystem. It combines a portfolio of focused products with shared services for accounts, workspaces, roles, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, data controls, wallet-aware records, and reporting.

The [FUZE Investor Overview](01-FUZE_INVESTOR_OVERVIEW_PUBLIC.md) provides the full public thesis.

### What does product-first mean?

FUZE begins with a defined user, a recurring problem, and a complete workflow. Shared infrastructure is developed when products need the same controlled capability. Broader ecosystem, token, wallet, or community functions follow only where they support an approved purpose.

Investors should therefore look first for:

- product completion;
- useful workflow execution;
- user and customer evidence;
- delivery economics;
- support and reliability;
- learning and iteration;
- clear boundaries.

Token or platform claims should not be evaluated in isolation from product evidence.

### Why use a portfolio rather than one product?

Different FUZE products address different audiences, workflows, and distribution routes. Portfolio breadth can create optionality, learning, and shared-service leverage, but it also creates prioritization, coordination, capital, and support risk.

The portfolio case becomes stronger when FUZE can:

- identify a small number of priority workflows;
- complete and support them;
- measure use and paid delivery;
- reuse common services across more than one operating product;
- pause or retire lower-priority work when evidence does not support continuation.

### Which products are included?

The public portfolio includes:

- SpeakShop AI;
- TrainLayer AI;
- CommunityLayer AI;
- ZAGA;
- ZAGA Arena;

The [FUZE AI SaaS Product Index](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/01-FUZE_AI_SAAS_PRODUCT_INDEX_PUBLIC.md) explains each product's audience and workflow.

### What are the strongest potential advantages?

Potential advantages include:

- recognizable user problems across several markets;
- reusable product and control services;
- multiple commercial formats;
- direct, partner, enterprise, community, event, and Web3 distribution routes;
- product-specific entry points into different user groups;
- structured public and private evidence;
- modular commitments that can be expanded, narrowed, or retired.

These remain propositions to test. Documentation alone does not establish competitive advantage.

### What is the main execution challenge?

FUZE must convert a broad portfolio into a focused sequence of complete, supportable products. It must also prove that shared rails are economically and operationally useful across products rather than becoming infrastructure built ahead of actual product demand.

### What would strengthen the investment thesis most?

The strongest improvements would be:

- a small number of priority products with working core workflows;
- real external-user completion evidence;
- completed paid delivery and reconciliation;
- repeat use within the product's natural frequency;
- reliable support and incident handling;
- measurable shared-platform reuse;
- clear ownership and current evidence packs;
- disciplined capital allocation and product prioritization.

## 2. Product Status and Evidence

### Are all FUZE products live?

No. The current public baseline is primarily product or game design and public documentation unless a later approved status record establishes stronger evidence.

The authoritative public baseline appears in the [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md).

### What does a public product paper prove?

A public product paper can establish the approved:

- target user;
- problem;
- workflow;
- product boundary;
- intended features;
- control model;
- risk language.

It does not by itself establish implementation, availability, adoption, reliability, paid delivery, or revenue.

### What evidence should an investor request?

Evidence may include:

- a working demonstration of the core workflow;
- exact product version and environment;
- test scope, results, defects, corrections, and retest;
- release, onboarding, monitoring, and support records;
- defined usage events and cohort reports;
- customer or partner acceptance where permissioned;
- completed paid-delivery and reconciliation records;
- implemented permissions, data, AI, security, and incident controls;
- current status, owner, limitations, and next evidence milestone.

The [FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix](14-FUZE_PRODUCT_STATUS_AND_EVIDENCE_MATRIX_PUBLIC.md) provides the detailed claim-to-evidence framework.

### Is a prototype the same as a live product?

No.

A prototype demonstrates implemented behavior for a defined scope. A live product additionally requires production access, current terms, support, monitoring, incident handling, and operating history.

### How should demonstrations be evaluated?

A useful demonstration identifies:

- product and module;
- version and environment;
- source material;
- participant and role;
- exact steps;
- output;
- permissions and controls;
- manual intervention;
- failures and unsupported cases;
- disposition and reviewer.

Investors should distinguish a prepared example from a workflow executed inside the product.

### What is the difference between internal testing, limited release, beta, and live status?

- **Internal testing** uses authorized internal or controlled participants.
- **Limited release** gives a defined cohort access under specific terms and support.
- **Public beta** provides public access with known limitations and an issue process.
- **Live** means the intended audience can use the product in production under current terms, support, monitoring, and incident controls.

### Can one module's success establish the status of the whole product family?

No. Evidence should remain scoped to the exact product, module, version, environment, and cohort.

A ShopOS AI trial does not establish readiness for SheetLayer AI or CommunityLayer AI. A ZAGA Arena build does not establish readiness for ZAGA Districts.

### How does FUZE report current progress?

FUZE uses scoped status labels and evidence proportionate to the claim. Public metrics should identify:

- definition;
- period;
- cohort;
- source;
- review;
- limitations;
- correction history.

See [FUZE Public Metrics and Transparency](09-FUZE_PUBLIC_METRICS_AND_TRANSPARENCY_PUBLIC.md).

### Can product status move backward?

Yes. A product may move to under review, limited access, paused, suspended, or retired when evidence, incidents, security, privacy, cost, provider dependency, support capacity, legal conditions, or priorities change.

A downgrade is a valid control action and should be reported accurately.

## 5. Platform Credits, Payments, and Stablecoins

### What are Platform Credits?

Platform Credits are product-consumption units for eligible actions. They may support:

- quoting;
- granting;
- purchasing;
- reserving;
- consuming;
- reversing;
- expiring;
- reporting usage.

They remain separate from FUZE token.

Detailed examples appear in [FUZE Platform Credits Usage Examples](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/06-FUZE_PLATFORM_CREDITS_USAGE_EXAMPLES_PUBLIC.md).

### Are Platform Credits an investment asset?

No. Platform Credits are intended as product-consumption units. They should not be presented as equity, a token investment, a claim on revenue, or a promise of future value.

### How are stablecoins used?

Stablecoins may provide operational payment, settlement, treasury, refund, reserve, vendor, or compensation routes where approved.

Each transaction still requires its real business classification, source record, permission, and reconciliation.

### Does a stablecoin balance create a user or holder entitlement?

No. A treasury or wallet balance does not by itself establish ownership, distribution rights, approved distributable value, revenue, or participation eligibility.

### Are product payments, Platform Credits, token activity, and treasury movements reported together?

They may appear in a consolidated operating report, but they should retain separate record classes, definitions, source evidence, and reconciliation.

## 6. FUZE Token and Wallet Participation

### What is FUZE token's role?

FUZE token is the ecosystem token for approved utility and participation functions. A product should use it only when a defined action requires that role and the applicable controls are ready.

### Does every product require FUZE token?

No. Products may provide value through:

- accounts;
- payments;
- subscriptions;
- services;
- Platform Credits.

Token functions should be added only where they serve a material product or ecosystem purpose.

### Does token deployment automatically activate utility?

No. Contract deployment, token initialization, allocation funding, transfer activation, product utility, distribution, market access, custody support, and participation activation are separate events.

### What is wallet-based participation?

Wallet-based participation is an activation-gated framework with separate treatment for:

- eligibility;
- approved distributable value;
- snapshots;
- custody;
- privacy;
- claims;
- corrections;
- governance;
- jurisdiction.

Ordinary token ownership does not automatically satisfy every condition.

See [FUZE Wallet-Based Participation Model](../TOKENOMICS-GOVERNANCE-COMPLIANCE-PAPERS/07-FUZE_WALLET_BASED_PARTICIPATION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

### Does product revenue automatically flow to token holders?

No. Product revenue and approved distributable value are separate concepts.

A participation mechanism, if activated for a defined scope, requires its own:

- source pool;
- approvals;
- eligibility;
- custody treatment;
- reporting;
- claim records;
- correction process.

### Does ordinary token holding create governance rights?

Not automatically. Governance rights, voting, delegation, eligibility, and scope require the applicable approved mechanism and current rules.

## 7. Token Launch and Market Access

### What does “token launch” mean?

FUZE separates:

- contract deployment;
- token initialization;
- allocation funding;
- transfer activation;
- utility activation;
- distribution;
- wallet support;
- custody support;
- DEX access;
- CEX process;
- participation activation.

Completion of one event does not establish the others.

The [FUZE Token Launch Mechanics](13-FUZE_TOKEN_LAUNCH_MECHANICS_PUBLIC.md) provides the detailed framework.

### Is FUZE pursuing DEX-first market access?

FUZE's public direction is decentralized-first.

A DEX route should be described as live only when the verified contract, network, pair, pool, public access route, monitoring, and support are active.

### Is a later CEX process possible?

Possible later CEX consideration depends on FUZE readiness and the venue's independent technical, custody, compliance, legal, commercial, and operational processes.

The following are different statuses:

- inquiry;
- application;
- review;
- integration;
- approval;
- deposit or withdrawal support;
- live market.

### Does a DEX pool or exchange discussion establish liquidity?

No. Availability, depth, spread, volume, permanence, custody, and user access depend on actual market and provider conditions.

FUZE does not promise price support, minimum liquidity, trading volume, or investor exit.

### How are self-custody and exchange custody different?

Self-custody gives the user direct control and responsibility for wallet credentials and transactions.

Exchange or platform custody operates through the custodian's accounts, wallets, terms, policies, and withdrawal support.

Custody treatment may affect address-level records and specialist participation mechanisms. It does not remove privacy obligations or automatically create eligibility.

### How should investors verify token-launch claims?

Investors should request the exact event, network, contract version, transaction references, approval, source verification, supply and allocation reconciliation, active functions, inactive functions, and current incident or correction record.

## 9. Diligence and Data Room

### What is publicly available versus private?

Public papers explain approved positioning, product definitions, status language, operating models, tokenomics policies, and public boundaries.

Qualified diligence may include more detailed product, commercial, financial, technical, governance, partner, token, and risk evidence.

Restricted materials may include:

- personal data;
- customer records;
- private agreements;
- credentials;
- security findings;
- detailed treasury controls;
- legal, tax, accounting, or other professional work;
- transaction-specific documents.

### How is investor readiness assessed?

Readiness connects each material claim to:

- current status;
- proportionate evidence;
- accountable owner;
- risks and controls;
- visible gaps;
- access class;
- review date;
- correction history.

See [FUZE Investor Readiness Summary](11-FUZE_INVESTOR_READINESS_SUMMARY_PUBLIC.md).

### How is the investor data room organized?

The room should route each investor question to:

- a current evidence pack;
- subject owner;
- access class;
- limitations;
- risk treatment;
- gap record;
- version and supersession history.

The [FUZE Investor Data Room Index](18-FUZE_INVESTOR_DATA_ROOM_INDEX_PUBLIC.md) governs the detailed structure.

### Does a large data room prove readiness?

No. Document quantity is not evidence quality.

A data room cannot compensate for missing operating records, unclear status, weak reconciliation, or unresolved ownership.

### How should inconsistencies be handled?

Important claims should be compared across public papers, decks, product records, technical evidence, contracts, tokenomics, financial records, and prior investor answers.

Differences should be explained by scope, period, version, or audience. Incorrect or outdated material should be visibly superseded.

Material decisions should identify:

- scope;
- owner;
- evidence;
- approver;
- dependencies;
- authority;
- effective date;
- monitoring;
- correction or stop route.

Token, treasury, smart-contract, wallet, market, legal, security, and related-party matters may require additional review and multisignature or timelock controls.

### Can products or priorities change?

Yes. FUZE may narrow, pause, combine, replace, or retire products and mechanisms as evidence, costs, dependencies, controls, and priorities develop.

The relevant owner should identify scope, preserve evidence, protect users and controlled assets, pause affected actions where possible, investigate, remediate, reconcile records, and issue appropriate correction or recovery communication.

An incident in one product or mechanism should not be described as affecting unrelated scopes unless evidence supports that conclusion.

## 12. Investor Review Checklist

Before advancing diligence, an investor should ask:

1. Which products and workflows have the strongest current evidence?
2. What status applies to each exact scope?
3. What can be demonstrated today?
4. Which evidence comes from real external users?
5. Which shared rails are implemented and reused?
6. Which offers have completed paid delivery?
7. What are the delivery, AI, infrastructure, payment, and support economics?
8. Which channels have measurable activation and repeat use?
9. Which claims rely on design rather than operation?
10. How are AI, data, security, reliability, and incidents controlled?
11. How are Platform Credits, stablecoins, product payments, token activity, wallet records, and treasury movements separated?
12. Which token, wallet, custody, market, or participation functions are active, prepared, or gated?
13. What will strategic capital fund, and what evidence will demonstrate delivery?
14. Which material gaps, assumptions, or external dependencies remain?
15. Which information is public, qualified, restricted, or transaction-specific?
16. How are status changes, corrections, and superseded records communicated?
17. Which products or mechanisms could be paused, narrowed, or retired?
18. What conclusion is supported today—and what conclusion is not?

The [FUZE Investor Readiness Summary](11-FUZE_INVESTOR_READINESS_SUMMARY_PUBLIC.md) provides the maturity and gap-management framework behind this checklist.
