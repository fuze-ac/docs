# FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix

## Executive Summary

This matrix helps investors compare FUZE products without treating a public paper, design, prototype, release, usage record, or commercial result as equivalent evidence.

The current public corpus defines FUZE products and their intended workflows. At the public-documentation level, the products are primarily in product or game design status. Stronger status requires product-specific evidence such as a working demonstration, controlled test, release record, usage history, support process, or reconciled commercial record.

This paper records the common evidence dimensions and the next evidence expected for each product. It does not repeat full product features, Platform Credit definitions, token mechanics, or consolidated risk language. Those subjects remain in their dedicated papers.

Status is scoped and time-sensitive. A product can have a working module while another module remains in design, and a product can be available to selected testers without being publicly live. Investors should use the latest approved status record and inspect the evidence appropriate to the claim.

---

## 1. Matrix Purpose

The matrix is designed to answer:

1. What is the product’s current public status?
2. Which user workflow is being evaluated?
3. What evidence currently exists?
4. What would justify the next status?
5. Which operating, commercial, data, AI, or support questions remain?
6. Where should a reviewer find the detailed product treatment?

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) owns the public status vocabulary. This paper applies that vocabulary to investor evidence review.

---

## 2. Claim-to-Evidence Rules

| Claim | Evidence expected |
|---|---|
| Product is defined | Approved audience, problem, workflow, outputs, controls, and boundary |
| Design is complete for a scope | Current flow, interface, data, permission, and acceptance design |
| Prototype exists | Reviewable implementation performing the named behavior |
| Internal testing is active | Test plan, authorized testers, results, defects, and owner |
| Limited release exists | Access route, cohort, terms, support, monitoring, and release record |
| Public beta exists | Public access, version notes, known limitations, feedback, and issue process |
| Product is live | Production access, current terms, support, monitoring, and operating history |
| Usage exists | Source records with period, cohort, event definition, and exclusions |
| Paid delivery exists | Customer offer, payment, fulfillment, adjustment, and transaction evidence |
| Revenue is reported | Confirmed classification, reconciliation, period, and review |
| Product is repeatable | Several completed deliveries with stable onboarding, support, and economics |

Evidence should match the exact product, module, cohort, period, and environment named in the claim.

---

## 3. Evidence Dimensions

Each product record can be assessed across nine dimensions.

| Dimension | Review focus |
|---|---|
| Definition | User, problem, workflow, output, and product boundary |
| Build | Implemented behavior, version, environment, and dependencies |
| Quality | Acceptance criteria, defects, evaluations, and human review |
| Access | Eligibility, onboarding, roles, terms, and release scope |
| Use | Activation, completion, repeat use, and user feedback |
| Reliability | Performance, failures, monitoring, incidents, and recovery |
| Commercial | Offer, price or usage basis, payment, fulfillment, and adjustments |
| Control | Data, permissions, AI, security, support, and change ownership |
| Reporting | Metric definitions, source records, period, review, and correction |

A maturity statement should identify which dimensions it covers. A successful user-interface demonstration, for example, may provide build evidence without establishing reliability or commercial readiness.

## 5. HerHelp Product Family Evidence

HerHelp includes the core suite and the SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, and CommunityLayer AI modules.

Investor evidence should keep module results separate. A ShopOS AI operator trial does not establish readiness for SheetLayer AI or CommunityLayer AI.

Useful family-level evidence includes:

- shared account, workspace, permission, credit, AI, and reporting behavior;
- module selection and cross-module onboarding;
- common support and incident ownership;
- product-specific data boundaries;
- cost and performance attribution by module;
- evidence that shared capabilities reduce repeated implementation.

Module evidence should show the real task from input through reviewed output. Samples prepared manually outside the proposed workflow need clear labeling.

## 6. ZAGA Evidence

ZAGA, ZAGA Arena, and ZAGA Districts require separate product records.

### ZAGA Arena

A stronger evidence pack can include a playable build, session start and completion, controls, boss or match behavior, telemetry, multiplayer operation, fair-play controls, defects, support, and player feedback.

### ZAGA Districts

Evidence can cover Telegram onboarding, persistent player and district state, city systems, NPC behavior, quests, community administration, economy simulation, safety, moderation, and recovery from state errors.

Game values, progression, rewards, and community economies should be reported as game-system evidence. Token-connected utility requires its own active scope and records.

## 7. QTB and AIMM Evidence

QTB and AIMM serve specialized market-aware workflows and therefore need stronger source, access, and interpretation controls.

### QTB

Review can examine source selection, timestamps, citation or reference handling, watchlists, research structure, uncertainty language, reviewer evaluation, correction, and output suitability for the stated audience.

### AIMM

Review can examine authorized users, venue data, operational inputs, order-book or liquidity observations, report generation, access logs, human decisions, incident handling, and separation from autonomous trading or price-support claims.

For both products, a polished report is only one evidence layer. Investors should also inspect how inputs were selected, how outputs were reviewed, and what action authority the product has.

---

## 8. AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad Evidence

### AIE

AIE evidence can follow an organizer workflow from event discovery or setup through planning, participant communication, sponsor handling, recap, feedback, and reporting. Records should distinguish generated material from confirmed event facts.

### ToolGrid AI

ToolGrid AI evidence can cover listing intake, utility classification, search or comparison behavior, destination review, sponsorship labeling, campaign delivery, moderation, and reporting. Sponsored placement and editorial or utility assessment should remain distinguishable.

### Botmad

Botmad evidence should begin with bounded tasks. The record can show approved sources, files and tools, permissions, proposed actions, human approval, execution, output, logs, reversal, and final review.

Success in one task class does not authorize broader desktop or account access.

## 9. Demonstration Record

A product demonstration should state:

| Field | Required detail |
|---|---|
| Product and version | Exact product, module, build, and environment |
| Purpose | User problem and behavior being demonstrated |
| Input | Representative, synthetic, or permissioned source material |
| Participant | Operator, reviewer, user type, or partner role |
| Steps | Start, product actions, review, and completion |
| Output | Artifact, workflow result, game session, report, or completed action |
| Controls | Permissions, AI review, data treatment, and action limits |
| Exceptions | Failures, manual work, known defects, or unsupported cases |
| Evidence | Recording, logs, screenshots, reports, or test references |
| Disposition | Pass, revise, retest, pilot, pause, or close |

A demonstration can establish implemented behavior for its scope. It should not be presented as user adoption or commercial evidence unless those records also exist.

## 10. Test and Release Evidence

Internal testing should identify the intended behavior, test cases, environment, participants, results, defects, severity, correction, and retest.

A limited release needs additional evidence:

- release owner and effective period;
- eligible cohort and access route;
- terms and user instructions;
- onboarding and first-completion behavior;
- monitoring and service support;
- privacy, security, and AI controls;
- payment or Platform Credit behavior where active;
- issue, pause, and offboarding route;
- status report at the end of the period.

Public beta and live status require a wider operating basis. The evidence should show that the actual intended audience can access the product and that FUZE can monitor and support the service under current terms.

---

## 11. Usage and Retention Evidence

Usage metrics need a defined event and cohort.

Relevant distinctions include:

- account created versus qualified user;
- onboarding started versus completed;
- workflow started versus useful output accepted;
- first use versus repeat use;
- invited test versus public access;
- internal or promotional activity versus customer activity;
- automated retries versus separate user actions.

Retention should match the product’s natural frequency. A daily shop operation, occasional event report, game session, and periodic training workflow require different review windows.

Definitions, evidence grades, audience classes, and correction controls for reported measures are maintained in [FUZE Public Metrics and Transparency](09-FUZE_PUBLIC_METRICS_AND_TRANSPARENCY_PUBLIC.md).

## 12. Commercial Evidence

Product and revenue status should be reviewed independently.

| Commercial stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Offer defined | Customer, service, scope, price basis, terms, and support |
| Flow tested | Quote, order, payment, fulfillment, adjustment, and reporting work in a controlled environment |
| Paid delivery | A customer payment is matched to the promised product or service |
| Repeated delivery | Several transactions use a supportable workflow |
| Reconciled period | Payments, refunds, disputes, costs, and classifications are reviewed for a period |

Platform Credit activity can support product-consumption evidence where a product uses credits. It should be measured through the applicable grant, purchase, reservation, consumption, reversal, and balance records.

The [FUZE Product Revenue Model](02-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) owns the full commercial classification.

## 13. Control Evidence

Control evidence should match product consequence.

### Data and permissions

Review the data inventory, purpose, role, action, provider, retention, deletion, export, and incident behavior for the workflow.

### AI

Review sources, model and tool routing, prompts or instructions, output controls, evaluations, human authority, error handling, and product-specific incidents.

### Security and reliability

Review environments, dependencies, access, testing, monitoring, availability, performance, backup, recovery, and support.

### Public communication

Review product status, claims, samples, customer or partner references, metric definitions, and corrections.

Control design, configured controls, test results, and operating evidence should remain distinguishable.

---

## 14. Evidence Register

Each product can maintain an evidence register with:

- stable evidence ID;
- product, module, version, and environment;
- claim or status supported;
- evidence type and date;
- cohort and period;
- owner and reviewer;
- source location and access class;
- limitations and open defects;
- related metric or commercial record;
- supersession or correction status;
- next evidence required.

The register lets investors locate current evidence without receiving every internal record. It also prevents an old screenshot, demonstration, or status report from circulating as the latest product position.

---

## 15. Status Update Process

A material status change should:

1. identify the exact product, module, and environment;
2. state the prior and proposed status;
3. cite evidence matching the new claim;
4. record unresolved limitations and dependencies;
5. confirm product, technical, support, data, security, commercial, and communication readiness as applicable;
6. obtain the required approval;
7. update public and private indexes;
8. preserve the previous record and correction history.

Products can move backward to paused or under-review status when evidence, incidents, cost, support, or priorities change.

---

## 16. Investor Review Questions

For each priority product, investors can ask:

- Which workflow is currently strongest?
- What can be demonstrated in the product today?
- Which status applies to that exact scope?
- What evidence was produced by real users rather than internal preparation?
- How large and how selected was the cohort?
- What failed, required manual work, or remains unsupported?
- What does it cost to deliver and support the workflow?
- Which shared platform services are implemented?
- How are data, permissions, AI, security, and incidents handled?
- Is there paid delivery or reconciled commercial evidence?
- What evidence is required for the next status?
- Who can pause, correct, or retire the product?

The answers should make strengths and gaps visible at the same time.

---

## 17. Public Boundary

This matrix is a public evidence framework, not a launch announcement, customer list, traction report, financial statement, forecast, or certification.

Customer identities, contracts, workspace content, payment records, partner terms, credentials, private test findings, and other protected evidence remain within their authorized review process.

Product evidence does not by itself establish future adoption, revenue, token demand, market access, or investment performance. Consolidated product risks remain in [FUZE Product Risk Boundaries](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/16-FUZE_PRODUCT_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md).

---

## Conclusion

FUZE product status should advance only when product-specific evidence supports the stronger claim. The current public baseline is primarily design and documentation; demonstrations, tests, releases, usage, commercial records, and controls provide the path to stronger operating status.

This matrix gives investors a consistent way to compare that evidence while preserving the different users, workflows, risks, and release paths of each FUZE product.
