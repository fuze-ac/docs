# FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix

## Executive Summary

This matrix helps investors compare FUZE products without treating a public paper, design, prototype, demonstration, release, usage record, paid delivery, or reconciled commercial result as equivalent evidence.

The current public corpus defines FUZE products, intended users, workflows, controls, and boundaries. At the public-documentation level, the portfolio is primarily in product or game design status unless a later approved status record cites stronger product-specific evidence.

Stronger status requires evidence proportionate to the claim. Examples include a reviewable build, controlled test, limited release, production record, real-user workflow completion, support history, reliability record, paid fulfillment, or reconciled commercial evidence.

Status is scoped and time-sensitive. A product may have one demonstrable module while another remains in design. A product may be available to selected testers without being publicly live. One product's evidence does not establish readiness for another product, and one successful workflow does not establish full-product maturity.

This paper defines claim-to-evidence rules, maturity dimensions, product-specific evidence expectations, evidence-register requirements, status-change controls, and investor review questions. It does not replace the detailed product papers, public status matrix, commercial model, Platform Credit policies, token papers, or consolidated risk disclosures.

## 1. Purpose and Primary Readers

This paper is written for investors, strategic partners, product owners, technical and security reviewers, commercial and finance owners, governance reviewers, and diligence coordinators.

It is designed to answer:

1. What is the product's current approved public status?
2. Which product, module, version, workflow, environment, and cohort does the status cover?
3. Which evidence currently exists?
4. What does that evidence establish—and what does it not establish?
5. What would justify the next status?
6. Which operating, commercial, data, AI, security, reliability, or support questions remain?
7. Where should a reviewer find the detailed product treatment and source evidence?

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) governs the public status vocabulary. This paper applies that vocabulary to investor evidence review.

## 2. Current Public Position

The approved public-paper corpus provides a documentation baseline for FUZE's product families and specialist tools.

Publication establishes that the products and intended models are documented. It does not by itself establish:

- implemented software;
- production access;
- public launch;
- external-user adoption;
- reliable operation;
- paid delivery;
- recurring use;
- repeatable onboarding;
- reconciled revenue;
- implemented shared-platform reuse;
- token utility activation;
- customer or partner deployment.

A stronger status should be supported by the latest approved evidence for the exact scope named in the claim.

The matrix should change only when the status-change process cites current supporting evidence and records unresolved limitations.

## 3. Claim-to-Evidence Rules

| Claim | Minimum evidence expected |
|---|---|
| Product is defined | Approved audience, problem, workflow, outputs, controls, boundaries, and owner |
| Design is complete for a scope | Current workflow, interface, data, permission, dependency, and acceptance design |
| Prototype exists | Reviewable implementation performing the named behavior in a stated environment |
| Demonstration passed | Versioned demo record showing inputs, steps, controls, output, exceptions, and disposition |
| Internal testing is active | Test plan, authorized testers, environment, results, defects, owner, and retest process |
| Limited release exists | Access route, cohort, terms, support, monitoring, controls, and release record |
| Public beta exists | Public access, version notes, known limitations, support, feedback, issue, and pause process |
| Product is live | Production access, current terms, support, monitoring, incident process, and operating history |
| Usage exists | Source records with event definition, cohort, period, environment, and exclusions |
| Repeat use exists | Defined returning cohort and repeated completion across the product's natural use cycle |
| Paid delivery exists | Customer offer, payment, fulfillment, adjustment, support, and transaction evidence |
| Revenue is reported | Confirmed classification, reconciliation, reporting period, owner, and review |
| Product is repeatable | Several completed deliveries using stable onboarding, fulfillment, support, controls, and economics |
| Shared platform reuse exists | More than one active product uses the named rail under defined ownership and service controls |

Evidence should match the exact product, module, build, cohort, period, environment, and business purpose named in the claim.

Evidence from internal staff, demonstrations, synthetic data, promotional users, unpaid pilots, and paying customers should remain separately classified.

## 4. Status and Readiness Are Different

Product status and investor readiness answer different questions.

- **Product status** describes what the product currently is and where it is available.
- **Evidence maturity** describes the strength of records supporting that status.
- **Investor readiness** describes whether the evidence, ownership, risks, gaps, and access process are organized for review.

A product may be demonstrable but not commercially ready. A live product may have incomplete reconciliation. A documented product may still be diligence-ready for its current early stage if the evidence and gaps are clear.

The [FUZE Investor Readiness Summary](11-FUZE_INVESTOR_READINESS_SUMMARY_PUBLIC.md) governs readiness levels and evidence-pack standards.

## 5. Evidence Dimensions

Each product record should be assessed across ten dimensions.

| Dimension | Review focus |
|---|---|
| Definition | User, buyer, problem, workflow, output, owner, and product boundary |
| Build | Implemented behavior, version, environment, dependencies, and deployment state |
| Quality | Acceptance criteria, defects, evaluations, human review, and unsupported cases |
| Access | Eligibility, onboarding, authentication, roles, terms, and release scope |
| Use | Activation, completion, accepted output, repeat use, and user feedback |
| Reliability | Performance, failure, monitoring, incident, recovery, support, and continuity |
| Commercial | Offer, price or usage basis, order, payment, fulfillment, refund, and adjustment |
| Control | Data, permissions, AI, security, change authority, and public communication |
| Economics | Delivery cost, provider cost, support burden, margin basis, and scale assumptions |
| Reporting | Metric definitions, source records, period, review, version, and correction |

A maturity statement should identify which dimensions it covers.

A successful user-interface demonstration may provide build evidence without establishing reliability, adoption, commercial readiness, or repeatable delivery.

A ShopOS AI operator trial does not establish readiness for SheetLayer AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, or CommunityLayer AI.

Family-level evidence may include:

- shared account and workspace behavior;
- roles and permissions;
- Platform Credit handling where active;
- AI routing and provider controls;
- module selection and cross-module onboarding;
- support and incident ownership;
- product-specific data boundaries;
- reporting and cost attribution by module;
- evidence that shared rails reduce repeated implementation.

Module evidence should show the real task from input through reviewed output.

## 9. QTB and AIMM Evidence

QTB and AIMM serve market-aware workflows and therefore require stronger source, access, interpretation, and authority controls.

### 9.1 QTB

Review may examine:

- approved sources;
- timestamps and freshness;
- citation or reference handling;
- watchlists and user scope;
- research structure;
- uncertainty language;
- reviewer evaluation;
- correction history;
- suitability for the stated audience;
- separation from financial advice or autonomous execution.

### 9.2 AIMM

Review may examine:

- authorized users;
- venue and market data;
- operational inputs;
- order-book or liquidity observations;
- report generation;
- access logs;
- human decisions and approvals;
- incident and escalation handling;
- provider dependencies;
- separation from autonomous trading, guaranteed liquidity, or price-support claims.

For both products, a polished report is only one evidence layer.

Investors should also inspect how inputs were selected, which limitations apply, how outputs were reviewed, and what action authority the product does or does not have.

## 11. Demonstration Evidence

A product demonstration should record:

| Field | Required detail |
|---|---|
| Evidence ID | Stable identifier for the record |
| Product and version | Exact product, module, build, and environment |
| Purpose | User problem and behavior being demonstrated |
| Input | Representative, synthetic, public, or permissioned source material |
| Participant | Operator, reviewer, user type, customer, or partner role |
| Steps | Start, product actions, review, and completion |
| Output | Artifact, workflow result, game session, report, or completed action |
| Controls | Permissions, AI review, security, data treatment, and action limits |
| Exceptions | Failures, manual work, known defects, unsupported cases, or workarounds |
| Evidence | Recording, logs, screenshots, reports, builds, or test references |
| Disposition | Pass, revise, retest, pilot, pause, or close |
| Reviewer | Owner and reviewer approving the conclusion |

A demonstration can establish implemented behavior for its exact scope.

It should not be presented as:

- external-user adoption;
- reliable production operation;
- paid delivery;
- recurring usage;
- reconciled revenue

unless those separate records also exist.

## 12. Test and Release Evidence

### 12.1 Internal testing

Internal testing should identify:

- intended behavior;
- acceptance criteria;
- test cases;
- environment;
- participants;
- results;
- defects and severity;
- correction;
- retest;
- unresolved limitations.

### 12.2 Limited release

A limited release should additionally identify:

- release owner;
- effective period;
- eligible cohort;
- access route;
- terms and user instructions;
- onboarding and first-completion behavior;
- monitoring and support;
- privacy, security, and AI controls;
- payment or Platform Credit behavior where active;
- issue, pause, offboarding, and closure route;
- end-of-period status report.

### 12.3 Public beta and live release

Public beta and live status require a wider operating basis.

Evidence should show that the actual intended audience can access the product and that FUZE can:

- operate the current version;
- monitor performance and failures;
- support users;
- manage incidents;
- maintain current terms and privacy notices;
- publish known limitations;
- correct or pause the service.

## 13. Usage, Completion, and Retention Evidence

Usage metrics require a defined event, product version, cohort, environment, period, and exclusions.

Relevant distinctions include:

- registered account versus qualified user;
- invitation versus accepted access;
- onboarding started versus completed;
- workflow started versus completed;
- output generated versus reviewed or accepted;
- first use versus repeat use;
- invited test versus public access;
- staff, promotional, partner, and customer activity;
- automated retries versus separate user actions;
- test and production records.

A meaningful product event should connect to user value rather than only page views, clicks, prompts, or background processing.

Retention should match the product's natural frequency.

A daily shop workflow, occasional event report, multiplayer game session, monthly business review, and periodic training workflow require different review windows.

Metric definitions, evidence levels, audience classes, privacy rules, and correction controls are governed by [FUZE Public Metrics and Transparency](09-FUZE_PUBLIC_METRICS_AND_TRANSPARENCY_PUBLIC.md).

## 14. Commercial Evidence

Product status and commercial status should be reviewed independently.

| Commercial stage | Evidence expected |
|---|---|
| Offer defined | Customer, service, scope, price basis, terms, delivery obligation, and support |
| Flow tested | Quote, order, payment, fulfillment, adjustment, refund, and reporting work in a controlled environment |
| Payment received | Receipt and settlement record with correct business classification |
| Paid delivery | Customer payment is matched to the promised product or service and fulfillment record |
| Repeated delivery | Several transactions use a supportable workflow with stable ownership and controls |
| Reconciled period | Payments, refunds, disputes, costs, taxes where applicable, and classifications are reviewed for a named period |

Payment receipt does not by itself establish fulfilled delivery or revenue.

Stablecoin settlement is a payment rail and does not change the underlying commercial classification.

Platform Credit activity may support product-consumption evidence where a product uses credits. It should be reviewed through applicable:

- grant;
- purchase;
- reservation;
- consumption;
- reversal;
- expiry;
- balance records.

Platform Credits remain separate from FUZE token activity and investment proceeds.

The [FUZE Product Revenue Model](02-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) governs the full commercial classification.

- user and service roles;
- allowed actions;
- providers;
- retention and deletion;
- export and portability;
- incident behavior;
- public-reporting treatment.

### 15.2 AI

Review may include:

- sources;
- model and provider routing;
- prompts or instructions;
- tool access;
- output controls;
- evaluations;
- human authority;
- error handling;
- escalation;
- product-specific incidents.

### 15.3 Security and reliability

Review may include:

- environments;
- dependencies;
- authentication and authorization;
- testing;
- monitoring;
- performance;
- availability;
- failure handling;
- backup and recovery;
- provider fallback;
- support and incident history.

### 15.4 Public communication

Review may include:

- current product status;
- claims and samples;
- customer or partner references;
- metric definitions;
- version dates;
- corrections and supersession.

Control design, configured controls, test evidence, and sustained operating evidence should remain separately classified.

## 16. Shared Platform Evidence

A shared platform claim should identify:

- named rail or service;
- product using it;
- second product using it;
- interfaces and dependencies;
- service owner;
- environment;
- reliability and support model;
- data and permission boundaries;
- cost allocation;
- migration and versioning;
- incident and change process.

A common codebase, roadmap, diagram, or planned API does not by itself establish shared-platform reuse.

Reuse becomes stronger evidence when multiple active products depend on the capability and the service is owned, monitored, supported, and versioned.

## 17. Evidence Register

Each product should maintain an evidence register with:

- stable evidence ID;
- product and module;
- version and environment;
- claim or status supported;
- evidence type;
- evidence date;
- cohort and period;
- owner and reviewer;
- source location;
- access class;
- limitations and open defects;
- related metric, incident, support, or commercial record;
- supersession or correction status;
- next evidence required.

The register allows investors to locate current evidence without receiving every internal record.

It also prevents an old screenshot, recording, demonstration, prototype, or status report from circulating as the latest product position.

## 18. Status Update and Downgrade Process

A material status change should:

1. identify the exact product, module, version, environment, workflow, and cohort;
2. state the prior and proposed status;
3. cite evidence matching the stronger claim;
4. record unresolved limitations, defects, dependencies, and exceptions;
5. confirm product, technical, support, data, security, commercial, finance, and communication readiness as applicable;
6. identify owner and reviewer;
7. obtain required approval;
8. update public and private indexes;
9. preserve the previous record and correction history;
10. define the next review trigger.

Products may move backward to:

- under review;
- limited access;
- paused;
- suspended;
- retired

when evidence, incidents, security, privacy, cost, support capacity, legal conditions, provider dependency, or priorities change.

A downgrade is a valid control action and should be reported accurately rather than hidden through unchanged launch language.

## 19. Investor Review Questions

For each priority product, investors should ask:

- Which workflow is currently strongest?
- What can be demonstrated in the product today?
- Which product, module, version, environment, and cohort does the status cover?
- What evidence was produced by real external users rather than internal preparation?
- How large and how selected was the cohort?
- What failed, required manual work, or remains unsupported?
- Which controls are designed, configured, tested, or operating?
- What does it cost to deliver and support the workflow?
- Which shared platform services are actually implemented and used?
- How are data, permissions, AI, security, reliability, and incidents handled?
- Is there paid delivery, repeated delivery, or reconciled commercial evidence?
- What evidence is required for the next status?
- Which dependencies could block or reverse progress?
- Who can pause, correct, downgrade, or retire the product?
- When was the evidence last reviewed?

The answers should make both strengths and unresolved work visible.

## 20. Public Reporting

A public product-status report may identify:

- product and module;
- current public status;
- version and environment;
- evidence period;
- evidence class;
- current access scope;
- major active workflow;
- known limitations;
- next evidence milestone;
- correction or supersession status.

Public reporting should not expose:

- customer identities or workspace content;
- private partner terms;
- personal data;
- credentials or security detail;
- restricted test findings;
- confidential contracts;
- private payment records;
- unsupported adoption, revenue, or market claims.

## 21. Public Boundary

This matrix is a public evidence framework.

It is not:

- a product launch announcement;
- customer list;
- traction report;
- audited usage statement;
- financial statement;
- forecast;
- certification;
- assurance opinion;
- representation that every product is built or live.

Customer identities, contracts, workspace content, payment records, partner terms, credentials, private test findings, and other protected evidence remain within their authorized review process.

Product evidence does not by itself establish:

- future adoption;
- revenue growth;
- profitability;
- token demand;
- exchange access;
- liquidity;
- valuation;
- investment return.

Consolidated product risks remain in [FUZE Product Risk Boundaries](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/16-FUZE_PRODUCT_RISK_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md).

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE product status should advance only when product-specific evidence supports the stronger claim.
- The current public baseline is primarily design and documentation unless a later approved status record establishes otherwise.
- Status must be scoped by product, module, version, environment, workflow, cohort, and period.
- Demonstration, testing, limited release, public beta, live operation, usage, paid delivery, and reconciled revenue are different evidence stages.
- One product's evidence does not establish readiness for another product or module.
- Platform Credits, stablecoin payments, FUZE token activity, product usage, and revenue remain separate record categories.
- Shared platform reuse requires operating adoption by more than one product, not only common design or code.
- Products may be downgraded, paused, or retired when current evidence or controls no longer support the prior status.
