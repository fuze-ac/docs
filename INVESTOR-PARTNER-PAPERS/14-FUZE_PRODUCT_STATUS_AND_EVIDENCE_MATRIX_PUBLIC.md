# FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix

## Executive Summary

FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix explains how FUZE organizes product readiness, evidence categories, status labels, demos, revenue readiness, Platform Credit relationship, AI risk boundaries, data sensitivity, token utility connection, and investor-review evidence across the FUZE product ecosystem.

FUZE is a transparency-first AI SaaS platform building practical products on shared infrastructure for identity, credits, payments, AI orchestration, wallet-based records, reporting, and ecosystem participation.

FUZE follows product-first execution. Product usage comes first, platform rails come second, and broader ecosystem participation comes after that.

FUZE uses one token only: FUZE token.

FUZE token is the single ecosystem token of FUZE. It supports product-connected utility, ecosystem participation, platform alignment, governance direction where applicable, and wallet-based participation ability.

A product status and evidence matrix helps investors, partners, and community members separate product concept, product design, prototype readiness, demo evidence, limited release, public launch, revenue readiness, reporting readiness, and risk boundaries.

The matrix is not a growth claim.

The matrix is not a revenue guarantee.

The matrix is not a token demand forecast.

The matrix is not a listing, liquidity, payout, acquisition, exit, or investment-return promise.

Platform Credits are product usage credits and are separate from FUZE token.

Stablecoins are payment, settlement, treasury, and compensation rails.

Wallet-based participation ability is not a second token, not automatic for every wallet, and not active as a guaranteed public payout right.

FUZE does not guarantee product launch timing, product adoption, product revenue, business revenue, customer growth, user growth, community growth, AI accuracy, token demand, token price, listing, liquidity, market support, trading volume, payout, income, yield, profit, acquisition, exit, game earnings, smart-contract security, or investment return.

## 2. FUZE Product Evidence Position

FUZE’s public product evidence position is:

FUZE product readiness should be shown through clear status labels, evidence categories, product-specific records, demos, user workflows, revenue-readiness categories, data and AI risk boundaries, and public-safe reporting.

This position has eight parts.

| Area | FUZE Position |
|---|---|
| Product status | Each product should have a clear readiness status. |
| Evidence categories | Evidence should be separated into paper, demo, prototype, user workflow, product usage, payment, and reporting evidence. |
| Product-specific review | Each product should be reviewed by its own purpose and user group. |
| Revenue readiness | Revenue paths should be identified separately from actual confirmed revenue. |
| Credit relationship | Platform Credit usage should be shown where relevant without turning credits into tokens. |
| Token relationship | FUZE token utility relationship should be explained without payout or price promises. |
| Risk boundaries | Product risks should be stated by product type. |
| Privacy and reporting | Public evidence should protect customer, user, investor, partner, and private verification data. |

The matrix exists to make FUZE reviewable.

It does not exist to overstate readiness.

## 3. Why a Product Status and Evidence Matrix Matters

FUZE has multiple products across AI SaaS, shops, communities, games, market interpretation, liquidity operations, event intelligence, tool visibility, and AI desktop work assistance.

Without a matrix, readers may confuse:

- public paper completion with product launch
- product design with working prototype
- prototype with public product
- limited release with full public launch
- usage evidence with revenue evidence
- revenue category with approved distributable value
- Platform Credit usage with token rights
- ZAGA game mechanics with financial earnings
- QTB market interpretation with financial advice
- AIMM liquidity reporting with price support
- smart-contract readiness with claim activation

The matrix helps prevent these misunderstandings.

Boundary:

A product evidence matrix supports investor review, but it does not guarantee product adoption, revenue, token demand, liquidity, listing, exit, or return.

## 4. Product Status Labels

FUZE can use clear product status labels to show readiness.

| Status Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concept | Product idea, positioning, and user problem are defined. |
| Research | Market, user, technical, or operational research is underway. |
| Design | Product flow, screens, data model, or workflow design is prepared. |
| Prototype | A working or partial test version exists. |
| Internal Testing | The product is being tested privately. |
| Limited Release | A controlled user group or partner group can use the product. |
| Public Beta | Public users can access a test version with known limitations. |
| Live | Product is publicly available for intended users. |
| Paused | Product work or access is paused. |
| Under Review | Product scope, risk, legal, technical, or revenue treatment is being reviewed. |
| Activation-Gated | Feature requires legal, accounting, treasury, audit, reporting, smart-contract, privacy, eligibility, operator, or jurisdiction gates before activation. |
| Deprecated | Product, feature, or language has been replaced. |

Status labels should be updated when evidence changes.

Boundary:

A status label is not a guarantee of delivery date, adoption, revenue, or market result.

## 7. Evidence Maturity Levels

FUZE can describe evidence maturity using clear levels.

| Level | Evidence Maturity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Paper Ready | Public explanation exists. |
| Level 2 | Design Ready | Workflow, scope, or product design exists. |
| Level 3 | Demo Ready | Demo, prototype, sample output, or mock workflow exists. |
| Level 4 | Test Ready | Internal or limited testing can occur. |
| Level 5 | Usage Ready | Real users or partners can use the product in a controlled way. |
| Level 6 | Revenue Ready | Pricing, billing, credits, or package logic is prepared. |
| Level 7 | Revenue Evidence | Confirmed payment or revenue records exist. |
| Level 8 | Reporting Ready | Public-safe metrics, dashboards, or report hashes can be produced. |
| Level 9 | Review Ready | Evidence can be reviewed in investor, partner, legal, or data-room context. |

Not every product needs to reach every level at the same time.

Boundary:

Evidence maturity does not guarantee product-market fit, revenue, or return.

## 20. Product Evidence and Revenue Separation

Product evidence and revenue evidence are different.

Product evidence may show:

- the product exists
- the workflow is defined
- a demo is available
- a user can complete a task
- a report can be generated
- a module can function
- a product has a roadmap
- a product has public documentation

Revenue evidence may show:

- a customer paid
- a subscription was created
- Platform Credits were purchased
- a report was sold
- a service was invoiced
- a stablecoin payment was received
- a package was purchased
- payment was reconciled
- revenue was classified

A product can have product evidence without revenue evidence.

A product can have revenue readiness without confirmed revenue.

Boundary:

Product evidence does not automatically prove revenue, and revenue evidence does not automatically create approved distributable value.

Product evidence does not create payout rights, claim rights, approved distributable value, or wallet-based participation distributions.

## 28. Development Direction

FUZE product status and evidence matrix can develop through phases.

### Phase 1: Product Inventory

List all FUZE products, product categories, target users, and product boundaries.

### Phase 2: Status Label Assignment

Assign concept, research, design, prototype, internal testing, limited release, public beta, live, paused, under review, activation-gated, or deprecated status where appropriate.

Collect product papers, demos, screenshots, workflows, sample reports, prototype records, usage records, partner records, and public-safe metrics.

### Phase 4: Revenue Readiness Mapping

Map pricing, Platform Credit usage, subscriptions, usage fees, package models, service fees, report fees, and revenue evidence where available.

### Phase 5: Risk and Data Mapping

Map AI risk, data sensitivity, privacy controls, permission needs, product-specific boundaries, and public communication risks.

### Phase 6: Public-Safe Reporting

Prepare public-safe product evidence summaries, status matrices, report hashes, dashboards, and correction notes.

### Phase 7: Investor Data-Room Alignment

Organize deeper evidence by product for private investor diligence.

### Phase 8: Matrix Update Process

Update matrix status when demos, releases, revenue records, customer records, partner records, risk reviews, or product decisions change.

### Phase 9: Ongoing Evidence Review

Continue improving the matrix as FUZE products, users, partners, revenue records, legal review, AI safety, privacy controls, and reporting mature.

These phases are development directions, not guaranteed launch dates.
