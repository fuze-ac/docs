# FUZE Web3 Community Expansion

## Executive Summary

FUZE can expand through Web3 communities by helping them operate, learn, play, coordinate, and report. The entry point is a community task rather than token promotion: handling repeated questions, preparing updates, moderating activity, organizing events, producing market-context material, running a game session, discovering useful tools, or converting community work into reviewed outputs.

Telegram is a primary operating environment because many Web3 groups already organize there. FUZE products can support distinct parts of that environment: CommunityLayer AI for administration, ZAGA for games and community programs, AIE for events, QTB for bounded market research, AIMM for authorized operations reporting, ToolGrid AI for utility discovery, TrainLayer AI for education, SpeakShop AI for approved announcements, and Botmad for permission-controlled work.

Expansion follows a controlled loop: qualify the community, select one workflow, define operator authority, configure roles and data boundaries, activate a limited program, support moderators and users, measure useful participation, and decide whether to repeat, widen, revise, pause, or close the scope.

Wallet records and token-connected features appear only when an approved program needs them. Platform Credits remain product-usage credits. Stablecoins remain operational payment, settlement, treasury, or compensation rails. Public community reporting can use aggregate or wallet-level evidence while keeping personal identity, private messages, moderation records, operator data, and sensitive commercial information permissioned.

This paper defines the public operating model for Web3 community expansion. It does not confirm current communities, live campaigns, product adoption, token demand, market access, revenue, or investment outcomes.

## 1. Purpose and Primary Readers

This paper is written for community operators, moderators, ecosystem teams, game communities, strategic partners, investors, product owners, and reviewers.

It explains:

1. how FUZE qualifies a community opportunity;
2. which products may fit which community needs;
3. how Telegram-native programs should be structured;
4. how operator authority, moderation, data, and support are controlled;
5. how games, research, events, education, utility discovery, and work assistance can enter a community;
6. how Platform Credits, payments, wallets, and token-connected features remain separated;
7. what evidence supports continuation or expansion;
8. when a community program should be revised, paused, or closed.

The [FUZE Go-To-Market](03-FUZE_GO_TO_MARKET_PUBLIC.md) paper governs the wider channel model. The [FUZE Strategic Partner Model](04-FUZE_STRATEGIC_PARTNER_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) governs partner-led programs. Product-specific behavior remains in the relevant product papers.

## 2. Current Public Position

FUZE's public documents define community use cases, product roles, operating controls, wallet boundaries, moderation principles, and evidence requirements.

They do not by themselves prove:

- that a community program is active;
- that a Telegram bot, Mini App, game, or workspace is live;
- that moderators or members have adopted a product;
- that a wallet requirement or token-connected function is active;
- that a sponsored, educational, research, event, or game campaign has launched;
- that partner or operator responsibilities have been approved;
- that participation, retention, payment, revenue, or market outcomes exist;
- that public community metrics have been reconciled.

A stronger claim requires dated evidence for the named community, product, program, cohort, period, operator, status, and reporting method.

## 3. Community Expansion Objective

The objective is to create sustained product use inside communities with accountable operators and measurable member value.

A community program should identify:

1. community type and purpose;
2. operator, moderator, and decision authority;
3. member problem or desired activity;
4. FUZE product and exact workflow;
5. eligible audience and program period;
6. onboarding and support;
7. moderation and escalation;
8. data, wallet, and privacy boundaries;
9. commercial or sponsor treatment where applicable;
10. evidence and continuation criteria;
11. closure and offboarding route.

Member count alone is a weak qualification signal. A smaller community with active operators, a clear need, reliable moderation, and a support process may be more suitable than a large audience with unclear ownership.

## 4. Community Qualification

Qualification should assess:

- community purpose and current platform;
- active audience rather than headline membership;
- operator and moderator capacity;
- recurring member or admin problem;
- language, geography, and time-zone needs;
- current rules and moderation process;
- product and technical fit;
- data sensitivity and access requirements;
- wallet or token expectations;
- support and escalation capacity;
- sponsor, partner, or commercial dependencies;
- public-communication risk;
- incident and offboarding readiness.

A community opportunity may be deferred or rejected when:

- no accountable operator exists;
- the program depends mainly on speculative token promotion;
- product scope or status is unclear;
- moderation and support are insufficient;
- requested access is disproportionate;
- wallet, identity, market, or financial claims exceed approved evidence;
- incentives are likely to create low-quality or abusive activity;
- data, legal, jurisdiction, or security risks cannot be controlled.

| Project community | Updates, support, moderation, product education | CommunityLayer AI, TrainLayer AI, Botmad |
| Token operations community | Clear market and liquidity communication | QTB, AIMM, CommunityLayer AI |

The initial product mix should remain narrow. A community does not need every FUZE product to begin.

## 6. Community Operating Journey

### 6.1 Qualification

Record the community's purpose, platform, active audience, operator team, current problems, language, geography, moderation process, support route, and technical constraints.

### 6.2 Workflow selection

Choose one frequent or valuable task, such as:

- weekly admin summary;
- event briefing;
- recurring research note;
- game session;
- utility directory;
- onboarding module;
- moderation-assistance queue;
- campaign or sponsor report.

### 6.3 Program design

Define:

- audience and eligibility;
- product and workflow;
- operator and moderator roles;
- permitted sources;
- data and retention boundaries;
- output audience;
- support contacts;
- Platform Credit budget where applicable;
- wallet or token requirements where approved;
- success, pause, and closure criteria.

### 6.4 Operator trial

Moderators or program owners use the workflow before member-wide access. The trial checks instructions, output quality, permissions, reliability, failure handling, support, and escalation.

### 6.5 Controlled activation

Invite the defined cohort or launch the named program. Public instructions should state current scope, rules, status, support, and any product, wallet, or payment requirements.

### 6.6 Evidence review

Assess:

- participation;
- completed actions;
- first-value completion;
- operator time;
- moderation and support demand;
- incidents;
- repeat use;
- member and moderator feedback;
- commercial or sponsor status.

### 6.7 Decision

Choose one outcome:

- continue;
- repeat with changes;
- expand;
- narrow;
- pause;
- close.

A community program can remain useful at a limited size without becoming a platform-wide feature.

## 7. Telegram-Native Design

Telegram workflows should fit the speed and social structure of the channel.

Useful design principles include:

- short commands and clear menus;
- visible official accounts and administrators;
- concise status and error messages;
- private handling for sensitive reports or support;
- links to current public instructions;
- moderator approval for member-impacting actions;
- separation of chat content from authoritative records;
- rate limits and anti-abuse controls;
- language and time-zone support;
- recovery when a bot or Mini App is unavailable;
- clear distinction between private chat, group chat, Mini App, and external product state.

A Telegram message can initiate or display a task, but significant product state should have a reliable underlying record.

Operator access, wallet signatures, payments, game actions, moderation decisions, and product entitlements need stronger evidence than ordinary chat messages.

Integrations should request the minimum permissions necessary and explain why each permission is needed.

## 8. Community Operations With CommunityLayer AI

CommunityLayer AI can support:

- recurring discussion summaries;
- FAQ and support-draft preparation;
- admin briefings;
- moderation-assistance queues;
- announcement drafts;
- onboarding and rule reminders;
- issue categorization;
- event or campaign recaps;
- public-safe activity reports.

The community owner defines policy. AI output can organize evidence and prepare suggestions, while authorized moderators decide warnings, removals, escalations, verification, appeals, and public statements.

A useful operating record distinguishes:

1. content observed;
2. AI classification, summary, or draft;
3. moderator review;
4. action taken;
5. correction or appeal where applicable.

This separation prevents an automated suggestion from being treated as final authority.

## 9. Game and Community Programs

### 9.1 ZAGA Arena

Arena programs may use scheduled sessions, community rooms, boss challenges, run summaries, leaderboards, share cards, or reviewed events.

The program owner should define:

- eligibility;
- session rules;
- moderation;
- results handling;
- game-value labels;
- support;
- incident and correction routes.

Useful evidence includes players who entered, completed sessions, returned, reported issues, or joined later events.

### 9.2 ZAGA Districts

Districts may organize communities around cities, roles, NPCs, buildings, quests, resources, and shared decisions.

Persistent state requires:

- operator ownership;
- permission rules;
- economy testing;
- moderation;
- conflict handling;
- state consistency;
- rollback or correction procedures.

Useful evidence includes successful onboarding, active roles, recurring participation, moderation load, state reliability, and community feedback.

ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts are separate products. Cross-product recognition, balances, or benefits require an explicit supported rule.

Game values must retain their game labels. Game participation does not automatically establish withdrawable value, token entitlement, market access, or investment return.

## 10. Research and Market-Operations Communication

### 10.1 QTB

QTB can help a research or project community prepare source-bounded market recaps, watchlist notes, journal structures, and explanatory material.

Outputs should identify source period, purpose, uncertainty, and reviewer. QTB is research and interpretation support, not autonomous trading or personalized financial advice.

### 10.2 AIMM

AIMM can help authorized teams organize venue, liquidity, inventory, provider, and operational reporting.

Public community updates should use approved facts and omit private strategy, credentials, counterparties, sensitive treasury details, and security-sensitive information.

AIMM is liquidity-operations intelligence and support. It should not be presented as price protection, guaranteed liquidity, guaranteed spread, or guaranteed market performance.

These products can improve community communication without directing members to trade or implying a market outcome.

## 11. Events, Education, and Utility Discovery

### 11.1 AIE

AIE can support event discovery, agendas, organizer briefs, participant updates, sponsor summaries, feedback analysis, and recaps.

Event records should distinguish registration, attendance, engagement, completion, and follow-up.

### 11.2 TrainLayer AI and SpeakShop AI

TrainLayer AI can turn approved material into onboarding guides, safety lessons, quizzes, game tutorials, or moderator training.

SpeakShop AI can prepare announcements and scripts for AMAs, events, booths, voice chats, and local-language programs.

Generated material remains subject to responsible human review before publication or member-impacting use.

### 11.3 ToolGrid AI

ToolGrid AI can provide reviewed utility listings, collections, comparisons, and clearly labeled sponsored placements.

Moderation and destination review remain important because a listed tool, website, or service may change after publication.

Sponsored visibility must remain separate from neutral ranking, editorial selection, endorsement, or guaranteed user response.

Educational and discovery programs should make the next product action clear instead of using content volume as the success measure.

## 12. Botmad for Community Operators

Botmad can assist an approved operator with bounded tasks such as:

- compiling an admin briefing;
- preparing a public update draft;
- converting an event recap into follow-up tasks;
- organizing approved documentation;
- drafting support responses;
- preparing a campaign checklist;
- assembling a report package.

The task should identify:

- permitted sources;
- available tools;
- output audience;
- reviewer;
- action limits;
- logging and retention;
- escalation route.

Sending, publishing, deleting, granting access, moving funds, or making member-impacting decisions requires the applicable approval.

Botmad is permission-controlled work assistance, not an autonomous employee with unrestricted authority.

## 13. Education Architecture

Web3 communities need education at the moment a concept affects the user.

| User moment | Education needed |
|---|---|
| Joining a product program | Purpose, access, rules, data, and support |
| Spending Platform Credits | Product action, amount, balance, expiry, and correction route |
| Connecting a wallet | Reason, network, authorization, requested signature or transaction, record, and privacy |
| Entering a game | Mechanics, status, conduct, game-value labels, and support |
| Reading QTB material | Source period, interpretation role, uncertainty, and reviewer context |
| Reading AIMM updates | Scope, venue or period, operational status, and limitations |
| Viewing sponsored content | Sponsor label, destination, period, and reporting |
| Participating in a token function | Current utility status, eligibility, and specialist instructions |

Just-in-time education is more useful than repeating a full token, wallet, Platform Credit, stablecoin, and risk explanation in every announcement.

Community administrators should have current links and escalation contacts for questions outside the program scope.

## 14. Platform Credits, Payments, and Sponsored Programs

Platform Credits may support eligible reports, summaries, event outputs, learning material, sponsored services, or work-assistance tasks.

Community-funded, partner-funded, promotional, included, and member-purchased balances should be distinguishable where their rules differ.

Platform Credits remain separate from FUZE token and do not by themselves prove cash collection or product revenue.

Stablecoins may support approved:

- product payments;
- partner settlement;
- event vendor payments;
- contributor compensation;
- treasury operations.

A stablecoin transfer does not by itself prove community status, fulfillment, revenue, sponsorship, eligibility, or token utility.

A sponsored community program should state:

- sponsor or funding category where public disclosure is approved;
- product service and audience;
- program period;
- content and moderation ownership;
- placement or delivery commitment;
- available reporting;
- data and permission treatment;
- correction, suspension, and closure route.

Sponsorship does not transfer moderator authority or guarantee member response, adoption, revenue, or market outcome.

## 15. Wallet Records, Eligibility, and Privacy

A community program may use a wallet for a specific approved action such as access, game utility, recognition, or another defined ecosystem function.

Instructions should state:

- purpose;
- supported network;
- eligibility condition;
- snapshot or checking method where relevant;
- requested signature or transaction;
- fees where applicable;
- resulting record;
- correction and support route;
- privacy treatment.

Wallet status for one program does not create access to unrelated products, roles, token rights, governance, claims, or distributions.

Public reporting may use wallet addresses, aggregate wallet activity, report hashes, or status records when useful.

It should not connect a wallet to a person's private identity or publish verification documents, private agreements, customer records, operator records, private messages, or sensitive community evidence.

Detailed wallet participation, custody, eligibility, claim, and correction mechanics remain in their dedicated papers.

## 16. Moderation, Safety, and Incident Handling

A community program should define:

- community rules;
- operator and moderator authority;
- AI-assistance boundaries;
- prohibited content and behavior;
- abuse, fraud, spam, impersonation, and scam handling;
- appeals and corrections;
- member-support route;
- escalation contacts;
- evidence retention;
- suspension and closure authority.

Incidents may include:

- compromised admin or bot account;
- phishing or impersonation;
- unauthorized wallet request;
- misleading token or market claim;
- exposed private data;
- moderation error;
- abusive automation;
- incorrect public metric;
- game or product-state inconsistency;
- partner or sponsor misuse.

The response should record scope, authority, containment, user communication, correction, access changes, and follow-up.

## 17. Partner-Led Community Programs

Qualified partners may operate regional communities, events, education, games, implementations, research programs, or utility campaigns.

The program plan should assign:

- community and moderator ownership;
- product configuration;
- content and public communication;
- user onboarding and support;
- data and permissions;
- event or campaign delivery;
- commercial and sponsor responsibilities;
- incident and escalation handling;
- metrics and reporting;
- renewal and offboarding.

Partners should be evaluated on delivery and member value rather than audience claims alone.

Public descriptions should match the actual relationship stage. An introduction, exploratory discussion, memorandum, pilot, and active program are different statuses.

## 18. Community Health and Product Metrics

### 18.1 Operations

- response and resolution time;
- repeated-question volume;
- moderator workload;
- escalation and appeal volume;
- incident status;
- correction time.

### 18.2 Product use

- activated users or communities;
- first-value completion;
- completed summaries, reports, sessions, or tasks;
- repeat use;
- retained operator use;
- support demand.

### 18.3 Programs

- event or game participation;
- training completion;
- campaign delivery;
- partner implementation milestones;
- sponsored-service status;
- wallet or eligibility activity where applicable.

### 18.4 Retention and quality

- returning participants;
- recurring operator use;
- renewal or continuation;
- product reliability;
- member and moderator feedback;
- moderation and incident burden.

Growth metrics need context. A temporary event spike, incentive campaign, bot activity, wallet connection, registration, and sustained product use are different signals.

Each metric should identify definition, source, period, cohort, exclusions, owner, and correction method.

## 19. Public Reporting

A community report may identify:

1. community or program category;
2. product and workflow;
3. period and audience;
4. participation and completion measures;
5. support or moderation findings;
6. incidents or material limitations;
7. partner or sponsor role where approved;
8. wallet-level or aggregate evidence where appropriate;
9. current status;
10. next decision.

Public-safe summaries should avoid:

- personal identity;
- private messages;
- moderation evidence that could harm users;
- wallet-to-person links;
- customer and operator records;
- private commercial terms;
- credentials and security detail;
- unreconciled estimates presented as confirmed results.

Corrections should remain visible when a material figure, method, or status changes.

## 20. Expansion Readiness

A community workflow may be ready to expand when:

- an accountable operator remains active;
- members reach meaningful product value;
- repeated use appears;
- moderation and support are manageable;
- product reliability is acceptable;
- data and permission controls operate;
- wallet and token instructions are clear where applicable;
- partner or sponsor duties are reliable;
- reporting is consistent;
- operating cost and capacity are understood.

Expansion may include:

- a larger cohort;
- more moderators;
- another language or region;
- recurring programs;
- another related product;
- partner-led delivery;
- a paid package;
- a reviewed public case study.

Each expansion step should receive updated approval and evidence requirements.

## 21. Pause, Revision, and Closure

A program should be revised, paused, or closed when:

- instructions are confusing;
- members do not reach useful value;
- support demand is excessive;
- incentives create low-quality or abusive activity;
- permissions are unclear;
- moderation or operator ownership is weak;
- game, wallet, token, research, or market language is misunderstood;
- privacy or security concerns remain unresolved;
- partner or sponsor duties are not met;
- public claims exceed evidence;
- product reliability is insufficient.

Closure should include:

1. member and operator communication;
2. stopping new activity;
3. access and credential removal;
4. unresolved support handling;
5. data retention, export, or deletion;
6. payment and Platform Credit reconciliation;
7. wallet or entitlement status treatment where relevant;
8. partner and sponsor closure;
9. final reporting and lessons learned.

## 22. Investor Review Questions

Investors should ask:

- Which community workflows have progressed beyond documentation?
- Which communities have accountable operators and active support?
- Which programs show meaningful activation rather than member-count growth?
- Which products are used repeatedly by moderators or members?
- Which programs have paid delivery, sponsorship, renewal, or partner evidence?
- How are incentives, bot activity, wallet connections, and genuine product use separated?
- Which programs involve sensitive data, moderation authority, or wallet activity?
- How are incidents, scams, impersonation, and misleading market claims handled?
- Which public metrics are reconciled and which remain preliminary?
- Which programs were paused or closed, and what evidence drove the decision?

## 23. Public Boundary

This paper defines a Web3 community operations and expansion model.

It does not forecast or confirm:

- community or member growth;
- product adoption or retention;
- token demand, price, liquidity, listing, or market access;
- wallet eligibility, claims, or distributions;
- game-value withdrawal;
- sponsorship or partner performance;
- revenue, profitability, or investment results;
- the success of a particular event, campaign, or community program.

Product, game, wallet, sponsored, market, partner, and community programs depend on current status, approved rules, operator capacity, and applicable review.

Detailed financial, token, liquidity, legal, and risk treatment remains in specialist papers.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE community expansion starts with a useful community workflow rather than token promotion.
- Telegram can be a primary operating environment, but significant state requires reliable underlying records.
- Community operators retain authority over moderation, member-impacting actions, and public statements.
- QTB, AIMM, ZAGA, AIE, ToolGrid AI, TrainLayer AI, CommunityLayer AI, SpeakShop AI, and Botmad have distinct roles and boundaries.
- Platform Credits remain separate from FUZE token, and stablecoins remain operational rails.
- Wallet status for one program does not create unrelated access, rights, claims, or distributions.
- Member count, wallet connections, incentive activity, and sustained product use are different signals.
- Expansion should follow repeatable value, reliable moderation, controlled permissions, supportability, and current evidence.
