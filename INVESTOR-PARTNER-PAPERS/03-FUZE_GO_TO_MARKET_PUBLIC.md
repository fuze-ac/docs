# FUZE Go-To-Market

## Executive Summary

FUZE goes to market through focused product motions rather than one ecosystem-wide campaign. Each motion starts with a defined user segment, a recurring problem, a product workflow that can be demonstrated, a bounded entry offer, an accountable channel owner, and evidence that determines whether the motion expands, changes, pauses, or stops.

The go-to-market objective is not maximum reach. It is a repeatable path from qualified discovery to meaningful product activation, paid delivery, retention, and expansion within a supportable operating scope.

HerHelp products can enter through practical workflow demonstrations, starter packages, direct operator outreach, workshops, and implementation support. ZAGA can build participation through playable experiences, community events, tutorials, and reliable game operations. QTB, AIMM, AIE, ToolGrid AI, and Botmad require narrower qualification because their data, permissions, domain expectations, or operating controls are more specialized.

Shared platform capabilities may support onboarding, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI orchestration, wallet-aware records, and reporting where a product requires them. These capabilities should follow the customer journey rather than replace the product story. Web3 education should appear only where it supports an approved product or ecosystem action.

This paper defines FUZE's public distribution model: segment selection, product entry offers, channel motions, funnel states, experiments, partner handoffs, localization, measurement, retention, and scale decisions. It does not forecast customers, revenue, market share, token demand, or fundraising outcomes.

## 1. Purpose and Primary Readers

This paper is written primarily for investors, product owners, commercial operators, channel leads, strategic partners, and reviewers who need to understand how FUZE intends to reach and retain users.

It is designed to answer:

1. Which user and problem should each motion target?
2. What is the smallest credible product entry offer?
3. Which channel can reach and support that user?
4. What event demonstrates meaningful activation?
5. How does activation connect to paid delivery and retention?
6. Which evidence supports channel expansion?
7. When should a motion be narrowed, revised, paused, or stopped?
8. How should Platform Credits, token language, partners, and public reporting be handled?

Detailed product status, commercial classification, partner governance, data controls, and token mechanics remain in their controlling specialist papers.

## 2. Current Public Position

FUZE's public documents define intended products, user groups, workflows, launch sequencing, commercial models, partner structures, and evidence requirements.

At the public-documentation stage, these materials do not by themselves prove:

- that each product is publicly available;
- that a channel is active or repeatable;
- that an offer is available for purchase;
- that users have activated or retained;
- that a partner is operationally integrated;
- that paid delivery or reconciled revenue exists;
- that Platform Credits or token-connected features are live;
- that a campaign has achieved favorable acquisition economics;
- that a market or regional expansion has been approved.

Stronger go-to-market claims require current product access, cohort, attribution, onboarding, activation, delivery, support, payment, retention, and reconciliation evidence for the stated scope.

The [FUZE Public Status and Roadmap Matrix](../PUBLIC-INDEX/02-FUZE_PUBLIC_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP_MATRIX.md) controls status vocabulary.

## 3. Go-To-Market Objective

The objective is to discover repeatable and supportable routes from a real user problem to sustained product value and paid delivery.

Every motion should be able to answer:

1. Who is the primary user, buyer, sponsor, or decision-maker?
2. Which recurring problem creates urgency?
3. Which FUZE product addresses that problem?
4. What is the smallest complete workflow that demonstrates value?
5. Which entry offer reduces friction without hiding delivery cost?
6. Which channel can reach and qualify the user?
7. What event represents meaningful activation?
8. What evidence supports conversion, retention, expansion, or referral?
9. Which owner can improve, narrow, pause, or stop the motion?

A channel is not successful merely because it produces impressions, followers, meetings, wallet connections, registrations, or announcements. It should move qualified users toward product value, paid delivery, repeat use, or another defined operating outcome.

## 4. Segment Selection

FUZE can address several segments, but each campaign should select one primary buyer and one primary workflow.

| Segment | Recurring problem | Product entry | Likely buyer or sponsor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shops and local operators | Fragmented menu, queue, staff, stock, reporting, and promotion work | ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, SheetLayer AI | Owner or operator |
| SMEs and small teams | Repetitive data, content, training, administration, and document tasks | HerHelp AI SaaS, SheetLayer AI, TrainLayer AI, Botmad | Founder, manager, operations lead |
| Communities | High support load, repeated questions, inconsistent moderation, weak reporting | CommunityLayer AI, TrainLayer AI | Community owner or project team |
| Educators and training teams | Slow conversion of source material into usable learning assets | TrainLayer AI, HerHelp AI SaaS | Program lead, employer, educator |
| Event organizers | Scattered discovery, planning, communication, and post-event evidence | AIE, SpeakShop AI | Organizer, sponsor, venue, community |
| Game and Telegram communities | Need for playable activity, progression, events, and community coordination | ZAGA Arena, ZAGA Districts | Players, community operators, ecosystem teams |
| Crypto research users | Fragmented information and inconsistent research workflows | QTB | Analyst, trader, research community |
| Token and liquidity operators | Complex venue, inventory, provider, access, and reporting coordination | AIMM | Authorized project or operations team |
| AI tool users and providers | Difficult utility discovery and controlled promotion | ToolGrid AI | User, tool provider, sponsor |
| Work-intensive teams | Repetitive desktop tasks requiring bounded AI assistance | Botmad | Operator, team lead, enterprise sponsor |

Segment selection should reflect:

- product status and reliability;
- onboarding complexity;
- support capacity;
- data and permission risk;
- delivery cost;
- channel access;
- customer urgency;
- ability to observe meaningful activation;
- ability to test a paid next step.

A large addressable market narrative cannot compensate for an incomplete product, unclear buyer, or unsupported onboarding process.

## 5. Ideal Customer Profile

Each motion should define an ideal customer profile narrow enough to qualify prospects consistently.

A useful profile may include:

- organization or user type;
- current workflow and pain point;
- frequency and cost of the problem;
- decision-maker and users;
- technical and data environment;
- budget or willingness-to-pay signal;
- urgency and timing;
- implementation and support needs;
- relevant language, geography, device, or payment constraints;
- disqualifying conditions.

The profile should identify who is not suitable for the current offer. Poor-fit users can create misleading reach, high support load, low activation, refund pressure, privacy risk, and product distraction.

## 6. Entry Offer Design

The entry offer gives a qualified user a low-friction way to experience the product's core value.

Possible formats include:

- guided demonstration using representative or authorized data;
- bounded trial or starter allowance;
- fixed-scope workflow package;
- diagnostic or setup session;
- sample output followed by a live product task;
- controlled pilot for an organization or community;
- event, campaign, or game session;
- implementation package with a qualified partner.

Every entry offer should define:

| Element | Required answer |
|---|---|
| User | Who is eligible, who decides, and who operates the product? |
| Problem | Which task, cost, delay, or risk is addressed? |
| Scope | What is included and excluded? |
| Input | What data, access, time, or setup does the user provide? |
| Output | What does the user receive or experience? |
| Duration | When does the offer begin and end? |
| Activation | Which event shows that meaningful value was reached? |
| Support | Who assists and through which route? |
| Commercial next step | Continue, purchase, renew, expand, integrate, or close |
| Evidence | Which records support the decision? |

A free offer should serve a defined purpose, such as testing activation, reducing setup friction, demonstrating a workflow, or qualifying demand.

Unbounded free usage can hide willingness to pay, create unsupported obligations, distort retention, and consume AI, infrastructure, or human-support capacity without improving the funnel.

## 7. Distribution Motions

### 7.1 Product-led motion

Product-led distribution uses the product workflow as the primary proof.

Useful assets may include:

- interactive or recorded demonstrations;
- before-and-after examples;
- reviewed sample outputs;
- templates and starter workspaces;
- short tutorials;
- onboarding checklists;
- product-specific landing pages;
- release notes and current status;
- self-service trial or starter package where supported.

This motion is strongest where users can understand the problem and reach first value quickly.

### 7.2 Founder and operator-led motion

Early products often require direct explanation, observation, and support.

Founder or operator-led sessions can reveal:

- objections and purchasing criteria;
- setup effort;
- missing features or integrations;
- language and localization needs;
- security or data concerns;
- support burden;
- willingness to continue or pay.

Each session should produce structured notes and a clear outcome: qualified pilot, commercial follow-up, product feedback, later review, or poor fit.

### 7.3 Content and education motion

Educational content should teach a useful workflow rather than repeat broad ecosystem or token claims.

Examples include:

- shop operating guides;
- spreadsheet cleanup and reporting lessons;
- community administration practices;
- event planning and recap methods;
- market-research organization;
- AI permission and review patterns;
- game tutorials and community play guides.

Content should connect to a measurable next action such as a demo request, starter workflow, completed tutorial, workspace creation, or qualified product task.

### 7.4 Community motion

Telegram, Discord, social, local-business, education, gaming, and Web3 communities can support discovery, onboarding, feedback, events, and peer learning.

A community motion requires:

- current product instructions;
- moderation;
- support and escalation;
- clear product status;
- privacy and permission boundaries;
- separation between product use and speculative expectations;
- attribution where the community is treated as a channel.

### 7.5 Partner-led motion

Partners may provide referrals, local distribution, implementation, technology, devices, training, content, events, enterprise access, or co-delivery.

The relationship should distinguish among:

- referral;
- reseller or commercial representative;
- implementation partner;
- integration partner;
- sponsor;
- technology provider;
- community or event partner;
- co-delivery partner.

Each role has different authority, responsibility, economics, data access, support, reporting, and public-reference rules.

The [FUZE Strategic Partner Model](04-FUZE_STRATEGIC_PARTNER_MODEL_PUBLIC.md) governs partner qualification and lifecycle controls.

### 7.6 Direct sales and enterprise motion

Larger deployments may require:

- discovery and workflow mapping;
- solution scoping;
- security, privacy, and data review;
- procurement;
- pilot acceptance;
- implementation and integration;
- training;
- support and service obligations;
- commercial and legal approval.

The sales process should present the narrow solution the buyer needs rather than the entire portfolio. Expansion should follow successful delivery within the first approved scope.

### 7.7 Event-led motion

Events may create product discovery, live demonstrations, community participation, organizer workflows, or sponsor opportunities.

Event activity should connect to a defined product action and post-event follow-up. Attendance alone is not activation, adoption, conversion, or retention.

## 8. Product-Family Motions

### 8.1 HerHelp AI SaaS and business workflows

HerHelp products can use demonstrations, templates, workshops, local implementation, direct outreach, and partner-led onboarding.

The entry point should be the product with the clearest urgent task for the selected segment.

Cross-product expansion should follow demonstrated need. For example, a ShopOS AI user may later adopt SpeakShop AI, SheetLayer AI, or TrainLayer AI without requiring the first campaign to explain the full suite.

### 8.2 ZAGA

ZAGA distribution begins with playable experiences, community events, tutorials, shareable results, and reliable support.

ZAGA Arena and ZAGA Districts require separate onboarding because their session structure, persistence, progression, social behavior, and operating evidence differ.

Game communication should distinguish current game features, in-game values, external token functions, wallet features, and withdrawal status. Token-connected features should appear only when approved and active for the stated scope.

### 8.3 QTB and AIMM

QTB and AIMM should enter through bounded reports, authorized workspaces, professional communities, selected projects, or qualified partners.

QTB should be positioned as market-research and interpretation support rather than autonomous trading.

AIMM should be positioned as liquidity-operations analysis, planning, monitoring, controls, and reporting rather than price support or guaranteed liquidity.

Samples should identify source periods, purpose, review state, and limitations.

### 8.4 AIE

AIE can use organizer pilots, event communities, sponsor workflows, venue relationships, and post-event reporting.

The strongest proof is a complete event workflow, not event-interest volume alone.

### 8.5 ToolGrid AI

ToolGrid AI can combine utility discovery with reviewed listings and clearly labeled sponsored visibility.

Sponsored placement must remain separate from neutral assessment, editorial selection, or guaranteed customer acquisition.

### 8.6 Botmad

Botmad can begin with bounded desktop tasks inside teams able to manage permissions, data access, human approval, and audit records.

Broad autonomous-assistant claims should not precede evidence from narrow, approved task workflows.

## 9. Funnel Vocabulary

FUZE can use a common funnel vocabulary while defining product-specific events.

| Stage | Meaning | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | A relevant audience encounters the product | Qualified content view, attendee, partner introduction |
| Engage | The user explores the problem or workflow | Demo completion, guide use, product interaction |
| Qualify | Need, authority, timing, constraints, and fit are understood | Discovery or qualification record |
| Start | The user begins the approved onboarding or entry offer | Workspace, pilot, session, or setup record |
| Activate | The user reaches first meaningful product value | Completed report, shop workflow, game session, event plan, or task |
| Convert | A paid offer, subscription, package, or approved service begins | Confirmed commercial record |
| Deliver | The purchased product or service obligation is fulfilled | Completion, acceptance, or service-period evidence |
| Retain | The user returns, renews, or repeats paid use in the relevant cycle | Repeat action, renewal, active period |
| Expand | Use grows to another module, seat, team, location, or service | Additional product, workspace, package, or contract scope |
| Refer | A satisfied user or partner introduces another qualified prospect | Attributed referral and resulting action |
| Close | The motion ends without further action | Reason, status, and future eligibility where applicable |

Activation must represent product value. Registration, account creation, page view, token holding, or wallet connection may be necessary steps but are weak activation events unless they complete the product's intended job.

## 10. Qualification and Disqualification

Qualification should evaluate:

- problem severity and frequency;
- user and buyer authority;
- timing and urgency;
- product fit;
- data and permission compatibility;
- implementation capacity;
- budget or willingness-to-pay signal;
- support requirements;
- legal, market, or jurisdiction constraints;
- expected next step.

A prospect may be disqualified or deferred when:

- the required product capability is not ready;
- expectations exceed approved scope;
- data or security conditions cannot be met;
- the user requires an unsupported region, device, language, payment route, or integration;
- the support burden is disproportionate;
- the request depends on unapproved token, wallet, market, or financial claims;
- the motion lacks a credible commercial or learning objective.

Disqualification protects product focus and improves the quality of funnel evidence.

## 11. Channel Experiments

Before scaling, FUZE should run a bounded experiment.

### 11.1 Experiment brief

Record:

- segment and user problem;
- product and entry offer;
- channel and owner;
- campaign period or cohort;
- message and content;
- partner, event, or sales activity;
- expected funnel events;
- spend and delivery capacity;
- data, privacy, and consent requirements;
- support capacity;
- success, hold, change, and stop criteria.

### 11.2 Execution

Track source and handoffs consistently. Users should receive current product scope, pricing or Platform Credit information, support instructions, privacy treatment, and known limitations.

### 11.3 Review

Useful findings include:

- which message produced qualified interest;
- which segment activated most reliably;
- where onboarding stalled;
- time and support required to activate;
- product defects or missing integrations;
- willingness to continue or pay;
- partner delivery quality;
- refunds, complaints, or confusion;
- reasons users declined, stopped, or failed to activate;
- direct acquisition and delivery cost.

### 11.4 Decision

Choose one outcome:

- scale;
- repeat with a defined change;
- narrow the segment;
- revise the offer;
- improve the product first;
- change the channel;
- pause;
- stop.

Activity volume should not be mistaken for channel fit.

## 12. Platform Credits in Acquisition

Platform Credits may support trials, starter allowances, prepaid packages, included plan usage, and metered product actions.

A campaign using Platform Credits should identify:

- whether credits are purchased, included, partner-funded, granted, or promotional;
- which product actions they cover;
- audience, period, quantity, and expiry;
- the activation event;
- the paid or retained next step;
- support, reversal, refund, and correction treatment;
- how promotional use remains separate from paid activity;
- how outstanding credits are treated as service obligations.

Credits simplify product consumption only when the user understands the task and output. Campaigns should lead with user value rather than credit balances.

FUZE token is not a substitute for customer acquisition. Token or wallet functions should appear only where an approved and active product or ecosystem function requires them.

## 13. Commercial Handoff

Marketing and product engagement should hand qualified users into a controlled commercial process.

A commercial handoff should include:

- source and attribution;
- user and decision-maker;
- problem and use case;
- product and requested scope;
- current funnel stage;
- expected offer;
- data, security, or implementation requirements;
- budget or purchasing signal where appropriate;
- next owner and action;
- consent and communication status.

Paid conversion, delivery, reconciliation, and revenue classification follow the [FUZE Product Revenue Model](02-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

## 14. Partner Handoffs

Partner-led distribution requires clear transitions among partner, marketing, sales, implementation, product, support, and reporting.

| Handoff | Required information |
|---|---|
| Partner to FUZE | Prospect source, authority, consent, use case, status, and expectations |
| Marketing to sales | Campaign source, engagement, qualification, and next action |
| Sales to implementation | Agreed scope, users, systems, owners, milestones, and terms |
| Implementation to support | Configuration, access, tests, known limits, and escalation |
| Product to reporting | Definitions, source events, cohort, period, and approved audience |
| Renewal or expansion | Performance, open issues, commercial status, and proposed scope |
| Offboarding | Access changes, records, data handling, obligations, and communication |

Attribution, commercial responsibility, data access, support ownership, and reporting rights should be agreed before activation.

Public partner references require verified relationship status and approval from the responsible parties.

## 15. Localization and Market Entry

Localization is more than translation.

A product may require local treatment for:

- language and tone;
- customer workflow;
- payments and refunds;
- devices and connectivity;
- support hours;
- pricing and purchasing behavior;
- tax and invoicing;
- privacy and data handling;
- legal and professional review;
- community norms;
- partner capacity;
- public claims and disclosures.

Before entering a new market, FUZE should review:

1. user problem and buyer behavior;
2. product and device compatibility;
3. language and content quality;
4. data and permission requirements;
5. payment, refund, and reconciliation capability;
6. support and implementation capacity;
7. delivery cost and pricing basis;
8. partner readiness;
9. legal, tax, and compliance dependencies;
10. release and reporting scope.

The initial launch should be limited to the audience, region, language, product status, and operating capacity actually supported.

## 16. Retention and Expansion

Retention should be defined according to the product's natural usage cycle.

Relevant signals may include:

- repeat completed workflows;
- active use in a later period;
- subscription renewal;
- repeat paid task or package;
- continued organization or workspace activity;
- repeated game sessions or community events;
- retained integration or partner deployment;
- increased seats, locations, modules, or usage.

Expansion should follow evidence from the first product or scope. Cross-selling an unrelated product before the initial value is proven can create confusion and weaken trust.

Retention reporting should distinguish:

- registered users;
- activated users;
- active users;
- paying users;
- renewed customers;
- retained cohorts;
- expanded customers.

These categories are not interchangeable.

## 17. Measurement

Metrics should follow the funnel and product economics.

### Acquisition

- qualified reach;
- cost per qualified prospect;
- cost per activated user or organization;
- source and campaign attribution;
- partner-sourced opportunities.

### Engagement and qualification

- demo or tutorial completion;
- qualification rate;
- reasons for poor fit or deferral;
- time from first contact to qualified next step.

### Activation

- onboarding completion;
- time to first meaningful value;
- workflow completion and failure rate;
- support contacts before activation;
- cohort activation rate.

### Conversion and delivery

- paid conversion within a defined cohort;
- sales-cycle length;
- payment and Platform Credit flow completion;
- delivery completion and acceptance;
- refund, dispute, and failed-delivery rate.

### Retention and expansion

- repeat product actions;
- active customers or workspaces by period;
- renewal, cancellation, and churn;
- retained cohort rate;
- cross-product, seat, location, or package expansion.

### Quality and economics

- delivery reliability;
- support load;
- AI and provider cost;
- direct delivery cost;
- acquisition cost;
- contribution view;
- partner performance;
- incident and correction rate.

Each metric should identify definition, source, owner, cohort, period, exclusions, and correction method.

Small or early cohorts should remain clearly labeled. Percentages without cohort size, timeframe, or definition can mislead.

## 18. Reporting to Investors and Partners

A go-to-market update may include:

1. product and segment;
2. user problem and entry offer;
3. channel and owner;
4. campaign or cohort period;
5. reach, qualification, activation, conversion, and retention results;
6. delivery, support, and product findings;
7. spend, capacity, and cost context;
8. partner or implementation performance;
9. product changes resulting from evidence;
10. next decision and criteria.

Investor reporting should separate:

- audience growth from qualified reach;
- qualified reach from product activation;
- activation from paid conversion;
- paid conversion from fulfilled delivery;
- delivery from reconciled revenue;
- revenue from retention or profitability.

Public-safe reporting may aggregate results while withholding personal identity, customer records, private pricing, partner terms, account data, security detail, and confidential commercial information.

## 19. Scale Readiness

A motion may be ready for broader investment when:

- the segment, buyer, and problem are clear;
- the product is available for the stated scope;
- onboarding and activation are repeatable;
- product quality and support can handle more users;
- acquisition and delivery costs are understood;
- payment, Platform Credits, refunds, and reconciliation operate correctly;
- retention evidence fits the product cycle;
- data, permissions, and privacy controls scale with the channel;
- a channel owner and operating team are assigned;
- partner responsibilities are reliable where applicable;
- reporting can explain performance, limitations, and corrections.

A high-volume campaign should wait when product reliability, onboarding, fulfillment, support, payment, or reconciliation remains the main bottleneck.

## 20. Stop and Pause Criteria

A motion should be paused, narrowed, or stopped when:

- users do not reach meaningful value;
- qualification quality remains low;
- support effort is disproportionate;
- product defects block activation;
- acquisition or delivery cost is not supportable;
- refunds, complaints, or disputes reveal product or expectation problems;
- partner execution is unreliable;
- data, security, legal, or permission requirements are unresolved;
- the campaign depends on misleading token, market, revenue, or outcome claims;
- stronger evidence does not emerge after the defined experiment period.

Stopping a weak motion preserves capital and product focus. It is not a failure when the experiment produced useful evidence.

## 21. Governance and Ownership

Each material motion should have named owners for:

- product status and scope;
- offer and pricing;
- channel execution;
- content and claims;
- qualification and sales handoff;
- onboarding and implementation;
- support and escalation;
- data and consent;
- payment and Platform Credit operation;
- measurement and reporting;
- experiment decision and closure.

Material changes to audience, offer, claims, pricing, payment route, partner role, geography, token connection, or reporting method should be reviewed and versioned.

## 22. Public Boundary

This paper describes FUZE's go-to-market operating model.

It does not state that:

- a product or offer is currently available;
- a named segment has adopted a product;
- a channel is repeatable or scalable;
- a partner is active;
- Platform Credits or payment routes are live;
- FUZE has achieved paid conversion, retention, or recognized revenue;
- a campaign will produce customers, revenue, market share, token demand, or investor outcomes.

Product availability, offers, channels, partners, pricing, credits, payments, geography, and market scope depend on current approved status, capacity, terms, and evidence.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE goes to market one segment, problem, product workflow, and entry offer at a time.
- Product activation matters more than raw reach, registrations, followers, or wallet connections.
- Every channel motion should have an owner, evidence, support capacity, and stop criteria.
- Product-led, founder-led, content, community, partner, enterprise, and event motions serve different purposes.
- Platform Credits may support acquisition and usage but remain separate from FUZE token and paid revenue.
- Paid conversion, fulfilled delivery, reconciled revenue, retention, and profitability are separate evidence states.
- Portfolio breadth should support focused entry points rather than one broad ecosystem campaign.
- Scale should follow repeatable activation, supportability, commercial operation, and measured retention.
