
# FUZE Investor Deck Outline

## Executive Summary

The FUZE investor deck should tell one evidence-led story: identifiable users have practical problems; selected FUZE products address those problems; reusable services can improve delivery and control across products; commercial models connect use to paid delivery; and strategic capital can fund observable milestones.

The main deck should remain concise. Product status and evidence should appear before token, market-access, or long-term optionality material. Detailed mechanics, transaction terms, legal treatment, risk schedules, technical records, financial models, and restricted evidence belong in the appendix or qualified data room.

This outline defines a 15-slide core narrative. Each slide has a decision purpose, central claim, evidence requirement, visual direction, and public boundary. The deck should be assembled from current source records rather than by copying executive summaries from the public-paper corpus.

The final deck may vary by investor type and diligence stage, but every variant should preserve claim accuracy, audience controls, source traceability, status consistency, protected-information handling, and alignment with FUZE's approved public position.

## 1. Deck Objective

The deck should help an investor decide whether to begin or deepen diligence.

By the final slide, the investor should understand:

1. the users and recurring problems FUZE is choosing to solve;
2. the products and workflows FUZE is prioritizing;
3. the current status, evidence, and unresolved gaps;
4. the intended value of shared platform services;
5. the commercial and distribution logic;
6. the role of Platform Credits, stablecoin rails, FUZE token, and wallet records;
7. the milestone use of strategic capital;
8. the principal risks, dependencies, and next review route.

The deck is not expected to answer every diligence question. It should make those questions easier to identify, prioritize, and route.

## 2. Core Narrative

The recommended sequence is:

```text
problem -> priority product -> current evidence -> product experience
-> shared-platform leverage -> commercial model -> go-to-market
-> execution milestones -> operating controls -> ecosystem role
-> capital plan -> risks -> diligence next step
```

This order keeps the investment case anchored in products and operating evidence.

Token, wallet, custody, participation, and market-access context should appear only after the product, commercial, control, and evidence foundations are clear.

## 3. Core Deck

### Slide 1: Cover

**Decision purpose:** identify the company, presentation, audience, version, and distribution class.

**Include:**

- FUZE name and approved positioning line;
- “Private Investor Presentation” or another reviewed audience label;
- date and version;
- confidentiality or distribution notice where applicable;
- presentation owner and approved contact route.

**Evidence required:** current approved company and round language.

**Visual:** clean brand cover with one product-oriented image or restrained product mosaic.

**Boundary:** no token-price, exchange, liquidity, return, valuation, or transaction claim belongs on the cover.

### Slide 2: The Investor Thesis

**Decision purpose:** explain why FUZE may become valuable.

**Central claim:** focused products can enter distinct user environments while reusing common capabilities for accounts, workspaces, permissions, Platform Credits, payments, AI operations, data controls, wallet-aware records, and reporting.

**Evidence required:**

- current priority products;
- strongest product evidence;
- shared-service adoption status;
- commercial paths;
- next proof milestones;
- principal unresolved assumptions.

**Visual:** three connected blocks labeled **Products**, **Shared Platform**, and **Ecosystem**, with current evidence beneath each.

**Source:** [FUZE Investor Overview](01-FUZE_INVESTOR_OVERVIEW_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** the slide should present a thesis to test, not a completed advantage.

### Slide 3: User Problems

**Decision purpose:** show the recurring work that may create demand.

Use three to five problem clusters, such as:

- fragmented operations for shops and small teams;
- slow spreadsheet, content, training, and community workflows;
- difficult event coordination and reporting;
- specialized market and liquidity-operation information;
- repetitive work that requires bounded AI assistance.

**Evidence required:**

- interviews;
- workflow observations;
- support requests;
- pilot notes;
- search or demand signals;
- partner or customer discovery;
- dated external market sources where used.

**Visual:** problem-to-user matrix rather than a generic market collage.

**Boundary:** problem evidence should not imply product-market fit, adoption, revenue, or market leadership.

### Slide 4: Product Entry Points

**Decision purpose:** show how FUZE addresses selected problems.

Group the portfolio:

| Entry area | Products |
|---|---|
| Practical AI SaaS | HerHelp, SheetLayer AI, ShopOS AI, SpeakShop AI, TrainLayer AI, CommunityLayer AI |
| Games and communities | ZAGA, ZAGA Arena, ZAGA Districts |
| Market-aware operations | QTB, AIMM |
| Events and discovery | AIE, ToolGrid AI |
| Permission-controlled work assistance | Botmad |

**Evidence required:** target user, core workflow, current approved status, owner, and next evidence for each priority product.

**Visual:** product-family map with priority products clearly distinguished from secondary or exploratory products.

**Source:** [FUZE AI SaaS Product Index](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/01-FUZE_AI_SAAS_PRODUCT_INDEX_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** portfolio inclusion does not establish implementation or active prioritization.

### Slide 5: Current Product Evidence

**Decision purpose:** establish what exists now.

Use a compact matrix:

| Product or workflow | Current approved status | Evidence available | Important limitation | Next proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priority workflow A | Exact scoped status | Demo, test, release, usage, or commercial record | Named gap | Named milestone |
| Priority workflow B | Exact scoped status | Demo, test, release, usage, or commercial record | Named gap | Named milestone |
| Priority workflow C | Exact scoped status | Demo, test, release, usage, or commercial record | Named gap | Named milestone |

**Evidence required:**

- demonstrations;
- test results;
- release records;
- user and cohort evidence;
- support records;
- reliability records;
- commercial evidence appropriate to the claim.

**Visual:** status matrix with appendix or evidence-register references; avoid a feature checklist.

**Source:** [FUZE Product Status and Evidence Matrix](14-FUZE_PRODUCT_STATUS_AND_EVIDENCE_MATRIX_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** documented, demonstrable, tested, released, operating, paid, repeated, and reconciled evidence remain distinct.

### Slide 6: Product Experience

**Decision purpose:** make one priority workflow tangible.

Show:

```text
user need -> onboarding -> input -> FUZE workflow
-> review or approval -> output -> record -> next action
```

**Evidence required:**

- real or clearly labeled representative screens;
- source or input class;
- permission states;
- AI and human-review steps;
- output sample;
- completion record;
- exceptions or manual work.

**Visual:** six-to-eight-step annotated workflow. A short live demonstration may replace dense slide content.

**Boundary:** synthetic, staged, manually assembled, or internal examples must be labeled and must not be presented as customer adoption or commercial evidence.

### Slide 7: Shared Platform Leverage

**Decision purpose:** explain why the portfolio can become more than unrelated products.

Show only capabilities relevant to current product work:

- accounts and workspaces;
- roles and permissions;
- Platform Credit handling;
- payment status and reconciliation;
- AI routing and usage records;
- data controls;
- wallet-aware records where required;
- reporting and evidence services.

**Evidence required:**

- which products use each capability;
- designed versus implemented state;
- service owner;
- interface and dependency;
- reliability and support status;
- measurable delivery, cost, or control benefit.

**Visual:** product-to-service matrix. Mark planned, implemented, tested, and operating connections differently.

**Source:** [FUZE Core Platform Rails](../CORE-PLATFORM-PAPERS/04-FUZE_CORE_PLATFORM_RAILS_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** a shared roadmap, codebase, diagram, or planned API does not by itself establish reusable platform leverage.

### Slide 8: Commercial Model

**Decision purpose:** show how product value can become paid delivery.

Present the commercial chain:

```text
customer -> offer -> price or usage basis -> order
-> payment -> fulfillment -> adjustment -> reconciliation -> report
```

Commercial formats may include:

- subscriptions;
- metered actions;
- Platform Credit packages;
- implementation services;
- reports;
- sponsored placements;
- event or community packages;
- partner deployments;
- approved game or ecosystem services.

**Evidence required:**

- current offers;
- price or usage basis;
- payment route;
- paid tests where available;
- delivery and support cost;
- refunds or disputes;
- repeated delivery;
- reconciliation status.

**Visual:** commercial flow plus a small evidence table.

**Source:** [FUZE Product Revenue Model](02-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_MODEL_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** payment receipt does not automatically establish fulfilled delivery or revenue. Stablecoins remain settlement rails, not a revenue classification.

### Slide 9: Go-to-Market

**Decision purpose:** show how qualified users may be reached, activated, and supported.

For each priority motion, identify:

| Element | Deck content |
|---|---|
| Audience | Specific buyer or user |
| Problem | Recurring need being addressed |
| Entry offer | Demonstration, trial, package, pilot, report, event, or game session |
| Channel | Direct, content, community, partner, event, enterprise, or Web3 |
| Activation | Observable first value |
| Owner | Person or team accountable |
| Support | Onboarding and service responsibility |
| Scale evidence | Conversion, completion, repeat use, economics, or renewal |

**Visual:** two or three product-specific funnel cards rather than one funnel for the whole ecosystem.

**Source:** [FUZE Go-To-Market](03-FUZE_GO_TO_MARKET_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** discussions, impressions, followers, introductions, pilots, active users, customers, and renewals are different evidence classes.

### Slide 10: Execution Plan and Milestones

**Decision purpose:** show what happens next and how progress will be judged.

Use three planning horizons:

1. immediate product and evidence milestone;
2. limited release, completed paid delivery, or shared-platform reuse milestone;
3. expansion milestone conditional on earlier evidence.

Each milestone should state:

- exact scope;
- owner;
- dependency;
- evidence required;
- target decision;
- stop, redesign, continue, or expand condition.

**Visual:** milestone sequence with evidence gates rather than a calendar of unsupported delivery dates.

**Source:** [FUZE Product Launch Sequence](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/20-FUZE_PRODUCT_LAUNCH_SEQUENCE_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** targets and plans should not be presented as completed delivery or guaranteed dates.

### Slide 11: Operating Controls

**Decision purpose:** show that execution includes protection, accountability, and correction.

Cover:

- AI evaluation and human authority;
- data purpose, ownership, roles, permissions, retention, and deletion;
- security, monitoring, incident, recovery, and support ownership;
- payment and Platform Credit reconciliation;
- partner and provider responsibilities;
- version, metric, evidence, and correction controls.

**Evidence required:** distinguish among:

- policy design;
- configured control;
- test result;
- operating evidence;
- incident or correction history.

**Visual:** control lifecycle around a product workflow.

**Sources:** [FUZE AI Safety and Reliability](07-FUZE_AI_SAFETY_AND_RELIABILITY_PUBLIC.md), [FUZE Data Privacy and Permission Model](08-FUZE_DATA_PRIVACY_AND_PERMISSION_MODEL_PUBLIC.md), and [FUZE Public Metrics and Transparency](09-FUZE_PUBLIC_METRICS_AND_TRANSPARENCY_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** policy language alone should not be presented as implemented or independently verified control effectiveness.

### Slide 12: Ecosystem, Credits, Payments, and Token Role

**Decision purpose:** explain Web3 and payment functions without displacing the product thesis.

Keep the following records separate:

| System | Deck treatment |
|---|---|
| Platform Credits | Eligible product-consumption and usage records |
| Payments and stablecoin rails | Approved payment, settlement, treasury, refund, reserve, or compensation operations |
| FUZE token | Approved ecosystem utility and participation functions |
| Wallet records | Address-linked evidence or status for approved use cases |
| Market access | Exact verified DEX or centralized-venue process stage |

Wallet-based participation should be described only as the current approved and gated framework.

Market-access language should identify the verified stage and preserve the DEX-first, possible-CEX-later boundary.

**Visual:** five-column separation diagram.

**Sources:** [FUZE Token Launch Mechanics](13-FUZE_TOKEN_LAUNCH_MECHANICS_PUBLIC.md) and the relevant tokenomics papers.

**Boundary:** do not include price targets, listing predictions, payout illustrations, implied holder economics, automatic revenue sharing, or guaranteed liquidity.

### Slide 13: Capital Plan

**Decision purpose:** connect strategic capital to milestones and operating capacity.

Group use into a small number of deck-level themes:

- priority product delivery;
- shared capabilities required by those products;
- engineering, AI, cloud, security, privacy, and operations;
- distribution, partners, onboarding, and support;
- finance, legal, compliance, reporting, and working capital.

**Evidence required:**

- milestone;
- budget owner;
- timing or decision window;
- dependency;
- completion evidence;
- variance process;
- stop or reprioritization condition.

**Visual:** milestone-to-capital map. Percentages or amounts should appear only when approved for that audience.

**Source:** [FUZE Round Structure and Use of Funds](12-FUZE_ROUND_STRUCTURE_AND_USE_OF_FUNDS_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** capital receipt does not assure product completion, adoption, revenue, token utility, exchange access, liquidity, acquisition, or investor return.

### Slide 14: Principal Risks and Mitigations

**Decision purpose:** show the uncertainties that could change the thesis.

Use a short matrix:

| Risk | Current treatment | Evidence or open gap |
|---|---|---|
| Product focus and adoption | Priority workflows and controlled tests | Current external-user evidence |
| Commercial sustainability | Offer, cost, and fulfillment review | Paid and reconciled delivery |
| AI, data, privacy, and security | Controls and incident ownership | Test and operating evidence |
| Platform complexity | Product-driven rail sequencing | Cross-product operating adoption |
| Token, wallet, and market conditions | Specialist gates and cautious status | Current verified mechanism status |
| Capital and team capacity | Milestone budgets and ownership | Runway, staffing, and dependency assumptions |

**Visual:** six-row risk table rather than a dense disclaimer slide.

**Sources:** [FUZE Investor Risk Disclosure](17-FUZE_INVESTOR_RISK_DISCLOSURE_PUBLIC.md) and the [FUZE Risk and Disclosure Appendix](../WHITEPAPER-PAPERS/05-FUZE_RISK_AND_DISCLOSURE_APPENDIX_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** mitigation should not be described as elimination of risk.

### Slide 15: Diligence and Next Step

**Decision purpose:** convert interest into a controlled review.

Include:

- current private round or discussion status suitable for the audience;
- evidence areas available for review;
- major open diligence questions;
- data-room access process;
- contact and owner route;
- requested next meeting, demonstration, specialist review, or document review.

**Visual:** diligence route from overview to product, commercial, technical, token, finance, governance, and risk evidence packs.

**Sources:** [FUZE Investor Readiness Summary](11-FUZE_INVESTOR_READINESS_SUMMARY_PUBLIC.md) and [FUZE Investor Data Room Index](18-FUZE_INVESTOR_DATA_ROOM_INDEX_PUBLIC.md).

**Boundary:** public or shared deck material does not establish private terms, allocation, investor rights, acceptance, or closing.

## 4. Appendix Structure

Appendix slides should answer predictable diligence questions without interrupting the main narrative.

Recommended modules:

1. full product portfolio and current status;
2. product demonstrations and sample outputs;
3. evidence register and status-change history;
4. shared-platform architecture and service ownership;
5. commercial classification and unit-economics method;
6. channel experiments and partner evidence;
7. detailed milestone plan;
8. team and operating responsibilities;
9. AI, data, privacy, security, and incident controls;
10. Platform Credits and payment treatment;
11. token supply, allocation, utility, release, wallet, custody, and market status;
12. public metric definitions and evidence levels;
13. use-of-funds detail and variance method;
14. principal risks, open gaps, and dependencies;
15. data-room index and diligence request log.

The appendix should use current source records and identify restricted material rather than reproduce it in a broadly shared file.

## 5. Evidence Rules

Every quantitative, commercial, status, relationship, or operating claim should identify:

- definition;
- source;
- product or mechanism scope;
- period;
- cohort or audience;
- environment;
- owner;
- review status;
- limitations;
- latest update, correction, or supersession.

Customer, partner, investor, provider, venue, or advisor logos require authorization and a precise description of the relationship.

The following claims should remain distinct:

- introduction;
- discussion;
- referral;
- memorandum;
- signed scope;
- pilot;
- customer;
- integration;
- deployment;
- renewal;
- strategic partner.

Screenshots should show the current product version and remove or mask protected information.

Mockups, prototypes, demonstrations, controlled tests, limited releases, and production interfaces require distinct labels.

Forecasts should state assumptions, preparation date, scenario, owner, and sensitivity. Historical evidence should remain visually separate from forecasts and targets.

## 6. Financial and Commercial Presentation Rules

The main deck should distinguish:

- product offer;
- payment receipt;
- fulfillment;
- paid delivery;
- repeated delivery;
- reconciled revenue;
- forecast;
- target.

Stablecoin receipts should retain their underlying business classification.

Investor receipts, customer payments, Platform Credit activity, FUZE token transactions, reserves, compensation, and treasury transfers should not be combined into one revenue measure.

Unit-economics slides should identify:

- product and cohort;
- price or usage basis;
- discounts;
- AI and infrastructure cost;
- payment cost;
- partner cost;
- support and implementation effort;
- refund or dispute treatment;
- period and source;
- historical, pilot, modeled, or target status.

## 7. Token and Market Presentation Rules

Token and market slides should identify the exact status of:

- contract deployment;
- token initialization;
- allocation funding;
- transfer activation;
- utility activation;
- distribution;
- wallet support;
- custody support;
- DEX access;
- centralized venue process;
- wallet-based participation.

Completion of one event does not establish the others.

The deck should not include:

- price targets;
- guaranteed liquidity;
- listing predictions;
- market-support promises;
- automatic holder revenue;
- payout forecasts;
- implied investor exit.

## 8. Design Rules

The main deck should:

- use one decision message per slide;
- prefer product screens, workflows, evidence charts, and simple diagrams;
- keep detailed tables in the appendix;
- label status, version, and source dates;
- use readable footnotes for material definitions;
- preserve visual separation between products, shared platform services, payment rails, and ecosystem mechanisms;
- avoid decorative token or market imagery before product evidence;
- avoid generic market-size graphics without sourced methodology;
- avoid dense disclaimer blocks repeated on every slide.

A short boundary should appear where a slide could otherwise create a specific misunderstanding.

Consolidated legal and risk material belongs in the appropriate slide and appendix.

## 9. Audience Variants

The same approved facts may be assembled differently for different audiences.

### Early investor orientation

Emphasize:

- thesis;
- priority products;
- current evidence;
- near-term milestones;
- capital purpose;
- principal risks.

### Product or strategic investor

Add:

- architecture;
- product roadmap;
- integrations;
- service ownership;
- partner opportunities;
- operational dependencies.

### Financial diligence

Add:

- commercial records;
- budgets;
- forecasts and assumptions;
- treasury classifications;
- liabilities;
- use of funds;
- reconciliation.

### Web3 or token specialist

Add:

- approved tokenomics;
- contract status;
- vault and release controls;
- utility;
- wallet and custody treatment;
- market-access status;
- governance;
- participation boundaries.

Audience variants should not create conflicting statuses, metrics, economics, or risk descriptions.

## 10. Review Workflow

Before distribution:

1. freeze the deck version, audience, and access class;
2. verify every status, metric, number, logo, screenshot, quote, and external reference;
3. confirm product and platform owners approve their claims;
4. complete finance review for commercial, forecast, capital, and treasury material;
5. complete privacy and security review for screenshots, customer records, and technical evidence;
6. complete legal, token, regulatory, tax, or specialist review where required;
7. compare deck statements with current public papers, evidence registers, and data-room records;
8. verify all public and private boundaries;
9. record approved distribution and confidentiality treatment;
10. preserve the superseded version and change log.

Material changes after approval require a new version rather than silent editing.

## 11. Preflight Checklist

- Does the thesis fit in one sentence?
- Are the current priority products clear?
- Does current evidence appear before future opportunity?
- Are statuses precise, scoped, and dated?
- Are product, platform, payment, token, wallet, and market claims separated?
- Are commercial paths distinguished from paid delivery and reconciled revenue?
- Do charts identify definitions, sources, periods, cohorts, and limitations?
- Are customer and partner relationships described accurately?
- Does capital map to milestones, owners, evidence, and variance review?
- Are Platform Credits, stablecoins, FUZE token, wallet records, and investment proceeds distinct?
- Is market-access status factual and current?
- Are principal risks, dependencies, and open gaps visible?
- Is protected information removed, masked, or permissioned?
- Does each appendix claim link to supporting evidence?
- Are forecasts clearly separated from historical records?
- Is the next investor action explicit?

## 12. Public Boundary

This outline is a presentation specification.

It is not:

- an investor deck;
- offer document;
- term sheet;
- financial model;
- valuation;
- token sale;
- exchange or liquidity announcement;
- statement that financing or another strategic outcome will occur.

Private terms, investor rights, customer evidence, financial records, professional advice, restricted technical findings, and sensitive treasury or security material should be included only in the appropriate qualified process.

Deck claims must remain consistent with the most current approved status and evidence even when a shorter, more persuasive, or more optimistic presentation would be easier.

## Key Takeaways

- The FUZE investor deck should lead with user problems, priority products, and current evidence.
- Product status and evidence should appear before token, market, or long-term optionality material.
- Shared-platform leverage should be supported by operating reuse across more than one product.
- Commercial paths, payment receipts, paid delivery, repeated delivery, and reconciled revenue are different claims.
- Platform Credits, stablecoin rails, FUZE token, wallet records, market access, and investment proceeds remain separate systems and record classes.
- Capital should map to milestones, owners, evidence, dependencies, and variance review.
- Risks, limitations, and open gaps should remain visible in the main narrative.
- Every deck version should be audience-controlled, source-traceable, reviewed, and preserved with a change history.
