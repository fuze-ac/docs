# FUZE Product Revenue Model

## Executive Summary

FUZE's product revenue model begins with paid delivery of a defined product service. A customer selects an approved offer, payment or Platform Credit authorization is recorded, FUZE fulfills the service, adjustments are handled, and the resulting activity is classified and reconciled before it supports management or investor reporting.

This paper separates five states that must not be collapsed into one claim: a possible revenue path, an approved offer, a tested commercial flow, paid delivery, and reconciled revenue evidence.

Platform Credits may meter supported product consumption but remain separate from FUZE token. Stablecoins may support approved payment or settlement routes. Investment proceeds, token-sale receipts, treasury transfers, custody movements, liquidity operations, and market-value changes do not become product revenue merely because funds or assets move.

This paper describes the evidence model investors should use when evaluating revenue quality, concentration, cost, retention, reconciliation, and scalability. It does not set accounting policy, forecast sales, or confirm that a FUZE product is currently generating revenue.

## 1. Purpose and Primary Readers

This paper is written for investors, product owners, commercial operators, finance reviewers, and strategic partners.

It explains:

1. what FUZE may charge customers for;
2. how an approved offer connects payment to fulfillment;
3. which records distinguish demand, payment, delivery, and revenue;
4. how Platform Credits and stablecoin payments should be treated;
5. how refunds, reserves, support costs, and obligations affect revenue quality;
6. what evidence supports management, investor, or public-safe reporting;
7. which inflows must remain outside product revenue.

The [FUZE Product Revenue Readiness](../AI-SAAS-PRODUCT-PAPERS/21-FUZE_PRODUCT_REVENUE_READINESS_PUBLIC.md) governs the pre-launch commercial gate. This paper governs the operating and reporting model once an offer and delivery route are being tested or used.

## 2. Current Public Position

FUZE's public papers describe possible revenue paths, pricing structures, operating controls, and evidence requirements.

They do not by themselves prove:

- that an offer is available for purchase;
- that a payment route or Platform Credit system is active;
- that paid delivery has occurred;
- that a reporting period has been reconciled;
- that recognized revenue exists;
- that revenue is recurring, retained, profitable, or scalable;
- that any amount is available for token-related participation.

A stronger revenue status requires dated offer, payment, fulfillment, adjustment, accounting, and reconciliation evidence for the stated scope.

## 3. Revenue Evidence Chain

```text
Customer problem
-> approved offer
-> purchase or authorization
-> payment or Platform Credit event
-> product fulfillment
-> acceptance or service-period completion
-> adjustment handling
-> classification
-> reconciliation
-> approved reporting
```

Each step answers a different question.

- **Customer problem:** identifies a task, workflow, output, access need, or service worth paying for.
- **Approved offer:** defines the product, customer, unit, duration, deliverable, price basis, limits, terms, and support route.
- **Purchase or authorization:** records the customer, contract, invoice, payment, allowance, or partner-funded authority.
- **Payment or credit event:** records the relevant provider, invoice, bank, wallet, subscription, or Platform Credit status.
- **Fulfillment:** records delivery of the product action, access period, implementation, report, campaign, event, or workspace service.
- **Acceptance or completion:** records customer acceptance, milestone completion, or completion of the applicable service period.
- **Adjustment:** links refunds, disputes, failed delivery, credits, reversals, and cancellations to the original activity.
- **Classification:** assigns the correct product, offer, customer segment, payment route, category, period, and recognition status.
- **Reconciliation:** matches product, payment, usage, support, contract, treasury, and finance records.
- **Approved reporting:** releases the resulting summary to its authorized audience.

A visible payment or wallet transaction cannot replace the missing steps.

## 4. Commercial Status Vocabulary

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue path identified | A possible way to charge has been described |
| Offer designed | Customer, service, unit, price basis, terms, and fulfillment are defined |
| Commercial flow tested | Purchase, payment or credit, fulfillment, adjustment, and reporting paths were tested |
| Available offer | Intended customers can access the offer under current terms |
| Paid delivery | A payment or funded arrangement is linked to delivered product value |
| Preliminary revenue record | Activity is recorded but remains subject to review or reconciliation |
| Reconciled revenue record | Source records were matched and exceptions resolved or documented |
| Recognized revenue | Revenue was classified under the applicable accounting treatment |
| Recurring revenue evidence | Repeat billing or paid use exists under a defined method and period |
| Retained revenue evidence | Renewal or repeat paid behavior exists under a defined cohort and method |

Do not use “revenue-generating,” “recurring,” “profitable,” or “scalable” based only on a designed offer, payment test, isolated transaction, or unreconciled dashboard.

## 6. Offer and Pricing Design

A testable offer should specify:

- customer and authorized users;
- included product service;
- usage unit, access period, milestone, or deliverable;
- input and output scope;
- quantity, allowance, or limit;
- price, quote, or pricing basis;
- Platform Credit treatment where applicable;
- payment timing and route;
- fulfillment and acceptance conditions;
- support responsibility;
- cancellation, revision, expiry, refund, and dispute treatment;
- renewal, completion, or termination conditions;
- current commercial status.

Supported pricing models may include subscription, metered use, fixed packages, enterprise or partner agreements, sponsored services, and approved event or game services.

A familiar pricing model does not establish demand or favorable economics.

## 7. Platform Credits

Platform Credits are product-usage credits for supported FUZE services. They remain separate from FUZE token.

```text
Purchase, allowance, or grant approved
-> credit balance recorded
-> reservation or authorization
-> product service consumed
-> balance adjusted
-> reversal, refund, or expiry handled
-> records reconciled
```

The ledger should distinguish purchased, included, promotional, partner-funded, reserved, consumed, reversed, refunded, expired, and corrected credits where their treatment differs.

Credit consumption proves that a product action was recorded. It does not by itself prove cash collection, recognized revenue, customer payment, or profitability.

Outstanding Platform Credits may represent future service obligations and should not automatically be treated as unrestricted economic value.

## 8. Payment and Settlement Routes

Approved routes may include fiat, card, bank transfer, invoice, stablecoin, partner-funded, or other provider flows.

The operating record should identify:

- payer or contracting account within authorized systems;
- product, offer, order, invoice, or plan reference;
- provider or settlement reference;
- amount, asset, currency, and network where applicable;
- payment status;
- fees, taxes, and conversion treatment;
- linked Platform Credit grant or product entitlement;
- fulfillment status;
- refund, reversal, or dispute status;
- reconciliation period and exceptions.

Stablecoins are payment or settlement instruments when used for an approved commercial purpose. A blockchain confirmation does not establish customer identity, product delivery, revenue classification, tax treatment, refund rights, or recognition by itself.

## 9. Fulfillment and Revenue Events

| Event | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Offer accepted | Customer and current terms are recorded |
| Order or contract approved | Scope, obligations, and authority are established |
| Payment initiated | A provider, invoice, bank, or wallet flow has begun |
| Payment confirmed | The relevant route reports confirmation |
| Platform Credits granted | Product-consumption capacity is recorded |
| Access activated | A service period or entitlement begins |
| Product action completed | The defined action or output condition is satisfied |
| Milestone completed | A contract or implementation milestone is fulfilled |
| Customer acceptance recorded | Required approval or acceptance is documented |
| Adjustment issued | Refund, credit, reversal, dispute, or correction is linked |
| Period closed | Activity is classified and reconciled |

Different offers require different completion and recognition logic. Formal revenue recognition remains subject to the applicable entity, contract, jurisdiction, accounting framework, and professional review.

## 11. Revenue Classification Boundaries

Product revenue must remain separate from:

- investment proceeds;
- token-sale or round proceeds;
- loans and financing;
- internal treasury transfers;
- custody movements;
- token allocation releases;
- liquidity deployments and recovered assets;
- grants unrelated to product delivery;
- test and demonstration transactions;
- deposits held for future service;
- partner funds not yet earned;
- unrealized token or asset-value changes;
- approved distributable value;
- wallet-based participation amounts.

These categories may appear in financing, treasury, token, or governance reports but should not be relabeled as customer revenue.

## 12. Adjustments, Refunds, and Disputes

Revenue-quality records should handle failed payments, duplicate charges, refunds, chargebacks, service credits, unused or expired balances, failed product actions, cancellations, partner settlement adjustments, pricing or tax corrections, fraud, and unauthorized activity.

Each adjustment should record its reason, authority, time, amount, affected service, and relationship to the original event.

Refund, dispute, correction, and failed-delivery rates may indicate product fit, service quality, payment reliability, support burden, or fraud exposure. Interpretation should account for product maturity, offer type, transaction size, and customer segment.

## 13. Costs and Product Economics

Relevant costs may include:

- AI model inference and processing;
- external data, API, or content services;
- cloud, storage, bandwidth, and monitoring;
- payment, network, conversion, and provider fees;
- implementation and onboarding;
- human review, moderation, support, and content operations;
- devices, integration, or venue support;
- refunds and dispute handling;
- partner delivery or revenue sharing;
- security, privacy, compliance, and incident response;
- taxes, licenses, and professional review;
- shared-platform services allocated to the product.

Investor analysis may consider cost per delivered action or customer, gross and contribution margin direction, support effort, refund impact, infrastructure scaling, acquisition cost, retention, payback, and concentration.

This paper establishes evidence categories rather than target margins or forecasts.

## 14. Deferred Service and Reserve Obligations

Collected funds or granted credits can create obligations such as unused prepaid access, outstanding Platform Credits, refunds, service credits, tax or provider amounts payable, partner obligations, implementation work still due, support commitments, remediation work, and customer data export or closure.

Investors should not assume that every collected amount is earned, unrestricted, distributable, or available for another purpose.

## 15. Reconciliation

Reconciliation should cover:

- transaction records against customer, offer, and payment sources;
- Platform Credit grants, reservations, consumption, reversal, refund, expiry, and balances;
- fulfillment, milestones, acceptance, and service corrections;
- refunds, fees, taxes, discounts, disputes, and conversion;
- AI, provider, cloud, support, partner, and other delivery costs;
- period-close exceptions, estimates, and unresolved items.

Common exceptions include missing references, duplicate events, delayed provider status, unmatched bank or stablecoin transfers, incorrect classifications, incomplete fulfillment evidence, ledger differences, and unsupported manual corrections.

A report should state whether it is preliminary, reconciled, reviewed, corrected, superseded, or recognized under the applicable process.

Reconciled does not automatically mean audited.

## 16. Revenue Reporting

Management and qualified-investor reporting may analyze:

- revenue by product, offer, segment, region, and period;
- subscription, usage, service, implementation, campaign, event, and partner categories;
- paying customers, shops, workspaces, or organizations;
- transaction, contract, and paid-use measures;
- renewal, retention, expansion, downgrade, and cancellation;
- refunds, disputes, credits, and failed payments;
- Platform Credit purchases, grants, use, reversal, and balances;
- direct delivery costs and contribution view;
- customer, product, provider, partner, and channel concentration;
- reconciliation exceptions and period-close status;
- methodology changes and corrections.

Public reporting should use approved aggregates, periods, methods, status labels, and limitations without exposing customer identity, private pricing, contracts, invoices, bank information, tax material, or accounting workpapers.

A screenshot, wallet transfer, provider dashboard, invoice, or chart alone may lack fulfillment, adjustment, cost, classification, and accounting context.

## 17. Revenue Quality Questions

| Area | Investor question |
|---|---|
| Demand | Which customers repeatedly pay for which task or service? |
| Offer clarity | Can scope, unit, terms, and fulfillment be verified? |
| Pricing | Does pricing reflect customer value and delivery cost? |
| Fulfillment | Are paid services completed reliably? |
| Retention | Do customers renew or repeat paid use? |
| Concentration | How dependent is activity on one product, customer, partner, provider, or channel? |
| Costs | Which costs vary with use and which remain fixed or shared? |
| Adjustments | What do refunds, credits, disputes, and failures reveal? |
| Records | Can money, credits, usage, delivery, support, and terms be connected? |
| Reconciliation | Are material exceptions understood and resolved? |
| Scalability | Can quality, support, security, controls, and economics survive wider use? |

Limited early evidence should be labeled accurately rather than converted into a broad conclusion.

## 18. Relationship to FUZE Token and Participation

Ordinary product revenue belongs to the product and finance operating model.

FUZE token utility, wallet eligibility, approved distributable value, and wallet-based participation use separate policies and evidence.

Product revenue does not automatically create:

- a token-holder entitlement;
- approved distributable value;
- a wallet claim;
- staking income;
- fixed or guaranteed yield;
- an automatic payment;
- a company dividend;
- token demand, price, liquidity, or listing.

Any later token-related treatment would require a separate approved and activated process with defined deductions, reserves, governance, accounting, legal, treasury, privacy, technical, eligibility, and reporting controls.

## 19. Ownership and Control

A mature revenue process should identify owners for:

- product and offer approval;
- pricing and commercial terms;
- payment and Platform Credit operations;
- fulfillment and customer acceptance;
- refunds, disputes, and corrections;
- ledger and finance classification;
- reconciliation and period close;
- privacy and access control;
- public and investor reporting;
- correction, suspension, and escalation.

Material changes to pricing, payment routes, recognition events, Platform Credit rules, refund treatment, or reporting methods should be approved, versioned, tested, and communicated to the affected audience.

## 20. Public Boundary

This paper describes possible FUZE commercial models and the evidence needed to evaluate them.

It does not state that:

- a particular product is available for purchase;
- FUZE has recognized product revenue;
- a path has achieved demand, retention, margin, growth, or profitability;
- a payment route or Platform Credit system is live;
- revenue will become distributable to FUZE token holders;
- any forecast, target, valuation, or investor return will be achieved.

Commercial availability, prices, routes, scope, terms, accounting treatment, tax treatment, and reporting depend on the current approved offer, operating entity, contract, jurisdiction, period, and evidence.

## Key Takeaways

- FUZE product revenue connects customer need, offer, payment or credit, fulfillment, adjustment, classification, reconciliation, and reporting.
- A possible path, tested flow, paid delivery, reconciled record, and recognized revenue are different states.
- Platform Credits meter supported product usage but remain separate from FUZE token and cash revenue.
- Stablecoin transfers require customer, product, fulfillment, treasury, fee, refund, and accounting context.
- Investment proceeds, token-sale receipts, treasury transfers, custody movements, liquidity operations, and market-value changes are not product revenue.
- Revenue quality depends on repeat use, retention, delivery cost, support, refunds, concentration, and record integrity.
- Product revenue does not automatically create a token-holder entitlement, approved distributable value, yield, or payment.
